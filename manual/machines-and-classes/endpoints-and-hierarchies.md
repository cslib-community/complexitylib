### Source-to-statement checks

Immerman's original theorem states closure for nondeterministic space at least logarithmic in input length. The inspected Lean endpoint has that lower bound and implements the inductive-counting argument on encoded configuration graphs; constructibility is not added. The source theorem and principal quantifier were checked against the author's paper, Theorem 1. [immerman1988][immerman1988]

Reingold's paper proves a deterministic logspace algorithm for undirected connectivity and obtains the standard symmetric-space corollary through completeness. The library's algorithmic endpoint matches the connectivity claim, under its explicit encoding of a positive-size simple undirected graph and two vertices. Its `SL` is **defined** as the languages logspace reducible to USTCON; a separate symmetric Turing-machine model/equivalence is not supplied. Thus `SL_eq_L` must be described with that definition. The `USTCON` completeness theorem for L is under reductions already allowed to compute in L. [reingold2008][reingold2008] [SL definition and equality](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/USTCON.lean#L142)

Shamir's paper establishes the IP/PSPACE characterization; the library reaches it through its own strict native oracle-PPT verifier and stateful-prover semantics. Those semantics support the same class endpoint without claiming all of the paper's extra observations about the particular protocol. The abstract and introductory model statement were checked against the original paper. [shamir1992][shamir1992]

Toda's original paper establishes PH containment in randomized parity and polynomial-time reducibility to PP. The library's headline takes the standard counting-oracle form and implements one selected output bit of a #P function. These are related formulations; the exact formal oracle interface and off-encoding quantifiers are part of the library statement. The publisher's original abstract was checked; no claim is made that every intermediate formal lemma was matched to a numbered original lemma. [toda1991][toda1991]

Dinur's paper presents the gap-amplification proof and states the logarithmic-randomness, constant-query PCP theorem with perfect completeness and one-half soundness in its opening definition and Theorem 1.1. Complexitylib combines Cook–Levin with a concrete encoded gap construction and verifier, then proves the converse by enumerating logarithmically many random strings and reconciling repeated queried addresses. The final fixed alphabet is encoded by seven bits; fixed construction constants are large. No near-linear proof-length bound should be inferred from this asymptotic class equality. [dinur2007][dinur2007] [Formal endpoint construction](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/PCP/Completeness.lean#L17)

## Deterministic time in square-root space

[`DTIME_subset_DSPACE_sqrt`](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/TimeSpace/Williams.lean#L111) proves, for every injective binary input encoding and every function `t` satisfying `n ≤ t(n)` pointwise,

`DTIME enc t ⊆ DSPACE enc (fun n => Nat.sqrt (t(n) * (Nat.log2 (t(n)) + 1)))`.

Both classes allow fixed multiplicative and additive constants. The input is read-only and space counts visited work-tape cells; the statement is for native binary multitape machines. It assumes neither monotonicity nor time constructibility. The simulator tries increasing clocks, so the supplied source clock is a proof witness rather than a function that the simulator must compute. The natural square root and `log₂+1` make rounding and small inputs explicit.

This maps to Williams's Theorem 1.1 in the February 25, 2025 version; its statement and the no-constructibility explanation in Section 3 were checked directly. The library implements a block-computation driver using fixed-fan-in trees and signed masking over a prime field. The [Tree Evaluation account](../algorithms-and-memory/catalytic-and-tree-evaluation.md) explains the underlying algebraic and native layers. Neither the source theorem nor this class inclusion asserts a general WordRAM time-to-space simulation. [williams25][williams25]

## Hardness versus randomness

The library connects nonuniform circuit lower bounds to its native uniform machine classes. [`P_eq_BPP_of_isAverageCaseHard`](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BPP/Derandomization.lean#L302) assumes a language in native `E` and a positive natural `c` such that, eventually at every length `n`, its Boolean slice is average-case hard for De Morgan circuits of size `2^(n/c)`. The hardness predicate bounds agreement by `1/2 + 1/2^(n/c)`, with natural-number division in the exponent. The conclusion is `P enc = BPP enc` for every supplied instance encoding. The proof uses an explicitly computed Nisan–Wigderson design and generator and enumerates the short seeds. [nisanwigderson94][nisanwigderson94]

The [worst-case endpoint](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BPP/WorstCaseHardness.lean#L80) instead assumes a native `E` language, a real `gamma > 0`, and an eventual lower bound `2^(gamma*n)` on the De Morgan gate complexity of each slice. It concludes the same equality for arbitrary instance encodings. This is the Impagliazzo–Wigderson conditional endpoint; the source uses the Sudan–Trevisan–Vadhan route through Reed–Muller/Hadamard encoding and local list decoding. The original result and this proof route are distinct credits. [impagliazzowigderson97][impagliazzowigderson97] [sudantrevisanvadhan01][sudantrevisanvadhan01]

The circuit-hardness hypothesis is not proved by these declarations. It must hold at **all sufficiently large lengths**; an infinitely-often hypothesis and infinitely-often simulation are not formalized by this endpoint. The [circuit-methods account](../circuits-logic-and-other-models/metacomplexity-and-methods.md) owns the nonuniform models. Publisher metadata and abstracts were checked for the three cited sources; the source-to-proof route above is reported from the inspected Lean declarations and module documentation, not from a new full literature proof audit.

## Constructibility and diagonalization

[TimeConstructible](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Constructible.lean#L68) computes the binary value `t(n)` from an arbitrary word of length `n` within `O(t(n))`; it does not build the lower bound `n≤t(n)` into the definition. [SpaceConstructible](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Constructible.lean#L259) analogously bounds binary computation in `O(s(n))` workspace. Hierarchy theorems state their lower-bound hypotheses explicitly. Unary clock construction is a separate implementation step.

The hierarchy uses framed native machine descriptions and a universal interpreter certified by the computability calculus. The streaming and windowed universal simulators have different overhead results; the latter provides an `n log n`-style simulation bound whose coefficients depend on description data. [computableInO_universal_nlogn](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/Universal/WindowedComplexity.lean#L494) is a local extension of the vendored interpreter, not an identification with a separately formalized Hennie–Stearns simulator.

The broad deterministic-time theorem has explicit simulation overhead. The sharper [exists_DTIME_ssubset_DTIME_polylog](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Hierarchy/TimePolylog.lean#L309) says that some absolute exponent `k` works for every time-constructible `f≥n` whenever eventually

`(f(n)+1) · (log₂(f(n)+1)+1)^k ≤ g(n)`.

It proves strict containment `DTIME(f) ⊊ DTIME(g)` on binary words. This is a polylogarithmic-gap theorem, not the optimal textbook hypothesis `f log f = o(g)`. The explicit clock construction and simulator cost remain visible instead of being replaced by an unproved unit-cost universal step.

Native padding yields the more familiar polynomial-degree endpoints: for integers `1≤a<b`, [DTIME_pow_ssubset_DTIME_pow](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Hierarchy/Polynomial.lean#L124) and [NTIME_pow_ssubset_NTIME_pow](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Hierarchy/NondeterministicPolynomial.lean#L92) establish strict separations. Nondeterministic diagonalization also has exponential-time separation results with explicit coefficient gaps, e.g. [NTIME_two_pow_ssubset_NTIME_two_pow_of_le](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Hierarchy/NondeterministicExponentialTime.lean#L872) assumes `8k+16≤e`. These specialized endpoints do not assert the complete general nondeterministic time-hierarchy theorem.

The [space hierarchy](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Hierarchy/Space.lean#L319) assumes space-constructible `g`, `n≤g(n)`, and that every `c f(n)+c` is eventually below `g(n)`. The gap is the usual strict asymptotic domination up to constants, but the lower threshold is linear because the current diagonal holds its input and decoded description. The sharper logarithmic threshold of the standard general theorem is not established by this endpoint. Concrete consequences include consecutive polynomial-space separation and [L_ssubset_PSPACE](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Hierarchy/Space.lean#L330).

## Evidence and unresolved boundaries

The source of [Space/Savitch.lean tests](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/ComplexityTests/Space/Savitch.lean#L1) supplies a small directed path, forward and backward reachability cases, and actual stack-machine examples. [Reingold tests](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/ComplexityTests/Reingold.lean#L1) supplies connected and disconnected instances, the concrete adjacency encoding and transformation checks. These are useful nonvacuity witnesses. They do not replace a source-level assessment of a bound or a comparison to the original theorem.

The following broader endpoints remain outside the completed results: optimal general time-hierarchy overhead, sublinear-space hierarchy at the logarithmic threshold, a general `NSPACE(s) → DSPACE(s²)` class theorem, and the NEXP-to-MIP lower inclusion. The existing bounded or conditional theorems remain valid useful results; they do not complete these stronger statements.
