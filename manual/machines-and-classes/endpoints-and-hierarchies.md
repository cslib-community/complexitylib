### Source-to-statement checks

Immerman's original theorem states closure for nondeterministic space at least logarithmic in input length. The inspected Lean endpoint has that lower bound and implements the inductive-counting argument on encoded configuration graphs; constructibility is not added. The source theorem and principal quantifier were checked against the author's paper, Theorem 1. [immerman1988][immerman1988]

Reingold's paper proves a deterministic logspace algorithm for undirected connectivity and obtains the standard symmetric-space corollary through completeness. The library's algorithmic endpoint matches the connectivity claim, under its explicit encoding of a positive-size simple undirected graph and two vertices. Its `SL` is **defined** as the languages logspace reducible to USTCON; a separate symmetric Turing-machine model/equivalence is not supplied. Thus `SL_eq_L` must be described with that definition. The `USTCON` completeness theorem for L is under reductions already allowed to compute in L. [reingold2008][reingold2008] [SL definition and equality](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/USTCON.lean#L142)

Shamir's paper establishes the IP/PSPACE characterization; the library reaches it through its own strict native oracle-PPT verifier and stateful-prover semantics. Those semantics support the same class endpoint without claiming all of the paper's extra observations about the particular protocol. The abstract and introductory model statement were checked against the original paper. [shamir1992][shamir1992]

Toda's original paper establishes PH containment in randomized parity and polynomial-time reducibility to PP. The library's headline takes the standard counting-oracle form and implements one selected output bit of a #P function. These are related formulations; the exact formal oracle interface and off-encoding quantifiers are part of the library statement. The publisher's original abstract was checked; no claim is made that every intermediate formal lemma was matched to a numbered original lemma. [toda1991][toda1991]

Dinur's paper presents the gap-amplification proof and states the logarithmic-randomness, constant-query PCP theorem with perfect completeness and one-half soundness in its opening definition and Theorem 1.1. cslib-frontier combines Cook–Levin with a concrete encoded gap construction and verifier, then proves the converse by enumerating logarithmically many random strings and reconciling repeated queried addresses. The final fixed alphabet is encoded by seven bits; fixed construction constants are large. No near-linear proof-length bound should be inferred from this asymptotic class equality. [dinur2007][dinur2007] [Formal endpoint construction](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/PCP/Completeness.lean#L17)

## Apply Savitch's theorem

Import `CslibFrontier.Computability.Complexity.Classes.Space.Nondeterministic.Savitch` and work in
`Cslib.Complexity`. Given `hs : ∀ n, Nat.log2 n ≤ s n`, `NSPACE_subset_DSPACE_sq hs` turns
`h : A ∈ NSPACE enc s` into `A ∈ DSPACE enc (fun n => s n ^ 2)`. No constructibility of `s` is
needed: the simulating machine does not compute `s`, but tries growing radices for the
configuration graph and moves to the next one when the simulated machine can leave the current
one. For a machine `ntm` that only recognizes `A` within space `c₁ * s n + c₂`, with branches
that may run forever, apply `mem_DSPACE_sq_of_recognizesInSpace hs h` instead. The bound is on
deterministic workspace only; the time of the simulation is not bounded by these theorems.

## Apply the classical randomized quadratic-space bound

Import `CslibFrontier.Computability.Complexity.Classes.LogSpace.Savitch` and work in
`Cslib.Complexity`. Apply `RL_subset_DSPACE_log2_sq` to a certificate `h : A ∈ RL enc`;
the result is `A ∈ DSPACE enc (fun n => Nat.log2 n ^ 2)`. It composes the accepting-coin-path
bridge `RL_subset_NL` with `NL_subset_DSPACE_log2_sq`, the case `s = log₂` of Savitch's theorem.
It establishes a deterministic workspace bound, not polynomial time or `RL = L`; the support
argument does not apply to two-sided `BPL`.

The complete [LogSpaceSavitch client](../../CslibFrontierTests/LogSpaceSavitch.lean) applies the
general inclusion at `s = id` and at `s = log₂²`, uses the existing logspace first-bit reader to
place words beginning in `true` in `RL`, obtains the quadratic-space native decider, and checks
accepted, rejected and empty inputs. Its runtime client also checks that named runtime
structures preserve the original framed encoding and that projection certificates retain the
caller's space budget.

For machine-level reuse, [LogSpace/Savitch.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/Complexity/LogSpace/Savitch.lean)
uses `Runtime`, `SimulationRound`, `RoundSetup` and `EdgeParams` structures.
Their `enc_structure` encodings preserve the original nesting: parameters and width share a
frame before the recursion state is paired with them. Generated field and constructor
certificates drive the space proofs; generated `enc*_budget` projections retain the caller’s
workspace budget. `SimulationRound.ofRound` is the explicit boundary to the generic Savitch
round algorithm; option inspection/defaults use the shared `ComputableInSpace.budget_option*`
rules. Binary vertex width returned in unary uses `ComputableInSpace.natSizeUnary` from
[InSpace/NatSize.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/Complexity/InSpace/NatSize.lean),
also used by the inductive-counting simulator. Keep it distinct from `natSize`, which returns
binary output. Neither operation expands the original number to unary. The original input
remains read-only; these API changes do not weaken the charged workspace or stack bound. A round
either searches for a reachable overflowing vertex (`EdgeParams.overflowMode`) or, once none is
reachable, for an accepting one; its radix comes from `ConfigurationGraph.roundRadix`, shared with
the inductive-counting simulator.

The callback proofs use `space`: `query_inSpace` is `space [query]`, while `next_inSpace` supplies the existing round, initialization and representation-conversion certificates before `space [next]`. This proves linear workspace in the encoded state. It does not bound repeated state growth.

The separate state proof exposes that accounting. `length_encRuntime` gives the exact framing cost; [length_encState_le_of_bounds](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/Complexity/Space/Reachability/Bounds.lean) turns the actual stack invariant and linear depth/width bounds into a quadratic bound. The common scale is `S + Nat.log2 n + 1` for a machine within space `S`, positive even for empty inputs: `length_encRuntime_le` bounds every run state by `sizeConst k State` times its square. The radix of round `j` has `O(j + log₂ n)` bits by [roundRadix_le_two_pow](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/Complexity/LogSpace/ConfigurationGraph/RadixRounds.lean) and `length_encNat_eq_size`, and no round past `4 * S + 1` is reached (`le_of_overflowReachable`); [NatLength](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/Encodings/NatLength.lean) bounds the input counter and length by `Nat.log2 n + 1`. `QueryMachine.computableInSpace` then combines the callback certificates, state bound, termination and output correctness into the native workspace theorem.

## Follow a path in Savitch's space

Savitch's algorithm answers whether `v` is reachable from `u` within `2 ^ d` steps; a client that must act on each vertex of such a path, such as a pebbling evaluator, needs the path itself. Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Reachability.SavitchWalk` and work in `Cslib.Reachability.Walk`. The walk `step edge w` acts on a state `(cur, stack)`: the current vertex and the pending targets with their levels. Start from `(u, [(v, d)])`. A target at level `0` is the next vertex; a target at level `i + 1` is split at the first midpoint, in counter order, that Savitch's `reach` accepts at level `i`, and at the word of zeros when there is none.

The shape of the run never depends on the edges: `iterate_step_run` puts the walk on `(v, [])` after exactly `2 ^ (d + 1) - 1` steps, and `width_iterate` and `levelsLE_iterate` keep every word at width `w` and the stack at `d + 1` targets with levels at most `d`. Only the path property needs `reach edge w d u v = true`: then `eq_or_edge_iterate` says each step stays put or follows an edge. A client that checks each move with its own edge test can therefore run the walk unconditionally and get correctness from the hypothesis alone.

For the native bound, import `CslibFrontier.Computability.Machines.Turing.MultiTape.Complexity.Space.SavitchWalk`. With the edge test as a `ComputableInO` certificate over `encPair encP (encPair encBits encBits)`, as for `computableInO_savitch`, `computableInSpace_roundStep` computes a step in work space `O(|round| + (ℓ + 1) * (ℓ + w + 1) + edgeMax Se p w)`, where `ℓ` is the level of the next target. The midpoint search streams candidates through a binary counter and never lists the `2 ^ w` words. `length_encState_le` bounds the states of a run by `6 * ((d + 1) * (d + w + 1))`, and `computableInSpace_run` bounds `n` steps by the inputs plus `O(log n + (d + 1) * (d + w + 1) + |p| + edgeMax Se p w)`. The [SavitchWalk tests](../../CslibFrontierTests/Space/SavitchWalk.lean) trace a run on a four-vertex path, a run to an unreachable target, and instances of both certificates.

## Apply the exponential-time bound for nondeterministic space

Import `CslibFrontier.Computability.Complexity.Classes.Space.Nondeterministic.DeterministicTime`
and work in `Cslib.Complexity`. `exists_mem_DTIME_of_mem_NSPACE h` turns `h : A ∈ NSPACE enc s`
into `∃ c, A ∈ DTIME enc fun n => 2 ^ (c * (Nat.log2 n + s n + 1))`; given
`hs : ∀ n, Nat.log2 n ≤ s n`, `exists_mem_DTIME_of_mem_NSPACE_of_log2_le hs h` gives
`DTIME (2 ^ (c * s n))`. No constructibility of `s` is needed: the simulation lists the reachable
views by breadth-first search until no new view appears (`MultiTapeNTM.reachableViews`), so the
bound enters only the analysis. For a machine that only recognizes `A` within space `s` of the
encoded length, `RecognizesInSpace.mem_DTIME` gives the same bound. In logarithmic space,
`DTIME_two_pow_mul_log2_succ_subset` turns `2 ^ (d * (log₂ n + 1))` into `n ^ d`, which is how
`mem_P_of_recognizesInSpace` and `NL_subset_P` follow.

## Deterministic time in square-root space

[`DTIME_subset_DSPACE_sqrt`](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/TimeSpace/Williams.lean#L111) proves, for every injective binary input encoding and every function `t` satisfying `n ≤ t(n)` pointwise,

`DTIME enc t ⊆ DSPACE enc (fun n => Nat.sqrt (t(n) * (Nat.log2 (t(n)) + 1)))`.

Both classes allow fixed multiplicative and additive constants. The input is read-only and space counts visited work-tape cells; the statement is for native binary multitape machines. It assumes neither monotonicity nor time constructibility. The simulator tries increasing clocks, so the supplied source clock is a proof witness rather than a function that the simulator must compute. The natural square root and `log₂+1` make rounding and small inputs explicit.

This maps to Williams's Theorem 1.1 in the February 25, 2025 version; its statement and the no-constructibility explanation in Section 3 were checked directly. The library implements a block-computation driver using fixed-fan-in trees and signed masking over a prime field. The [Tree Evaluation account](../algorithms-and-memory/catalytic-and-tree-evaluation.md) explains the underlying algebraic and native layers. Neither the source theorem nor this class inclusion asserts a general WordRAM time-to-space simulation. [williams25][williams25]

The driver does not depend on Williams's evaluator. A schedule evaluator, `ScheduleSearch.Evaluator` in [ScheduleSearch](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/BlockComputation/ScheduleSearch.lean), is a query machine with a start state for the arguments `(N, b, σ, q)` and an outcome read from its halted state. `Evaluator.Correct E M A` in [ScheduleSearchCorrect](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/BlockComputation/ScheduleSearchCorrect.lean) requires that, for every input `w`, block width `b > 0`, horizon `q` and schedule number `σ < 3^((k+1)q)`, the evaluator halts. Its outcome must be the source's boundary decision when `σ` names the actual movement schedule and `none` otherwise, and every state through that halt must store at most `A |w| b q` bits. The halt need not be the first one. `OuterSearch.Parameters` gives the block width and horizon for each time guess. For any correct evaluator and any parameters `P`, [`Driver.computableInSpace_decision`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/BlockComputation/DriverComputable.lean) compiles the source's decision within space `c · Driver.space k A P N τ`. Suppose `X` bounds `A N b q + |b| + q` for the parameters of every guess up to `max(N+1, τ)`. Then `Driver.space_le_of_le` bounds `Driver.space k A P N τ` by `64(k+2)(X + |N| + |max(N+1, τ)| + 1)`, where `|·|` is binary length. Williams's instance is `InitializedEvaluator.scheduleEvaluator_correct` with `BalancedParameters.parameters`. A different evaluator can be proved correct against `RootDecision.decision_value_eq_boundary`, which gives the block computation's root decision for any schedule.

## Simultaneous time and space, and the alternation slowdown

[`TISP enc t s`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/TimeSpace/Basic.lean) is the class of problems that a single native binary multitape machine decides within time `c₁ * t n + c₂` and work space `c₁ * s n + c₂` simultaneously. It uses the read-only input and visited-work-cell conventions of `DTIME` and `DSPACE`. `TISP_subset_DTIME`, `TISP_subset_DSPACE` and `TISP_self_eq_DTIME` relate it to the separate classes.

[`SigmaTwoTIME enc t`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Time/SigmaTwo.lean) is two-quantifier alternating time, given in verifier form. A verifier that is linear on the encoded triple `(a, (u, v))` decides membership through `∃ u ∀ v` over blocks of any common length `L ≥ c * t n + c`. `mem_SigmaP_two_iff_exists_mem_SigmaTwoTIME` shows that the union over polynomial bounds is the library's `SigmaP 2`, so `SigmaTwoTIME` is a resource refinement of `Σ₂ᵖ`, not a parallel definition.

[`SigmaTwoTIME_subset_NTIME`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/TimeSpace/Slowdown.lean) is the slowdown step of the time–space lower bounds for nondeterministic linear time in Arora and Barak, Section 5.4. If `NTIME encBits id ⊆ DTIME encBits t'` and `t` is time-constructible, then `Σ₂TIME(t) ⊆ NTIME(τ)` whenever `n + t n + t'(O(n + t n)) = O(τ n)`. `SigmaTwoTIME_succ_pow_subset_NTIME_succ_pow` gives the polynomial form, `Σ₂TIME((n+1)^a) ⊆ NTIME((n+1)^b)` for `t' m ^ q = O((m+1)^p)` with `0 < q`, `1 ≤ a ≤ b` and `a * p ≤ b * q`. The proof pads virtually: the existential certificate already makes the universal block's instance long, as the module documentation explains. [`TISP_subset_SigmaTwoTIME`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/TimeSpace/Speedup.lean) is the speedup step: `TISP(t, s) ⊆ Σ₂TIME(τ)` whenever `(√t + 1) · (s + (n + 1) (log n)² + (log t)²) = O(τ n)`. The existential player guesses `√t` configurations of the universal machine, and the universal player checks one block of `√t` steps through `mem_SigmaTwoTIME_of_chain`. This is weaker than the textbook `Σ₂TIME(√(t s))`, because each simulated step copies a configuration of `O(s + log n)` bits. It still gives the Arora–Barak instance `TISP_pow_twelve_subset_SigmaTwoTIME`, `TISP(n¹², n²) ⊆ Σ₂TIME(n⁸)`. The general form `mem_SigmaTwoTIME_of_virtualRun` reads the simulated input through an oracle on a short parameter, which virtual padding needs. The lower bound `NTIME(n) ⊄ TISP(n^1.2, n^0.2)` and the `√(t s)` speedup remain open ([#2896](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/2896)). [Checked clients](../../CslibFrontierTests/TimeSpace.lean) place parity in linear time and constant space, in `Σ₂TIME(n)` and in `Σ₂ᵖ`, give a problem outside `TISP(n + 1, n + 1)`, instantiate the slowdown lemma at the Arora–Barak exponents, and place parity in `Σ₂TIME(n⁸)` through the speedup.

## Hardness versus randomness

The library connects nonuniform circuit lower bounds to its native uniform machine classes. [`P_eq_BPP_of_isAverageCaseHard`](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BPP/Derandomization.lean#L302) assumes a language in native `E` and a positive natural `c` such that, eventually at every length `n`, its Boolean slice is average-case hard for De Morgan circuits of size `2^(n/c)`. The hardness predicate bounds agreement by `1/2 + 1/2^(n/c)`, with natural-number division in the exponent. The conclusion is `P enc = BPP enc` for every supplied instance encoding. The proof uses an explicitly computed Nisan–Wigderson design and generator and enumerates the short seeds. [nisanwigderson94][nisanwigderson94]

The [worst-case endpoint](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BPP/WorstCaseHardness.lean#L80) instead assumes a native `E` language, a real `gamma > 0`, and an eventual lower bound `2^(gamma*n)` on the De Morgan gate complexity of each slice. It concludes the same equality for arbitrary instance encodings. This is the Impagliazzo–Wigderson conditional endpoint; the source uses the Sudan–Trevisan–Vadhan route through Reed–Muller/Hadamard encoding and local list decoding. The original result and this proof route are distinct credits. [impagliazzowigderson97][impagliazzowigderson97] [sudantrevisanvadhan01][sudantrevisanvadhan01]

The circuit-hardness hypothesis is not proved by these declarations. It must hold at **all sufficiently large lengths**; an infinitely-often hypothesis and infinitely-often simulation are not formalized by this endpoint. The [circuit-methods account](../circuits-logic-and-other-models/metacomplexity-and-methods.md) owns the nonuniform models. Publisher metadata and abstracts were checked for the three cited sources; the source-to-proof route above is reported from the inspected Lean declarations and module documentation, not from a new full literature proof audit.

## Constructibility and diagonalization

[TimeConstructible](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Constructible.lean#L68) computes the binary value `t(n)` from an arbitrary word of length `n` within `O(t(n))`; it does not build the lower bound `n≤t(n)` into the definition. [SpaceConstructible](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Constructible.lean#L259) analogously bounds binary computation in `O(s(n))` workspace. Hierarchy theorems state their lower-bound hypotheses explicitly. Unary clock construction is a separate implementation step. Constants, `n`, `(n + 1) ^ k` for every `k`, `2 ^ n` and `2 ^ (2 ^ n)` are time-constructible; the routines behind them write the length of a word in binary and convert between binary and unary in linear time ([BigO/Constructible.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/Complexity/BigO/Constructible.lean)).

The hierarchy uses framed native machine descriptions and a universal interpreter certified by the computability calculus. The streaming and windowed universal simulators have different overhead results; the latter provides an `n log n`-style simulation bound whose coefficients depend on description data. [computableInO_universal_nlogn](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/Universal/WindowedComplexity.lean#L494) is a local extension of the vendored interpreter, not an identification with a separately formalized Hennie–Stearns simulator.

The broad deterministic-time theorem has explicit simulation overhead. The sharper [exists_DTIME_ssubset_DTIME_polylog](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Hierarchy/TimePolylog.lean#L309) says that some absolute exponent `k` works for every time-constructible `f≥n` whenever eventually

`(f(n)+1) · (log₂(f(n)+1)+1)^k ≤ g(n)`.

It proves strict containment `DTIME(f) ⊊ DTIME(g)` on binary words. This is a polylogarithmic-gap theorem, not the optimal textbook hypothesis `f log f = o(g)`. The explicit clock construction and simulator cost remain visible instead of being replaced by an unproved unit-cost universal step.

Native padding yields the more familiar polynomial-degree endpoints: for integers `1≤a<b`, [DTIME_pow_ssubset_DTIME_pow](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Hierarchy/Polynomial.lean#L124) and [NTIME_pow_ssubset_NTIME_pow](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Hierarchy/NondeterministicPolynomial.lean#L92) establish strict separations. Nondeterministic diagonalization also has exponential-time separation results with explicit coefficient gaps, e.g. [NTIME_two_pow_ssubset_NTIME_two_pow_of_le](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Hierarchy/NondeterministicExponentialTime.lean#L872) assumes `8k+16≤e`. These specialized endpoints do not assert the complete general nondeterministic time-hierarchy theorem.

Deterministic padding goes through the translation lemma [`DTIME_comp_subset_of_subset`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Padding/Translation.lean): if `DTIME(f₁) ⊆ DTIME(f₂)` with `f₁` monotone and `n ≤ f₁(n), f₂(n)`, then `DTIME(f₁ ∘ G) ⊆ DTIME(f₂ ∘ G)` for every `G` with `n < G(n)` that is computable in polynomial time in binary. Inputs are padded to length exactly `G(n)`, and the guard and writer cost only constant factors, so the transfer loses no gap. `DTIME_pow_mul_subset_of_subset` is its instance at `G(n) = (n + 1)^k`.

Translation also sharpens the polylogarithmic gap at polynomial bounds. [`DTIME_pow_ssubset_DTIME_of_pow_mul_log_rpow_le`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Hierarchy/Translational.lean) proves `DTIME(n^a) ⊊ DTIME(g)` for every integer `a ≥ 1` and real `ε > 0` whenever `n^a (ln n)^ε ≤ g(n)` eventually. It follows from `DTIME_pow_ssubset_DTIME_mul_iterSqrtLog_pow`, `DTIME(n^a) ⊊ DTIME((n · r_p(n))^a)`, where `r_p = iterSqrtLog p` applies `v ↦ ⌊√v⌋ + 1` to `⌊log₂ n⌋ + 1` `p` times and so lies within constant factors of `(log n)^(1/2^p)`. A collapse would travel along the padding chain `g₀(n) = n + 1`, `g_{j+1}(n) = g_j(n) · r_p(g_j(n))` and, after `2^p (k + 1)` steps, cross the polylogarithmic gap of `exists_DTIME_ssubset_DTIME_polylog`. At `2^n` the chain is additive, `g_{j+1}(n) = g_j(n) + ⌊log₂(g_j(n) + 1)⌋ / q + 1`, and `DTIME_two_pow_ssubset_DTIME_of_two_pow_mul_rpow_le` proves `DTIME(2^n) ⊊ DTIME(g)` whenever `2^n n^ε ≤ g(n)` eventually, again a `(log t)^ε` gap at `t = 2^n`. This covers the bounds `n^a` and `2^n` only. It does not give a `(log t)^ε` gap for every time-constructible `t`, and it is not W. J. Paul's hierarchy for a fixed number of tapes, which remains open under [#1572](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/1572).

The [space hierarchy](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Hierarchy/Space.lean) `DSPACE_ssubset_DSPACE` assumes space-constructible `g`, `⌊log₂ n⌋≤g(n)`, and that every `c f(n)+c` is eventually below `g(n)`: the textbook gap and logarithmic threshold. The diagonal reads its input in place. It diagonalizes only over *tiny* descriptions, of at most `log₂(log₂ n+1)` bits ([TinyDesc.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Hierarchy/TinyDesc.lean)), so decoding takes logarithmic space; the input of the budgeted universal machine is recomputed on demand rather than stored. Space-constructible bounds are closed under sums and constant multiples, and `⌊log₂ n⌋^(k+1)` is space-constructible ([Constructible/Space.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Constructible/Space.lean)). Concrete consequences include `L_ssubset_DSPACE_log2_sq`, the polylogarithmic chain `DSPACE_log2_pow_ssubset_DSPACE_log2_pow_succ`, consecutive polynomial-space separation and `L_ssubset_PSPACE`; [the checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/SpaceHierarchy.lean) instantiate them. For nondeterministic space, the [diagonal](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Hierarchy/NondeterministicSpace.lean) `nspaceDiagonal G` asks whether the tiny description accepts within the budget of `G(n)` bits for its configurations, a reachability question that needs no clock. Under the same gap and `4⌊log₂ n⌋≤G(n)` no nondeterministic machine within space `O(f)` recognizes it, so it is not in `NSPACE f` (`nspaceDiagonal_notMem_NSPACE`; [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/NondeterministicSpaceHierarchy.lean)). Its membership in `NSPACE G`, and so the nondeterministic space hierarchy itself, is not yet established ([#4090](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4090)).

## Exponential-time completeness and separations

[`BoundedHalting`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Problems/BoundedHalting.lean) is the set of bit words `frame (eDesc d) ++ frame x ++ c` such that the binary machine described by `d` accepts `x` within `binaryValue c` steps. Acceptance means the universal function reports a halted run with output `[true]`. Malformed words are read through the total decoders, so the problem is a plain `Set (List Bool)`. `mk_desc_mem_iff` relates membership on real machine descriptions to `runFrom`. The step bound is written in binary, and that is essential: with a unary bound the simulation is polynomial and the problem is in `P`.

`mem_E` caps the step bound at `2^|w|`, which cannot change the answer because the bound is written inside `w`, and applies the `E` padding lemma. `polyTimeReducible` takes any problem decided within time `c₁ · 2^(p n) + c₂`, with `p` polynomial-time in unary. It maps an instance to the decider's description, the framed encoded instance and `p(n) + c₁ + c₂ + 1` ones. `p n = k n` and `p n = n^k` give `polyTimeHard_E` and `polyTimeHard_EXP`, and with membership `polyTimeComplete_E` and `polyTimeComplete_EXP`. `notMem_P` follows from `P_ssubset_EXP`. To prove another problem `C` hard for `EXP`, reduce bounded halting to it and apply `BoundedHalting.polyTimeHard_EXP.trans` (`PolyTimeHard.trans`).

[`EXP.Separation`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/EXP/Separation.lean) proves `P_ssubset_E` with the existing diagonal problem. It proves `E_ssubset_EXP` with the clocked time diagonal for the constructible clock `2^((n+1)^2)`, whose running time is within `2^(n^3)` from `n = 5` on. With bounded halting this gives Book's theorem that `E` is not closed under polynomial-time many-one reductions (`exists_polyTimeReducible_mem_E_notMem_E`). Hence `E` differs from every class closed under them (`E_ne_of_polyTimeReducible_mem`), including `NP` and `PSPACE`. These are inequalities only; neither inclusion is claimed. The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/BoundedHalting.lean) decides two accepted instances of a concrete one-step machine, rejects every instance with step bound zero, and reduces the hierarchy's diagonal problem.

## Immunity and complete sets

[`Immune`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Immune.lean) defines `IsImmune C A` for an arbitrary class `C` of sets: `A` is infinite and every member of `C` contained in `A` is finite. `IsBiImmune C A` asks the same of `A` and `Aᶜ`. For `C = P enc`, `isBiImmune_P_iff` gives the form diagonalizations use: every infinite problem in `P` meets `A` and leaves it.

[`Immune.Construction`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Immune/Construction.lean) builds `BiImmune.language` by priority diagonalization over Ladner's index enumeration of framed machine descriptions. Requirement `2i` (resp. `2i+1`) asks for a word accepted by machine `i` inside (resp. outside) the language. At stage `n`, the least requirement `e < n` that has not acted and whose machine accepts the `n`-th word within `n` steps acts. `language_mem_E` places the language in native `E = ⋃ₖ DTIME(2^(kn))` on bit words, not only in `EXP`. `isBiImmune_language` proves `P`-bi-immunity. The `E` bound uses the course-of-values combinator for the action table and the `E` padding lemma `setOf_mem_E_of_computableInPolyTime_pad`. This is the Balcázar–Schöning existence result for `E`; no stronger `DTIME(t)`-bi-immunity is claimed.

[`Immune.Complete`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Immune/Complete.lean) proves Berman's theorem for polynomial-time many-one completeness (`PolyTimeComplete`, on `encBits`): a problem complete for `E` or `EXP` has an infinite subset in `P`, and so does its complement. Hence it is neither `P`-immune nor `P`-bi-immune. The test runs the machine described at the start of the input on the input itself, using the universal function, with an exponential clock. Padding puts the test in `E` (resp. `EXP`), so a reduction maps it to the complete problem, and the reduction applied to its own padded description lengthens it. `BiImmune.not_polyTimeComplete_E_language` and its `EXP`/hardness variants combine the two modules. Bounded halting with a binary time bound is a library witness for the hypothesis: `BoundedHalting.exists_infinite_subset_mem_P` and `BoundedHalting.not_isBiImmune_P` apply the theorem to it, although it is not in `P` (`BoundedHalting.notMem_P`). The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/Immune.lean) states small immune and non-immune cases, a concrete non-member, and the meeting of a concrete infinite `P` problem.

[`Immune.Core`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Immune/Core.lean) defines `IsComplexityCore C A X`: every member of `C` contained in `A` or in `Aᶜ` meets `X` in a finite set. When `A` and `Aᶜ` are infinite, the universe is a core if and only if `A` is `C`-bi-immune (`isBiImmune_iff_isComplexityCore_univ`). Both infiniteness hypotheses are needed for a general `C`: for `C` the finite sets, `A = ∅` has the universe as a core but is not immune. `exists_infinite_isComplexityCore_subset` is the class-generic core theorem, generalizing Lynch's Lemma 1 by a standard diagonal argument. For an arbitrary countable class containing the finite sets and closed under unions, a set outside the class has an infinite *proper* core, one inside the set. The proof enumerates the class and picks fresh points outside finite unions of members. `P` is such a class because it is countable (`countable_P`, from `Turing.MultiTapeTM.countable_setOf_isDecidable` in [`Halting.Countable`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/Halting/Countable.lean): machines have encoded descriptions). [`Immune.PolyTimeCore`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Immune/PolyTimeCore.lean) gives the machine form of Lynch's polynomial complexity cores (J. ACM 1975). `IsPolyTimeCore enc A X` says that every finite-state binary machine deciding `A` halts within a polynomially bounded `p (|x|)` steps on only finitely many `x ∈ X`. For decidable `A` this coincides with `IsComplexityCore (P enc) A X` (`isPolyTimeCore_iff_isComplexityCore`). The forward direction simulates the decider with a polynomial clock in the universal function. The converse runs the decider of `A` outside a `P` subset, through the plain per-input case-distinction `ComputableInO.ite`. For undecidable `A` every set is a machine core, which the tests check for the halting problem. `notMem_P_iff_exists_infinite_isPolyTimeCore` states Lynch's Lemma 1 (§3) in a proper-core form. Lynch gives a recursive set outside `P` an infinite *recursive* core among all words. The core in that statement lies inside the set, which is stronger, but it is not claimed to be recursive. Lynch allows any deterministic machine; the Lean statement uses binary machines as in `P`, which changes running times only polynomially. Problems hard for `E` or `EXP`, such as bounded halting, have infinite cores, and the `P`-bi-immune language has the universe as a machine core (`BiImmune.exists_mem_E_isPolyTimeCore_univ`).

[`Immune.RecursiveCore`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Immune/RecursiveCore.lean) makes the core recursive for problems on bit strings: `exists_infinite_isDecidable_isPolyTimeCore` gives a decidable problem `A ⊆ List Bool` outside `P` an infinite decidable core inside `A`, and `notMem_P_iff_exists_infinite_isDecidable_isPolyTimeCore` is the iff form. This is Lynch's Lemma 1 with a proper core. The proper and recursive form is a strengthening proved here by a standard delayed diagonalization; no primary source stating it in this form was verified. Requirement `e` is the problem of words accepted by machine `e` within `(2|w| + 2)^e` steps; these are exactly the problems in `P` (`RecursiveCore.exists_requirement_eq`). The decider of `A` is run through the clocked runs `Turing.MultiTapeTM.Universal.runBool` of [`Universal.Clocked`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/Universal/Clocked.lean), which are sound and eventually complete (`Turing.MultiTapeTM.IsComputable.exists_runBool`). Stage `n` decides whether the `n`-th word is selected, with every simulation clocked by `n`: the word is skipped when it is known to lie in a requirement, below the number of selected words accepted so far, that has not been refuted by a word the decider rejects. The table of the first `n` stages is polynomial-time in unary, so the selected words form a problem in `E` (`RecursiveCore.selected_mem_E`, through the shared stage lemma `Ladner.setOf_index_mem_E_of_computableInPolyTime` in [`Ladner.Stages`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Ladner/Stages.lean), which also places the `P`-bi-immune language in `E`). The core `A ∩ selected d` is decidable through `isDecidable_of_mem_E` and `Turing.MultiTapeTM.IsDecidable.inter`. The core is a core because a requirement inside `A` is never refuted, and it is infinite because otherwise `A` would be a finite union of requirements inside `A` up to a finite set. The statement is restricted to `encBits`. The construction runs a decider of `A` on every bit string and enumerates every bit string as a candidate. Under a general encoding `enc`, a decider of `A` only answers on the range of `enc`, so the construction would need at least that the range of `enc` be decidable on all bit strings, which a general encoding does not provide. Decidable cores for general encodings are left open. The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/ComplexityCores.lean) covers finite cores, a problem in `P` with no infinite core, bounded halting (including its decidable core), the bi-immune language and the halting problem.

## Uniform diagonalization and Ladner density

[`RecursivelyPresentable`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/RecursivelyPresentable.lean) defines `RecursivelyPresentable` (classes of the form `{a | F (i, a) = true}` for a total computable `F`, with the index `i` in unary) and `ClosedUnderFiniteVariation` (closure under finite symmetric differences), together with closure under complementation (`RecursivelyPresentable.compl`, `ClosedUnderFiniteVariation.compl`), uniformly computable countable unions (`RecursivelyPresentable.iUnion_of_isComputable`), and intersection/union with `P` (`ClosedUnderFiniteVariation.of_inter_union_mem_P`). [`RecursivelyPresentable.PolyTime`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/RecursivelyPresentable/PolyTime.lean) proves computability of polynomial clocks (`Ladner.isComputable_clock`), presents `P` (`recursivelyPresentable_P`), enumerates polynomial-time transducers on bit words (`Ladner.transducerIndexed`), and proves that lower `≤ₘᵖ`-cones of decidable problems and `≤ₘᵖ`-closed classes with a decidable `≤ₘᵖ`-complete problem are recursively presentable (`recursivelyPresentable_polyTimeReducible_le`, `PolyTimeComplete.recursivelyPresentable`), without importing delayed diagonalization. [`UniformDiagonalization`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/UniformDiagonalization.lean) proves Schöning's uniform diagonalization theorem for classes of sets of bit words (`exists_mem_P_ite_notMem`): for two such classes and decidable `A₁ ∉ C₁`, `A₂ ∉ C₂`, some `G ∈ P` makes `G.ite A₁ A₂` lie in neither class. `exists_mem_P_forall_ite_ne` is the effective form against two computable families. `isDecidable_ite`, `polyTimeReducible_ite_markedUnion` and `polyTimeReducible_ite_of_mem_P` give the corollaries: the mixed set is decidable, reduces to `markedUnion`, and reduces to `A₂` when `A₁ ∈ P` and `A₂` has a member and a non-member.

The construction is delayed diagonalization with clocked universal runs (`Turing.MultiTapeTM.Universal.runBool`, in `Universal/Clocked.lean`). `IsComputable.exists_runBool` makes those runs sound and eventually complete. Stage `k` targets index `k / 2` of the first family for even `k` and of the second for odd `k`. The stage at length `n` advances over the stage at `n − 1` when a word of index below `n` separates the two sets within `n` steps. The stage table is built with the course-of-values combinator, so `G`, the words whose length is at an even stage, is in `P`. `recursivelyPresentable_P` presents `P` by Ladner's machine enumeration run within the clocks `2^i (n + 1)^i`, through `Ladner.exists_index_of_mem_P`, which Ladner's theorem also uses. Computing that presentation pads the clock in unary, so it is computable but not polynomial-time. With `closedUnderFiniteVariation_P`, `exists_mem_P_ite_notMem_P` specializes the theorem to `C₁ = P`.

[`Reduction.Equiv`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Reduction/Equiv.lean) defines mutual polynomial-time many-one reducibility `PolyTimeEquiv`, and [`Ladner.Density`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Ladner/Density.lean) proves that for any decidable `B ∉ P` on bit words, its polynomial-time `m`-degree `polyTimeDegree B` is closed under finite variations (`closedUnderFiniteVariation_polyTimeDegree`) and recursively presentable (`recursivelyPresentable_polyTimeDegree`): index `⟨i, j⟩` outputs the `i`-th transducer preimage of `B` on words `w` as long as transducer `j` reduces `B` back to that preimage on all words strictly shorter than `|w|`, and falls back to `B` on longer words once a shorter counterexample appears, yielding a finite variation of `B`. Uniform diagonalization between `polyTimeDegree B` and `P` gives Ladner's downward theorem (`exists_isDecidable_notMem_P_lt`, `exists_mem_NP_notMem_P_lt`) and places an `NP`-intermediate problem strictly below every `NP`-complete language on bit words whenever `P ≠ NP` (`NPComplete.exists_NP_not_P_not_npComplete_lt`). Presenting the marked-union cone `{L | L ≤ₘᵖ B ∧ B ≤ₘᵖ markedUnion A L}` against the lower cone of `A` yields the full Ladner many-one density theorem (`exists_isDecidable_between_of_notMem_P`, `exists_mem_NP_between_of_notMem_P`): between any two problems `A <ₘᵖ B` on bit words with `B` decidable (respectively `A, B ∈ NP`) and `B ∉ P`, `markedUnion A (G.ite B A)` when `A ∉ P` (or the downward witness when `A ∈ P`) lies strictly between them. The hypothesis `B ∉ P` is necessary, because `∅ <ₘᵖ {[true]}` has no strictly intermediate problem. Only the many-one (`≤ₘᵖ`) order and many-one non-reductions are formalized here (Ladner's paper also treats `≤ᵀᵖ`); printed theorem numbers in Ladner (1975) and Schöning (1982) were not directly consulted, and statements are paraphrased in the binary-alphabet (`encBits`) and native multi-tape conventions.

[`RecursivelyPresentable.Classes`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/RecursivelyPresentable/Classes.lean) proves `ClosedUnderFiniteVariation` on arbitrary encodings and `RecursivelyPresentable encBits` on bit words for `SigmaP k`, `PiP k`, `NP`, `coNP`, `PH`, `PSPACE`, and `EXP`: `PSPACE` and `EXP` use their complete bit-word languages `encQBF '' TQBF` and `BoundedHalting`, while `SigmaP k` and `PH` evaluate uniform clocked alternating blocks by folding truth tables over `BitString.enumList`. The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/UniformDiagonalization.lean) exercises stage progression, `markedUnion` membership, two intermediate decidable degrees `∅ <ₘᵖ A <ₘᵖ C <ₘᵖ BoundedHalting`, an `NP`-intermediate language strictly below `cnfDecoder.decode ⁻¹' SAT` under `P ≠ NP`, the `∅ <ₘᵖ {[true]}` sharpness counterexample showing `B ∉ P` cannot be dropped, and the presentations of `NP`, `coNP`, `SigmaP k`, `PiP k`, `PH`, `PSPACE`, and `EXP`.

## Resource-bounded measure

[`Measure.Basic`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Measure/Basic.lean) sets up Lutz's p-measure on languages `Set (List Bool)` in the martingale form of Lutz's survey (Section 3) and of Juedes and Lutz (Section 3). A language is identified with its characteristic sequence along the standard enumeration `λ, 0, 1, 00, …` (`stdWord`, `stdIndex`, `charPrefix`). `IsMartingale d` asks for `0 ≤ d` and `d w = (d (w0) + d (w1)) / 2`. `successSet d` holds the languages along whose characteristic prefixes `d` is unbounded; `mem_successSet_iff_limsup` identifies this with Lutz's `limsup = ∞`. `IsPMartingale d` asks for an approximation `dhat : ℕ × List Bool → ℕ`, `ComputableInPolyTime` from the precision `r` in unary and the word `w` in bits, with `|dhat (r, w) / 2 ^ r - d w| ≤ 2 ^ (-r)`. Time is polynomial in `r + |w|`. Martingale inputs are characteristic prefixes, so this is time `2 ^ O(n)` on strings of length `n`. A natural-number numerator over `2 ^ r` is equivalent to Lutz's rational approximations: round at precision `r + 2` and clamp at `0`. `HasPMeasureZero X` says that some p-martingale succeeds on all of `X`. `HasMeasureZeroInE X` is `HasPMeasureZero (X ∩ E)`. Both are closed under subsets and finite unions (`HasPMeasureZero.union`, `hasPMeasureZero_biUnion`), because `IsPMartingale.add` rounds two precision-`r + 2` approximations.

[`Measure.Conservation`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Measure/Conservation.lean) proves the Measure Conservation Theorem in its qualitative form: `not_hasPMeasureZero_E`, `not_hasMeasureZeroInE_E` and, for every prefix cylinder, `not_hasMeasureZeroInE_cylinder` (Lutz's Corollary 3.14 for cylinders of prefixes). The diagonal sequence `diagBits` extends the prefix `z` by the bit whose approximation at precision `|w| + 2` is smaller. Each step raises `d` by at most `2 ^ (-(|w| + 1))`, so `d` stays below `d z + 1` (`le_of_diagBits`). The bits are produced by the polynomial-time iteration combinator, and the `E` padding lemma places `diagLanguage` in `E`. Lutz's quantitative bound `d(λ) ≥ 2 ^ (-|z|)` for density functions covering `C_z ∩ E` is not formalized.

[`Measure.DTIME`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Measure/DTIME.lean) proves `hasPMeasureZero_DTIME`: each fixed `DTIME (2 ^ (k * n))` has p-measure zero. The martingale `TimeMartingale.martingale k` is an exact weighted sum over Ladner's machine indices `i`, with value `num k w / 2 ^ |w|`. From bit `i` on, the `i`-th summand stakes everything on the prediction of machine `i`, run by the universal function for `(j + 1) ^ (k + 1)` steps on the `j`-th word. Lutz 1992 does not state this result explicitly. It follows from his Lemma 3.12(b), because `DTIME (2 ^ (k * n))` lies in the p-countable class of languages of the clocked machines, together with closure under subsets (Lemma 3.10(a)). The weighted sum specializes the proof of Lemma 3.12(b) to the clocked machines. The general p-countable and p-union lemmas are not formalized. Consequences: `DTIME_ssubset_E`, `hasPMeasureZero_singleton` and `Set.Finite.hasPMeasureZero` for finite subsets of `E` (Lutz's Lemma 3.12(a)), and `hasPMeasureZero_P`. The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/ResourceBoundedMeasure.lean) evaluates the enumeration on its first eight words. It checks a constant p-martingale that succeeds nowhere, and an all-in p-martingale that succeeds on the universe and fails on the empty language.

## Evidence and unresolved boundaries

The source of [Space/Savitch.lean tests](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/ComplexityTests/Space/Savitch.lean#L1) supplies a small directed path, forward and backward reachability cases, and actual stack-machine examples. [Reingold tests](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/ComplexityTests/Reingold.lean#L1) supplies connected and disconnected instances, the concrete adjacency encoding and transformation checks. These are useful nonvacuity witnesses. They do not replace a source-level assessment of a bound or a comparison to the original theorem.

The following broader endpoints remain outside the completed results: optimal general time-hierarchy overhead, the nondeterministic space hierarchy at the logarithmic threshold, a general `NSPACE(s) → DSPACE(s²)` class theorem, and the NEXP-to-MIP lower inclusion. The existing bounded or conditional theorems remain valid useful results; they do not complete these stronger statements.
