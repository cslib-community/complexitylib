# Homomorphic encryption: models, parameters, and endpoints

The library's FHE statements combine three obligations: distributional encryption security, circuit-level correctness with explicit noise control, and native efficiency of encoded operations. GSW arithmetic alone is not the endpoint: the current snapshot contains the decision-LWE-to-leveled and LWE-plus-circular-security-to-full theorems in [Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/GSW/FullyHomomorphic.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/GSW/FullyHomomorphic.lean) [complexitylib][complexitylib].

## Base scheme and concrete assumptions

`LWE.publicMatrixLaw` draws independent uniform `B` and secret `t`, plus independent error entries, and reveals `[B*t+e | B]`. `DecisionLWE` compares the complete row-major binary encoding with the identically shaped uniform matrix against uniform classical strict-PPT distinguishers. This is the exact parameter-family hypothesis used in the reductions; it does not assert worst-case lattice hardness or quantum security.

[abstracts/ComplexityAbstracts/GSWSecurityAbstract.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/abstracts/ComplexityAbstracts/GSWSecurityAbstract.lean) (since retired; its bridge applied [GSW/Security.lean](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/GSW/Security.lean)) exposes the fresh bit-encryption endpoint. Its security parameter `λ` gives `n = λ+1`, binary modulus width `w = 16 + 8*clog₂(λ+1)`, and sample count `m = (n+1)*w + 2λ`. The centered-binomial error distribution and full serialization are explicit. Key generation and encryption have native certificates, and encryption is a total operation on raw public-key words. The theorem gives the staged chosen-message IND-CPA security of this specified family from its decision-LWE assumption; it is not a generic claim that all bounded-noise GSW choices are secure.

The formalization follows the approximate-eigenvector construction of Gentry, Sahai and Waters. The primary ePrint metadata confirms the original construction's matrix-based perspective; the detailed correspondence and parameter adaptations here are grounded in the Lean definitions and their docstrings [gsw13][gsw13].

## Correctness, circuits, and encoding

The implementation separates matrix/gadget algebra, decryption and noise estimates, serialization, circuit evaluation and machine certificates. [Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/GSW/DecryptionCircuit.lean](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/GSW/DecryptionCircuit.lean) constructs the decryption circuit, while [Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/GSW/DecryptionCircuit/PolyTime.lean](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/GSW/DecryptionCircuit/PolyTime.lean) certifies uniform construction. The scheme uses finite De Morgan circuits, with input/gate references and a charged encoding; circuit description and complete input-ciphertext list belong to the evaluator's input size.

[Scheme.Correct and Scheme.FixedWidth](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/Basic.lean) state fresh-input correctness and fixed secret/ciphertext lengths. Correctness holds on supported honest keys and pointwise supported fresh encryptions; inputs may repeat ciphertexts or share randomness. [DecryptionCircuits](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/Refresh.lean) requires agreement with decryption on **all raw words of the declared lengths**, including earlier evaluation outputs. Native efficiency is stated separately through `IsPPTOn` and `ComputableInPolyTime` certificates for the encoded operations.

The base correctness contract concerns fresh inputs. The constructed fixed-key scheme has the stronger [FixedKey.evaluate_spec](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/FixedKey/Correctness.lean): inputs may be well-sized ciphertexts from prior evaluations under the same key, and evaluation preserves their plaintext semantics and output width. This does not assert circuit privacy.

## The bootstrappable depth family

The family used for bootstrapping differs from the earlier fresh-security example. In [Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/GSW/DepthParameters.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/GSW/DepthParameters.lean), put `t = 1+clog₂(λ+2)`, `w = 16384*t²`, `n = η = λ+1`, `N = (n+1)*w`, `m = N+2λ`, and `L = 512*t`. The checked noise inequality is

`4*m*η*(N+1)^L < 2^w`.

It leaves enough noise budget for the constructed augmented decryption circuits. The modulus is represented in binary; its value is quasi-polynomial in `λ`, while the encoded widths and supplied parameter schedules have the needed polynomial-time certificates. The concrete constants are formalization parameters, not asserted to be practical cryptographic choices.

The exported [Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/GSW/DepthParameters/Scheme.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/GSW/DepthParameters/Scheme.lean) supplies the base scheme witness. Generic `Homomorphic/KeyChain`, `Layered`, `Leveled` and `FixedKey` modules carry the bootstrapping construction and security reductions. Gentry's bootstrapping paradigm is the mathematical source; the local statement explicitly distinguishes fresh-key chains from a fixed key encrypted under itself [gentry09][gentry09].

## Leveled versus full endpoints

| Endpoint | Hypotheses | What the statement supplies | Important quantifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| [exists_leveled_of_decisionLWE](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/GSW/FullyHomomorphic.lean) | Exact depth-family decision LWE | A constructed `Leveled.scheme` covering every polynomially bounded depth family, with compact ciphertexts, correctness, native efficiency and IND-CPA | One uniformly generated decryption-circuit family and fixed polynomial widths are chosen before the depth family |
| [fullyHomomorphic_of_decisionLWE_of_circularSecure](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/GSW/FullyHomomorphic.lean) | The same LWE plus separate `Encryption.CircularSecure` | The constructed `FixedKey.scheme`, permitting all finite circuits, with native efficiency, compactness, correctness and IND-CPA | One self-loop key is used for every circuit; key generation has no depth parameter |

The leveled theorem chooses an efficiently computable budget dominating the requested polynomially bounded depth family. The separate [Leveled.schemeFamily_spec](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/Leveled/Bootstrapping.lean) supplies joint polynomial runtime in unary security parameter and depth. The constructions themselves are explicit in [Leveled.Basic](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/Leveled/Basic.lean) and [FixedKey.Basic](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/FixedKey/Basic.lean): the former uses a chain of keys, while the latter reuses one key and encryptions of its own secret bits.

The library also proves quantitative reduction bounds in `GSW/FullyHomomorphic.lean` and [Leveled.ExactSecurity](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/Leveled/ExactSecurity.lean). The former uses power-of-two `dyadicSize` sampling. The latter removes that rounding: each key-chain hop has loss `(2 * budget n) * capacity n`, while the unified `exactLeveledAdversary` has loss `3 * (2 * budget n) * capacity n`. Its strict-PPT modular counterpart has an explicit statistical error bound. `exists_leveledAdversary_overhead` supplies polynomial artifact-length bounds and component evaluator witnesses; polynomial boundedness of its composite coin-budget expressions alone does not identify them with the reduction's execution cost.

[Leveled.CoinTape](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/Leveled/CoinTape.lean) makes that execution connection for the modular link reduction's choice stage. Supply a key evaluator whose output on `keyCoins` uniform bits has the original key-generation law. `eval_modLinkAdversary_choose_eq_map_coins` then realizes the original choice using exactly `2 * indexCoins + depth * keyCoins` bits: two selector blocks followed by separate blocks for the generated keys. The selector remains binary decoding modulo the range, including its non-power-of-two bias and zero-range behavior. `exists_modLinkAdversary_choose_coin_evaluator` obtains a polynomial component budget and a native polynomial-time replay certificate directly from strict-PPT key generation. [Checked replay examples](../../ComplexityTests/Crypto/LeveledCoinTape.lean) inspect saved contexts, independent key seeds, modular bias and rejection. The original choice algorithm and its selection distribution are unchanged.

[Leveled.GuessCoinTape](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/Leveled/GuessCoinTape.lean) realizes the corresponding guessing stage. `eval_modLinkAdversary_guess_eq_map_coins` takes a choice supported by the original modular sampler and component seed laws for encryption, adversary choice and adversary guessing. Generated secret lengths must fit `capacity`. It proves the exact existing `leveledGuessCoinBudget`: `depth * capacity * encCoins + chooseCoins + guessCoins`. Each surrounding link reserves `capacity` independent encryption blocks, the selected splice reserves one fewer, and the actual `publicKeyGame` supplies the final encryption. Unused padding is discarded; repeated keys and plaintexts still receive separate blocks. The saved context, supplied challenge ciphertext, adversary state and ciphertext tag are preserved. Rejected choices return false.

The native `computableInPolyTime_modLinkGuessCoins` certificate charges the full saved state, challenge and tape, with computable unary budget families. The semantic law derives valid cut indices and chain length from choice support; it does not assert the small budget for arbitrary decoded active words. [Checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/Crypto/LeveledGuessCoinTape.lean) instantiate all component laws with randomized samplers, replay both boundary cuts and padding, and certify the combined evaluator. The complete-game refinement below handles the unified branches. Gentry's literal loss and multiplicative runtime comparison remain open in [#2585](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/issues/2585).

[Leveled.GuessOverhead](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/Leveled/GuessOverhead.lean) derives the supported guessing budget from native strict-PPT hypotheses. `exists_modLinkAdversary_guess_coin_evaluator` requires a generated target key, a choice supported by the original modular sampler, and a challenge supported by encryption under that target key. Supported secrets have a fixed width bounded by `capacity`, and the depth is `2 * budget` with computable unary budget and capacity families. It supplies computable component budgets, a native total guessing evaluator, and the exact polynomial composite seed law on those supported inputs. No globally fixed seed law for arbitrarily large public keys, artifacts or states is assumed.

The extended `exists_leveledAdversary_overhead` supplies an encryption evaluator whose common bound covers both coins and supported ciphertext length. The actual splice fits `publicKeyLengthBound`; the adaptive choice then bounds the saved state, and the fresh ciphertext plus its tag bounds the guess input. The support-local public-key-game law retains the correlation between messages, saved state and ciphertext. [Checked clients](../../ComplexityTests/Crypto/LeveledSecurity.lean) include an adversary whose coin counts grow with its actual artifact/state lengths, zero-depth rejection, and supported versus oversized challenge words. These results concern the link branch; the complete-game refinement below derives the support premises internally.

[Leveled.GameOverhead](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/Leveled/GameOverhead.lean) completes saved-tape realization of the existing `Encryption.game` with `modLeveledAdversary`. `exists_modLeveledGame_coin_evaluator` derives computable component budgets, a native total evaluator and an exact polynomial coin law in both challenge worlds directly from strict-PPT components, fixed supported secret width bounded by capacity, and computable unary selector, budget and capacity families. Target keys, choices and challenges come from the game's original sampling steps; there is no external support premise or assumed global replay law. The canonical overhead witness now also supplies the actual key-generation evaluator.

[Leveled.GameCoinTape](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/Leveled/GameCoinTape.lean) names the modular choice allocation: `3 * indexCoins + depth * keyCoins + depth * capacity * encCoins + chooseCoins`. The complete game adds one outer key-generation block, one challenge-encryption block and `leveledGuessCoinBudget`. These are padded uniform tapes: branches may ignore blocks. The original modulo-three bias, true-world message swap and saved tags are retained. A zero depth or capacity invokes the zero-link fallback; at depth zero it still calls the adversary on the singleton target key and later on the tagged challenge. [Checked replays](../../ComplexityTests/Crypto/LeveledGuessCoinTape.lean) inspect all selector tags, modulo-three bias, the swap, unused padding and a concrete 24-bit complete-game law. Both fallback tests return the actual challenge world, so they detect accidental replacement by rejection.

The native complete-game certificate charges the unary parameter and full tape. This execution theorem does not change the ideal exact reduction's factor `3 * (2 * budget) * capacity` or the modular reduction's statistical error. Gentry's literal `ℓ(d+1)` parameter mapping and multiplicative `poly(ℓ,d)` machine-runtime comparison remain [#2585](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/issues/2585).

[BitEncryption.Time](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/BitEncryption/Time.lean) gives a quantitative native bound for the existing `encryptBitsCoins`. `exists_encryptBitsCoins_time_bound` first fixes a preparation polynomial `P` from the encoded input functions, before choosing the encryption callback or its bounds. For encoded input size `M`, `m` plaintext bits, participating callback time `R`, workspace `Q` and supported ciphertext length `L`, time is `O(P(M) + (m+1)*(R + m*(L+1)))` and workspace is `O(P(M) + Q + m*(L+1))`. Thus callback time stays linear and peak callback workspace is charged once alongside the retained output. The ordinary Big-O constants may depend on the fixed callback certificate.

To apply the theorem, supply the callback's native `ComputableInO` certificate and its exact component seed law, then bound time and workspace on the actual rows and ciphertext length on encryption support. The rows always have the declared width, even when the raw tape is short: missing bits are false, and excess bits are ignored. The proof derives row-output support from the seed law. [The checked native client](../../ComplexityTests/Crypto/LeveledGuessCoinTape.lean) prepends the message bit to a variable-width nonce and covers zero width, empty messages, short and overlong tapes, and separate rows for repeated bits. This component theorem does not bound primitive encryption costs relative to adversary runtime; complete relative runtime and the literal source loss remain [#2585](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/issues/2585).

[Leveled.FlatSecurity](../../Complexity/Crypto/Computational/Homomorphic/Leveled/FlatSecurity.lean) gives a separate strict-PPT reduction with a single finite-attempt selector over `N = 2 * depth * capacity + 1` elementary hybrid steps. The first two segments reuse the original link callbacks, message swap and tags; the last index runs the zero-link callback. `sampleUniformInterval N attempts` keeps exhaustion as the rejecting tag. If `s = intervalSuccess N attempts`, every accepted index has unconditional mass `s / N`. `gap_flatLeveledAdversary` telescopes the signed gaps before taking absolute values, and `advantage_flatLeveledAdversary` proves that the new base advantage is exactly `s / N` times the leveled scheme advantage, with `depth = 2 * budget` and fixed supported secret width bounded by capacity.

With at least three attempts, the concrete loss is at most `(8 / 7) * N`; `flatLeveled_loss_lt` makes it strictly smaller than the old ideal factor `3 * depth * capacity` when `depth * capacity ≥ 2`. There is no additive selector error. `isPPT_flatLeveledAdversary` charges the unary attempt, depth and capacity families and the original native callbacks. Zero attempts abort in both worlds; even the singleton selector at zero depth can exhaust. [Security fixtures](../../ComplexityTests/Crypto/LeveledSecurity.lean) check a nonzero signed gap of `-417 / 4096`, the halved gap at a one-attempt singleton, and both worlds of the one-slot selector. [Callback fixtures](../../ComplexityTests/Crypto/LeveledGuessCoinTape.lean) inspect all three accepted tags. This is an original selector improvement; it supplies neither Gentry's literal source-parameter identification nor his relative machine-time bound.

## Review path and remaining boundaries

Start with the scheme contracts in `Homomorphic/Basic.lean`, then read `Refresh.lean` for the refresh algorithm and its decryption-preservation proof. Next check `GSW/DepthParameters/Scheme.lean` for the concrete bootstrappability premises. Follow `Leveled/Bootstrapping.lean` or `FixedKey/Bootstrapping.lean` to the generic construction, then `GSW/FullyHomomorphic.lean` for the final instantiation. Each property has separate correctness, security or runtime dependencies that can be reviewed in turn.

Existing clients include [GSWNative](../../ComplexityTests/Crypto/GSWNative.lean), [GSWDepthParameters](../../ComplexityTests/Crypto/GSWDepthParameters.lean), and [GSWFullyHomomorphic](../../ComplexityTests/Crypto/GSWFullyHomomorphic.lean). The last checks concrete widths and applies the endpoints to NAND evaluation and shared decryption across depth budgets. The decision-LWE and circular-security premises remain hypotheses in these tests.

These endpoints do not prove a worst-case lattice reduction, hardness for this exact noise family, or circuit privacy. Reviewing the endpoint assembly does not replace reviewing its supporting matrix/noise arithmetic, native certificates and security reductions.
