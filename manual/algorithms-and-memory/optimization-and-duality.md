# Linear programming, optimization and lattices

Part of the [representative algorithm endpoints](algorithm-endpoints.md); the conventions and limitations stated there apply to this page.

## Lovász theta and Shannon capacity

These are graph invariants with no algorithm attached yet. They are the mathematical side of the semidefinite route to perfect-graph optimization ([ROADMAP](../../ROADMAP.md#optimization-and-duality)). Modules under `CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/` build them on Mathlib's `SimpleGraph.indepNum`, `chromaticNumber` and `cycleGraph`; [IndepNum.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/IndepNum.lean) adds the general facts about `indepNum` they need (monotonicity under embeddings, invariance under isomorphism, `α(G) ≤ |V|`). `import CslibFrontier.Foundations.Combinatorics.SimpleGraph.Pentagon` brings in all of them, and the notation `⊠` needs `open SimpleGraph`:

- [StrongProduct.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/StrongProduct.lean) defines `G ⊠ H` (scoped notation in `SimpleGraph`) and the powers `G.strongPow k` on `Fin k → V`. It proves supermultiplicativity `α(G) α(H) ≤ α(G ⊠ H)` and `α(Gᵏ) ≤ |V|ᵏ`. Mathlib has no strong product at the pin; mathlib4#43170 proposes one.
- [ShannonCapacity.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/ShannonCapacity.lean) defines `shannonCapacity G` as the supremum over `k ≥ 1` of `α(Gᵏ)^(1/k)`. `tendsto_shannonCapacity` shows that the sequence converges to it (Fekete's lemma); for an empty vertex type both sides are `0`.
- [LovaszTheta.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/LovaszTheta.lean) defines `IsOrthonormalRepresentation` (unit vectors, orthogonal on *nonadjacent* distinct pairs, as in [Lovász][lovasz1979]) and `lovaszTheta G`. The latter is the infimum over representations `u` in some `ℝᵈ` and unit handles `c` of `max_v 1/⟪c, u v⟫²`; `lovaszTheta_le_of_rep` accepts representations in any real inner product space. It proves the sandwich `indepNum_le_lovaszTheta` and `lovaszTheta_le_chromaticNumber_compl`, that is `α(G) ≤ ϑ(G) ≤ χ(Gᶜ)` for finite `G` (`lovaszTheta_le_of_cliqueCover` states the upper half for a cover by cliques), together with `lovaszTheta_strongProd_le` and `shannonCapacity_le_lovaszTheta`. The [Knuth][knuth1994] locators in the module docstring give the sandwich and product lemmas.
- [Pentagon.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Pentagon.lean) gives Shannon's five-word code in `C₅ ⊠ C₅` [Shannon][shannon1956] and Lovász's umbrella in `ℝ³`. Together they prove `shannonCapacity_cycleGraph_five` and `lovaszTheta_cycleGraph_five` (both `√5`) and `indepNum_cycleGraph_five`.

The theta function is a real infimum without a computational interface. Its SDP and eigenvalue characterizations, polynomial-time approximation (Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver) and the resulting perfect-graph algorithms are open ([#2998](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/2998)). The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/LovaszTheta.lean) compute `ϑ` for empty, complete and edgeless graphs and `Θ` for empty and edgeless graphs. They separate `α(C₅) = 2` from `Θ(C₅) = √5`, and restate the Shannon-capacity statement of the pentagon from first principles.

## Linear-programming duality over ordered fields

Import `CslibFrontier.Foundations.LinearAlgebra.Matrix.LinearProgramming` ([LinearProgramming.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/LinearProgramming.lean)). This is mathematics with no machine model or running time. For a matrix `A : Matrix ι κ K` over any linearly ordered field `K` (so `ℚ` is included), the primal program maximizes `c ⬝ᵥ x` subject to `A *ᵥ x ≤ b` and the dual minimizes `y ⬝ᵥ b` subject to `0 ≤ y` and `y ᵥ* A = c`. Programs are plain matrix data; there is no bundled LP structure and no encoding.

- **Fourier–Motzkin elimination:** `Matrix.FourierMotzkin.exists_projection` ([FourierMotzkin.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/FourierMotzkin.lean)) eliminates a finite block of variables `y` from `A *ᵥ y + B *ᵥ z ≤ b`. It returns an entrywise nonnegative `T` with `T * A = 0` and the same solvability in `z`. One step is `exists_smul_add_le_iff`.
- **Farkas' lemma:** `Matrix.not_exists_mulVec_le_iff` ([Farkas.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Farkas.lean)) shows that `A *ᵥ x ≤ b` is infeasible iff some `y ≥ 0` has `y ᵥ* A = 0` and `y ⬝ᵥ b < 0`. `exists_nonneg_mulVec_eq_iff` is the equality form with `x ≥ 0`. Mathlib's `ProperCone.hyperplane_separation` covers only closed cones in real locally convex spaces.
- **Duality:** weak duality is `dotProduct_le_dotProduct_of_mulVec_le`. Strong duality, `exists_isGreatest_isLeast_dotProduct`, says that when both programs are feasible both optima are attained and equal. `bddAbove_image_dotProduct_iff` and `bddBelow_image_dotProduct_iff` give the unbounded outcomes: a feasible program is bounded iff the other program is feasible. `exists_isGreatest_of_bddAbove` gives attainment, `dotProduct_eq_dotProduct_iff_of_mulVec_le` complementary slackness, and `exists_dual_le_of_forall_le` the affine Farkas lemma. `exists_isGreatest_isLeast_dotProduct_of_nonneg` covers the canonical pair `max {c ⬝ᵥ x | A *ᵥ x ≤ b, 0 ≤ x}` and `min {y ⬝ᵥ b | c ≤ y ᵥ* A, 0 ≤ y}`.

The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/LinearProgramming.lean) give an infeasible system with its certificate, a feasible system with no certificate, an empty system, programs with values `3` and `22 / 5`, and an unbounded program with infeasible dual. The [simplex method with Bland's rule](#the-simplex-method-with-blands-rule) below solves these programs exactly from a feasible start.

### Basic solutions and LP feasibility in NP ∩ coNP

- **Basic solutions and size bounds:** in [BasicSolution.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/BasicSolution.lean), `Matrix.exists_nonneg_mulVec_eq_forall_mulVec_eq_zero` moves a nonnegative solution of `A *ᵥ x = b` to a basic one, whose support carries no nonzero kernel vector of `A`. For integer data with entries bounded by `B`, Cramer's rule on the Gram system of the support columns, with Mathlib's Leibniz bound `Matrix.det_le` in place of Hadamard's inequality, gives `exists_int_nonneg_mulVec_eq`: a solution `p / d` with integers `0 ≤ p j, 0 < d ≤ (card n)! * (card m * B ^ 2 + 1) ^ card n`. `exists_int_mulVec_le` gives a bound of the same form for `A *ᵥ x ≤ b`, with `B + 1` for `B` and `2 * card n + card m` columns, and `exists_int_farkas_certificate` gives integer Farkas certificates with entries at most `(card m)! * ((card n + 1) * (B + 1) ^ 2 + 1) ^ card m`. The original solution may live in any linearly ordered field.
- **The decision problem:** `Cslib.Complexity.LPFeasibility` ([LinearProgramming.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Problems/LinearProgramming.lean)) is the set of systems `∑ j, a j * x j ≤ t` with integer coefficients and a rational solution, encoded by `LPFeasibility.encSystem`, the abbreviation for `encList (encPair (encList encIntBin) encIntBin)`. `mem_iff_exists_mulVec_le` connects it to the matrix form. The `NP` check `LPFeasibility.check` takes a scaled witness `(d, w)` and tests `0 < d` and `∑ j, a j * w j ≤ d * t` for each row. The `coNP` check `LPFeasibility.certCheck` takes multipliers `q` and tests `q ≥ 0`, that every column of `∑ i, q i * row i` vanishes, and that `∑ i, q i * t i < 0`. Both are plain `ComputableInPolyTime` certificates. Witnesses and certificates have entries of absolute value at most `2 ^ ((3L + 3) * 6L)` for an encoded system of length `L`. `mem_NP`, `mem_coNP` and `mem_NP_inter_coNP` state `LPFeasibility ∈ NP ∩ coNP` with the library's classes.

The [complexity examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Computability/Complexity/Problems/LinearProgramming.lean) give a system feasible only at `x = 1 / 2`, accepted with denominator `2` and with no integer solution, and an infeasible system with its Farkas certificate. They also reject a nonpositive denominator, a violated row, a negative multiplier and two invalid certificates, and show that no certificate passes for a feasible system. Not formalized: LP ∈ P (Khachiyan's ellipsoid method or interior-point methods with bit complexity and intermediate precision accounted for) and optimization (as opposed to feasibility) as a decision or search problem ([ROADMAP](../../ROADMAP.md#optimization-and-duality)).

## Gauss–Jordan elimination and matrix rank

Import `CslibFrontier.Foundations.LinearAlgebra.Matrix.GaussJordan` ([source](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/GaussJordan.lean)). Like the duality theorems above, this is a pure Lean function over a field, with no machine model or running time. The rows of `A : Matrix m (Fin c) K` are finite and linearly ordered, and the field `K` has decidable equality. `Matrix.GaussJordan.pivotRow A j` is the first row that is zero on the columns before `j` and nonzero at `j`. `stage A j` uses that row to clear column `j` in every other row, and leaves `A` unchanged when there is none. `reduce A` runs the stages for columns `0, …, c - 1` in order, with no row swaps, so each stage is a fixed function of the current matrix.

- **Rank:** `rank_stage` and `rank_reduce` show that the stages preserve rank. `exists_pivot_of_reduce_ne_zero` gives every nonzero row of `reduce A` a column where it is nonzero and every other row is zero, and `rank_eq_card_reduce_ne_zero` concludes that `A.rank` is the number of nonzero rows of `reduce A`. A row may own several such columns, so this is a row count, not a count of pivot columns.
- **Output form:** the rows of `reduce A` are not sorted into row echelon form, so Mathlib's `Matrix.IsPivotedBy` does not apply to it.
- **Over `ZMod 2`:** `stage_apply_zmod_two` writes a stage without division, as an XOR of ANDs of entries and pivot indicators, and `pivotRow_eq_some_iff` and `pivotRow_eq_none_iff` state the pivot row as a prefix condition on column `j`. This is the form a Boolean circuit for the GF(2) rank uses.

The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/GaussJordan.lean) reduce a rank-`2` matrix over `ZMod 2` to `!![1, 0, 1; 0, 1, 1; 0, 0, 0]` and read off its rank, check that `reduce` fixes the identity, show that the single nonzero row of `!![1, 1]` owns both columns while the rank is `1`, and evaluate the division-free form of a stage.

## The simplex method with Bland's rule

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.Simplex.LinearProgram` ([source](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Simplex/LinearProgram.lean)); the dictionaries are in [Dictionary.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Simplex/Dictionary.lean) and the pivoting rule in [Bland.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Simplex/Bland.lean). Like the duality theorems above, this is a pure Lean function over an exact linearly ordered field, with no machine model, running time or bit complexity. A `Dictionary ι K` on finitely many linearly ordered variables `ι` writes each basic variable as `x i = rhs i + ∑ j ∉ basis, coeff i j * x j` and the objective as `objConst + ∑ j ∉ basis, objCoeff j * x j` ([Chvátal][chvatal83], Chapter 2, with `coeff i j = -a_ij`). Its program maximizes the objective over the nonnegative solutions of the rows.

- **Pivoting:** `pivot D r s` solves row `r` for `x s`. `equivalent_pivot` shows that a pivot on a nonzero coefficient preserves the solutions and the objective. `feasible_pivot` shows that the ratio test preserves feasibility, and `objConst_le_pivot` that entering a variable with nonnegative reduced cost does not decrease the objective. `basicSolution_pivot` moves the basic solution along the edge `ray s` by the ratio of the leaving row.
- **Certificates:** `isGreatest_value` gives optimality when no reduced cost is positive. `not_bddAbove_value` gives unboundedness when an entering variable has no leaving row.
- **Bland's rule:** `blandStep` takes the smallest nonbasic variable with a positive reduced cost and, among the rows of minimal ratio, the smallest basic variable ([Bland][bland77]). `basis_ne_of_blandPivot` proves that no basis repeats along any sequence of Bland pivots from a feasible dictionary, by the standard argument about the largest variable that enters and leaves during a cycle. Since pivots preserve the number `m` of basic variables, `add_one_le_choose_of_blandPivot` bounds such a sequence by `N.choose m - 1` pivots, where `N` is the number of variables. `simplex D` runs with that much fuel. `simplex_spec` shows that, from a feasible dictionary, it returns an equivalent feasible dictionary whose basic solution is optimal, or proves the objective unbounded.
- **Matrix programs:** `simplex_spec_of_represents` states the result for `max c ⬝ᵥ x` subject to `x ≥ 0`, `A *ᵥ x = b` from any feasible dictionary that represents the program, and `Represents.eq_zero_of_mulVec_eq_zero` shows that the basic columns of `A` are linearly independent. For `x ≥ 0`, `A *ᵥ x ≤ b` the start is `slackDictionary A b c`, which is feasible iff `b ≥ 0` (`feasible_slackDictionary_iff`), and `simplex_slackDictionary_spec` returns an optimal `x` or unboundedness. The feasible sets are written `{x | 0 ≤ x ∧ A *ᵥ x ≤ b}` and `{y | 0 ≤ y ∧ c ≤ y ᵥ* A}`, as in `exists_isGreatest_isLeast_dotProduct_of_nonneg`. From the final dictionary `D`, `D.slackDual` takes the negated reduced costs of the slack variables. `slackDual_nonneg`, `le_slackDual_vecMul` and `slackDual_dotProduct` show that it is dual feasible with value `D.objConst`, the primal optimum. `isLeast_dotProduct_slackDual` combines these with weak duality (`dotProduct_le_dotProduct_of_nonneg_of_mulVec_le`) to show that it is dual optimal. Strong duality itself is the theorem above; `slackDual` is the explicit dual solution the method produces.

Phase I is not formalized: the method needs a feasible starting dictionary, so a program `A *ᵥ x ≤ b` with some `b i < 0` is outside the scope of `simplex_slackDictionary_spec`. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Algorithms/Lean/Simplex.lean) run Chvátal's introductory program (optimum `13` after two pivots, with `slackDual` evaluating to the dual optimum `(1, 0, 1)`) and his cycling example. On the cycling example, Dantzig's largest-coefficient rule returns to the initial basis after six degenerate pivots, so the anti-cycling theorem is not vacuous; Bland's rule reaches the optimum `1` after seven pivots. The examples also cover an unbounded program, an already-optimal start and an infeasible slack dictionary. Smoothed analysis and polynomial-time LP algorithms remain open ([ROADMAP](../../ROADMAP.md#optimization-and-duality)).

## Dantzig's rule and the Klee–Minty programs

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.Simplex.KleeMinty` ([source](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Simplex/KleeMinty.lean)); Dantzig's rule is in [Dantzig.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Simplex/Dantzig.lean). `dantzigStep` enters a nonbasic variable of largest positive reduced cost (the smallest one in case of ties) and leaves by the same ratio test as Bland's rule; `dantzigRun D fuel` iterates it. Both runs are instances of `Dictionary.runWith step D fuel` in Bland.lean, whose `runWith_eq_optimal` and `runWith_eq_unbounded` prove a stopped run correct for any rule from what the rule's steps guarantee; `dantzigRun_eq_optimal` and `dantzigRun_eq_unbounded` apply them, as `run_eq_optimal` and `run_eq_unbounded` do for Bland's rule. The rule can cycle on degenerate programs, so there is no termination theorem.

- **Klee–Minty:** `kleeMintyMatrix n`, `kleeMintyRhs n` and `kleeMintyObj n` are the `n`-dimensional program `max ∑_{j<n} 10^(n-1-j) x_j` subject to `2 ∑_{j<i} 10^(i-j) x_j + x_i ≤ 100^i`, `x ≥ 0` ([Klee–Minty][kleeminty72]; [Chvátal][chvatal83], Chapter 4, with `0`-based indices). `dantzigRun_kleeMinty` shows that, from its slack dictionary and over any linearly ordered field, Dantzig's rule runs out of fuel after `f < 2^n - 1` pivots and stops at an optimal dictionary after exactly `2^n - 1` pivots; `exists_dantzigRun_kleeMinty_eq_outOfFuel_iff` is the pivot count. `isGreatest_kleeMinty` gives the optimum `100^n` of the `(n + 1)`-dimensional program.
- **Proof:** the run visits the vertices `N = 0, …, 2^n - 1` in order, and the dictionary at vertex `N` is written in closed form (`KleeMinty.dict`): `x_k` is basic iff bits `k` and `k + 1` of `N` differ, so the basic original variables form the Gray code of `N`. If `j` is the lowest zero bit of `N`, the nonbasic variable of pair `j` has the unique largest reduced cost (`dantzigEntering_dict`), the basic variable of pair `j` has the unique smallest ratio (`blandLeaving_dict`), and the pivot is exactly the dictionary at `N + 1` (`pivot_dict`). Since no ties occur, the tie-breaking conventions do not matter. `objConst_dict_lt_of_lt` and `eq_of_dict_basis_eq` show that the objective strictly increases and that the `2^n` bases are distinct.

The lower bound counts pivots of this rule only; no machine-level running time is certified. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Algorithms/Lean/KleeMinty.lean) list the `8` vertices that Dantzig's rule visits for `n = 3` and check `15` pivots for `n = 4`. On the same programs Bland's rule takes `5` and `9` pivots; exponential lower bounds for Bland's rule are not formalized.

## Extended formulations and Yannakakis's factorization theorem

Import `CslibFrontier.Foundations.Analysis.Convex.ExtendedFormulation` ([ExtendedFormulation.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Analysis/Convex/ExtendedFormulation.lean)). As with the LP section, this is mathematics over a linearly ordered field with no machine model. A polytope is given twice, as `P = {x | A *ᵥ x ≤ b}` and as `P = convexHull K (Set.range V)` for finitely many points `V`, and both descriptions are hypotheses. Its slack matrix is `Matrix.slackMatrix A b V`, with entries `b i - A i ⬝ᵥ V j`. An extended formulation (`IsExtendedFormulation`) is a system `E *ᵥ x + F *ᵥ y ≤ g`, `E' *ᵥ x + F' *ᵥ y = g'` whose projection onto `x` is `P`. Its size is the number of inequalities, and equations cost nothing. This is definition (1) of Fiorini, Massar, Pokutta, Tiwary and de Wolf [fmptw15][fmptw15]. `extensionComplexity P : ℕ∞` is the least size.

- **Nonnegative rank:** `Matrix.nonnegRank` ([NonnegRank.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/NonnegRank.lean)) is the least inner dimension of a factorization `M = T * U` with `T, U ≥ 0`, and `⊤` if there is none. It is finite exactly for nonnegative matrices (`nonnegRank_ne_top_iff`), and is at most the number of rows and of columns. It is at least the rank (`rank_le_nonnegRank`), invariant under transposition, and monotone under submatrices.
- **Yannakakis's theorem:** `extensionComplexity_eq_nonnegRank_slackMatrix` proves `xc P = rank₊ S` for `P.Nontrivial`, which is FMPTW's `dim P ≥ 1` (their Theorem 3, (i) ⇔ (iii); the theorem is due to Yannakakis [yannakakis91][yannakakis91], cited here through FMPTW). The direction `xc P ≤ rank₊ S` needs no dimension hypothesis. The converse uses the affine Farkas lemma (`IsExtendedFormulation.exists_nonneg_dotProduct_eq`, FMPTW Lemma 2). The module documentation gives two counterexamples. The point `{0}` with a redundant inequality has `xc = 0 < 1 = rank₊ S`. A segment shows that equations must be free. FMPTW state the equivalence for a positive number `r` of inequalities. In that form, `extensionComplexity_le_iff_nonnegRank_slackMatrix_le` proves `xc P ≤ r ↔ rank₊ S ≤ r` for every `r > 0` with no dimension hypothesis, because a slack matrix of a set with at most one point has nonnegative rank at most `1` (`nonnegRank_slackMatrix_le_one`). The point counterexample fails only at `r = 0`.
- **Communication bound:** see [query and communication complexity](../circuits-logic-and-other-models/query-and-communication.md#major-communication-bridges-and-bounds) for the rectangle covering bound and `N¹`.

The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Foundations/Analysis/Convex/ExtendedFormulation.lean) prove that the triangle has extension complexity `3` and the unit square `4`. For the square, the slack matrix has rank `3`. Both lower bounds come from fooling sets on the slack matrix. The hexagon with vertices `(±2, 0)`, `(±1, ±1)` has six facets, but an explicit nonnegative factorization of inner dimension `5` and an extended formulation with five inequalities give `xc ≤ 5`. The point counterexample is also checked. Not formalized: the facet form of the theorem (FMPTW Theorem 3 (ii)), and the exponential lower bounds for the correlation, cut, stable-set and TSP polytopes ([ROADMAP](../../ROADMAP.md#optimization-and-duality)).

## The central-cut ellipsoid method over the reals

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Ellipsoid.Basic` ([Basic.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Ellipsoid/Basic.lean)); the geometry is in `CslibFrontier.Foundations.Analysis.Convex.Ellipsoid` ([Ellipsoid.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Analysis/Convex/Ellipsoid.lean)). This is the geometric core of Khachiyan's method with exact real arithmetic. There is no machine model, no rounding and no bit-size accounting. The sources are Grötschel, Lovász and Schrijver, *Geometric Algorithms and Combinatorial Optimization* (1988), Sections 2.1 and 3.1, and Bertsimas and Tsitsiklis, *Introduction to Linear Optimization* (1997), Section 8.2 and Theorem 8.1. The finite-precision central-cut method of Grötschel, Lovász and Schrijver's Section 3.2 is not formalized.

- **Ellipsoids:** `Matrix.ellipsoid A c = {x | (x - c) ⬝ᵥ A⁻¹ *ᵥ (x - c) ≤ 1}` on `ι → ℝ`. For positive definite `A`, `volume_ellipsoid` gives `√(det A)` times the volume of `ellipsoid 1 0`, and `volume_ellipsoid_one_zero` identifies that set with the closed unit ball of `EuclideanSpace ℝ ι`. `ellipsoid_sq_smul_one` identifies the shape `r ^ 2 • 1` with the Euclidean ball of radius `r`, and `convex_ellipsoid` gives convexity.
- **One central cut:** `centralCutCenter A c a = c - b / (n + 1)` and `centralCutShape A a` with `b = A a / √(aᵀ A a)` and `n = card ι`. For `n ≥ 2` the shape is the textbook `n² / (n² - 1) · (A - 2 / (n + 1) · b bᵀ)` (`centralCutShape_of_two_le_card`). In dimension one the same expression is `A / 4`, which is bisection (`centralCutShape_of_card_eq_one`). For `a ≠ 0` the new shape is positive definite (`PosDef.centralCutShape`, with explicit inverse `inv_centralCutShape`). It contains the half-ellipsoid `E(A, c) ∩ {x | a ⬝ᵥ x ≤ a ⬝ᵥ c}` (`ellipsoid_inter_halfspace_subset`). Its determinant is `(n² / (n² - 1)) ^ (n - 1) · (n / (n + 1))² · det A` (`det_centralCutShape`). The volume shrinks strictly below `exp (-1 / (2 (n + 1)))` times the old volume, for every `n ≥ 1` (`volume_ellipsoid_centralCut_lt`; Bertsimas and Tsitsiklis state `n ≥ 2`, and the bisection case `n = 1` is added). Bertsimas and Tsitsiklis keep `a ⬝ᵥ x ≥ a ⬝ᵥ c` instead; replacing `a` by `-a` converts.
- **The method:** `Cslib.Algorithms.Ellipsoid.IsStrongSeparationOracle P sep` asks that `sep z = none` certify `z ∈ P`, and that `sep z = some a` give `a ≠ 0` with `a ⬝ᵥ x < a ⬝ᵥ z` for all `x ∈ P`. It then decides membership (`eq_none_iff`). `run sep A₀ c₀ N` performs `N` steps and keeps the state once the center is accepted. If `P ⊆ E(A₀, c₀)` with `A₀` positive definite:
  - `center_mem_or_volume_le`: the `N`-th center is in `P`, or `vol P ≤ exp (-N / (2 (n + 1))) · vol E(A₀, c₀)`;
  - `center_mem_or_volume_lt`: for `0 < v` and `N > 2 (n + 1) ln (vol E(A₀, c₀) / v)`, the center is in `P` or `vol P < v`;
  - `center_mem_of_ball_subset`: if `P` contains a ball of radius `r > 0` and lies in the ball of radius `R` about `c₀`, the method started from that ball has its center in `P` after any `N > 2 n (n + 1) ln (R / r)` steps.

The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Algorithms/Ellipsoid/Basic.lean) give a strong separation oracle for a Euclidean ball, one-dimensional bisection steps (shape `1 / 4`, and the center `1 / 2` after a cut towards `3`), and the ball theorem in the plane with `N = 9`. Not formalized: a finite-precision update with its rounding error, a volume lower bound for full-dimensional rational polytopes, the reduction of rational LP feasibility to that case, and any polynomial running-time bound. LP ∈ P is therefore not claimed ([ROADMAP](../../ROADMAP.md#optimization-and-duality)).

## LLL-reduced bases, lattice basis reduction and Babai's nearest-plane algorithm

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.LatticeReduction.Potential` ([Reduced.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/LatticeReduction/Reduced.lean), [Potential.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/LatticeReduction/Potential.lean)). This is mathematics with no machine model or running time, following [Lenstra, Lenstra and Lovász, §1][lll82]. A family `b : Fin n → E` lives in an arbitrary real inner product space (for example `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin m)`). Its Gram–Schmidt vectors are Mathlib's `gramSchmidt ℝ b`, and its coefficients are `gramSchmidtCoeff ℝ b i j = ⟪b*ⱼ, bᵢ⟫ / ‖b*ⱼ‖²` ([GramSchmidtOrtho.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/GramSchmidtOrtho.lean), a Mathlib candidate). The lattice is `Submodule.span ℤ (Set.range b)`.

- **Reduced bases and Proposition (1.11):** `IsReduced δ b` is size reduction (1.4) together with the exchange condition (1.5), where the constant `3/4` is replaced by `δ`. `IsReduced.norm_sq_le` proves `‖b₀‖² ≤ (4/(4δ-1))ⁿ ‖x‖²` for `b : Fin (n + 1) → E`, `1/4 < δ ≤ 1` and every nonzero lattice vector `x`. `IsReduced.norm_le_sqrt_two_pow_mul` is the case `δ = 3/4`, `‖b₀‖ ≤ (√2)ⁿ ‖x‖`. Neither statement assumes linear independence. Against the first minimum `ZLattice.minimum` of [Minimum.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Analysis/Lattice/Minimum.lean), `SVP.isSolution_of_isReduced` in [ShortestVector.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Crypto/Computational/Lattice/ShortestVector.lean) restates the case `δ = 3/4`: if `b₀ ≠ 0`, then `b₀` solves `SVP_γ` for `γ = (√2)ⁿ`.
- **Steps:** `IsSizeStep` (`bᵢ ← bᵢ - r bⱼ`, `j < i`, `r ∈ ℤ`) keeps the lattice and every Gram–Schmidt vector. `abs_gramSchmidtCoeff_round_le_of_ne_zero` shows that rounding `μᵢⱼ` gives `|μᵢⱼ| ≤ 1/2` when `b*ⱼ ≠ 0` (`abs_gramSchmidtCoeff_round_le` for linearly independent `b`). `IsSwapStep δ` swaps `bᵢ, bᵢ₊₁` when (1.5) fails, and `gramSchmidt_swap_self` is the swap formula (1.22).
- **Potential:** `gramPrefixDet b k = ∏_{j<k} ‖b*ⱼ‖²` equals the Gram determinant of the first `k` vectors (`gramPrefixDet_eq_det`, via `det_gram_eq_prod_norm_gramSchmidt_sq`). `potential b` is their product `D`. `IsSwapStep.potential_lt` proves `D' < δ D`. For linearly independent `b` with integral inner products (`HasIntegralGram`, e.g. `bᵢ ∈ ℤᵐ`) and `‖bᵢ‖² ≤ B`, `one_le_potential` and `potential_le` give `1 ≤ D ≤ B^{n(n-1)/2}`. Along any run of `IsStep`s, `inv_pow_swapCount_le` and `swapCount_le` bound the number of exchange steps by `(n(n-1)/2) log B / log (1/δ)` for `0 < δ < 1`.
- **The algorithm:** import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.LatticeReduction.LLL` ([RationalGramSchmidt.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/LatticeReduction/RationalGramSchmidt.lean), [LLL.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/LatticeReduction/LLL.lean)). An integer basis is `B : Vector (Vector ℤ m) n`, with rows `rows B` and `realRows B` in `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin m)`. `gramSchmidtRows B` computes the Gram–Schmidt vectors exactly over `ℚ` (`gramSchmidtRowsAux_spec` identifies them with `gramSchmidt ℝ`). `lllLoop δ fuel B k` is the loop (1.15)–(1.21), with stage `k` here equal to stage `k + 1` of the source: at `0 < k < n` it rounds `μₖ,ₖ₋₁` (1.18), then either swaps `bₖ₋₁, bₖ` and steps back when (1.5) fails (Case 1) or size-reduces the rest of row `k` and advances (Case 2). `lll B` runs it with `δ = 3/4` for `n + 2 (swapBudget n (sqNormSum B) + 1)` rounds, where `sqNormSum B = Σᵢ ‖bᵢ‖²` (the source uses `max ‖bᵢ‖²`). Each size-reduction step subtracts `⌊μᵢⱼ⌉ bⱼ` unconditionally, while the source reduces only when `|μᵢⱼ| > 1/2`; the two differ only at `μᵢⱼ = ±1/2`. If the input rows are linearly independent over `ℝ` (decidable by `linearIndependent_realRows_iff`, a nonzero integer Gram determinant), `isReduced_lll` proves that the output is `3/4`-reduced and linearly independent and `dotProduct_lll_zero_le` gives (1.11) for it, `|b₀|² ≤ 2ⁿ |x|²` for every nonzero `x` in the lattice of `n + 1` rows. `span_lll` proves that the lattice is unchanged, with no hypothesis. The loop invariant `lllLoop_spec` is stated for every `δ > 0` but is meaningful only for `0 < δ < 1` (its potential hypothesis fails for `δ ≥ 1`), and the round budget comes from the potential (`potential_realRows_lt`). Each round recomputes the Gram–Schmidt rows with `O(n² m)` rational operations instead of the source's incremental update, and there is no cost model.
- **General parameter `δ`:** the source notes after (1.5) that `3/4` may be replaced by any fixed `y` with `1/4 < y < 1`. `lllWith δ B` runs `lllLoop δ` for `n + 2 (swapBudgetOf δ n (sqNormSum B) + 1)` rounds, where `swapBudgetOf δ n B = ⌈δ/(1-δ)⌉ (⌊log₂ B⌋ + 1) · n(n-1)/2` (`swapFactor δ = ⌈δ/(1-δ)⌉`). Bernoulli's inequality gives `(1/δ)^⌈δ/(1-δ)⌉ ≥ 2` (`two_le_inv_pow_swapFactor`), so `potential_realRows_lt_inv_pow` bounds the potential by `δ⁻¹ ^ (swapBudgetOf δ n B + 1)`. For `0 < δ < 1` and linearly independent rows, `isReduced_lllWith` proves that the output is `δ`-reduced and linearly independent, and `span_lllWith` that the lattice is unchanged. For `1/4 < δ < 1`, `dotProduct_lllWith_zero_le` gives (1.11) with the constant of the remark after (1.9), `|b₀|² ≤ (4/(4δ-1))ⁿ |x|²` in `ℚ`, which tends to `(4/3)ⁿ` as `δ → 1`. `lll_eq_lllWith` identifies `lll` with `lllWith (3/4)` (`swapFactor (3/4) = 3`). The round count `O(n² log B / (1 - δ))` is explicit in the fuel, but there is no cost model.
- **Babai's nearest-plane algorithm:** import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.LatticeReduction.Babai` ([NearestPlane.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/LatticeReduction/NearestPlane.lean), [Babai.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/LatticeReduction/Babai.lean)), following [Regev's lecture notes, Lecture 3][regev04] for the algorithm of [Babai][babai86]. `IsNearestPlane b x y` says that `y` is a lattice point and that every Gram–Schmidt coordinate of `x - y` lies in `[-1/2, 1/2]` (`|⟪b*ⱼ, x - y⟫| ≤ ‖b*ⱼ‖²/2`). For a `δ`-reduced `b : Fin (n + 1) → E` with `1/4 < δ ≤ 1`, `IsNearestPlane.norm_sq_le` proves `‖x - y‖² ≤ (∑_{i ≤ n} (4/(4δ-1))ⁱ) ‖x - v‖²` for every lattice vector `v` and every real target `x`. For `δ = 3/4` and `n` vectors, `IsNearestPlane.norm_sq_le_two_pow_mul` and `IsNearestPlane.norm_le_sqrt_two_pow_mul` give `‖x - y‖ ≤ 2^{n/2} ‖x - v‖`, Lemma 6 of the notes. At `δ = 1` the same theorem gives `‖x - y‖² ≤ 3 ((4/3)ⁿ⁺¹ - 1) ‖x - v‖² ≤ 4 (4/3)ⁿ ‖x - v‖²`, the factor `2 (2/√3)ⁿ` that the notes attribute to [Babai][babai86], but only for a `1`-reduced basis: `lll` fixes `δ = 3/4`. The proof is not the notes' induction on the rank: it splits off the residual orthogonal to the span and compares Gram–Schmidt coordinates at the last index where the coefficients of `y` and `v` differ. On integer inputs, `nearestPlane B t` runs the loop of the notes' §1 with the exact Gram–Schmidt rows (for `j = n - 1, …, 0`, `r ← r - ⌊⟨r, b*ⱼ⟩/⟨b*ⱼ, b*ⱼ⟩⌉ bⱼ`, output `t - r`), and `isNearestPlane_nearestPlane` proves the nearest-plane condition for any rows. `babai B t = nearestPlane (lll B) t`. `babai_mem_span` places the output in the lattice of `B`, and for linearly independent rows `babai_dotProduct_le` proves `|y - t|² ≤ 2ⁿ |v - t|²` for every lattice vector `v`. The target is an integer vector, as in the notes, and there is no cost model.

The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Algorithms/LatticeReduction.lean) show that the standard basis of `ℤ²` is `3/4`-reduced and apply the SVP bound to it, and that `(e₀, ½ e₀ + e₁)` is `3/4`-reduced with `μ₁₀ = 1/2`. They also show that `(e₀, e₀ + e₁)` is not size reduced, and that swapping `(2 e₀, e₀ + e₁)` is an exchange step taking the potential from `4` to `2 < (3/4) · 4`. The [algorithm examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Algorithms/LatticeReduction/LLL.lean) evaluate `lll` and `gramSchmidtRows` on small bases with `#guard`, and instantiate `isReduced_lll`, `span_lll` and `dotProduct_lll_zero_le` on the integer basis `((1, 0), (7, 1))`, which has an integral Gram matrix and linearly independent rows. For `δ = 99/100` they show that `D = ((10, 0), (0, 9))` is returned unchanged by `lll` but swapped by `lllWith (99/100)`, that `D` is not `99/100`-reduced (`not_isReduced_D`, checked by kernel `decide`), and they instantiate `isReduced_lllWith`, `span_lllWith` and `dotProduct_lllWith_zero_le` on `D`. On the knapsack-style basis `((1, 0, 0, 1345), (0, 1, 0, 35), (0, 0, 1, 154))`, `lllWith (99/100)` returns a first row of squared norm `119` where `lll` returns one of squared norm `134`. The [Babai examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Algorithms/LatticeReduction/Babai.lean) evaluate `nearestPlane` and `babai` with `#guard`. On the knapsack-style basis `((1, 0, 0, 1345), (0, 1, 0, 35), (0, 0, 1, 154))` with target `(3, 7, 11, 100)`, the unreduced loop returns a point at squared distance `244052` and `babai` one at squared distance `35`; both distances are checked with `#guard`. The examples also instantiate `babai_dotProduct_le` on a linearly independent basis. In `ℝ` with basis `(1)`, which is `δ`-reduced for every `δ` (`isReduced_one`), they apply `IsNearestPlane.norm_sq_le` at `δ = 1` and show a lattice point that violates both `IsNearestPlane` and the conclusion of `norm_sq_le_two_pow_mul`. Not formalized: a cost model for `lll` with the arithmetic-operation and bit-size bounds of (1.26), polynomial-time statements for `lll` and `babai`, `babai` run over `lllWith δ` for `δ` near `1`, and rational targets for `babai` ([ROADMAP](../../ROADMAP.md#optimization-and-duality)).

## Motzkin–Straus theorem and matrix copositivity in coNP

Import `CslibFrontier.Foundations.LinearAlgebra.Matrix.Copositive` ([Copositive.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Copositive.lean)) for bounded integer witnesses, `CslibFrontier.Foundations.Combinatorics.SimpleGraph.MotzkinStraus` ([MotzkinStraus.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/MotzkinStraus.lean)) for the graph theorem and `CslibFrontier.Computability.Complexity.Problems.Copositivity` ([Copositivity.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Problems/Copositivity.lean)) for the complexity results. The complexity endpoints use `encSymRatMatrix`, the binary encoding of symmetric rational matrices via row lists (`SymRatMatrix`), and prove `NP`-completeness of non-copositivity and `coNP`-completeness of copositivity; [Motzkin–Straus][motzkinstraus1965] and [Murty–Kabadi][murtykabadi1987] are cited at paper level from bibliographic metadata without inspecting their published text.

- **Motzkin–Straus identity:** for a finite simple graph `G` on a nonempty vertex type `V` over any linearly ordered field `K`, `SimpleGraph.quadraticForm_adjMatrix_le_stdSimplex` bounds `x ⬝ᵥ G.adjMatrix K *ᵥ x` by `1 - 1 / G.cliqueNum` on `Convexity.StdSimplex K V` using support-shrinking along nonadjacent pairs and Cauchy–Schwarz on a clique support, and `SimpleGraph.exists_stdSimplex_quadraticForm_adjMatrix_eq_of_isNClique` attains it uniformly on a clique. `SimpleGraph.isGreatest_quadraticForm_adjMatrix` (and `SimpleGraph.sSup_quadraticForm_adjMatrix` over `ℝ`) gives `max_{x : StdSimplex K V} xᵀ A_G x = 1 - 1 / ω(G)` (differing by a factor of `2` from [Motzkin–Straus][motzkinstraus1965], which maximizes `∑_{ij ∈ E} x_i x_j = (1 / 2) xᵀ A_G x`). `SimpleGraph.copositiveCliqueMatrix G k` packages `(k - 2) J - (k - 1) A_G`, and `SimpleGraph.exists_nonneg_dotProduct_copositiveCliqueMatrix_lt_zero_iff` proves it is non-copositive iff `k ≤ G.cliqueNum` for `2 ≤ k`; both the arbitrary-`K` generalization and this matrix packaging are original to this library.
- **Bounded integer witnesses:** `Matrix.IsCopositive` holds when `0 ≤ x ⬝ᵥ M *ᵥ x` for all entrywise nonnegative vectors `x`. For a symmetric integer matrix `M` on any `[Fintype V]` with `n = Fintype.card V` and `|M i j| ≤ B` that is not copositive over `ℝ`, `Matrix.exists_nat_dotProduct_lt_zero_of_isSymm` uses first-order optimality on the support of a simplex minimizer and Cramer's rule (`Matrix.exists_int_nonneg_mulVec_eq`) to produce a nonnegative integer vector `p` with `p i ≤ n! · (n · max B 1 ^ 2 + 1) ^ n` and `p ⬝ᵥ M *ᵥ p < 0`; this KKT + basic-solution proof and explicit bound are original to this library. `Matrix.exists_nat_dotProduct_lt_zero_of_rat` and `Matrix.isCopositive_rat_iff_map_real` transfer the bound and real-to-rational equivalence to denominator-cleared symmetric rational matrices.
- **`coNP`-completeness of copositivity:** `Cslib.Complexity.Copositive` and `Cslib.Complexity.NonCopositive` classify `SymRatMatrix` under `encSymRatMatrix`. `NonCopositive.check` scales by `SymRatMatrix.commonDenom` and checks `p.length = M.rows.length` and `p ⬝ᵥ M.intRows *ᵥ p < 0`, yielding `NonCopositive.mem_NP` and `Copositive.mem_coNP`. `cliqueToSymRatMatrix` reduces `(G, k) ∈ Clique` to `NonCopositive` in polynomial time (`polyTimeReducible_Clique_NonCopositive`), giving `NonCopositive_npComplete` and `Copositive_coNPComplete` (the completeness statements are credited to [Murty–Kabadi][murtykabadi1987], which secondary summaries describe as reducing from subset sum; the `Clique`/Motzkin–Straus proof and bounded-witness verification are original to this library). `Cslib.Circuits.encRatArithCircuit` and `Cslib.Circuits.encRatArithCircuitInput` (in [Arithmetic/Encoding.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Encoding.lean), over `Turing.MultiTapeTM.encRat` from [Encodings/Rat.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/Encodings/Rat.lean)) provide binary encodings for rational arithmetic circuits and circuit-input pairs.

The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Copositivity.lean) verify Motzkin–Straus values on `K₃`, `C₄` and the edgeless graph on three vertices, evaluate `NonCopositive.check` on concrete copositive and non-copositive matrices, instantiate `Matrix.exists_nat_dotProduct_lt_zero_of_isSymm` and `cliqueToSymRatMatrix_mem_iff` on positive, negative and boundary instances, and exercise the headline completeness theorems and `encRatArithCircuitInput`. Not formalized: quadratic-programming optimization over arbitrary polyhedra and non-symmetric copositivity membership.
