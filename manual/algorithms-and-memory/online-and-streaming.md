# Online and streaming models

## Deterministic online strategies

[Complexity/DataStructure/Online/Basic.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/DataStructure/Online/Basic.lean) gives a `Problem` with state, requests, admissible initial states/transitions and a common step cost. `DetStrategy` has hidden memory and responds to the current request; it cannot inspect future requests through its interface. Competitive predicates compare its accumulated cost against every admissible offline schedule, either from the same initial state or arbitrary valid initial states. The basic coefficients are natural multiplicative/additive bounds; paging also has explicit rational-ratio impossibility theorems [complexitylib][complexitylib].

`adaptiveRequests` constructs a request list by observing the deterministic strategy. Replaying that list produces the same deterministic behavior. This is suitable for deterministic lower bounds; it is not a randomized online-adversary interface identifying adaptive and oblivious adversaries in expectation.

## Ski rental

[Complexity/DataStructure/Online/SkiRental.lean](../../Complexity/DataStructure/Online/SkiRental.lean) instantiates the online problem for rent-or-buy. Each request is a ski day, the state records whether the skis are owned, buying cannot be undone, a rented day costs `1` and buying costs `B`. Offline schedules live in the same model, so the optimum on `n` days is `min n B` (`exists_optimal_schedule`, `min_le_scheduleCost`). `cost_rentThenBuy` computes the cost of every rent-then-buy threshold strategy exactly. The break-even strategy (`breakEven`, rent for `B - 1` days) satisfies `B * ALG ≤ (2B - 1) * OPT` against every valid offline schedule (`breakEven_mul_cost_le`). This is ratio `2 - 1/B` with no additive constant, stated cross-multiplied because the predicates take natural coefficients. `exists_witness` stops skiing on the day an arbitrary deterministic strategy buys, or after `2B - 1` days. `exists_ratio_violation_of_lt` then rules out every rational ratio below `2 - 1/B`. The break-even strategy is `(1, B - 1)`-competitive (`breakEven_isCompetitive_one`), so with an additive constant the factor-`1` question is trivial; the meaningful ratio has no additive constant. The rent-or-buy framing is credited to [karlin88][karlin88]; [borodinelyaniv98][borodinelyaniv98] is the textbook pointer.

## Randomized strategies and randomized ski rental

[Complexity/DataStructure/Online/Randomized.lean](../../Complexity/DataStructure/Online/Randomized.lean) adds randomized strategies against an **oblivious adversary**, which fixes the whole request sequence before any coin is tossed. A `RandStrategy P` is a `PMF (DetStrategy P)`, a **discrete (countably supported) mixture** of deterministic strategies whose coins are all drawn before the first request. Against an oblivious adversary, drawing the coins up front is equivalent to tossing them online whenever the induced law on deterministic strategies is countably supported. That always holds for ski rental, where every strategy is a threshold in `ℕ ∪ {∞}`, so the model loses nothing there. Algorithms that toss fresh independent coins at unboundedly many steps, such as the marking algorithm for paging, generally induce a non-atomic law. They need a behavioural or bounded-horizon variant of `RandStrategy` before they can be stated.

`RandStrategy.expectedCost` is `∑' A, μ A * A.cost s₀ reqs` in `ℝ≥0∞`. `RandStrategy.expectedCost_map` computes it for strategies built by `PMF.map` from a parametrization. `RandStrategy.IsCompetitive μ c b` asks for `E[ALG] ≤ c * OPT + b` against every valid offline schedule, with `c b : ℝ≥0∞`, so irrational ratios can be stated. Point masses recover the deterministic notions (`expectedCost_pure`, `isCompetitive_pure_iff`). `RandStrategy.exists_le_mul_expectedCost` is the easy direction of Yao's principle [yao77][yao77]. Let `ν` be a distribution on indices, each naming a request sequence and a benchmark `g i`. Suppose the `ν`-mean of `g` is finite and at most `b` times every deterministic strategy's `ν`-mean cost. Then every randomized strategy has an index in the support of `ν` with `g i ≤ b * E[cost]`. The averaging lemmas of [Minimax](../../Complexity/Probability/Minimax.lean) bound an outcome from above by a mean, so they do not give this direction. Adaptive adversaries, which see the strategy's earlier actions, are not modelled.

[SkiRental/Randomized.lean](../../Complexity/DataStructure/Online/SkiRental/Randomized.lean) settles the randomized ratio of the discrete ski-rental problem above. **Ratio:** `randomizedRatio B = 1 / (1 - (1 - 1/B)^B) = B^B / (B^B - (B-1)^B)` (`randomizedRatio_eq`), which is `4/3` at `B = 2` and `27/19` at `B = 3`. It is at most `e/(e-1)` (`randomizedRatio_le`) and tends to `e/(e-1)` (`tendsto_randomizedRatio`); monotonicity in `B` is not proved. For every `B ≥ 2` it is below the deterministic `2 - 1/B` (`randomizedRatio_lt_two_sub`).

**Upper bound.** The equalizing strategy `randomRentThenBuy B` rents for `t < B` days and then buys, drawing `t` with weight `buyWeight B t = (B-1)^(B-1-t) * B^t`. That weight is proportional to `(1 - 1/B)^(B-1-t)` (`buyWeight_eq`), so the strategy buys on day `i` with probability proportional to `(1 - 1/B)^(B-i)`. Its expected cost on `n` days is exactly `randomizedRatio B * min n B` (`expectedCost_randomRentThenBuy`). Hence `randomRentThenBuy_isCompetitive` (ratio `randomizedRatio B`, additive constant `0`) and `randomRentThenBuy_isCompetitive_exp` (ratio `e/(e-1)`).

**Lower bound.** `adversary B` skis `k + 1` days with weight `(B-1)^k * B^(B-2-k)` for each `k < B - 1`, and `2B - 1` days with weight `(B-1)^(B-1)`. On these lengths every deterministic strategy acts like a rent-then-buy strategy (`SkiRental.exists_rentThenBuy_cost_eq`), including one that never buys. Each such strategy pays on average at least `randomizedRatio B` times the mean optimum (`le_sum_advWeight_mul_cost`, `sum_advWeight_mul_min`). Yao's principle then gives `exists_randomized_witness`: a sequence with `OPT ≥ 1` and `randomizedRatio B * OPT ≤ E[ALG]`. By `not_isCompetitive_of_lt`, no randomized strategy is `(c, 0)`-competitive for any `c < randomizedRatio B`.

**Scope.** As in the deterministic case, ratios carry no additive constant for fixed `B`. The asymptotic form, that no `c < e/(e-1)` works with any fixed additive constant once `B` is large, is not formalized. The model is discrete, with an integer price `B ≥ 1` and one request per ski day, and the adversary is oblivious. The continuous-time rent-or-buy problem is not formalized. The equalizing strategy and the `e/(e-1)` ratio are credited to [karlin94][karlin94], with [borodinelyaniv98][borodinelyaniv98] as the textbook pointer. The [test file](../../ComplexityTests/SkiRentalRandomized.lean) checks the `B = 2` and `B = 3` distributions and adversaries with their exact rational ratios, the break-even point mass, and the `5/4 < 4/3` lower bound.

## List update and move-to-front

[Complexity/DataStructure/ListUpdate/Basic.lean](../../Complexity/DataStructure/ListUpdate/Basic.lean) takes as configurations the arrangements of a finite universe (`IsArrangement`). Moving from `s` to `s'` while serving `r` costs `inversions s s' + (s'.idxOf r + 1)`: the Kendall tau distance, paid as exchanges, plus the access position. The module docstring explains, informally, why this is the cheapest Sleator–Tarjan implementation of the step with paid and free exchanges. `scheduleCost_eq_accessCost_add_exchangeCost` splits a schedule's cost into its access and rearrangement parts. [MoveToFront.lean](../../Complexity/DataStructure/ListUpdate/MoveToFront.lean) proves Sleator–Tarjan Theorem 1 with different initial lists, using the inversion potential (`inversions_moveToFront_le`, `mtf_cost_add_inversions_add_length_le`). Corollaries: `mtf_isCompetitive` (factor `2`, no additive constant) and `mtf_isCompetitiveAnyInit` (additive `n(n-1)/2`) [sleatortarjan85][sleatortarjan85].

[ListUpdate/LowerBound.lean](../../Complexity/DataStructure/ListUpdate/LowerBound.lean) is the cruel-adversary bound. `cruelRequests` always asks for the strategy's last item, and `le_cost_cruelRequests` charges at least `n` per request. Against it, the cheaper of the two static schedules (keep `s₀`, or reverse it once) is charged at most `(m(n+1) + n(n-1)/2)/2` over `m` requests. `exists_ratio_violation_of_lt` therefore rules out every rational ratio below `2n/(n+1)`, with any additive constant. For `n ≥ 2`, `not_isCompetitive_one` combined with move-to-front shows that `2` is the best integer factor. Textbook proofs average over all `n!` static lists; the two-list average gives the same constant. The bound is the standard deterministic list-accessing argument. It is credited to Karp and Raghavan as reported in [irani91][irani91], with [borodinelyaniv98][borodinelyaniv98] as a textbook pointer.

## Demand paging

[Complexity/DataStructure/Paging/Basic.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/DataStructure/Paging/Basic.lean) instantiates an online problem with a cache of capacity `k`, a page request, and unit charge for a fault. [Complexity/DataStructure/Paging/LRU.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/DataStructure/Paging/LRU.lean) implements LRU with a duplicate-free recency list and proves a potential inequality. `lruStrategy_isCompetitive` gives factor `k` with no additive charge from a common initial cache. `lruStrategy_isCompetitiveAnyInit` gives additive `k` for arbitrary valid initial caches.

[Complexity/DataStructure/Paging/LowerBound.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/DataStructure/Paging/LowerBound.lean) constructs an adaptive missing-page sequence on `k+1` pages. The online strategy faults each time; a computable offline lookahead schedule separates its faults by `k` requests after warmup. `exists_ratio_violation_of_lt` rules out every rational ratio below `k`, regardless of additive constant, even from the empty cache. These specialize the equal-capacity part of Sleator–Tarjan's paging theorems. The source's unequal-capacity resource-augmentation factor is not yet the local endpoint [sleatortarjan85][sleatortarjan85].

The metric is number of page faults. The recency-list Lean implementation is not claimed to take constant RAM time per request. This is competitive resource analysis over a state-transition model.

## Adaptive search trees: splay trees

[SplayTree/Basic](../../Complexity/DataStructure/SplayTree/Basic.lean) implements bottom-up splaying on Mathlib's `BinaryTree` with no stored balance data. Each result records the exact number of edge rotations. [SplayTree/AccessLemma](../../Complexity/DataStructure/SplayTree/AccessLemma.lean) proves the Access Lemma for arbitrary positive weights. The weights are analysis parameters only: the tree never stores them. One splay costs `splayTime = max rotations 1`, and `splaySeqTime xs t` sums this cost over the access sequence `xs`, splaying each tree in turn. The module also proves:

- the Balance Theorem `(3m + n) log₂ n + m`, which also covers absent keys;
- Static Optimality `m + 4 ∑ q(y) log₂(m / q(y))`, where `q(y)` is the access frequency of `y`, for accesses to stored keys when every stored key is accessed at least once (`q(y) ≥ 1`; the bound fails without this hypothesis);
- a general fixed-weight bound `splaySeqTime_le_fixed_weights`;
- unit-weight update bounds.

[SplayTree/Impl](../../Complexity/DataStructure/SplayTree/Impl.lean) packages these as a self-adjusting `OrderedSet` with an amortized rotation bound [sleatortarjan85b][sleatortarjan85b].

[SplayTree/WorkingSet](../../Complexity/DataStructure/SplayTree/WorkingSet.lean) proves two locality bounds with explicit constants. Both apply to `m` accesses to keys of an `Ordered` tree with `n` keys:

- **Static Finger** (Theorem 3): `splaySeqTime_le_static_finger` bounds the cost by `7m + 6 ∑ log₂(d(xⱼ) + 1) + n(2 + 2 log₂ n)` for a fixed finger key `f` in the tree. Here `d = fingerDist (elements t) f` counts the stored keys in the half-open interval from `f` (exclusive) to `xⱼ` (inclusive), so `d(f) = 0`. On keys `1, …, n` this is `|xⱼ − f|`, and it is defined for any linear order.
- **Working Set** (Theorem 4): `splaySeqTime_le_working_set` bounds the cost by `4m + 6 ∑ log₂(t(j) + 1) + n(1 + 2 log₂ n)`. Here `workingSetNumbers` computes `t(j)`, the number of distinct keys accessed since the previous access of the same key, or since the start of the sequence for a first access.

The working-set proof follows the source. It assigns the weights `1, 1/4, …, 1/n²` in move-to-front order and reassigns them after every access. The reassignment changes the potential explicitly, and `potential_reweight_root_le` accounts for that change. The accessed key is at the root, so its subtree size is unchanged, while every other key's weight can only decrease, so the potential does not increase. [Checked instances](../../ComplexityTests/SplayTree.lean) compute working-set numbers and finger distances and apply both theorems to a degenerate seven-key spine [sleatortarjan85b][sleatortarjan85b].

## The k-server problem

[KServer/Basic](../../Complexity/DataStructure/KServer/Basic.lean) instantiates the online `Problem` with `k` servers on a metric whose distances are natural numbers (`NatMetric`). A configuration is a labeled tuple `Fin k → α`, a request is a point, a transition must put some server on the request, and its cost `moveCost` is the summed per-server distance. Coincident servers are allowed. Relabeling is free, so the cheapest move to a configuration's multiset is the minimum-cost perfect matching `matchCost` (`matchCostAlong_eq_moveCost`), and the offline optimum is that of the source's multiset formulation. `NatMetric.line` is the integer line and `NatMetric.uniform` the uniform metric, on which `k`-server is paging up to the cost convention.

[KServer/LowerBound](../../Complexity/DataStructure/KServer/LowerBound.lean) proves the Manasse–McGeoch–Sleator lower bound (Theorem 6 with `h = k` and Corollary 7) [manassemcgeochsleator90][manassemcgeochsleator90]. `lazify` turns any strategy into one that moves at most one server, only to an uncovered request, at no greater cost. The adversary requests the point of a fixed `(k+1)`-point set that the lazy simulation leaves uncovered. The `k` offline shadow schedules `shadowStates` together pay the request path once, plus the initial distances (`sum_holeCost`). `exists_ratio_violation` rules out every rational ratio `p/q < k` with any additive constant, from any `k` distinct initial points with a further point available. `not_isCompetitive_of_lt` and `not_isCompetitiveAnyInit_of_lt` state the integer form on any metric with `k + 1` distinct points. The source's comparison with `h < k` offline servers (`k/(k-h+1)`) is not formalized.

[KServer/DoubleCoverage](../../Complexity/DataStructure/KServer/DoubleCoverage.lean) defines Double Coverage on the integer line. `dcStep` sorts the configuration, moves the outer server for an outer request or the two neighbouring servers toward an inner request, and restores the labels. The potential is `k·matchCost + Σ_{i<j}|s_i - s_j|` (doubled as `2k·matchCost + pairSum`, since `pairSum` counts ordered pairs). `dcStep_amortized_offline` is the step inequality `2·cost_DC + Δ(2Φ) ≤ 2k·cost_OPT`. `doubleCoverage_cost_le` bounds the cost by `k·OPT + k·matchCost s₀ s_off + Σ_{i<j}|s₀ i - s₀ j|` against offline schedules from any start, and `doubleCoverage_isCompetitiveFrom` gives ratio `k` from every initial configuration with that additive spread. This is the Chrobak–Karloff–Payne–Vishwanathan theorem [chrobakkarloffpaynevishwanathan91][chrobakkarloffpaynevishwanathan91], restricted to integer points. With the lower bound, the deterministic ratio on the line is exactly `k` from any start with distinct servers, with a per-start additive constant. Double Coverage is not competitive with one additive constant for every start: from `0, 10` the requests `5, 0` cost it `15` against an offline `5`, and scaling the instance defeats any fixed constant. The [test file](../../ComplexityTests/KServer.lean) computes a three-server Double Coverage run, an offline schedule and the additive constant. It also checks the uniform-metric lower bound, that Double Coverage from `0, 1, …, k-1` is not `(k-1)`-competitive, and the two-server instance above.

## Online bin packing

[BinPacking/Online](../../Complexity/Algorithms/Lean/BinPacking/Online.lean) models deterministic online bin packing outside the `Problem` interface. An `OnlinePacker C` chooses a bin for the next item from the items seen so far and the current bins. If the chosen bin has no room, or none is chosen, the item opens a new last bin (`onlineInsert`), so every rule gives a valid packing (`OnlinePacker.isPacking_run`). Two facts drive lower bounds against all online packers. First, the packing of `l₁ ++ l₂` continues that of `l₁` (`OnlinePacker.run_append`). Second, items failing a predicate leave the number of bins with an item satisfying it unchanged (`OnlinePacker.countP_any_runFrom`). `run_firstFitPacker` identifies First Fit as an online packer.

[BinPacking/OnlineLowerBound](../../Complexity/Algorithms/Lean/BinPacking/OnlineLowerBound.lean) proves Yao's lower bound `3/2` for deterministic online bin packing, stated in the abstract of [yao80][yao80], with the item types as reported in [baloghbekesidosaepsteinlevin18][baloghbekesidosaepsteinlevin18].
- The input has capacity `41` and three phases of `6m` items of sizes `6`, `14` and `21`. These sizes give the same 20 feasible bin patterns as `1/7 + ε`, `1/3 + ε` and `1/2 + ε` with capacity `1`.
- `OnlinePacker.yao_lower_bound`: after one of the phases, every online packer uses at least `3/2` times the optimum, exactly. The optima are `m`, `3m` and `6m` (`isPacking_yao₁`, `le_length_of_isPacking_yao₁`, and so on).
- The proof is an explicit LP dual certificate: the weights `1`, `2`, `2` (`yaoWeight`) and the per-bin inequality `weight b ≤ 2 + 2·[b has a 6] + 2·[b has a 6 or a 14]` (`sum_map_yaoWeight_le`), which `omega` checks against all 20 feasible patterns.
- `exists_optimal_three_mul_le` and `exists_ratio_violation_of_lt` state the bound against an optimal packing, for every rational ratio below `3/2` and every additive constant. `four_thirds_lower_bound` is the two-phase `4/3`, with its optima `3m` and `6m` (`isPacking_twoPhase₁`, `le_length_of_isPacking_twoPhase₁`, and so on).
- The [test file](../../ComplexityTests/BinPackingOnline.lean) enumerates the 20 patterns and runs First Fit on the input. It also checks an online packer that follows the LP optimum and attains `3/2` after every phase for `m = 8`, so the constant is the best this input gives. Its last examples pair `run_firstFitPacker` with `ten_mul_length_firstFit_le` to show the online upper bound `10 * FF ≤ 17 * OPT + 8`, so the library brackets the best deterministic asymptotic ratio between `3/2` and `17/10`.

## Count-Min: a stored streaming sketch

[Complexity/DataStructure/CountMin/Basic.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/DataStructure/CountMin/Basic.lean) models a weighted insertion stream `List (X × Nat)`. Each update increments one bucket in each row of a vector-of-vectors counter table; a query takes the minimum row counter. The exact bucket invariant proves no underestimation, each counter's bound by total mass, and entrywise merge agreement with stream concatenation.

[Complexity/DataStructure/CountMin/Probability.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/DataStructure/CountMin/Probability.lean) analyzes independently sampled two-universal rows for a stream and query fixed before the hash seeds. `dens_estimate_error_le_inv_two_pow` gives failure at most `2^-d` when `2 ≤ width*ε`. This is the one-sided additive-error form of the fixed-query Count-Min guarantee; it makes no adaptive-query or adaptive-update claim [cormode05][cormode05].

[Complexity/DataStructure/CountMin/Program.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/DataStructure/CountMin/Program.lean) supplies `sampleSketch` and `sampleQuery` as `ProbComp` algorithms, retaining seeds with the materialized table. `eval_sampleSketch` and `eval_sampleQuery` identify their laws with the deterministic uniform-seed analysis; `sampleQuery_error_le_inv_two_pow` transfers the accuracy bound. The supplied sampler/hash operation and the Lean array work do not receive machine-time or heap-consumption bounds here.

This is a stream algorithm model and an exact probability theorem, distinct from a general complexity class of one-pass bounded-space machines. It also does not imply differential privacy.

## One-pass streaming algorithms and the AMS sketch

[Complexity/Computability/Streaming/Basic.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/Streaming/Basic.lean) defines a deterministic one-pass `Streaming.Algorithm σ α β`: an initial memory state, an update reading one item and an output. Updates are arbitrary functions, so the model is non-uniform and charges only the memory surviving between items. `A.SpaceLE m b` says that the states reached on streams of at most `m` items have an injective `b`-bit encoding, so space is `⌈log₂⌉` of the number of reachable states. A `Streaming.Randomized` algorithm is a `PMF` over deterministic algorithms, with randomness drawn before the stream as for public-coin protocols. Its memory bound is checked separately for each algorithm in the support. A drawn algorithm's update may depend on the random choices, so random bits are free; this is the convention under which communication lower bounds transfer. An algorithm that tosses fresh coins at every item fits the model, for example after adding a step counter, which costs `⌈log₂(m+1)⌉` bits, so `Ω(n)` lower bounds still apply to it. `Solves ε R m` bounds the error on every stream of at most `m` items. `Algorithm.reduce` prefixes items, translates items and post-processes answers while keeping memory bounds.

[Complexity/Computability/Streaming/Communication.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/Streaming/Communication.lean) turns an algorithm with `b` bits into a one-way protocol in which Alice sends the encoded state after her part of the stream. `Algorithm.ecomplexityFun_le_of_spaceLE` and `Randomized.ecomplexityFun_le_of_spaceLE` bound deterministic and public-coin communication by `b + 1`. [Complexity/Computability/Streaming/Distinctness.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/Streaming/Distinctness.lean) applies this to the disjointness lower bounds:

- `le_of_solves_nodup` covers distinctness of at most `2n` items of `Fin n` with error `ε ≤ 1/2`. It gives `n ≤ 2^10 r (b+1) + 2^12` whenever `(4ε(1-ε))^r ≤ 1/64`; at error `1/3`, `n ≤ 36864 (b+1) + 2^12`.
- `le_of_solves_maxFreq` is the `F∞` lower bound of Alon–Matias–Szegedy, Proposition 3.1 [alonmatiasszegedy99][alonmatiasszegedy99], for estimates within less than `F∞/3`. A fixed item is prefixed to make the stream nonempty, so the statement uses `Fin (n+1)` and `2n+1` items.
- `Algorithm.le_of_run_eq_decide_nodup` and `Algorithm.le_of_run_eq_maxFreq` show that exact deterministic algorithms need `n ≤ b`.

The [test file](../../ComplexityTests/StreamingAMS.lean) shows that an algorithm storing the items seen and a flag uses `n + 1` bits.

[Complexity/Computability/Streaming/MomentLowerBound.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/Streaming/MomentLowerBound.lean) proves the two other `Ω(n)` bounds of Alon–Matias–Szegedy §3, for the frequency moments `F_k` with `k ≠ 1` [alonmatiasszegedy99][alonmatiasszegedy99]. Both compare `F_k` on two concatenated graph streams `(i, a i)` ([GraphStream.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/Streaming/GraphStream.lean)), for which `F_k = 2^k·#{i | a i = b i} + 2·d(a, b)`.

- `Algorithm.le_of_approx_freqMoment` is Proposition 3.7: a deterministic algorithm approximating `F_k` within `η F_k`, `7η < 1`, on streams of at most `2L` items of `Fin L × Fin 16` needs `L ≤ b`. The proof pigeonholes the memory states over the graphs of a code with `2^L` words and relative distance `1/2` (`Code.exists_two_pow_le_card_and_le_two_mul_hammingDist`, from the Gilbert–Varshamov bound). `Algorithm.div_le_of_approx_freqMoment` restates it over `Fin N` as `N/16 ≤ b` for streams of at most `N/8` items, where the source uses `n/2` items.
- `le_of_solves_freqMoment` is Proposition 3.8: computing `F_k` exactly with error `ε ≤ 1/2` on streams of at most `2n` items of `Fin n × Fin 3` gives `n ≤ 2^10 r (b+1) + 2^12` whenever `(4ε(1−ε))^r ≤ 1/64` (so only `ε < 1/2` gives a bound), and `n ≤ 36864 (b+1) + 2^12` at error `1/3`. Alice's and Bob's graphs agree exactly at their common ones, so `F_k = 2n` decides disjointness with an answer map that does not depend on the inputs (`disjointness_eq_decide_freqMoment_eq`). The extra coordinate `Fin 3` is the price of that fixed threshold. `Algorithm.le_of_run_eq_freqMoment` gives `n ≤ b` deterministically, and the `div_le_` forms restate both over `Fin N`.

`PublicCoin.le_of_ecomplexityFun_disjointness_le` packages amplification together with the disjointness bound for such reductions. In the [test file](../../ComplexityTests/StreamingMomentLowerBound.lean), an algorithm storing every frequency meets the hypotheses of all of these bounds.

There are two ways to prove a new streaming lower bound. The first starts from a communication lower bound, as `le_of_solves_nodup` does: split the stream into Alice's part `u x` and Bob's part `v y`, then apply `Randomized.ecomplexityFun_le_of_spaceLE`. The second reduces to an existing streaming lower bound, as `le_of_solves_maxFreq` does: use `Algorithm.reduce` with `Randomized.SpaceLE.map_reduce` and `Randomized.errorProb_map_reduce_le` to prefix fixed items, translate items and post-process answers.

[Complexity/Computability/Streaming/AMS.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/Streaming/AMS.lean) implements the AMS `F₂` sketch from Theorem 2.2. It keeps `2r` groups of `k` tug-of-war counters, each adding `sgn (h θ a) = ±1` per item, and answers the median over groups of the mean squared counter. The moment computations `𝔼 Z² = F₂` and `𝔼 (Z² - F₂)² ≤ 2F₂²` for four-wise independent signs are in [KWiseIndependent/TugOfWar.lean](../../Complexity/Probability/KWiseIndependent/TugOfWar.lean); the first is an instance of additivity of second moments under pairwise independence, in [KWiseIndependent/SecondMoment.lean](../../Complexity/Probability/KWiseIndependent/SecondMoment.lean). The median-of-means amplification is in [MedianOfMeans.lean](../../Complexity/Probability/MedianOfMeans.lean), built on the lower median of [Finset/Median.lean](../../Complexity/Foundations/Data/Finset/Median.lean). The frequency moments `freqMoment` and the largest frequency `maxFreq` are in [Streaming/Frequency.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/Streaming/Frequency.lean). `dens_amsSketch_error_le` and `solves_ams` give error `(8/9)^r` for relative accuracy `η` when `6 ≤ kη²`. Under the public-coin convention the seeds are not charged, and `spaceLE_ams` shows that every sketch drawn uses `b` bits once `(2m+1)^(2rk) ≤ 2^b`. Only the initial memory depends on the seeds (`init_amsSketch`, `update_amsSketch`, `output_amsSketch`). So `exists_injOn_iUnion_reachable_amsSketch` gives one `b`-bit encoding for the states reachable from all seeds, with the seeds stored, once `(|ι|(2m+1))^(2rk) ≤ 2^b`. This seed-inclusive count matches the `O(η⁻² log δ⁻¹ (log n + log m))` bound of Theorem 2.2. The test file instantiates the hypotheses with the four-wise independent trinomial hash family. It also shows that with a constant sign the sketch reports `F₁²` instead of `F₂`.

[Complexity/Computability/Streaming/JoinSize.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/Streaming/JoinSize.lean) implements the tug-of-war join signature of Alon–Gibbons–Matias–Szegedy, §4.3 [alongibbonsmatiasszegedy99][alongibbonsmatiasszegedy99]. It estimates the join size `joinSize u v = ∑ₐ count a u · count a v`, the inner product of two frequency vectors. The self-join is `F₂` (`joinSize_self`), and `joinSize_sq_le` gives `|F ⋈ G|² ≤ F₂(F)F₂(G)`. The moments of Lemma 4.4 are in [KWiseIndependent/TugOfWarProduct.lean](../../Complexity/Probability/KWiseIndependent/TugOfWarProduct.lean). For two tug-of-war sums `X`, `Y` with shared signs, pairwise independence gives `𝔼 XY = ∑ f g`. Four-wise independence gives the exact variance `(∑ f²)(∑ g²) + (∑ f g)² − 2∑ f²g²` (equation (3) of the source), which is at most `2(∑ f²)(∑ g²)`. `joinSketch` reads one stream of tagged items `α ⊕ α`, with `Sum.inl a` for the first relation and `Sum.inr a` for the second, and keeps one counter of each relation per seed. Each relation's counters are the AMS sketch of its own items (`joinSketch_state_left`, `joinSketch_state_right`). The answer is the median of means of the products of the two counters. With `6 ≤ kη²`, `dens_joinSketch_error_le` and `solves_joinSketchRandom` give additive error `η√(F₂(F)F₂(G))` except with probability `(8/9)^r`. `solves_joinSketchRandom_relative` is the relative form of Theorem 4.5: given `B₁ ≤ |F ⋈ G|`, self-joins at most `B₂`, and `6B₂² ≤ kε²B₁²`, the error is at most `ε|F ⋈ G|`. The source takes a plain mean of `k` copies and bounds its failure probability by Chebyshev, so the median step here is an addition. For memory, `spaceLE_joinSketchRandom` counts the `4rk` counters, `(2m+1)^(4rk) ≤ 2^b`, and `exists_injOn_iUnion_reachable_joinSketch` stores the seeds as well, `(|ι|(2m+1)²)^(2rk) ≤ 2^b`. The relations are insert-only: the source's deletions would need turnstile stream items. The [test file](../../ComplexityTests/StreamingJoinSize.lean) evaluates join sizes and instantiates the hypotheses with the trinomial hash family. It also shows that with a constant sign the sketch reports `|F||G|` for two relations with an empty join.

[Complexity/Computability/Streaming/FkEstimator.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/Streaming/FkEstimator.lean) implements the AMS estimator for every `F_k` with `k ≥ 1` from Theorem 2.1 [alonmatiasszegedy99][alonmatiasszegedy99]. The basic estimator `fkBasicEstimate` samples a position `p` and sets `X = m(r^k − (r−1)^k)`, where `r = suffixCount s p` counts the occurrences of the item `a_p` at positions `q ≥ p`. Telescoping gives `expect_fkBasicEstimate` (`𝔼X = F_k`). The bound `(c+1)^k − c^k ≤ k(c+1)^(k−1)` (`Nat.succ_pow_sub_pow_le`) gives `expect_fkBasicEstimate_sq_le` (`𝔼X² ≤ kF₁F_{2k−1}`). The source's power-sum fact `F₁F_{2k−1} ≤ n^(1−1/k)F_k²` is `Finset.sum_mul_sum_pow_le` in [Finset/PowerSum.lean](../../Complexity/Foundations/Data/Finset/PowerSum.lean), raised to the `k`-th power to avoid real exponents. `fkSketch` keeps a step counter and, for each of `2rK` copies, a sampled position, the item there and its count. Its answer is the median of means of the basic estimator (`run_fkSketch_eq_medianOfMeans`). As in the source's main argument, the stream length `m` is fixed in advance: positions are drawn from `Fin m` and the guarantees are for streams of exactly `m` items. `dens_fkSketch_error_le` and `solves_fkEstimator` give error `(8/9)^r` for relative accuracy `η` when `(3k)^k n^(k−1) ≤ (Kη²)^k`. `solves_fkEstimator_of_rpow` takes the hypothesis in the form `3k·n^(1−1/k) ≤ Kη²`. The group size `3k·n^(1−1/k)/η²` replaces the source's `8k·n^(1−1/k)/λ²`, because the median step uses the `(8/9)^r` bound of `MedianOfMeans.lean` instead of a Chernoff bound. For memory, `spaceLE_fkEstimator` does not charge the positions, while `exists_injOn_iUnion_reachable_fkSketch` stores them (`m^(2rK)(M+1)((n+1)(M+1))^(2rK) ≤ 2^b`). Together they give the `O(rK(log n + log m))` memory of Theorem 2.1. The [test file](../../ComplexityTests/StreamingFkEstimator.lean) evaluates the basic estimator on the stream `0, 1, 0`, shows that `k = 0` must be excluded, and instantiates the error and memory bounds.

## Distinct elements: the k-minimum-values sketch

[Complexity/Computability/Streaming/DistinctElements.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/Streaming/DistinctElements.lean) implements the first algorithm of Bar-Yossef, Jayram, Kumar, Sivakumar and Trevisan [bjkst02][bjkst02] (Section 2, Theorem 1). It estimates the number of distinct items `F₀ = #s.toFinset` (`freqMoment_zero`).

Each copy of `kmvSketch` hashes the items with a pairwise independent family `h θ : α → Fin M` and keeps the `t` lowest keys `kmvKey h θ a = (h θ a, a)` in lexicographic order. Selection by `Finset.lowest` can be maintained online (`Finset.lowest_insert_lowest`, in [Finset/LowestInsert.lean](../../Complexity/Foundations/Data/Finset/LowestInsert.lean)). So `state_kmvSketch` shows that after the stream `s` a copy holds the `t` lowest keys of the distinct items of `s`.

While a copy holds fewer than `t` keys, it reports their number, which is exactly `F₀` (`run_kmvSketch_of_card_lt`). Otherwise it reports `tM/(v+1)`, where `v` is the largest hash value it keeps (`kmvEstimate`). The sketch answers the median over its copies, and `kmv` draws the seeds uniformly.

The error analysis uses `Finset.forall_mem_lowest_iff`: the `t` lowest hash values of `d ≥ t` items are all below `K` exactly when at least `t` items hash below `K`. The number of such items has variance at most its mean, so Chebyshev's inequality bounds each tail. `dens_abs_kmvEstimate_sub_gt_le` gives a per-copy failure probability `Pr[|estimate − d| > εd] ≤ 2/(tε²)` for `0 < ε ≤ 1` and `d ≤ M`: the upper tail contributes `(1+ε)/(tε²)` and the lower tail `(1−ε)/(tε²)`. The source instead bounds each tail by `1/6` with `t = ⌈96/ε²⌉`.

With `6 ≤ tε²` one copy fails with probability at most `1/3`. The median trick (`Cslib.Probability.dens_abs_median_sub_gt_le`, in [MedianOfMeans.lean](../../Complexity/Probability/MedianOfMeans.lean)) amplifies this: `dens_kmvSketch_error_le` shows that the median of `2r` copies misses `F₀` by more than `εF₀` with probability at most `(8/9)^r`. `solves_kmv` packages the result as `(kmv h t (2r)).Solves δ (fun s Y => |Y − F₀ s| ≤ ε F₀ s) m` whenever `(8/9)^r ≤ δ` and `m ≤ M`. The bound `m ≤ M` on the stream length only serves to guarantee `d ≤ M`.

Ties between hash values are broken by the item, so the source's `1/m` collision term and its requirement `M = m³` disappear; only `d ≤ M` is needed.

For fixed seeds, the reachable states of a copy are determined by the at most `t` items behind its keys. The memory bounds therefore depend neither on the stream length nor on `M`:

- under the public-coin convention, `spaceLE_kmv` shows that every drawn sketch uses `b` bits whenever `(n+1)^(ct) ≤ 2^b`, for a universe of `n` items;
- `exists_injOn_iUnion_reachable_kmvSketch` gives one `b`-bit encoding with the seeds stored as well, whenever `(|ι|(n+1)^t)^c ≤ 2^b`.

With `t = ⌈6/ε²⌉` and `c = 2r = O(log(1/δ))`, this is the `O(ε⁻² log(1/δ) log n)` memory of Theorem 1. The bound complements the `Ω(n)` lower bounds for exact and for deterministic approximate `F₀` (`le_of_solves_freqMoment`, `Algorithm.le_of_approx_freqMoment` with `k = 0`). The matching `Ω(ε⁻²)` lower bound is not formalized.

The [test file](../../ComplexityTests/StreamingDistinctElements.lean) runs the sketch with a fixed hash on a concrete stream. It applies the error bound to the affine maps over `ZMod 31`, a pairwise independent family with a 10-bit seed, and instantiates the memory bounds with 240 and 260 bits. It also shows two failure cases: a constant hash overestimates `F₀` by a factor `2`, and `t = 0` always answers `0`.

## Heavy hitters: Misra–Gries and the Count-Sketch

[Complexity/Computability/Streaming/MisraGries.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/Streaming/MisraGries.lean) implements the deterministic Misra–Gries summary `misraGries k` [misragries82][misragries82]. It stores its at most `k` counters as a multiset whose multiplicities are the counters. Reading an item increments its counter if it has one or if fewer than `k` counters are in use, and otherwise decrements every counter. Each decrement discards `k + 1` distinct stream items, which gives the following statements:

- `MisraGries.run_le_count` and `MisraGries.mul_count_sub_run_add_card_le` give `f̂_a ≤ f_a` and `(k + 1)(f_a − f̂_a) + m̂ ≤ m`, where `m̂` is the sum of the counters. This is Theorem 1.3.1 with the sharpening of Exercise 1-1 of [chakrabarti25][chakrabarti25].
- `MisraGries.count_sub_div_le_run` is the real form `f_a − m/(k+1) ≤ f̂_a`.
- `MisraGries.mem_state_of_length_lt_mul_count` says every item with `f_a > m/(k+1)` keeps a counter. This is the deterministic heavy-hitters guarantee.
- `MisraGries.toFinset_state_one_eq` is the one-counter majority case.
- `MisraGries.spaceLE_misraGries` bounds the memory: `(n(m+1)+1)^k ≤ 2^b` reachable states suffice on streams of at most `m` items over a universe of `n` items.

Chakrabarti's parameter `k` allows `k − 1` counters, so `misraGries k` is his algorithm with parameter `k + 1`.

[Complexity/Probability/KWiseIndependent/CountSketch.lean](../../Complexity/Probability/KWiseIndependent/CountSketch.lean) analyzes one row of the Count-Sketch [charikar04][charikar04] for arbitrary real weights. The bucket family `h` must be two-universal, and the sign family `ε ∘ g` must be independent of it, pairwise independent and of mean zero. Under these hypotheses:

- the estimate is unbiased (`CountSketch.expect_sum_ite_mul_sign_mul_sign`);
- the collision noise has second moment at most `∑_{b≠a} f_b²/w` (`CountSketch.expect_sq_sum_ite_mul_sign_le`);
- by Chebyshev, a row misses by more than `t` with probability at most `1/3` once `3∑_{b≠a} f_b² ≤ wt²` (`CountSketch.dens_abs_sum_ite_mul_sign_gt_le`).

These are equations (5.1)–(5.6) of [chakrabarti25][chakrabarti25]. Only an upper bound on collision probabilities is assumed.

The amplification step is the median trick `dens_abs_median_sub_gt_le`, added to [MedianOfMeans.lean](../../Complexity/Probability/MedianOfMeans.lean): if a single estimate misses with probability at most `1/3`, the median of `2r` independent estimates misses with probability at most `(8/9)^r`. `dens_abs_medianOfMeans_sub_gt_le` is now derived from it.

[Complexity/Computability/Streaming/CountSketch.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/Streaming/CountSketch.lean) assembles `2r` rows into the streaming algorithm `countSketch`, and `countSketchRandom` draws its seeds uniformly. Its results:

- `dens_countSketch_error_gt_le` and `solves_countSketchRandom` answer every fixed point query `a` within `ε‖f_{−a}‖₂` with error `δ`, once `3 ≤ wε²` and `(8/9)^r ≤ δ`.
- `solves_countSketchRandom_freqMoment` gives the same with the bound `ε√F₂`.
- `spaceLE_countSketchRandom` charges `(2m+1)^(2rw) ≤ 2^b` under the public-coin convention.
- `exists_injOn_iUnion_reachable_countSketch` gives the bound with the seeds stored, `(|ι||κ|)^(2r)(2m+1)^(2rw) ≤ 2^b`.

The [test file](../../ComplexityTests/StreamingHeavyHitters.lean) checks concrete Misra–Gries runs, including one where the error `m/(k+1)` is attained. It also instantiates the Count-Sketch hypotheses with the trinomial hash families, and shows that one bucket with a constant sign reports the stream length instead of the frequency.

## MinHash and locality-sensitive hashing

[Probability/LocalitySensitiveHash.lean](../../Complexity/Probability/LocalitySensitiveHash.lean) works with keyed families `hash : ι → X → Y` with seed law `seed : PMF ι`, as for universal hashing. `collisionProb seed hash x x'` is the real probability that `x` and `x'` collide. It differs from `PMF.collisionProbability`, the probability that two independent samples of one law agree. `IsLocalitySensitive prob near far p₁ p₂` says that near pairs collide with probability at least `p₁` and far pairs with probability at most `p₂`. With `near := d ≤ r₁` and `far := r₂ ≤ d` this is the `(r₁, r₂, p₁, p₂)`-sensitive family of Indyk and Motwani [indykmotwani98][indykmotwani98] [harpeledindykmotwani12][harpeledindykmotwani12]. Every `ε`-universal family with `ε ≠ ⊤` is `(=, ≠, 1, ε)`-sensitive (`IsUniversalHash.isLocalitySensitive`). Independent repetitions use `PMF.iid`, and the collision laws are exact:

- the AND construction `andHash` (concatenate `k` independently keyed hashes) collides with probability `p^k` (`collisionProb_andHash`);
- the OR construction (some one of `L` independent hashes collides) collides with probability `1 − (1 − p)^L` (`orCollisionProb_eq`);
- `L` bands of `k` rows give the S-curve `sCurve k L p = 1 − (1 − p^k)^L` (`orCollisionProb_andHash`).

The general event laws behind these, `Pr[∀ j, s j ∈ E] = μ(E)^k` and `Pr[∃ j, s j ∈ E] = 1 − (1 − μ(E))^k` under `PMF.iid μ k`, are in [IID/Event.lean](../../Complexity/Probability/IID/Event.lean). `IsLocalitySensitive.and`, `.or` and `.banded` transport sensitivity. For `0 ≤ p₂ < p₁ ≤ 1`, `δ > 0` and `δ' > 0`, `exists_sCurve_gap` and `IsLocalitySensitive.exists_banded` choose `k` and `L = ⌈log(1/δ)/p₁^k⌉₊` so that near pairs collide with probability at least `1 − δ` and far pairs with probability at most `δ'`. The explicit bounds are `le_sCurve` (`1 − δ ≤ sCurve k L p` once `log(1/δ) ≤ L p₁^k`) and `sCurve_le` (`sCurve k L p ≤ L p₂^k`, Bernoulli's inequality). `collisionProb_triangle` is Charikar's Lemma 1 [charikar02][charikar02]: `1 − collisionProb` satisfies the triangle inequality.

[LocalitySensitiveHash/MinHash.lean](../../Complexity/Probability/LocalitySensitiveHash/MinHash.lean) proves Broder's theorem [broder97][broder97]. On a finite linear order, `minHash σ A` is the least rank `min σ(A)` (`⊤` for `∅`). Under a uniform `σ : Equiv.Perm α`, `collisionProb_minHash` gives `Pr[minHash σ A = minHash σ B] = jaccard A B = |A ∩ B|/|A ∪ B|`, with `jaccard ∅ ∅ = 1`. Consequences: `isLocalitySensitive_minHash` (MinHash is `(d₁, d₂, 1 − d₁, 1 − d₂)`-sensitive for the Jaccard distance `1 − J`), `orCollisionProb_andHash_minHash` (the banding curve `1 − (1 − J^r)^b` of [leskovecrajaramanullman20][leskovecrajaramanullman20], Section 3.4.2), and `jaccard_triangle` (the triangle inequality for the Jaccard distance on finite subsets of any type with decidable equality). With `jaccard_comm` and `jaccard_eq_one_iff` (`J(A, B) = 1 ↔ A = B`), it makes `1 − J` a metric. [LocalitySensitiveHash/BitSampling.lean](../../Complexity/Probability/LocalitySensitiveHash/BitSampling.lean) proves the bit-sampling law `collisionProb_bitSampling`: a uniform coordinate of `x, y : n → β` agrees with probability `1 − d_H(x, y)/|n|`. It follows that bit sampling is `(r₁, r₂, 1 − r₁/|n|, 1 − r₂/|n|)`-sensitive (`isLocalitySensitive_bitSampling`).

The [test file](../../ComplexityTests/LocalitySensitiveHash.lean) contains:

- Jaccard values and exact MinHash collision probabilities on `Fin 4`;
- banding numerics for `r = 5`, `b = 20` (`sCurve 5 20 0.8 > 0.9996`, `sCurve 5 20 0.2 < 0.007`);
- a bit-sampling family on `Fin 8 → Bool`, banded to `(0.9965, 0.48)` and amplified with `exists_banded`;
- a negative example: the same family is not `(7/8, 1/4)`-sensitive.

### Approximate near neighbours

[DataStructure/NearNeighbor/LSH.lean](../../Complexity/DataStructure/NearNeighbor/LSH.lean) builds the `(c, r)`-approximate near-neighbour structure of Indyk and Motwani [indykmotwani98][indykmotwani98] [harpeledindykmotwani12][harpeledindykmotwani12] on top of these families. There are `L` tables keyed by `g (s j)`, with the seeds `s : Fin L → ι` drawn from `PMF.iid ν L`. `candidates g s P q` lists the points of `P` colliding with `q`, table by table and with repetitions. `query g far s T P q` scans the first `T` candidates and returns the first one that is not far from `q`, where `far` is a decidable relation.

The results:

- **Soundness.** Every answer is a point of `P` not far from `q` (`query_eq_some`).
- **Exact failure bound** (`toOuterMeasure_query_eq_none_le`). Suppose `x₀ ∈ P` is not far from `q` and collides with it with probability at least `P₁`, and every far point of `P` collides with probability at most `P₂ ≥ 0`. Then for `T > 0` the query returns `none` with probability at most `(1 − P₁)^L + L|P|P₂/T`. The first term is the event that `x₀` collides with `q` in no table, using independence of the tables (`PMF.toOuterMeasure_iid_forall`). The second is Markov's inequality for the number of far candidates (`le_countP_candidates`, `tsum_iid_mul_countP_candidates_le`). That bound uses only the marginal law of each table (`PMF.toOuterMeasure_iid_eval`).
- **AND tables** (`toOuterMeasure_query_andHash_eq_none_le`). For a `(p₁, p₂)`-sensitive family with `0 ≤ p₂ < p₁` and `g = andHash hash k`, and some point of `P` near `q`, the bound is `(1 − p₁^k)^L + L|P|p₂^k/T`.
- **Explicit parameters** (`toOuterMeasure_query_numTables_eq_none_le`). Assume `0 < p₂ < p₁ ≤ 1`, and take `n = |P|`, `ρ = rho p₁ p₂ = log(1/p₁)/log(1/p₂)`, `k = numHashes n p₂ = ⌈log n / log(1/p₂)⌉₊`, `L = numTables n p₁ p₂ = ⌈n^ρ/p₁⌉₊` and `T = 3L`. Then `n p₂^k ≤ 1` (`mul_pow_numHashes_le`) and `L p₁^k ≥ 1` (`one_le_numTables_mul_pow`), so the query fails with probability at most `e⁻¹ + 1/3 < 0.71`. Stopping after `3L` candidates is the scanning rule of Indyk and Motwani [indykmotwani98][indykmotwani98], as presented by Andoni and Indyk [andoniindyk08][andoniindyk08], §2.4 ("Strategy 1", p. 119). This is the static structure; [harpeledindykmotwani12][harpeledindykmotwani12], Theorem 3.4, also supports insertions and deletions.
- **Repetition.** `queryRepeat` runs `R` independent structures, and all of them fail with probability at most `(e⁻¹ + 1/3)^R` (`toOuterMeasure_queryRepeat_numTables_eq_none_le`).
- **What is bounded.** The query inspects at most `min T (L|P|)` candidates, so it tests `far` at most `T = 3L` times (`length_take_candidates_le`, `length_candidates_le`). The parameters satisfy `k < log n / log(1/p₂) + 1` and `L < n^ρ/p₁ + 1` (`numHashes_lt`, `numTables_lt`), with `0 ≤ ρ < 1` (`rho_nonneg`, `rho_lt_one`). No declaration counts operations. The counts of a bucketed implementation, namely `kL` base-hash evaluations per query and `nL` stored entries, are not formalized: `candidates` describes a bucket by filtering `P`, so it evaluates each table hash on all of `P`.

[NearNeighbor/Hamming.lean](../../Complexity/DataStructure/NearNeighbor/Hamming.lean) specializes to bit sampling on words of length `d` with `0 < r`, `1 < c` and `cr < d`. `rho_one_sub_le` proves `ρ(1 − r/d, 1 − cr/d) ≤ 1/c` by concavity of `log`. `toOuterMeasure_query_bitSampling_eq_none_le` is the Hamming-space guarantee, and `numTables_bitSampling_lt` gives `L < n^{1/c}/p₁ + 1`.

The [test file](../../ComplexityTests/NearNeighbor.lean) runs the query on `Fin 8` with two bit-extraction tables. With `T ≤ 2` two far candidates exhaust the budget; with `T = 3` the near point is returned. It also runs `queryRepeat`, evaluates `e⁻¹ + 1/3 < 1` and `(e⁻¹ + 1/3)^10 < 0.03`, computes `numHashes 16 (1/2) = 4`, and instantiates the Hamming-space theorem on three words in `Fin 8 → Bool`.

## Native streaming folds

[Complexity/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/Complexity/Streaming.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Machines/Turing/MultiTape/Complexity/Streaming.lean) gives actual machine-backed scans of raw bit strings through Christian Reitwiessner's streaming backend. A round holds an encoded accumulator and one input bit instead of copying the remaining input list. Time sums the round computation, old/new state encoding sizes and control; workspace takes a peak bound and charges retained accumulators. Read-only input and write-only output are excluded from work-tape space.

`foldl_bits_stream_sum` and `_spec` give cumulative costs and invariants. `foldl_bits_pair_stream_sum` includes a runtime initial accumulator. `iterate_unary_stream_sum` gives repeated state updates emitting output without workspace proportional to the iteration count. `foldl_bits_finite` specializes to linear time and constant workspace for a fixed finite state, and `map_bits` gives linear time with zero work space. The encoding and combinator source markers record the upstream/vendored backend origin.

These are compositional native resource theorems on the [machine model](../machines-and-classes/index.md), not a separate RAM primitive. Their current declarations still expose tidy backend predicates in this source module; broader plain-computability migration policy does not change what this pinned file actually states.

## Dimension reduction: the Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma

[Complexity/Probability/JohnsonLindenstrauss.lean](../../Complexity/Probability/JohnsonLindenstrauss.lean) proves the Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma [johnsonlindenstrauss84][johnsonlindenstrauss84] for random sign matrices, following Achlioptas, Theorem 1.1, with the `±1` distribution [achlioptas03][achlioptas03]. Achlioptas states the theorem for every `ε > 0`. Lean requires `0 < ε < 3/2`, and some restriction is necessary: for `ε = 2` the dimension threshold is negative, yet one sign row maps `(1, 1, 1, 1)` to the squared norm `16 > 3 · 4` with probability `1/8`. The seed is `b : Fin k → ι → Bool`, drawn uniformly, and `signProjection b : EuclideanSpace ℝ ι →ₗ[ℝ] EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin k)` is the linear map with matrix entries `signVal (b j i) / √k`. Probabilities are densities of finsets of seeds, as for the AMS sketch. For a fixed vector `x` and `0 ≤ ε`, each tail `Pr[‖signProjection b x‖² > (1 + ε)‖x‖²]` and `Pr[‖signProjection b x‖² < (1 - ε)‖x‖²]` is at most `exp (-(k/2)(ε²/2 - ε³/3))` (`dens_lt_norm_signProjection_sq_le`, `dens_norm_signProjection_sq_lt_le`). The two-sided bound `dens_lt_abs_norm_signProjection_sq_sub_le` is twice that. A union bound over the `n(n-1)/2` pairs of `n` points gives `one_sub_le_dens_forall_norm_signProjection_sq_mem_Icc`. For `0 < ε < 3/2`, any real `β` and `k ≥ (4 + 2β) log n / (ε²/2 - ε³/3)`, `one_sub_rpow_le_dens_forall_norm_signProjection_sq_mem_Icc` keeps every squared pairwise distance within `[1 - ε, 1 + ε]` with probability at least `1 - n^(-β)`. Here `log` is the natural logarithm. With `β = 0`, `exists_signProjection_forall_norm_sq_mem_Icc` is the Dasgupta–Gupta dimension `k ≥ 4 log n / (ε²/2 - ε³/3)` [dasguptagupta03][dasguptagupta03]. `exists_linearMap_forall_norm_sq_mem_Icc` states the lemma for `0 < ε ≤ 1` and `k ≥ 24 log n / ε²` without mentioning sign matrices.

The proof goes through the moment generating function rather than Achlioptas's even-moment comparison. This is the standard comparison of squared sub-Gaussian and Gaussian variables, as in Gupta's lecture notes, §11.6.2, Lemma 11.9 [gupta23][gupta23]. Each row gives a Rademacher sum `Y = ∑ i, x i signVal (b j i)`. [BitString/Rademacher.lean](../../Complexity/Probability/BitString/Rademacher.lean) derives its second and fourth moments from the tug-of-war moments of the AMS sketch, using the identity hash family. It also shows that `Y` is sub-Gaussian, `𝔼 exp (sY) ≤ exp (s²‖x‖²/2)`, and that `𝔼 exp (tY²) ≤ (1 - 2t‖x‖²)^(-1/2)`. The last bound averages the sub-Gaussian bound against a Gaussian, using the Gaussian integral `∫ exp (cz²) d𝒩(0, v) = (1 - 2cv)^(-1/2)` of [Gaussian/ExpSq.lean](../../Complexity/Foundations/Probability/Gaussian/ExpSq.lean). The lower tail uses `exp (-u) ≤ 1 - u + u²/2` and `𝔼 Y⁴ ≤ 3‖x‖⁴`, then closes with an explicit polynomial inequality. The scalar inequalities are in [SpecialFunctions/ExpBounds.lean](../../Complexity/Foundations/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/ExpBounds.lean). The [test file](../../ComplexityTests/JohnsonLindenstrauss.lean) exhibits a seed whose projection doubles a squared norm and one that preserves it. It also instantiates the dimension hypotheses with explicit parameters, for example probability at least `1/2` for two points with `ε = 1/2` and `k = 50`.

## Optimal stopping

[OptimalStopping/Basic](../../Complexity/Probability/OptimalStopping/Basic.lean) models stopping on independent observations with discrete laws. `jointLaw` is the product `PMF` of a list of laws. A deterministic stopping rule sees the history so far, most recent observation first, and decides whether to stop. `payoff` evaluates a rule, paying `0` if it never stops. `tsum_payoff_le_valueFrom` is the backward-induction bound: no rule beats the dynamic-programming value `valueFrom`. `tsum_payoff_optimalRule` shows that `optimalRule` attains it; that rule stops exactly when stopping is at least as good as continuing. `valueFrom_eq_iSup` combines the two: the value is the optimum over all rules. Randomized rules are mixtures of deterministic ones. They are modelled only where needed, by pairing each observation with an independent coin, as in the i.i.d. prophet inequality below.

[OptimalStopping/Secretary](../../Complexity/Probability/OptimalStopping/Secretary.lean) treats the classical secretary problem. `n` candidates arrive in the order of a uniform `Equiv.Perm (Fin n)`, and a `Rule` sees only relative ranks. `uniform_map_relRanks` shows that the relative ranks are independent and uniform, which reduces the problem to the stopping model. `successProb_cutoffRule` proves that rejecting `r - 1 ≥ 1` candidates and then hiring the first best-so-far wins with probability `(r-1)/n · ∑_{i=r}^{n} 1/(i-1)`. `successProb_le_successProb_optimalCutoff` proves the Lindley–Dynkin optimality theorem: no rank-based rule beats the cutoff `optimalCutoff n`, the least `r ≥ 1` with `∑_{i=r}^{n-1} 1/i ≤ 1`. The quantitative forms are `successProb_le_exp_neg_one_add` (every rule is at most `1/e + 1/n`), `exp_neg_one_sub_le_successProb_floor` (cutoff `⌊n/e⌋ + 1` achieves at least `1/e - 1/n`) and `tendsto_successProb_optimalCutoff` (limit `1/e`). The full-information variant, where values are observed, is not covered.

[OptimalStopping/Prophet](../../Complexity/Probability/OptimalStopping/Prophet.lean) proves the prophet inequality of Krengel–Sucheston for independent `ℝ≥0`-valued `PMF` laws. `expectedMax_le_two_mul_expectedReward` is a single threshold statement. Let a rule accept every value above `t` and only values of at least `t`. Let `R` be its probability of accepting nothing. If `t` and `R` lie on opposite sides of `E[max]/2` and `1/2`, that is, `2t ≤ E[max]` and `1 ≤ 2R`, or `E[max] ≤ 2t` and `2R ≤ 1`, the rule earns at least `E[max]/2`. This needs no integrability hypothesis. The corollaries are Kleinberg–Weinberg's threshold `t = E[max]/2` (`expectedMax_le_two_mul_expectedReward_half`, assuming `E[max] < ∞`) and Samuel-Cahn's median threshold (`expectedMax_le_two_mul_expectedReward_median`). For a median `m`, accepting values `≥ m` works when `m ≥ E[max]/2`, and accepting values `> m` works when `m ≤ E[max]/2`. A median of the maximum always exists (`exists_median_listMax`), so some single threshold always earns half (`exists_expectedMax_le_two_mul_expectedReward`). Unlike Samuel-Cahn's rules, which take the last value when nothing was accepted, these rules receive `0`, so the bounds imply hers. `exists_tightLaws_lt` shows that `½` is optimal for all stopping rules, using the two values `1` and `k+1` with probability `1/(k+1)`.

[PMF/TailQuantile](../../Complexity/Probability/PMF/TailQuantile.lean) supplies the thresholds. `Cslib.Probability.PMF.exists_tail_quantile` gives every level `0 < q ≤ 1` of a law `μ : PMF ℝ≥0` an upper quantile `τ` with `P(X > τ) ≤ q ≤ P(X ≥ τ)`. The support may be countably infinite and have atoms, unlike `Cslib.Probability.PMF.exists_quantile`, which needs a finite type. Mathlib's `ProbabilityTheory.cdf` gives no quantile.

[OptimalStopping/ProphetIID](../../Complexity/Probability/OptimalStopping/ProphetIID.lean) treats `n` i.i.d. values. Each value comes with an independent coin (`withCoin`). The rule `tieRule τ` accepts the first value above `τ`, or equal to `τ` when its coin shows `true`. `one_sub_pow_mul_expectedMax_le` proves that if the rule accepts each value with probability `1/n`, it earns at least `1 - (1 - 1/n)ⁿ` of `E[max]`. The proof combines two facts. The prophet earns `E[max] ≤ τ + n E[(X - τ)⁺] = n E[X; accept]` (`expectedMax_replicate_le`). The rule earns `E[X; accept] ∑_{i<n} (1 - 1/n)ⁱ`. `ofReal_one_sub_exp_mul_expectedMax_le` is the `1 - 1/e` form. `exists_tieAccept_eq` and `exists_one_sub_pow_mul_expectedMax_le` choose `τ` as a quantile at level `1/n` and use the coin to fill the atom at `τ`, so the bound holds for every law. Since the randomized rule is a mixture, the optimal deterministic gambler `optimalRule` does at least as well (`tieExpectedReward_le_valueFrom`, `one_sub_pow_mul_expectedMax_le_expectedReward_optimalRule`). The tight i.i.d. constant `≈0.745` is not formalized.

[Checked instances](../../ComplexityTests/OptimalStopping.lean) evaluate the three-candidate case (success `1/2`, optimal cutoff `2`), confirm that the never-stop rule has probability `0`, compute the tight prophet instance's backward-induction value `1` through `valueFrom_eq_iSup`, and exercise the median and tightness statements. They also show that the tie-breaking coin is needed. On two i.i.d. values that are `5` with probability `1/5` and `1` otherwise, every deterministic time-invariant rule earns at most `9/5 < 3/4 · E[max] = 183/100`, and the threshold `1` with a `3/8` coin attains the bound.

## Known Limitations

The four meanings of “streaming” here should not be conflated: an input-list fold, an accurate stored probabilistic sketch, the abstract one-pass memory model with communication lower bounds, and a native machine scan with measured workspace.

The abstract model counts reachable memory states and imposes no uniformity or time bound on updates. Its randomized lower bounds are for error at most `1/2` with public randomness that is not charged to the memory, and the AMS sketches have no `ProbComp` sampler or machine realization. The `F_k` estimator assumes that the stream length is known in advance; the source's reservoir-sampling variant for unknown lengths is not formalized. Multi-pass algorithms and turnstile updates as stream items are not formalized, so the join-size sketch covers insertions only. The Theorem 4.3 signature-size lower bound of Alon–Gibbons–Matias–Szegedy is not formalized either. The Count-Sketch guarantees are for a query fixed before the seeds, not for adaptively chosen queries. Neither heavy-hitter algorithm has a `ProbComp` sampler or machine realization. Recovery of all heavy hitters over the universe, merging of Misra–Gries summaries and heavy-hitter space lower bounds are not formalized. The Proposition 3.7 bound is stated for streams of at most `N/8` items rather than the source's `n/2`, and the Proposition 3.8 bound for a universe of `3n` items (`Fin n × Fin 3`) rather than `n`, so that the threshold `2n` does not depend on the inputs.

The k-minimum-values sketch for `F₀` has no `ProbComp` sampler or machine realization either. Its error analysis needs a pairwise independent family into `Fin M` with at least as many values as distinct items. The space-optimal estimators for `F₀` (Bar-Yossef et al., Theorems 2–3; Kane–Nelson–Woodruff's `O(ε⁻² + log n)`) are not formalized, and neither are the `Ω(ε⁻²)` and `Ω(log n)` lower bounds for approximate `F₀`.

The splay-tree cost is a rotation count, not a RAM or Turing-machine time. The Static Finger and Working Set theorems cover only accesses to keys present in the tree. Sleator–Tarjan's Unified Theorem, Cole's Dynamic Finger theorem, BST access-and-rotation lower bounds (Wilber-style) and dynamic optimality are not formalized.

No automatic native realization of the Count-Min sketch was identified in its four source modules.

The locality-sensitive hashing modules prove probabilistic statements only. The near-neighbour structure bounds the parameters `k`, `L` and `ρ` and the number of inspected candidates, but no declaration counts hash evaluations, stored entries or time. Its buckets are described by filtering the point set, so the following are not formalized:

- a dictionary from hash values to buckets, its word-RAM lookup time, the `n^ρ` query time, and the time to build the tables;
- the cost of one hash evaluation or distance test, and the bits needed to store the seeds;
- dynamic insertions and deletions, and the reduction from approximate nearest neighbour to the `(c, r)` problem;
- SimHash, p-stable LSH, and LSH lower bounds;
- MinHash over smaller min-wise independent families: it uses a uniform permutation of the whole universe.

The Johnson–Lindenstrauss development covers only dense `±1` matrices: the sparse `{-√3, 0, √3}` distribution of Achlioptas, Gaussian and spherical projections, fast and sparse JL transforms, the optimal Larsen–Nelson lower bound, a `ProbComp` sampler and any native cost for applying the projection are not formalized.

Randomized online strategies are modelled only against the oblivious adversary (`RandStrategy`); adaptive-online and adaptive-offline adversaries are not. Randomized list update (BIT, COMB) and randomized paging (the marking algorithm, the `H_k` bound) are open, as is continuous-time ski rental. List update covers access requests only; insertions and deletions are not modelled.

The optimal-stopping modules are semantic probability statements over deterministic rules, with randomization only through explicit coin observations. They do not charge the rules' computation and are not phrased through the online `Problem` interface.

The `k`-server modules use natural-number distances, so Double Coverage is proved on the integer line rather than the real line. Double Coverage on trees, the work-function algorithm's `2k-1` bound, the `k`-server conjecture, randomized `k`-server and an algorithm computing the offline optimum are not formalized, and the Lean step functions carry no time bounds.

Online bin packing is modelled by `OnlinePacker`, not through `Problem`/`DetStrategy`, and only deterministic packers are covered. For deterministic packers, the formalized bounds bracket the best asymptotic competitive ratio in `[3/2, 17/10]`: Yao's lower bound, and First Fit's upper bound through `run_firstFitPacker`. The stronger lower bounds (Brown–Liang `1.536`, van Vliet `1.5401`, Balogh–Békési–Galambos `248/161`, and the fully adaptive constructions) are not formalized. Neither are the better online upper bounds of Harmonic-type algorithms, or randomized lower bounds.
