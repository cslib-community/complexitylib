# Dictionaries, hashing and randomized algorithms

Part of the [representative algorithm endpoints](algorithm-endpoints.md); the conventions and limitations stated there apply to this page.

## B-trees

Classic B-trees are pure Lean data structures with `TimeM` node-visit and `FreeM (LEQuery α)` comparison-query cost models, not machine programs. They were ported from Jesse Alama's EpsilonEridani development.

- **Trees and well-formedness.** Import `CslibFrontier.DataStructure.BTree.Height` ([source](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/BTree/Basic.lean)). `BTree α β` is a map from keys to values. A node is a first child followed by `(key, value, child)` triples, so a node with `m` keys has `m + 1` children. `IsWF t tr` says that the minimum degree `t` is at least `2`, that non-root nodes have `t - 1` to `2t - 1` keys and a non-empty root `1` to `2t - 1`, that all leaves lie at one depth, and that `toList` is sorted by key (`List.IsSortedAssoc`). Well-formedness is a hypothesis, never part of the type; `isWF` decides it, so concrete trees are checked by `decide`. `IsWF.height_le_log` bounds the height, counted in nodes, by `Nat.log t ((size + 1) / 2) + 1` [CLRS, Chapter 18][clrs09].
- **Operations.** Each operation has its own module under `CslibFrontier.DataStructure.BTree`: `Lookup` (`lookup`, `contains`), `MinMax` (`min?`, `max?`), `PredSucc` (`pred?`, `succ?`), `Range` (`range`), `Rank` (`rank`), `Insert` (`insert`), `Erase.Basic` (`erase`), `Erase.MinMax` (`eraseMin?`, `eraseMax?`) and `OfSortedList` (the bulk loader `ofSortedList`). The operations are specified on `toList` by the list operations of `Cslib.SortedAssoc` ([source](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/List/SortedAssoc.lean)), for example `toList_insert` and `toList_erase`, and `IsWF.insert` and `IsWF.erase` preserve well-formedness. Insertion and erasure are bottom-up; erasure replaces an interior key by its successor. Each operation is written once over a monad, with a comparator and a node-visit action, and the pure operation is its `Id` instance.
- **Bulk loading.** `ofSortedList t l` ([source](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/BTree/OfSortedList.lean)) builds the tree level by level without comparing keys, so it keeps the input order: `toList_ofSortedList` says that the entries of `ofSortedList t l` are `l`, for every list and every `t`. Only sorted input gives a well-formed tree: `isWF_ofSortedList` assumes `2 ≤ t` and `l.IsSortedAssoc`. Degrees below two are clamped, since the loader builds at minimum degree `max t 2` (`ofSortedList_eq_build`). Importing `CslibFrontier.DataStructure.BTree.TreeMap` adds `toList_ofSortedList_eq_treeMap`: on a sorted list, bulk loading gives the entries of Lean core's `Std.TreeMap.ofList l compare`.
- **Costs.** Import `CslibFrontier.DataStructure.BTree.Cost.Query` ([source](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/BTree/Cost/Query.lean)) for the queries other than `range`, and `Cost.Range`, `Cost.Insert`, `Cost.Erase` (which covers `eraseMin?` and `eraseMax?`) and `Cost.OfSortedList` for the other operations. The `TimeM` instances `lookupT`, `insertT`, `eraseT` and their siblings tick once per node visited, and `ret_lookupT` and its siblings show that their values are the pure operations. On every tree, well-formed or not, point queries and insertion visit at most `height` nodes, `erase` at most `2 * height`, and `eraseMin?` and `eraseMax?` at most `3 * height`; bulk loading `n` entries builds at most `n` nodes, for every `t`. Range restriction visits at most `2 * height + k` nodes for `k` returned entries on a well-formed tree: `time_rangeT` assumes `IsWF t tr`. `time_rangeT_of_occupied` weakens this to `2 ≤ t`, sorted `toList` and every child of the root satisfying `Occupied t` (every node below the root has `t - 1` to `2t - 1` keys). The bound does not hold for arbitrary raw trees. The `_le_log` variants, such as `time_lookupT_le_log` and `time_rangeT_le_log`, combine these bounds with `IsWF.height_le_log`. Comparisons, allocation and the rebuilding of nodes are not charged, so these are not machine-time bounds. Import `Comparisons.Query`, `Comparisons.Range` and `Comparisons.Insert` for exact comparison-count bounds in CSLib's query framework `FreeM (LEQuery α)`. Under any oracle, point queries and insertion make at most `2 * t * height` comparisons on well-formed trees. Under `orderOracle`, range queries returning `k` entries make at most `4 * t * height + 2 * k` comparisons.

The [examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/BTreeInsert.lean) include a root split at `t = 2`, bulk loading at `t = 2` and `t = 3`, and a client that builds a well-formed map by repeated insertion. The [query](../../CslibFrontierTests/BTreeQuery.lean), [erasure](../../CslibFrontierTests/BTreeErase.lean), [cost](../../CslibFrontierTests/BTreeCost.lean) and [comparison](../../CslibFrontierTests/BTreeComparisons.lean) examples check the other operations and exact visit/comparison counts. Erasure comparison bounds, binary in-node search, search comparison lower bounds, a node-access oracle model and B+-trees are not formalized.

## Prime modulus collisions

Import `CslibFrontier.Foundations.NumberTheory.PrimeCollisionWindow` for
`Nat.closedPrimes lo hi`, the primes in the **closed** interval `[lo,hi]`.
`Nat.exists_closedPrimes_collision_bound` states: for every real `δ > 0`, there
is `C > 0` such that, for all `M ≥ 2` and natural `L ≥ C * (Nat.log2 M)^2`,
both `[3,L]` and `[L,2L]` contain primes. For each fixed distinct `a,b ≤ M`,
the fraction of those primes satisfying `Nat.ModEq p a b` is at most `δ`.
The constant is independent of `M,L,a,b`; this is a pairwise guarantee.
`Nat.exists_ceil_closedPrimes_collision_bound` supplies the natural choice
`L = ⌈C * (Nat.log2 M)^2⌉₊`. Apply these theorems with a proof that `δ > 0`.

The finite fraction is the collision probability under uniform choice **among
primes** in the chosen window. Uniform integers in the interval have a different
law. These results provide no efficient prime sampler or runtime guarantee.
They give a quantified interpretation of Goldreich's April 2007 draft Exercise
9.7 (PDF64/printed446), whose `L = poly(log M)` does not specify an error
parameter or degree. Source inventory admission awaits a human maintainer check.
The [checked ordinary-import clients](../../CslibFrontierTests/PrimeCollisionWindow.lean)
include `M=2`, difference one, reversed arguments and `a=0,b=21,L=7`.
For that last example the fractions are `2/3` on `{3,5,7}` and `1/3` on
`{7,11,13}`; it checks the included lower endpoint `7` without asserting that
the conservative quadratic threshold holds for this small example.

For pure cuckoo tables, import `CslibFrontier.DataStructure.Dictionary.CuckooHashing.Basic`
([source](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/CuckooHashing/Basic.lean)). Its
`Cslib.DataStructure.Dictionary.CuckooHashing` namespace contains `PureTable`, `pureReplay`,
`GoodForSet`, and the table-scale parameters. Add
`CslibFrontier.DataStructure.Dictionary.CuckooHashing.HashFamily` for the generic
`IsCuckooHashPair` contract, or
`CslibFrontier.DataStructure.Dictionary.CuckooHashing` for the graph and probability bounds;
these imports do not depend on Word RAM. The [graph examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/CuckooGraph.lean)
exercise successful and failed replay and concrete uniform and polynomial hash pairs. The concrete
regime-switched family is connected to the analysis by
`CslibFrontier.DataStructure.Dictionary.WordRAM.CuckooHashing.Analysis`, exporting
`Cslib.DataStructure.Dictionary.WordRAM.CuckooHashing.isCuckooHashPair_provableFamily`.
Import `CslibFrontier.DataStructure.Dictionary.WordRAM.CuckooHashing` for the complete dictionary;
its [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/WordRAMCuckooHashing.lean) opens both cuckoo namespaces
to use the pure table API alongside the machine implementation.

[abstracts/ComplexityAbstracts/HashTablesAbstract.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/f65cf6aabf60f13270367fea7d080141c7a910f5/abstracts/ComplexityAbstracts/HashTablesAbstract.lean) (retired; its bridge applied [ChainedHash.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/ChainedHash.lean), [LinearProbing.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/LinearProbing.lean), [UniformProbing.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/UniformProbing.lean) and [CuckooHashing.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/CuckooHashing.lean)) selected four expected-amortized dictionary implementations: multiply-shift chained hashing, five-wise linear probing, odd-stride uniform probing and cuckoo hashing. Each supports `query` and `insert` with overwrite, up to bounded distinct-key capacity. **Deletion is not included.** The operation list is fixed before initialization randomness; these are oblivious-sequence guarantees.

The initializer and operation programs are independent of runtime word width and base address, but their dependence on capacity/universe differs by theorem. The abstract did not bound static program-list length. Executed steps exclude caller argument preparation/dispatch. Universe restrictions include the chosen exponent-four polynomial regime; load-factor assumptions and exact initialization costs are visible. These distinctions prohibit a blanket claim of fully dynamic adaptive constant-time dictionaries. The uniform-probing and cuckoo per-operation constants are derived from the capacity threshold `2^320000`: below it they only cap quantities that grow with capacity (the deterministic probe count for uniform probing, the polynomial-hash degree for cuckoo hashing). Their constant bounds are therefore asymptotic statements met trivially at realizable sizes: a linear scan of the stored keys meets the uniform-probing charge at every capacity below `2^320000` and the cuckoo charges (over `8*10^11` instructions per operation) up to about `10^10` keys. For uniform probing the abstract also stated the threshold-free bound: at capacity `2^320000` or more, at most three expected probes give at most `13778` expected instructions per operation (`upLargeOpCharge`). No threshold-free charge is stated for cuckoo hashing.

[abstracts/ComplexityAbstracts/HashFamiliesAbstract.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/f65cf6aabf60f13270367fea7d080141c7a910f5/abstracts/ComplexityAbstracts/HashFamiliesAbstract.lean) (retired; its bridge applied `provableImpl` in [DoubleTabulation.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/HashFamily/WordRAM/DoubleTabulation.lean)) gives high-independence hashing with an explicit regime switch at capacity `2^320000`. Below it, finite-field polynomial hashing supplies exact k-wise independence; above it, fixed-parameter double tabulation has a high-probability transcript coupling, with an explicit condition under which the error bound becomes nontrivial. Queries per resampling epoch are restricted to at most `k` distinct keys. Programs may depend on `(k, capacity, universe, outputWidth)`; static code size is not bounded. The all-capacity evaluation bound grows linearly in `k`. At capacity `2^320000` or more, the abstract also bounded every evaluation by `6862` instructions whatever `k` is (`doubleTabEvalTime`, the double-tabulation evaluation cost plus dispatch).

Across capacities the stated space is at most `(k+9232)*(capacity+1)` words, evaluation at most `111230*k+6862` instructions, and resampling at most `(5*k+46096)*(capacity+1)`. These constants are theorem bounds, not measured runtime or a practical implementation recommendation. The large-regime construction adapts Thorup's double-tabulation work; its fixed-k error statement is explicitly different from the source's original growing-independence asymptotic formulation [thorup13][thorup13].

### Fixed-capacity succinct dynamic dictionary

[CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/Succinct.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/Succinct.lean) provides `Succinct.impl` (over the exact-instruction basis `Basis.exact`) and `Succinct.implStandard` (over the standard basis `Basis.standard`). [CslibFrontierTests/WordRAMSuccinctDictionary.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/WordRAMSuccinctDictionary.lean) gives a checked client. The self-contained formal abstract [SuccinctDictionaryAbstract.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/f65cf6aabf60f13270367fea7d080141c7a910f5/abstracts/ComplexityAbstracts/SuccinctDictionaryAbstract.lean) restated it until the abstracts were retired.

The specification `Dynamic.spec U V n_max` ([CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/Dynamic/Spec.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/Dynamic/Spec.lean)) supports `query u`, `insert u v` (for key `u : Fin U` and value `v < V`), and `delete u` while the active key set `Dictionary.dom s` has size `#(Dictionary.dom s) ≤ n_max`. For `n_max ≤ U`, initialization draws one hash seed, and every operation keeps that seed unchanged. The seed marginal stays exact (`seedSlack = 1`) against the uniform seed distribution. No persistent flag is kept to latch hash or overflow failures across operations. Each bucket's item count and expected extra collision or overflow probes (`probeExcess`) per operation depend only on the current active key set. The physical chain order and the division of a bucket's items between fixed-size full slabs and the shared tail pool under lazy promotion and demotion thresholds depend on operation history. The single memory block `[base, base + Succinct.space U V w n_max)` uses at most `Dynamic.succinctWords 81 213 U V w n_max` words (the information-theoretic baseline `OPT(U, V, n_max)` plus `81 * n_max * ⌈log₂ ⌈log₂ (max n_max 4)⌉⌉` bits, packed into `w`-bit words, plus `213` words) without requiring a polynomial bound on `U`. Initialization runs in `initTime ≤ Succinct.initSteps U n_max + 1 ≤ 31 * n_max + 1092` expected body instructions (`Succinct.initTime_impl_le`), where body instructions count executed instructions inside the subroutine excluding caller argument setup and the final `halt`. At the `Memory.Prog` level ([CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/SuccinctHash.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/SuccinctHash.lean)), `queryWithHashProg_dict_spec`, `insertWithHashProg_dict_spec`, and `deleteWithHashProg_dict_spec` bound per-seed amortized `memoryCost` (the `Memory.Prog` instruction count) by `26 + 8 * probeExcess`, `184 + 8 * probeExcess`, and `270 + 8 * probeExcess` with potential `66 * slotPotential`, where `slotPotential` sums the per-bucket slab-hysteresis potentials. In turn, `tsum_uniform_probeExcess_reducedPolyHash_le_five` bounds expected `probeExcess` by `5`. At the compiled Word-RAM level ([CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/SuccinctDispatch.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/SuccinctDispatch.lean)), the five generic `Memory.Prog` subroutines (`extractQuotGenProg`, `queryWithHashGenProg`, `insertWithHashGenProg`, `deletePrepWithHashGenProg`, `deleteFinishWithHashGenProg`) do not depend on `(U, V, w, n_max)`. The full program `subroutineCode U V w n_max` depends on `(U, V, w, n_max)` only through the `space` constant in `allocSpan` (the bump-allocator step that advances the frontier register by `space`), the 36-instruction parameter-loading prefix `loadParamsCode`, and the hash code. It does not depend on `base`. Consequently, both the register budget `Succinct.rSuccinct` (`≥ 292`) and the per-operation charge `Succinct.chargeVal = 313 * Succinct.opFactor + 284725` (where `Succinct.opFactor` is the maximum of `1` and the five parameter-free subroutines' `Realizes.factor` instruction-per-memory-step constants) are universal `O(1)` constants independent of `(U, V, w, n_max)`, `base`, and `ops.length`. Theorem `Succinct.seq_expected_amortized` bounds expected total body instructions across any valid oblivious sequence `ops` (fixed before initialization) by `initTime + ops.length * Succinct.chargeVal`. No theorem bounds static code size. The hash (`ReducedKWise`) and bit-packed accessors (`BitPackedArray`) are hand-written `WordRAM.Code` primitives. The deterministic bucket, slab, tail, chain, and Feistel bit-extraction subroutines ([CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/SuccinctProg.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/SuccinctProg.lean) to [CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/SuccinctHash.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/SuccinctHash.lean)) are `Memory.Prog` programs lowered by `realize`. Because `ReducedKWise` has no `Realizes` certificate, the top-level dispatcher ([CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/SuccinctDispatch.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/WordRAM/SuccinctDispatch.lean)) is hand-written `WordRAM.Code`. Before calling `Succinct.seq_expected_amortized`, verify `0 < w`, `n_max ≤ U`, `Fits w U` (`U < 2 ^ w`), `Fits w V` (`V < 2 ^ w`), `Fits w (base + Succinct.space U V w n_max)`, word-bounded initial machine state `x.Bounded w` (all initial registers and memory cells hold `< 2 ^ w`), `x.regs Succinct.frontierReg = base`, zeroed initial memory on `[base, base + Succinct.space U V w n_max)`, and `(Dynamic.spec U V n_max).ValidHistory (Dictionary.empty U) ops` for an oblivious sequence `ops` fixed before initialization.

### FKS two-level perfect hashing (static, abstract cost model)

Import `CslibFrontier.DataStructure.Dictionary.PerfectHashing.Construction` ([Construction.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/PerfectHashing/Construction.lean)), which re-exports the table layer. [CslibFrontierTests/PerfectHashing.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/PerfectHashing.lean) is a complete checked client: it instantiates a family, builds and checks a concrete table, and applies the construction theorems.

[CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/PerfectHashing/Table.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/PerfectHashing/Table.lean) and [Construction.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/PerfectHashing/Construction.lean) formalize the static dictionary of Fredman, Komlós and Szemerédi [fks84][fks84] in the form of CLRS 3rd ed., Section 11.5 [clrs09][clrs09]. That account uses an arbitrary universal family; FKS's own `k x mod p mod s` family and their `n + o(n)` refinement are not formalized. A `PerfectHashing.Family X` gives, for each range `r`, a seed law `seed r` and functions `hash r s : X → Fin (r + 1)`. Every theorem about the construction assumes `∀ r, IsTwoUniversal (F.seed r) (F.hash r)` and a duplicate-free key list (`keys.Nodup`). The `Valid` conditions are: `n` top buckets (top range `n - 1`), `∑ b_j² ≤ 4n`, a block of exactly `b_j²` cells for each nonempty bucket, and secondary hashes injective on their buckets.

- **Correctness and probes.** `PerfectHashing.lookup F x` is an `OracleComp` that reads memory cells and depends only on the family and the key. `runLookup_memory` proves it answers `decide (x ∈ keys)` on the laid-out `memory D keys` whenever the secondary hashes are injective. `isQueryBounded_lookup` bounds every lookup by `3` reads on any memory (hash evaluation is not charged).
- **Space.** The table is counted in **typed cells**, not words or bits: a cell holds one key, one seed, or one block address with one seed. The cell bound is an `O(n)`-word bound only for families whose seeds fit in `O(1)` words. No such family is formalized yet; a seed of the uniform-function family in the tests is a whole function table. A valid table occupies exactly `n + 1 + ∑ b_j²` cells for `n ≥ 1` (`size_eq`), at most `5n + 2` (`size_le`), and every later cell is empty (`memory_of_size_le`).
- **Construction.** `PerfectHashing.build F fuel keys` is an `OracleComp` over `buildSpec F`. Its calls are `draw r` (a fresh seed) and `eval r s x` (one hash value). Every table in the support of the honest run is `Valid` (`valid_of_mem_support_build`), so it answers correctly and fits in `5n + 2` cells. The cost model is **abstract**: `expectedCost` with `drawCost` or `evalCost` counts seed draws or hash evaluations. Bucketing, success tests and layout are free local computation, and no word-RAM time is claimed. Each level retries at most `fuel` times (`OracleComp.retry`) and the run returns `none` when a level runs out. For `n ≥ 1` keys, failure has probability at most `(n + 1)·2^{-fuel}` (`evalWith_build_none_le`, stated with `keys.length - 1 + 2`). The construction makes at most `2(n + 1)` expected draws (`expectedCost_build_draw_le`) and at most `4n` expected evaluations (`expectedCost_build_eval_le`), uniformly in `fuel`. There is no unbounded Las Vegas form.

The probability core is in [CslibFrontier/Probability/HashFamily/Collisions.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/HashFamily/Collisions.lean):

- `sum_card_filter_sq`: the identity `∑_j b_j² = n + #ordered collisions`;
- `IsUniversalHash.tsum_orderedCollisionCount_le`: at most `n(n - 1)ε` expected ordered colliding pairs, which is `2·C(n,2)ε`;
- `IsTwoUniversal.toOuterMeasure_not_injOn_le`: `Pr[not injective] ≤ 1/2` when `n² ≤ m`;
- `IsTwoUniversal.toOuterMeasure_four_mul_lt_sum_card_filter_sq_le`: `Pr[4n < ∑_j b_j²] ≤ 1/2` when `n ≤ m`.

CLRS states the last two for `m = n²` and `m = n` with strict bounds (`< 1/2`, and `≥ 4n`). The non-strict forms here are the ones the construction's tests use. Markov's inequality for a `PMF` is `Cslib.Probability.PMF.mul_toOuterMeasure_le_tsum` in [CslibFrontier/Probability/PMF/Markov.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/PMF/Markov.lean).

The generic retry combinator, with its geometric failure and cost bounds, is in [CslibFrontier/Languages/Probabilistic/Retry.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Languages/Probabilistic/Retry.lean). That file also has the product-of-programs lemmas (`expectedCost_pi`, union bound `toOuterMeasure_evalWith_pi_exists_le`). The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/PerfectHashing.lean) cover:

- the uniform-function family (`isDeltaUniversalHash_uniformOfFintype`);
- a three-key table in `Fin 8` with every lookup and its size checked by `decide`;
- a broken table whose lookup fails;
- a top seed that fails the `4n` test;
- a successful construction run.

Remaining work ([#3276](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3276)) includes:

- dynamic perfect hashing;
- a word-RAM realization and a bridge to `w`-bit cell probes;
- the FKS family itself, and the deterministic construction;
- the Fredman–Komlós lower bound on description size.

### Feistel permutation families

Import `CslibFrontier.DataStructure.Dictionary.FeistelPerm` ([CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/FeistelPerm.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/FeistelPerm.lean)); see [CslibFrontierTests/FeistelPerm.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/FeistelPerm.lean) for checked proper-subset and mixed-domain clients. It formalizes pure mathematical one-round Feistel involutions (`feistelPairEquiv`, `feistelMixedEquiv`, `feistelBitEquiv`, compatible with `FeistelQuotient`) together with two assumption-free permutation families from Li, Liang, Yu, and Zhou (SODA 2024, `arXiv:2310.20536v1`, Section 5.4 Lemmas 5.1–5.2 and Appendix B Corollaries B.5–B.6): a double-tabulation child router (`ChildRouterSeed`, [thorup13][thorup13]) with seed-space bound `childRouterSeedBits_pow_le` and bucket-load concentration bounds `dens_childRouter_bucketLoad_outside_le`, `dens_childRouter_bucketLoad_outside_le_of_threshold`, and `dens_childRouterBit_bucketLoad_outside_le`, and a `2`-wise trinomial key-ID compressor (`IdCompressorSeed`) with seed-space bound `idCompressorSeedBits_le` and collision bounds `dens_not_injOn_idCompressorHead_le`, `dens_not_injOn_idCompressorHead_le_two_div_sq`, and `dens_not_injOn_idCompressorBit_le`. The underlying variance-sensitive `2m`-wise central moment and Markov tail bounds (`expect_sum_sub_expect_pow_le`, `dens_abs_sum_sub_expect_le`, `dens_abs_sum_sub_expect_le_two_pow`) live in [CslibFrontier/Probability/KWiseIndependent/Concentration.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/KWiseIndependent/Concentration.lean).

Two explicit quantitative premises replace the source's asymptotic hypotheses: bucket concentration uses `2 ^ s * (150 * childRouterHalfInd #S) ^ 3 ≤ #S` in place of `2 ^ s * s ^ 4 ≤ #S` (implied by `2 ^ s * s ^ 4 ≤ #S` when `childRouterMinS ≤ s` via `childRouter_conc_of_two_pow_mul_pow_four_le`, and valid down to `s = 1` once `#S` is large enough), and the closed-form double-tabulation theorems additionally assume `childRouterThreshold ε_inv α_inv ≤ #S` (an explicit astronomical finite-size constant from Thorup's high-probability `k`-uniqueness bound, absent from the source's idealized Claim B.2; below it, the parametric `2m`-wise bound `dens_exists_bucketLoad_sub_ge_le` applies without `childRouterThreshold`). This module supplies permutation, bucket-concentration/collision probability, and seed-size results only; Word-RAM evaluation certificates for the Feistel wrapper and an end-to-end subconstant-redundancy dictionary remain separate.

### Subconstant-redundancy leaf primitives

[CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/Spillover.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/Spillover.lean), [CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/DynamicMatching.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/DynamicMatching.lean), and [CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/Minimap.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/Dictionary/Minimap.lean) formalize the pure leaf-storage building blocks of Li, Liang, Yu, and Zhou, *Dynamic Dictionary with Subconstant Wasted Bits per Key* (SODA 2024, [arXiv:2310.20536v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20536v1), `LiLiangYuZhou2024`); [CslibFrontierTests/SubconstantMinimap.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/SubconstantMinimap.lean) gives a checked client.

- **Power-of-two spillover (`Spillover`):** for payload universe `Fin X` (`1 ≤ X`) and parameter `r_sp ≥ 1`, `Spillover.encode` splits `x < X` at modulus `2 ^ memBits X r_sp` (`0` bits when `X ≤ r_sp`) with spillover range `spillUniv X r_sp ≤ 2 * r_sp` (`spillUniv_le_two_mul`), at most `2 / r_sp` wasted bits in real logarithm (`memBits_add_logb_spillUniv_le`), and at most `2` ceiling-log wasted bits across `n` independent payloads at `r_sp = n` (`clog_mul_spillUniv_pow_add_le`, adapting Pătrașcu FOCS 2008, `Patrascu2008`).
- **Multi-round unit-circle dynamic matching (`DynamicMatching`):** for key-ID universe `Fin U'` and `n'` slots (`|A| ≤ n'`), `DynamicMatching.matching` assigns active keys `A ↪ Fin n'` deterministically from `U'` round permutations `Scheme U' n'` (`Fin U' → Equiv.Perm (Fin (U' + n'))`). Under uniform independent permutations (`schemePMF`), `expect_rounds_le` bounds expected active rounds `T` by `3 * ⌈log₂ (max |A| 2)⌉`, and `relocDist_insert_le`, `relocDist_delete_le`, `relocDist_replace_le` (with `expect_relocDist_*_le`) bound single-update ball relocations by `O(T)` and `O(log (max |A| 2))` in expectation.
- **Fixed- and bounded-capacity leaf minimaps (`Minimap`):** combines `Cslib.BitString.subsetEquiv` ([CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/BitString/Combination.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/BitString/Combination.lean)), `Spillover` (`r_sp = n'`), and `DynamicMatching` into `FixedEncoded U' n' X` (`m = n'`) and `BoundedEncoded U' n' X` (`0 ≤ m ≤ n'`). At full capacity `m = n'`, for `1 ≤ n' ≤ U'` and `1 ≤ X`, `bitWidth_le_infoBits_add_two` bounds total leaf bit width `headerBits U' n' X n' + n' * memBits X n'` by `⌈log₂(C(U', n') * X ^ n')⌉ + 2` (`≤ 2` wasted bits per leaf); for `1 ≤ n'`, `lookupHeader` specifies the shared metadata-only lookup function (`queryTableSize_le`); `eq_of_query_eq_fixed` proves history-independent canonical uniqueness from `query` on leaf states satisfying `WellFormed le_rfl h`; and `expect_slotDiff_replace_encode_le` bounds expected slot rewrites under full-leaf `replace` by `38 * ⌈log₂ (max n' 2)⌉`.

These modules are pure mathematical specifications; the multi-level tree schedule, Word-RAM lookup-table and bit-packed evaluation, variable-length adapter trees, strong history independence, resizing, and super-polynomial universes are not formalized here.

### Balls-to-slots specification and pure k-kick tree

Import `CslibFrontier.DataStructure.BallsToSlots.Spec` ([Spec.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/BallsToSlots/Spec.lean)) and `CslibFrontier.DataStructure.BallsToSlots.KickTree` ([KickTree.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/BallsToSlots/KickTree.lean)); see [CslibFrontierTests/DataStructure/KickTree.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/DataStructure/KickTree.lean) for a checked execution trace and `trinomialPolyHash` hypothesis-satisfiability checks. They formalize the pure balls-to-slots problem and `k`-kick tree of Bender, Farach-Colton, J. Kuszmaul, W. Kuszmaul, and Liu [bfckkl22][bfckkl22] (`arXiv:2111.00602v2`, Sections 2.1, 2.4, 3, 3.1, and 5.1) in namespace `Cslib.DataStructure.BallsToSlots`:

- **Specification (`BallsToSlots.Spec`):** `SlotAssignment α M` stores present balls injectively in primary slots `Fin M` under `Op` (`insert`, `delete`, `query`), with integer floor probe cost `slotProbeComplexity = 1 + Nat.log 2 (probeIndex + 1)` (differing by `< 1` per ball from Section 2.1's real-logarithm `1 + log₂ i`), XOR probe sequence `xorProbeSeq`, `finProdFinEquiv`-based `dyadicSplitEquiv`, deterministic `switchingCost`, `StateTransitionSpec` instances `spec` / `schemeSpec`, and snapshot concentration `dens_sum_totalProbeComplexity_gt_le_of_snapshotBound` (built on general `k`-wise fiber conditioning in [Restriction.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/KWiseIndependent/Restriction.lean), disjoint-restriction moment bounds in [Concentration.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/KWiseIndependent/Concentration.lean), and two-stage product union tails in [TwoStage.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Probability/TwoStage.lean)).
- **`k`-kick tree (`BallsToSlots.KickTree`):** bridges `max (Nat.iterLog 2 n) 1` within a factor of `2` of `Dynamic.logLogFactor n` (via `Nat.max_iterLog_two_eq` in [IterLog.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/Nat/IterLog.lean)), and defines `KickTree.Params`, priority-kick `insert` via `Fin.find?` (`switchingCost_insert_le`, `switchingCost_standardInsert_le : switchingCost ≤ k + 1`), kick-depth invariance (`assignedDepth_insert_of_mem`), removal-only `delete` (Section 5.1, printed p. 27 / PDF p. 28; vacating only the deleted ball's slot without moving other balls, though state still depends on insertion order), schedule inequality `depthSteps_le_binExp`, 3-wise expected probe bounds `expect_ballProbeComplexity_standardInsert_le` (Lemma 1) and `expect_totalProbeComplexity_standardRun_le` (Theorem 2), and multi-tree concentration `dens_sum_totalProbeComplexity_standardRun_gt_le` / `dens_sum_totalProbeComplexity_standardRun_gt_le_of_whp`.

Explicit adaptations and quantitative thresholds: `SlotAssignment` omits Section 3's unused special slots and uses `xorProbeSeq` to enumerate each depth-`i` dyadic bin within offset `< 2 ^ binExp i`; `standardParams m₀ k` clamps depth to `0` below `m₀ = 32` (`effectiveDepth`) and rounds the paper's `s_d ≈ (log^(d) n)^6` and `Pr[depthCap < i + 1] ≈ (log^(i+1) n)^(-2)` schedule to powers of two via `capExp i = 2 * iterLog (i + 2) (2 ^ m₀)` and `binExp (i + 1) = 6 * iterLog (i + 2) (2 ^ m₀)` (the `+2` offset aligns 0-based indexing with `Nat.iterLog`, and Chebyshev's `L^4 / L^6 = 1 / L^2` ratio gives a geometric series so 3-wise independence suffices for Lemma 1 and Theorem 2; the per-ball constant `96 * max (Nat.iterLog (k + 1) (2 ^ m₀)) 1` improves on the deterministic ceiling `1 + m₀` starting at `m₀ ≥ 96` for `k ≥ 3` and for all `m₀ ≥ 288` for `k = 2`); and `dens_sum_totalProbeComplexity_standardRun_gt_le` differs from Theorem 3 in four ways — (a) the disjoint-footprint partition `R` is assumed as a hypothesis, (b) special slots are absent in this pure primary-slot model, (c) the tail bound is polynomial (`2m`-th moment Markov) rather than an exponential Chernoff bound, and (d) it assumes `max 3 (2 * m * 4 ^ m₀)`-wise independence (with nonempty tail events only for `m₀ ≥ 96`). Universal key quotienting (the upcoming `Cslib.DataStructure.Dictionary.KickTree` in #5129), Four-Russians metadata and local query-routing cubbies, fixed-capacity Word-RAM dictionary realization, and resizable Word-RAM migration remain tracked in [#5186](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/5186).

## Approximate membership filters

Import `CslibFrontier.DataStructure.ApproximateMembership.WordRAM.QuotientFilter` ([CslibFrontier/DataStructure/ApproximateMembership/WordRAM/QuotientFilter.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/ApproximateMembership/WordRAM/QuotientFilter.lean)) and construct `QuotientFilter.implOfEps` (for target error `ε ≠ 0` via `epsLog ε`) or `QuotientFilter.impl` (for dyadic error `(1 / 2) ^ k`); see [CslibFrontierTests/WordRAMQuotientFilter.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/WordRAMQuotientFilter.lean) for a complete checked client. The self-contained formal abstract [ApproximateMembershipAbstract.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/f65cf6aabf60f13270367fea7d080141c7a910f5/abstracts/ComplexityAbstracts/ApproximateMembershipAbstract.lean) restated the result until the abstracts were retired.

The specification `ApproximateMembership.spec U n ε` enforces strict set validity on active set `S` (`insert u` requires `u ∉ S ∧ #S < n`; `delete u` requires `u ∈ S`; `query u` is always valid) and guarantees zero false negatives on every random branch together with per-absent-query false-positive probability at most `ε` across any fixed valid operation sequence. The constructors require key bit-width `0 < w₀`, word width `2 * w₀ ≤ w` (needed to bit-pack remainders and derive `quotBits w₀ n < w`), universe bound `U ≤ 2 ^ w₀`, memory fit `base + QuotientFilter.space w w₀ n (epsLog ε) < 2 ^ w`, and zeroed owned memory `[base, base + QuotientFilter.space w w₀ n (epsLog ε))` with `qfReg 240 = base`. The single allocated block uses `QuotientFilter.space w w₀ n (epsLog ε) ≤ 640 * ((n * epsLog ε + w - 1) / w + 1)` words, and initialization takes `QuotientFilter.setupSteps w₀ n` expected body instructions. The randomized hash (`ReducedKWise`) and pure bit-packed layout accessors (`SlotArray`) are implemented as low-level primitives. The deterministic cluster navigation, scan, and shift phases in [CslibFrontier/DataStructure/ApproximateMembership/WordRAM/QuotientFilter/Cluster.lean](../../CslibFrontier/DataStructure/ApproximateMembership/WordRAM/QuotientFilter/Cluster.lean) are authored as `QuotientFilter.clusterOpProg : ClusterInput → Memory.Prog ℕ` and lowered via the `realize` tactic (`realizes_clusterOp.code`). Compiled programs `p_init` and `p_op` depend on `(w, w₀, n, ε)` with no static code-size bound.

## Freivalds' matrix-product verification

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.Freivalds` ([CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Freivalds.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Freivalds.lean)). It checks a claimed product `A * B = C` of rectangular matrices `l × m`, `m × n` and `l × n` over any ring with decidable equality, following [Freivalds][freivalds79]. The deterministic check `checkM A B C x` computes `A *ᵥ (B *ᵥ x)` and `C *ᵥ x` in `TimeM` and compares them. The cost model charges `2` per dot-product entry (one multiplication, one addition) and `1` per compared entry; reads, sampling and the vector encoding are free, and ring operations have unit cost whatever the operand size. Under it, `time_checkM` is exactly `2 * (m * n + l * m + l * n) + l`, independent of `x`, and `time_checkAllM_square` gives `k * (6 * n ^ 2 + n)` for `k` checks of square matrices.

`verify e k A B C` is a `ProbComp` that draws `k` uniform vectors in `Fin n → S`, encodes the entries with `e : S → R` and runs every check. The guarantees are one-sided:

- **Completeness:** `eval_verify_of_mul_eq` gives acceptance with probability exactly `1` when `A * B = C`.
- **Soundness:** `eval_verify_true_le` bounds acceptance of an incorrect product by `(1/|S|)^k`, given that `s ↦ d * e s` is injective for every nonzero `d`. With `0`/`1` vectors (`bit`) this hypothesis holds over every ring and `eval_verify_bool_true_le` gives `(1/2)^k`; for `k = 1` this is Theorem 1.4 of [Mitzenmacher and Upfal][mitzenmacherupfal17]. Over a finite field with uniform vectors, `eval_verify_field_true_le` gives `(1/|F|)^k`.

The probability core is in [CslibFrontier/Probability/Freivalds.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/Freivalds.lean). It contains the counting bound `card_filter_mulVec_eq_mul_card_le`, its `k`-vector form, and `isUniversalHash_mulVec`, which states `M ↦ M *ᵥ (e ∘ x)` as a `1/|S|`-universal family under the existing `IsUniversalHash` predicate. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Freivalds.lean) include several things. One is a `2 × 2` product that one vector misses and another exposes. Another is a `1 × 1` instance whose acceptance probability is exactly `1/2`, which shows the `0`/`1` bound is attained. A third is a `ZMod 4` instance where the encoding `id` fails the injectivity hypothesis and the counting bound fails with it. The randomized verifier is not a machine program: Karp–Rabin fingerprinting, rolling-hash matching, the fingerprint equality protocol and bit-level costs remain open ([ROADMAP](../../ROADMAP.md#randomized-verification-and-fingerprinting)).

## Bilinear matrix fingerprints

Import `CslibFrontier.Probability.BilinearFingerprint`
([source](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/BilinearFingerprint.lean)).
For a nonzero rectangular matrix over any finite field,
`toOuterMeasure_bilinear_ne_zero_ge` gives probability at least
`(1 - 1 / |F|)^2` that `∑ i, r i * (M *ᵥ s) i ≠ 0`.
Sampling is explicitly the uniform law on the product of the column-vector and row-vector
spaces, hence the vectors are independent. There is no positive-dimension assumption:
a nonzero matrix already rules out empty row or column types.

The prime specialization `toOuterMeasure_bilinear_zmod_ne_zero_ge` is Goldreich's
Exercise 9.22 (r5.pdf, printed page 452 / PDF page 70).
`toOuterMeasure_bilinear_ne_zero_eq` factors success into `1 - 1 / |F|` times the
probability that `M *ᵥ s ≠ 0`.
`toOuterMeasure_bilinear_vecMulVec` proves sharpness for outer products of nonzero vectors.
This is a probability endpoint, with no running-time or general rank-formula claim.
The [ordinary-import examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/BilinearFingerprint.lean) apply
the prime theorem to a rectangular matrix, attain equality in characteristic two, and
check why the zero-matrix hypothesis is necessary.

## Luby's randomized maximal independent set

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.LubyMIS` ([CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/LubyMIS.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/LubyMIS.lean)). It formalizes the random-priority variant of Luby's algorithm, Algorithm 7.12 ("Fast MIS 2") of [Wattenhofer][wattenhofer16], which the source attributes to Métivier, Robson, Saheb-Djahromi and Zemmari. The graph is a Mathlib `SimpleGraph V` on a finite type, and the target is Mathlib's `Maximal G.IsIndepSet`. A `State` holds the chosen and the live vertices. `phase` is a `ProbComp` that draws a uniform ranking `σ : V ≃ Fin (card V)`, a uniform random permutation. It then takes the deterministic `step`: every live vertex that ranks below all its live neighbours joins (`joiners`), and the joiners and their neighbours stop being live. `run k` iterates `phase` `k` times from the all-live state (`OracleComp.iterate`), and `lubyMIS k` answers the chosen set. Probabilities are statements about `ProbComp.eval`.

- **Correctness:** `isIndepSet_of_mem_support_lubyMIS` says that every output is independent after any number of phases. `Invariant.maximal_iff` says that a reachable chosen set is maximal exactly when nothing is live. `maximal_of_mem_support_lubyMIS` says that after any `k ≥ card V` phases every output is maximal, because the least-ranked live vertex always joins. `liveGraph L` is Mathlib's `(G.induce L).spanningCoe` (`liveGraph_eq_spanningCoe_induce`).
- **Edge removal:** `two_mul_expect_liveEdges_phase_le` says that one phase keeps at most half the live edges in expectation (Lemma 7.14). It follows from the counting form `two_mul_sum_liveEdges_step_le`, a sum over all rankings, and from `card_filter_least_mul_card`: a fixed vertex of a set `S` ranks least on `S` for exactly `1/|S|` of the rankings.
- **Round bounds:** iterating gives `two_pow_mul_expect_liveEdges_run_le`, at most `|E| / 2^k` expected live edges after `k` phases. Markov's inequality then gives `two_pow_mul_toOuterMeasure_live_nonempty_le`. If `|E| ≤ 2^m`, `toOuterMeasure_not_maximal_lubyMIS_le` says that `m + t + 1` phases fail to give a maximal independent set with probability at most `2^-t`. `one_sub_le_toOuterMeasure_maximal_lubyMIS` and its `_clog` and `_card` forms state this as success at least `1 - 2^-t`, with `m` instantiated to `⌈log₂ |E|⌉` and to `2⌈log₂ n⌉`. `toOuterMeasure_not_maximal_lubyMIS_le_inv_pow` bounds the failure probability after `(c + 2)⌈log₂ n⌉ + 1` phases by `n^-c`.

The source draws independent reals in `[0, 1]`. Restricted to the live vertices, a uniform ranking has the same law as the order of those reals, but this equivalence is not formalized. The source gets its high-probability bound from Lemma 7.14 by a Chernoff argument (Corollary 7.18). Here the `O(log n)` bound comes from the first moment, with explicit constants. Theorem 7.15, the expected number of phases, is not formalized. The model is a centralized simulation of the synchronous phases, not a LOCAL message-passing algorithm. Messages, the rounds inside a phase and per-vertex views are not modelled, so it is not connected to the oriented-cycle LOCAL model of [Distributed rounds in the LOCAL model](../circuits-logic-and-other-models/query-and-communication.md#distributed-rounds-in-the-local-model). Luby's original marking algorithm with pairwise independence, its NC derandomization, Ghaffari's `O(log Δ) + poly(log log n)` bound and the Kuhn–Moscibroda–Wattenhofer lower bounds remain open; see `ROADMAP.md`. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Algorithms/Lean/LubyMIS.lean) run the phases on the paths with three and four vertices and on the triangle. They show that a phase need not remove every edge, count the live edges left on the four-vertex path over all `24` rankings, and show that the intermediate chosen set is not maximal. They also instantiate the Las Vegas statement nonvacuously and check one instance of the high-probability bound.
