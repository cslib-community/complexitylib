# Network flows, cuts and matching

Part of the [representative algorithm endpoints](algorithm-endpoints.md); the conventions and limitations stated there apply to this page.

## Global minimum cut

[abstracts/CslibFrontierAbstracts/KargerMinCutAbstract.lean](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/abstracts/CslibFrontierAbstracts/KargerMinCutAbstract.lean) (retired; its bridge applied [KargerMinCut.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/WordRAM/KargerMinCut.lean) and [KargerStein.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/WordRAM/KargerStein.lean)) treats weighted undirected multigraphs, `n≥2`, fitted total doubled edge weight and word widths sufficient for workspace/trial counters. A single contraction trial is one fixed 16-register program: `42*n²+12*n+21` worst-case instructions and at most `n-2` random calls, always returning a genuine nontrivial cut value. When minimum cut is positive, it succeeds with probability at least `2/(n*(n-1))`.

The Karger–Stein endpoint uses one fixed 24-register program, `O(n² log n)` worst-case steps and `O(n²)` matrix scratch per tree, with success at least `1/(floorlog₂(n)+15)`. Repetition gives minimum-cut **value** with failure at most `1/(2n)` in `O(n² log³ n)`. It does not enumerate all minimum cuts or attain the source's sparse-space formulation. The threshold is an integer inequality rather than a literal square-root expression; the abstract labels the implementation and proof adaptations.

### Each minimum cut, and the number of minimum cuts

The output cut and the per-cut bound are in `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.Karger.Contraction` ([Contraction.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Karger/Contraction.lean)). The count is in `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.Karger.MinCutCount` ([MinCutCount.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Karger/MinCutCount.lean)), which imports it. The contraction structure `Karger.spec` keeps only the weight matrix, so `karger` answers a weight, not a cut. Following [Karger and Stein, Section 2][kargerstein96], `trackedSpec n` also keeps the block of original vertices merged into each supervertex (`TrackedGraph`). Blocks are contracted by `Cslib.WeightedCut.contractBlocks` ([Blocks.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/WeightedCut/Blocks.lean)), which agrees with `contractPart` on the fibres of a supervertex map (`contractBlocks_pullbackCut`).

- **Bookkeeping:** `map_run_trackedSpec` shows that forgetting the blocks maps every run over `trackedSpec n` to the run of the same client over `spec`. It is an instance of `Cslib.ProbOracleComp.run_interpret_map_state` with `TrackedGraph.toGraph` as the state representation. The proofs run over the blocks and transfer to `spec` through `map_run_trackedSpec`: `run_tracked_contractStep_bind{,_of_eq_zero}` give `run_contractStep_bind{,_of_eq_zero}`, the survival bound and the support lemma (`trackedSurvives_contractTimes` with `TrackedSurvives.survives`, `exists_of_mem_support_tracked_contractTimes`) give `survives_contractTimes` and `isContractionOf_of_mem_support_contractTimes`, `run_karger_tracked` gives `answerLaw_eq`, and the output cut and the per-cut bound give `exists_cut_of_mem_support_answerLaw` and `two_div_le_toOuterMeasure_karger`. Statements about the blocks have no untracked counterpart.
- **Output cut:** one run of `karger` also outputs the block of supervertex `1` of the last two (`TrackedGraph.side`); `cutLaw W₀` is its law. On a multigraph (`IsMultigraph W₀`) with `n ≥ 2` vertices, `answer_eq_cutWeight_side` shows the output cut is non-trivial and the answer is its weight; `answerLaw_eq_map_cutLaw` states this as `answerLaw ⟨n, W₀⟩ = (cutLaw W₀).map (cutWeight W₀)`.
- **Per-cut bound** (Theorem 2.2 of the source): `two_div_le_cutLaw`. If also `0 < minCut W₀`, each fixed minimum cut `{C, Cᶜ}` (with `C` non-trivial, which forces `n ≥ 2`) is output with probability at least `2 / (n (n - 1)) = 1 / C(n, 2)`. The proof uses the survival induction over the blocks (`trackedSurvives_contractTimes`): a cut that survives to two supervertices is the output cut, up to complement.
- **Count:** `card_minCuts_le_choose_two`. Each run outputs one cut, so the events for distinct minimum cuts are disjoint and their probabilities sum to at most `1`. A connected multigraph therefore has at most `n.choose 2` minimum cuts, counted by the side `C` with `x₀ ∉ C` and `cutWeight W₀ C = minCut W₀`. The bound is due to Dinitz, Karzanov and Lomonosov (1976); the proof is the one in [Karger and Stein][kargerstein96].

The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/KargerMinCutCount.lean) count by brute force the `C(4, 2) = 6` minimum cuts of the unit 4-cycle, where the bound is tight, and the `C(5, 2) = 10` minimum cuts of the unit 5-cycle. They also instantiate both theorems on the 4-cycle. The empty graph on four vertices has `7` non-trivial minimum cuts. The filter, which then also contains `∅`, has `8 > C(4, 2)` elements, so `0 < minCut W₀` is needed.

Not formalized:
- the `O((2n)^(2α))` bound on `α`-minimal cuts (Theorem 8.4 of the source);
- the enumeration and the cactus representation of all minimum cuts;
- the per-cut bound for the recursive Karger–Stein algorithm.

### Deterministic minimum cut: Stoer–Wagner

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.StoerWagner.Basic` ([Basic.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/StoerWagner/Basic.lean)). It uses the weighted multigraphs of `Cslib.WeightedCut` (`W : Fin n → Fin n → ℕ`, symmetric with zero diagonal) and the contraction of [Contraction.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/WeightedCut/Contraction.lean) (`contractMatrix`, `contractPart`, `contractCut`), which it shares with Karger's algorithm. The algorithm follows [Stoer and Wagner][stoerwagner97].

- **MA orderings:** [MAOrdering.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/WeightedCut/MAOrdering.lean) defines `attachWeight W A x = ∑ a ∈ A, W a x`, maximum adjacency orderings (`IsMAOrdering`, decidable) and maximum adjacency search `maSearch`, with ties broken by list order. `cutWeight_singleton_le_of_isMAOrdering` is the MA-ordering lemma ([Nagamochi and Ibaraki][nagamochiibaraki92]; the cut-of-the-phase lemma of [Stoer and Wagner][stoerwagner97]). If an MA ordering ends with `s, t`, then `{t}` weighs at most every vertex set containing exactly one of `s` and `t`. The proof is an induction over the prefixes of the ordering. `cutWeight_singleton_eq_minCutBetween_of_isMAOrdering` restates it as `cutWeight W {t} = minCutBetween W s t`, using the minimum `s`–`t` cut weight shared with the Gomory–Hu development. `maSearch_spec` shows that the search produces an MA ordering.
- **Phase recursion:** `Cslib.WeightedCut.cutWeight_contractCut` shows that merging `t` into `s` preserves the cuts that do not separate them. `minCut_eq_min_contractMatrix` says that `minCut W` is the minimum of the weight of a minimum `s`–`t` cut and the `minCut` after the merge. `minCut_eq_min_minCutBetween` states it as `minCut W = min (minCutBetween W s t) (minCut (contractMatrix W s t))`. `minCut_eq_min_of_isMAOrdering` applies this to one phase.
- **Algorithm:** `stoerWagner W` returns a `CutResult` with one side of a cut and its weight. It is structurally recursive (`run`, with fuel in `maSearch`), so `decide` evaluates it. `stoerWagner_spec`: for `2 ≤ n` and `IsMultigraph W`, the side is a non-trivial cut whose weight is the answered weight, which equals `minCut W`.
- **Cost:** `stoerWagnerM` adds `TimeM.tick` charges to the same program, and `ret_stoerWagnerM` shows that it returns the same result. `time_stoerWagnerM_le` bounds the time by `n ^ 3 + 4 n ^ 2`, and the tests pin the charged time `509` on the 8-vertex example. The unit-cost model is an array implementation; for a phase on `m` supervertices of a graph on `n` vertices:
  - initializing the keys costs `m`;
  - a search step over `r` candidates costs `2 r − 1`;
  - recording a cut of the phase costs `m + n`;
  - a merge costs `4 m + n + 1`: merging row and column `t` into `s`, moving row and column `m − 1` into the freed slot, relabelling the original vertices, and one comparison.

  The charges are annotations; they are not derived from the Lean evaluation, which uses closures. One-time setup is not charged: the weight matrix is taken as given, and the initial map `part = id` of the original vertices to supervertices is free. The bound `n ^ 3 + 4 n ^ 2` is loose. Summing the charges by hand, a phase on `m ≥ 3` supervertices costs `m ^ 2 + 6 m + 2 n + 1`, so the charged time is `n ^ 3 / 3 + O(n ^ 2)` (`509` against the bound `768` on the 8-vertex example); only the weaker bound is proved.

The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/StoerWagner.lean) test three graphs against brute force over all vertex sets. The first is a weighted 4-cycle. The second is the 8-vertex example graph of [Stoer and Wagner][stoerwagner97], with edge weights from a secondary reproduction (Wikipedia's article). It has minimum cut `4`, and search from its vertex `2` gives the ordering `2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 6, 5, 1` of the worked example's first phase, with cut of the phase `5`. The third is the Boost Graph Library example, which asserts minimum cut weight `7`. The examples also show that an ordering without the MA property can end in a pair whose last-vertex cut is not minimal. They cover a disconnected graph and `n = 0, 1, 2`. Not formalized:
- the `O(n m + n² log n)` priority-queue version;
- real weights (rational weights need scaling);
- enumeration of all minimum cuts;
- Nagamochi–Ibaraki sparse certificates;
- Karger's near-linear algorithm;
- Queyranne's symmetric submodular minimization ([#3369](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3369)).

The contraction API (`mergePair`, `embedCompact`, `contractMatrix`, `contractPart`, `contractCut`, `isContractionOf_contract`) lives in `Cslib.WeightedCut` and imports no probability, so this deterministic module does not depend on the randomized Karger development. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/WeightedCutContraction.lean) contract a weighted 4-cycle.

## Maximum flow: Dinitz's blocking-flow algorithm

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.MaxFlow.LevelGraph` ([CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/LevelGraph.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/LevelGraph.lean)) for the phase analysis of [Dinitz's algorithm][dinic70] ([Schrijver's monograph][schrijver03], Section 10.6) on the integral networks of `MaxFlow.Basic`. `Flow.levelNet f d` keeps the residual arcs `u → v` with `d v = d u + 1`. `Flow.augmentLevel` adds a flow of that network to `f`, with `value_augmentLevel` and `resCap_augmentLevel`. `Flow.IsBlocking` says every simple `s`-`t` path along positive-capacity arcs has a saturated arc. For `d = f.resDist`, the Edmonds–Karp residual distance, `resDist_le_resDist_augmentLevel` shows that no distance from `s` decreases. `resDist_lt_resDist_augmentLevel`, the phase lemma, shows that a blocking flow strictly increases the distance of a reachable `t`. `IsDinitzRun.length_le` then bounds any run of phases, from any initial flow, by `|V| - 1`. `Flow.bfsDist` computes `resDist` layer by layer (`bfsDist_eq_resDist`). `isBlocking_iff_bfsDist_slackNet` makes `IsBlocking` decidable by one breadth-first search in the network of unused capacities (`Flow.slackNet`).

`CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.MaxFlow.Dinitz` ([CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/Dinitz.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/Dinitz.lean)) is a pure Lean function over `Network (Fin n)` with candidate neighbour lists `adj` (`AdjValid`, as for Edmonds–Karp). It is not a `TimeM` program. Each phase of `dinitz N adj` computes `bfsDist` and stops once `t` is unreachable. Otherwise it builds a blocking flow of the level network with `greedyBlockingFlow` and augments by it. `greedyBlockingFlow` repeatedly pushes the bottleneck of a `bfsM` path of unsaturated arcs. A level network has no antiparallel arcs, so each push saturates a new arc. `greedyBlockingFlow_spec` gives a blocking flow after at most `|unsatArcs|` pushes, which `card_unsatArcs_zero_levelNet_le` bounds by `|N.posArcs|` for a level network. For `N.s ≠ N.t`, `dinitz_correct` returns a maximum flow, a minimum cut of equal capacity, the identification of that cut with the residual-reachability cut, at most `n - 1` phases, and at most `(n - 1) * |N.posArcs|` pushes. `dinitz_isDinitzRun` exhibits the phases as an `IsDinitzRun` from the zero flow. The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/Dinitz.lean) runs the CLRS network (value `23`, `2` phases) and a network attaining the phase bound (`3 = |V| - 1` phases). It also checks a later phase cancelling flow sent by an earlier one, a blocking flow that is not maximum, and the `s = t` and unreachable-sink cases. Limitation: the blocking flow comes from a fresh breadth-first search per push, not from the depth-first search with dead-end deletion, and no running time is charged. The `O(|V|^2 |E|)` bound is not formalized; ROADMAP records it as remaining.

## Gomory–Hu trees

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.GomoryHu.WeightedCut` ([source](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/GomoryHu/WeightedCut.lean)) for the structure theory and `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.GomoryHu.Construction` for the algorithm. The declarations live in `Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.GomoryHu` and `Cslib.WeightedCut`. For a weighted multigraph `W : V → V → ℕ` with `IsMultigraph W`, `minCutBetween W s t` is the minimum `s`–`t` cut weight `λ(s, t)` ([source](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/WeightedCut/MinCutBetween.lean)). `Network.Flow.IsMaxFlow.value_eq_minCutBetween` identifies it with the value of a maximum `s`–`t` flow in the network with capacities `W`, using the existing max-flow min-cut theorem. [MinCutBetween.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/WeightedCut/MinCutBetween.lean) also proves:

- the triangle inequality `min_le_minCutBetween`: `min (λ(u, v)) (λ(v, w)) ≤ λ(u, w)` for `u ≠ w`;
- its consequence `minCutBetween_eq_of_lt`;
- submodularity of `cutWeight W` (`cutWeight_union_add_cutWeight_inter_le`);
- posimodularity of `cutWeight W` (`cutWeight_sdiff_add_cutWeight_sdiff_le`).

The general theory, in [Basic.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/GomoryHu/Basic.lean) and [Existence.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/GomoryHu/Existence.lean), works for any set function `f : Finset V → β` with values in a linearly ordered cancellative commutative monoid. Three definitions carry it:

- `IsMinCutBetween f s t X`: `X` contains `s`, misses `t` and minimizes `f`.
- `SimpleGraph.edgeSide T u v`: the vertices reachable from `u` in `T` once the edge `uv` is deleted. This and its lemmas live in [EdgeSide.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/EdgeSide.lean), since they are plain tree facts.
- `IsGomoryHuTree f T`: `T` is a spanning tree and, for every tree edge `uv`, `edgeSide T u v` is a minimum `u`–`v` cut. This is the cut-equivalent tree of [Gomory and Hu][gomoryhu61] ([Schrijver 2003][schrijver03], Section 15.4).

`exists_isGomoryHuTree` proves existence for every symmetric (`f Xᶜ = f X`) submodular `f` on a nonempty finite `V`, and `exists_isGomoryHuTree_cutWeight` is the graph case. Gomory and Hu treat graphs only; the symmetric submodular form is the standard abstraction of their proof, which uses only these two properties. The proof follows Gomory and Hu's recursion without contracting vertices. It splits the terminals along a minimum cut, uses `IsMinCutBetween.inter` to uncross the minimum cuts on each side into that side, and joins the two partial trees built recursively with one edge. Three consequences follow:

- `IsGomoryHuTree.exists_isMinCutBetween_of_isPath`: some edge on the `s`–`t` tree path has a side that is a minimum `s`–`t` cut.
- `IsGomoryHuTree.isLeast_minCutBetween`: flow-equivalence. `λ(s, t)` is the least `λ(u, v)` over the edges `uv` of the tree path.
- `card_image_minCutBetween_le`: there are at most `Fintype.card V - 1` distinct values `λ(s, t)` with `s ≠ t`.

To check a candidate tree, first compute its edge sides with `edgeSide_eq_of_forall_adj`: if `S` contains `u`, misses `v` and no other tree edge crosses `S`, then `edgeSide T u v = S`. Then decide the minimum-cut conditions. The [tests](../../CslibFrontierTests/GomoryHu.lean) do this for the path `0 - 1 - 2 - 3` on a five-edge graph. They also refute a star, and a path for another graph, as Gomory–Hu trees.

### Gusfield's construction

[Gusfield][gusfield90]'s algorithm computes the tree with `n - 1` minimum cut computations in the original graph, without contraction. [Gusfield.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/GomoryHu/Gusfield.lean) states it on parent functions `p : V → V` with an arbitrary cut oracle `C : V → V → Finset V` (and cut-value oracle `val : V → V → β`). Every vertex starts below the root `r`. Each other vertex `s` is processed once, with `t = p s` and `X = C s t`: the other vertices whose parent is `t` and which lie in `X` are re-hung below `s`, and if `p t ∈ X`, `s` takes the place of `t`. `gusfieldParent C r l` computes the parent function over a list `l` of vertices; `gusfieldWithWeights C val r w₀ l` (`gusfieldStepWeight`, `gusfieldWeightLoop`) returns `(p, w)` updating the edge-weight function `w` alongside `p` (`w[s] := val s t` normally, or on a swap `w[s] := w[t]` and `w[t] := val s t`). `SimpleGraph.parentGraph p`, the graph of a parent function on any vertex type, is in [Acyclic.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Acyclic.lean), next to the parent arrays `parentTree` on `Fin (n + 1)`. `parentTree_eq_parentGraph` identifies the two when `p 0 = 0`. `isGomoryHuTree_gusfieldParent` and `isGomoryHuTree_gusfieldWithWeights` prove the result is a Gomory–Hu tree for every symmetric submodular `f`, whenever `C s t` is a minimum `s`–`t` cut for all `s ≠ t` and `l` is duplicate-free and contains every vertex other than `r`; `gusfieldWithWeights_weight_eq` shows `w u = f (edgeSide (parentGraph p) u (p u))` for every non-root `u` whenever `val s t = f (C s t)`. The oracle may return any minimum cut. The proof uncrosses each oracle cut with the sides of the existing tree edges at `t`, using `IsMinCutBetween.union` and `IsMinCutBetween.sdiff` from [Basic.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/GomoryHu/Basic.lean).

[Construction.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/GomoryHu/Construction.lean) makes the algorithm executable. `gomoryHuM W adj r` is a `TimeM ℕ` program over `Fin n` returning `Output n` (`parent : Vector (Fin n) n` and `weight : Vector ℕ n`). It uses the minimum cut (`ekCut`) and maximum flow value (`ekValue`) of the Edmonds–Karp solver `edmondsKarpM` as the oracle, once per non-root vertex (`ekValue_eq_cutWeight` identifies `ekValue` with `cutWeight W (ekCut ...)`). `vget_gomoryHuM_parent`, `vget_gomoryHuM` and `vget_gomoryHuM_weight` identify the returned arrays with `gusfieldWithWeights (ekCut W adj) (ekValue W adj) r 0 (List.finRange n)`. So `isGomoryHuTree_gomoryHuM` gives a Gomory–Hu tree of `cutWeight W` for every multigraph `W` and adjacency lists `adj` covering its edges (the `AdjValid` condition of Edmonds–Karp), and `vget_gomoryHuM_weight_eq_minCutBetween` (with `vget_gomoryHuM_weight_root` giving root weight `0` at `r`) proves `vget weight u = minCutBetween W u (vget parent u)` for every vertex `u : Fin n`. The time bounds:

- `gomoryHuM_time_le_of_forall`: at most `3 n + (n - 1) (B + n + 3)` charged steps, when every Edmonds–Karp call costs at most `B`;
- `gomoryHuM_time_le`: the same with `edmondsKarpM_time_le` substituted, which is `O(n m²)` per call for `m` edges;
- `gomoryHuM_time_le_dense`: `O(n⁶)` for dense adjacency lists.

The cost model is the Edmonds–Karp one, extended with `Finset` membership on the returned cut and evaluating `out.flow.value` (`netOut N.s`, an `O(n)` sum over `Fin n`) as free primitives inside the charged step. One tick is charged per slot allocated in the parent and weight arrays (`2 n`), per vertex for the loop test (`n`), per parent read (`1`), per vertex scanned when re-hanging (`n`), per swap test and parent writes (`1`), and per weight-array update (`1`). Vector reads and writes, `Finset` membership tests on the returned cut, evaluating `out.flow.value`, and `Fin n` comparisons are free primitives inside those steps. (Evaluating `out.flow.value` once per step would add `O(n²)` in total, subsumed by the re-hanging scan, whereas `O(n)` `Finset` membership tests inside each `n`-vertex scan would add `O(n³)` in total.) The [tests](../../CslibFrontierTests/GomoryHuConstruction.lean) run `gomoryHuM` by `decide` on the five-edge graph of the structure tests. From root `0` they obtain `parent = [0, 0, 1, 2]` (the path `0 - 1 - 2 - 3`) and `weight = [0, 5, 4, 4]`; from root `3` they obtain `parent = [1, 2, 3, 3]` and `weight = [5, 4, 4, 0]`, taking the swap branch at `s = 1` (`weight` `[5, 4, 0, 0]`). The tests also instantiate the time bounds (`313` steps against `2013` and dense `3705`), refute the output of `gusfieldParent` with a non-minimum cut oracle and the weights of `gusfieldWithWeights` with an inconsistent value oracle, and check the `1`-vertex and edgeless cases.

### Real capacities and other oracles

`cutWeight W X` is defined for weights in any additive commutative monoid, so the same definition and laws serve `ℕ` and a linearly ordered field `K` such as `ℝ` or `ℚ`. Submodularity (`cutWeight_union_add_cutWeight_inter_le_of_nonneg`) needs non-negative `W`; the complement law (`cutWeight_compl_of_symm`) and posimodularity (`cutWeight_sdiff_add_cutWeight_sdiff_le_of_symm`) also need symmetric `W`. The `ℕ` lemmas above are their special cases. [RealCut.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/WeightedCut/RealCut.lean) adds the noncomputable minimum `s`–`t` cut weight `minRealCutBetween` over `K` (`0` by convention when `s = t`), with attainment (`exists_cutWeight_eq_minRealCutBetween`, for `s ≠ t`), symmetry for symmetric `W` (`minRealCutBetween_comm`) and the triangle inequality (`min_le_minRealCutBetween`). [Real.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/GomoryHu/Real.lean) feeds this cut function to the abstract theory. For symmetric non-negative `W`, `exists_isGomoryHuTree_cutWeight_of_symm` gives a Gomory–Hu tree when `V` is nonempty ([Schrijver 2003][schrijver03], Theorem 15.14, p. 250), `IsGomoryHuTree.isLeast_minRealCutBetween` is flow-equivalence for distinct `s` and `t` (Theorem 15.13, p. 249), and `card_image_minRealCutBetween_le` bounds the number of distinct values by `|V| - 1`.

[RealFlow.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/RealFlow.lean) relates `minRealCutBetween` to flows. A flow is a function `f : V × V → K`. `IsFeasibleRealFlow W s t f` requires `0 ≤ f (u, v) ≤ W u v` and conservation at every vertex other than `s` and `t`, and `realNetOut f s` is the value. `IsFeasibleRealFlow.realNetOut_le_cutWeight` is weak duality. With irrational capacities augmenting paths need not terminate, so `exists_isMaxRealFlow` obtains a maximum flow from linear-programming duality over `K` instead. `isMaxRealFlow_minRealCutBetween` is the max-flow min-cut theorem `IsMaxRealFlow W s t (minRealCutBetween W s t)` for non-negative `W` and `s ≠ t` ([Schrijver 2003][schrijver03], Theorem 10.3, p. 150). It extends the source from `ℝ` to any linearly ordered field, encodes the arcs as all ordered pairs (absent arcs have capacity `0`, loops cancel), and uses linear-programming attainment where the source can use compactness of the real flow polytope. The residual-reachability cut certifies optimality: if `t` were reachable, an augmentation built along the reachability relation would increase the value.

[Oracles.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/GomoryHu/Oracles.lean) runs Gusfield's construction with any correct cut oracle (`gusfieldOutput`, `isGomoryHuTree_gusfieldOutput`, `vget_gusfieldOutput_weight`). It instantiates the construction with the minimum cuts of push–relabel (`pushRelabelGomoryHu`) and of Dinitz's algorithm (`dinitzGomoryHu`). Like those algorithms, both instances take capacities in `ℕ`, not `K`. Both are pure functions and are not charged. The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/GomoryHuRealAndOracles.lean) runs both instances by `decide` on a five-vertex graph (`parent = [0, 0, 0, 1, 3]`, `weight = [0, 8, 13, 10, 9]`). It also applies the real-capacity theorems to a two-vertex graph with capacity `√2`.

Limitations:

- `minRealCutBetween` and the maximum flow of `exists_isMaxRealFlow` are noncomputable. No algorithm computes a maximum flow or minimum cut over `K`, so Gusfield's construction has no executable real-capacity oracle (Item 2 of [#3422](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3422)).
- The push–relabel and Dinitz instances charge no running time, so they give no time bound better than `gomoryHuM_time_le` (Item 3 of [#3422](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3422)).
- Minimum cuts are not enumerated.

[#3422](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3422) tracks the remaining optional items.

## Minimum vertex separators

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.MaxFlow.VertexSeparator` ([source](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/VertexSeparator.lean)). `minSeparatorM G Z A B` runs the dense Edmonds–Karp solver `edmondsKarpDenseM` on the vertex-split network `vertexSplitNetwork G Z A B` and returns a minimum set `X` of vertices outside `Z` such that `Z ∪ X` separates `A` from `B` ([`SimpleGraph.IsSeparator`](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Separator.lean)). It is a `TimeM ℕ` program with Edmonds–Karp's unit-cost model, bounded by `time_minSeparatorM_le`. Its client is odd cycle transversal by iterative compression; see [parameterized complexity](../machines-and-classes/parameterized-complexity.md#iterative-compression-odd-cycle-transversal). [`SimpleGraph.isVertexSeparator_iff_isSeparator`](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Separator.lean) bridges single-terminal Menger separators (`SimpleGraph.IsVertexSeparator`) to `SimpleGraph.IsSeparator`.

## Maximum flow: generic push–relabel

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.MaxFlow.Preflow` ([CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/Preflow.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/Preflow.lean)) for the objects of the push–relabel method of [Goldberg and Tarjan][goldbergtarjan88], following [CLRS, Section 26.4][clrs09], on the integral networks of `MaxFlow.Basic`. Antiparallel arcs are allowed. `Network.Preflow` bounds arc flows by the capacities and requires inflow at least outflow at every vertex other than `s`. `Preflow.IsValidLabeling f h` says `h s = |V|`, `h t = 0` and `h u ≤ h v + 1` on every residual arc. `Preflow.push` and `Preflow.relabel` have the preconditions `CanPush` and `CanRelabel`. `IsValidLabeling.push` and `.relabel` keep labelings valid (Lemma 26.16). `exists_canPush_or_canRelabel` shows that an active vertex admits one of the two operations (Lemma 26.14). `not_resReachable` rules out a residual `s`–`t` path (Lemma 26.17). `resReachable_source_of_excess_pos` gives every vertex with positive excess a residual path back to `s` (Lemma 26.19), and `le_of_excess_pos` bounds its height by `2|V| - 1` (Lemma 26.20). With no active vertex, `isMaxFlow_toFlow` shows the preflow is a maximum flow (Theorem 26.18). `isMinCut_gapCut` shows that the vertices above the least empty height level form a minimum cut of the same value. This gap cut is read off from the heights alone.

`CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.MaxFlow.PushRelabel` ([CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/PushRelabel.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/PushRelabel.lean)) states the operation bounds for every run of the generic algorithm. A run is a list of operations, each admissible in the state it is applied to (`PushRelabel.State.IsRun`), so the bounds hold for every selection rule. For `s ≠ t`, a run from `PushRelabel.init N` has:

- at most `(|V| - 2)(2|V| - 1) < 2|V|²` relabels (`numRelabels_le`, `numRelabels_lt`; Corollary 26.21);
- at most `|V|` saturating pushes per arc pair of `N.symArcs`, so at most `2|V||E|` (`numSatPushes_le`, `numSatPushes_le_posArcs`; Lemma 26.22, where `E = N.posArcs`);
- fewer than `4|V|²(|V| + |E|)` nonsaturating pushes (`numNonsatPushes_le`, `numNonsatPushes_lt_posArcs`; Lemma 26.23), by the potential `Σ_{v active} h v`;
- at most `maxOps N` operations in total (`length_le`; the operation count of Theorem 26.24), where `maxOps N ≤ 4|V|²(1 + |E|)` (`maxOps_le`), that is, `O(|V|² (|E| + 1))`.

`isMaxFlow_isMinCut_of_isRun` makes correctness independent of the selection rule: a run from `PushRelabel.init N` that reaches a state admitting no operation ends with a maximum flow and a minimum gap cut of equal value (Lemma 26.14 and Theorem 26.18). By `length_le`, every run reaches such a state within `maxOps N` operations.

`pushRelabelWith N vs norm` scans the vertex list `vs` for the first active vertex and pushes along its first admissible arc, or relabels it. `pushRelabelWith_correct` assumes that `vs` lists every vertex, that `norm` is the identity (`∀ σ, norm σ = σ`) and that `s ≠ t`. Under these hypotheses it shows that the function performs a run of at most `maxOps N` operations and returns a maximum flow and a minimum cut of equal value. `pushRelabel` on `Fin n` takes `vs = List.finRange n` and `norm = tabulate`, which stores the state as a matrix and a vector after every operation, so the kernel can evaluate it. `pushRelabel_correct` then needs only `s ≠ t`. The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/PushRelabel.lean) evaluates it by `decide +kernel` on the CLRS six-vertex network (value `23`, `29` operations). It evaluates it by `decide` on edge cases: antiparallel arcs, an unreachable sink, a single arc `s → t`, and `s = t`.

Limitations: the bounds count operations and do not charge the selection of an operation, so there is no `TimeM` running-time bound. The FIFO, relabel-to-front and highest-label rules, the gap and global-relabelling heuristics and dynamic trees are not formalized ([#3373](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3373)).

## Menger's theorem

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.MaxFlow.Menger` ([source](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/Menger.lean)). The Menger theorems live in `Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MaxFlow`. This is a theorem-level development: there is no algorithm and no running time. Every form of [Menger's theorem][menger27] is derived from the integral max-flow min-cut theorem `Network.exists_maxFlow_eq_minCut` together with flow decomposition (`exists_flowPaths`). Pure graph-theoretic connectivity notions, cut certificates and weak-duality bounds live in modular foundations files:

- [`Digraph/Connectivity.lean`](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/Digraph/Connectivity.lean): `Digraph.IsDipath` (duplicate-free vertex lists), `Digraph.ArcDisjoint`, `Digraph.InternallyDisjoint`, `Digraph.IsArcCut`, `Digraph.IsVertexCut`, `Digraph.IsSeparator` and `Digraph.outArcs` (`δ^out(S)`).
- [`Multidigraph/Connectivity.lean`](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/Multidigraph/Connectivity.lean): `Cslib.Multidigraph.IsDipath` (`List E` arc chains whose visited vertex list `s :: q.map tgt` is duplicate-free), `Cslib.Multidigraph.IsArcCut` and `Cslib.Multidigraph.outArcs`.
- [`SimpleGraph/Separator.lean`](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Separator.lean): `SimpleGraph.IsVertexSeparator` and its bridge `isVertexSeparator_iff_isSeparator` to `SimpleGraph.IsSeparator`.
- [`SimpleGraph/Connectivity/EdgeConnectivity.lean`](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Connectivity/EdgeConnectivity.lean) and [`VertexConnectivity.lean`](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Connectivity/VertexConnectivity.lean): `SimpleGraph.InternallyDisjoint`, `SimpleGraph.IsVertexConnected` (`|V| > k` and `G - X` connected for `|X| < k`; [Diestel 2025][diestel25], §1.4), weak duality, cut certificates and path-family conversions (`injective_of_edgeDisjoint`, `exists_injective_internallyDisjoint_iff_exists_finset`, `edgeDisjoint_of_injective_internallyDisjoint`).

Graphs are finite (`[Finite V]`, and `[Finite E]` for directed multigraphs). All forms are summarized below.

| Form | Source | `iff` version | Matching-cut / witness version |
|---|---|---|---|
| integral capacity | [Schrijver 2017][schrijver17], §4.1 / Thm 4.2 remark | `exists_arcCap_iff_forall_isCut` | `exists_arcCap_eq_cutCapacity` |
| multidigraph, arc-disjoint | [Schrijver 2017][schrijver17], §4.1, Cor. 4.1b | `exists_multidigraph_arcDisjoint_iff_forall_isArcCut` | `exists_multidigraph_arcDisjoint_eq_outArcs` |
| directed, arc-disjoint | [Schrijver 2017][schrijver17], Corollary 4.1b | `exists_arcDisjoint_iff_forall_isArcCut` | `exists_arcDisjoint_eq_outArcs` |
| directed, internally disjoint | [Schrijver 2017][schrijver17], Corollary 4.1a | `exists_internallyDisjoint_iff_forall_isVertexCut` | `exists_internallyDisjoint_eq_isVertexCut` |
| directed, `A`–`B` sets | [Schrijver 2017][schrijver17], Theorem 4.1 | `exists_vertexDisjoint_iff_forall_isSeparator` | `exists_vertexDisjoint_eq_isSeparator` |
| undirected, edge-disjoint | [Diestel 2025][diestel25], Corollary 3.3.5 (ii) | `isEdgeReachable_iff_exists_edgeDisjoint` | `exists_edgeDisjoint_eq_not_reachable` |
| undirected, internally disjoint | [Diestel 2025][diestel25], Corollary 3.3.5 (i) | `exists_internallyDisjoint_iff_forall_isVertexSeparator` | `exists_internallyDisjoint_eq_isVertexSeparator` |
| undirected, `A`–`B` sets | [Diestel 2025][diestel25], Theorem 3.3.1 (endpoint convention of [Schrijver 2017][schrijver17], §4.1) | `exists_disjoint_walk_iff_forall_isSeparator` | `exists_disjoint_walk_eq_isSeparator` |
| global `k`-edge-connected | [Diestel 2025][diestel25], Theorem 3.3.6 (ii) | `isEdgeConnected_iff_forall_exists_edgeDisjoint` | `isEdgeConnected_iff_forall_ne_exists_injective_edgeDisjoint` |
| global `k`-vertex-connected | [Diestel 2025][diestel25], Theorem 3.3.6 (i) | `isVertexConnected_iff_forall_exists_injective_internallyDisjoint` | `isVertexConnected_iff_of_nontrivial`, `isVertexConnected_iff_forall_exists_finset` |

- **Capacity and multidigraph arc forms.** `exists_arcCap_iff_forall_isCut` packs `k` simple paths in `N.posCapDigraph` using each arc `(u, v)` at most `N.cap u v` times iff every cut has capacity at least `k`. Applying it to the multiplicity network of `src tgt : E → V` yields `exists_multidigraph_arcDisjoint_iff_forall_isArcCut`, handling parallel arcs and self-loops.
- **Two-terminal simple digraph and undirected forms.** `exists_arcDisjoint_eq_outArcs` achieves `|(G.outArcs S)|` paths (`δ^out(S)`). The internally disjoint forms split vertices (`splitNetwork`); `isEdgeReachable_iff_exists_edgeDisjoint` cancels antiparallel flow (`Network.Flow.cancel`). The `iff` versions handle degenerate cases (`s = t`, or adjacent `s`, `t` in vertex forms).
- **Set-to-set `A`–`B` forms.** `exists_vertexDisjoint_eq_isSeparator` attaches a supersource and supersink to `A` and `B` (`extDigraph`) and applies the single-terminal digraph theorem. `exists_disjoint_walk_iff_forall_isSeparator` lowers this to `SimpleGraph` walks. Both use the endpoint convention of [Schrijver 2017][schrijver17], §4.1 p. 54: an `A`–`B` path starts in `A` and ends in `B`, and may pass through further vertices of `A` or `B`. [Diestel 2025][diestel25], §1.3 p. 7, instead requires `A`–`B` paths to meet `A` and `B` only at their ends, so the undirected form adapts his Theorem 3.3.1 to the endpoint convention. Single-vertex paths at vertices of `A ∩ B` are allowed, so every separator meets `A ∩ B`. The theorems do not guarantee that such vertices appear as single-vertex paths: in the path `0 – 1 – 2` with `A = {0, 1}` and `B = {1, 2}`, the one-path family `[0, 1, 2]` and the separator `{1}` satisfy them. Trimming paths to Diestel's convention is not formalized.
- **Global `k`-connectivity.** The edge form reuses Mathlib's `SimpleGraph.IsEdgeConnected`, which extends [Diestel 2025][diestel25], §1.4 p. 12: Diestel requires more than one vertex for `k`-edge-connectedness, but `IsEdgeConnected k` holds for every `k` on empty and one-vertex types, and `isEdgeConnected_iff_forall_exists_edgeDisjoint` then provides `k` copies of the trivial walk at `u = v`. `isVertexConnected_iff_forall_exists_injective_internallyDisjoint` characterizes `G.IsVertexConnected k` by `|V| > k` (needed for complete graphs such as `K₁` and `Kₖ`) together with `k` distinct pairwise internally disjoint paths between every `u ≠ v`; `Function.Injective` (or a `k`-element `Finset` in `isVertexConnected_iff_forall_exists_finset`) prevents repeating `[u, v]` on adjacent pairs, proved via [Diestel 2025][diestel25]'s edge-deletion argument (`isVertexSeparator_insert_of_not_reachable`). `isEdgeConnected_of_isVertexConnected` derives `κ(G) ≤ λ(G)` ([Diestel 2025][diestel25], Proposition 1.4.2).

Weak duality and decidable closed-set certificates in the foundations modules turn cut checks into finite computations (`Digraph.card_le_card_of_arcDisjoint_of_isArcCut`, `Digraph.card_le_card_of_internallyDisjoint_of_isVertexCut`, `Digraph.card_le_card_of_disjoint_of_isSeparator`, `Cslib.Multidigraph.card_le_card_of_disjoint_of_isArcCut`, `SimpleGraph.le_encard_of_edgeDisjoint_of_not_reachable`, `SimpleGraph.card_le_card_of_internallyDisjoint_of_isVertexSeparator`, `SimpleGraph.card_le_card_of_disjoint_of_isSeparator`, `card_le_cutCapacity_of_cap`, plus `Digraph.isArcCut_outArcs`, `Digraph.isVertexCut_of_forall_adj`, `Digraph.isSeparator_of_forall_adj`, `Cslib.Multidigraph.isArcCut_outArcs`, `SimpleGraph.not_reachable_deleteEdges_of_forall_adj`, `SimpleGraph.isVertexSeparator_of_forall_adj`, `SimpleGraph.isSeparator_of_forall_adj`).

The reusable flow layer is [Decomposition.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/Decomposition.lean). `Network.Flow.exists_flowPaths` decomposes an integral flow of value `k` into `k` simple paths along positive-flow arcs, with each arc `(u, v)` on at most `f u v` of them. `Network.Flow.cancel` removes antiparallel flow and keeps the value.

The [tests](../../CslibFrontierTests/Menger.lean) exercise every form without `native_decide`: the 3-cube (antipodal `0`–`7` connectivity `3`, matching separator pin `m = 3`, `A`–`B` Menger `m = 3`, global `3`-vertex- and `3`-edge-connectivity, non-`4`-vertex-connectivity), a 4-vertex digraph (`2` paths, overlapping `A = {0, 1}`/`B = {1, 3}` sets), global `k`-connectivity edge cases (`K₃`, `K₂`, `K₁`), a capacity-`3` network (`capNet`), and a 5-arc multidigraph with parallel arcs (`mdSrc`/`mdTgt`, max `3` arc-disjoint dipaths).

Limitations:

- Graphs are finite (`[Finite V]`, and `[Finite E]` for directed multigraphs); infinite graphs and the Erdős–Menger theorem (Aharoni–Berger) are not formalized.
- No algorithm or running time is given for computing local or global connectivity.

[#3409](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3409) tracks the remaining work.

## Bipartite matching and Hopcroft–Karp

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.MaxFlow.HopcroftKarp` ([CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/HopcroftKarp.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/HopcroftKarp.lean)). It is a native `TimeM ℕ` program, not a RAM-model program. It takes `G : Fin m × Fin r → Bool` and candidate neighbour lists `adj` with `BipAdjValid G adj` (every edge of `G` occurs in `adj`; extra candidates only cost time). `hopcroftKarpM_correct` gives a maximum matching (`IsMaxMatching`), a minimum König cover (`IsMinVertexCover`), equal sizes, and at most `2 * Nat.sqrt ν` augmenting phases, where `ν` is the maximum matching size. `hopcroftKarpM_time_le` bounds the charged steps by `(2 * Nat.sqrt ν + 1) * (7 * m + 3 * r + 1 + 4 * bipAdjSteps adj) + m + 3 * r`. Ticks are charged per entry of each allocated table (mate, matched-left, layer and visited tables), per mate entry scanned to mark matched left vertices, per left vertex examined when seeding BFS layer `0`, per expanded BFS layer, per frontier left vertex and candidate edge inspected by the BFS, per DFS root and candidate edge scanned by the DFS, per rematched right vertex, and per step of the final matching and cover extraction. Vector reads and writes, list cons, `Finset` insertion, `ℕ`/`Option` comparisons and edge queries `G (u, v)` are free primitives inside those charged steps, as in the Edmonds–Karp solver (the module docstring's "Cost model and limitations" gives the per-phase breakdown). Converting the input into `adj` is not charged. `hopcroftKarpDenseM` uses every pair as a candidate, giving `O(m r √(min m r))`. The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/HopcroftKarp.lean) builds `adj` for small graphs, discharges `BipAdjValid` by `decide`, evaluates complete runs, and applies `hopcroftKarpM_correct` and `hopcroftKarpDenseM_time_le`.

Outputs use the same `IsMatching`/`VertexCover` vocabulary as the Edmonds–Karp reduction `bipartiteMatchingM`. `card_hopcroftKarpM_matching` shows the two solvers return matchings of the same size, so a client can switch solvers without changing its statements. `decidePerfectMatchingHKM_eq_true_iff` gives the same characterisation of `HasPerfectMatching` as `decidePerfectMatchingM`.

The combinatorial layer is [CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/AugmentingPath.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/AugmentingPath.lean). It contains Berge's theorem (`isMaxMatching_iff_forall_not_isAugmentingPath`), the phase lemma (`IsMaximalLayeredAugmentation.noShortAug_augment`) and the phase bound (`IsPhaseRun.le_two_mul_sqrt`). These proofs use a layered counting lemma, `card_mul_le_of_noShortAug`, instead of decomposing `M ⊕ M*` into disjoint augmenting paths; that decomposition is not formalized.

## Chain covers: Dilworth's and Mirsky's theorems

Both theorems are stated for finite posets. Chains and antichains are Mathlib's `IsChain (· ≤ ·)` and `IsAntichain (· ≤ ·)`, and a covering by `k` chains (or antichains) is a colouring `α → Fin k` whose fibres are chains (or antichains). [CslibFrontier/Foundations/Order/Mirsky.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Order/Mirsky.lean) proves weak duality in both directions: a chain meets each antichain fibre at most once, and an antichain meets each chain fibre at most once. It also proves Mirsky's theorem, following [Schrijver's lecture notes][schrijver17], Theorem 7.5. Colouring by `Order.height` gives a chain `C` and a covering by `|C|` antichains (`exists_isChain_and_antichain_coloring`). In min–max form, `α` is covered by `k` antichains if and only if every chain has at most `k` elements (`exists_antichain_coloring_iff`). [Mirsky's paper][mirsky1971] was not consulted.

[CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/Dilworth.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/Dilworth.lean) proves Dilworth's theorem ([Schrijver][schrijver17], Theorem 7.6; [Dilworth][dilworth1950]) by the bipartite-matching reduction usually credited to [Fulkerson][fulkerson1956], instead of Schrijver's induction. Its graph `ltGraph` joins the left copy of `x` to the right copy of `y` when `x < y`. Folding `mergeLabel` over a matching appends chains along matching edges. `exists_chain_coloring_of_isMatching` turns a matching `M` into `|α| - |M|` chains. `isAntichain_uncovered` and `card_sub_size_le_card_uncovered` show that the elements a vertex cover `C` misses on both sides form an antichain of at least `|α| - |C|` elements. König's theorem (`exists_maxMatching_eq_minVertexCover`) then gives `exists_isAntichain_and_chain_coloring` (an antichain `A` and a covering by `|A|` chains) and the min–max form `exists_chain_coloring_iff`. The Dilworth and Fulkerson papers were not consulted, so the attribution of the reduction to Fulkerson is secondary.

`minChainCoverM e` in [CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/ChainCover.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/ChainCover.lean) is the executable version. It is a native `TimeM ℕ` program, not a RAM-model program, on a poset enumerated by `e : Fin n ≃ α` with decidable `<`. It runs `hopcroftKarpDenseM` on `enumLtGraph e`, merges label-table chains along the matching, and reads the antichain off the König cover. `minChainCoverM_correct` shows four things: the label fibres are chains, the returned indices form an antichain, the number of labels (`numChains`) equals the antichain's size, and that size plus the matching's size is `n`. The two optimality statements follow from this. `card_le_card_antichain_of_isAntichain` bounds every antichain by the output's size. `card_antichain_le_of_isChain_fibre` bounds the output's size by the number of chains in any chain cover. `minChainCoverM_time_le` bounds the charged steps by `(2 * Nat.sqrt n + 1) * (10 * n + 1 + 4 * n²) + 2 * n² + 6 * n`, that is `O(n^{5/2})`. Ticks are charged for Hopcroft–Karp, one per label entry allocated or rewritten by a merge, one per scanned pair of indices, and one per index when collecting the antichain. `Finset` membership, `Vector` reads, `Fin n` comparisons and order queries `e i < e j` are free primitives inside those steps; computing `e` is not charged.

[CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/ErdosSzekeres.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/ErdosSzekeres.lean) derives the Erdős–Szekeres monotone subsequence theorem from Mirsky's theorem ([Erdős–Szekeres][erdosszekeres1935], pp. 467–468). `exists_monotoneOn_or_strictAntiOn_of_mul_lt` gives, for any sequence of more than `r * s` terms in a linear order, a monotone subsequence of `r + 1` terms or a strictly decreasing subsequence of `s + 1` terms. `exists_strictMonoOn_or_strictAntiOn_of_mul_lt` is the injective case. The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/Dilworth.lean) covers four instances: width `6` and height `4` for divisibility on `{1, …, 12}`, width `3` and height `4` for the Boolean lattice `Finset (Fin 3)`, complete solver runs on divisibility on `{1, …, 6}` and on the rank-`3` Boolean lattice as bitmasks, and the tight sequence `2, 1, 4, 3`.

## Assignment problem and the Hungarian method

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.MaxFlow.Hungarian` ([CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/Hungarian.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/Hungarian.lean)). It is a native `TimeM ℕ` program, not a RAM-model program. `hungarianM c` takes integer costs `c : Fin n × Fin n → ℤ` on the complete bipartite graph and minimizes. `hungarianM_correct` shows three things: the output matching is a minimum-cost perfect matching (`IsMinCostPerfectMatching`), the output potentials `y`, `z` are feasible (`IsFeasiblePotential`), and the cost equals `∑ y + ∑ z`. `hungarianM_time_le` bounds the charged steps by `6 * n ^ 3 + 6 * n ^ 2 + 3 * n`. Ticks are charged per column scanned for a row minimum, per entry of each allocated or rebuilt table (potentials, mate, tree, slack and path tables), per column scanned by the argmin, per tree extension, and per rematched column. Vector reads and writes, list cons, `ℤ` arithmetic and comparisons, and cost queries `c (u, v)` are free primitives inside those charged steps, as in the Edmonds–Karp and Hopcroft–Karp solvers. The module docstring's "Cost model and limitations" gives the per-round breakdown. Rectangular instances and forbidden edges have to be padded or given large costs. For maximum weight, negate the costs.

Egerváry's theorem is available without running the algorithm. `exists_isMinCostPerfectMatching_potential_eq` gives the integral form. `isMinCostPerfectMatching_iff_exists_potential` gives the min–max form, and `exists_perm_assignment` the permutation form. The weak-duality and complementary-slackness lemmas in [CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/WeightedMatching.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/MaxFlow/WeightedMatching.lean) are stated for arbitrary graphs and arbitrary matchings, for reuse by minimum-cost flow. The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/Hungarian.lean) evaluates complete runs, including an instance where greedy fails and one that needs dual adjustments. It checks the optimum against all permutations of a `3 × 3` instance, verifies the dual certificates independently, and applies `hungarianM_correct` and `hungarianM_time_le`.

## General matching: Berge's lemma and the Tutte–Berge formula

These results are about matchings in arbitrary finite graphs, not necessarily bipartite. They are stated on Mathlib's `SimpleGraph` and `SimpleGraph.Subgraph.IsMatching`, not on the bipartite `L × R → Bool` model above. This mathematical layer is complemented by the native unweighted endpoint below.

[CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Berge.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Berge.lean) defines:
- `Subgraph.IsAugmentingPath M p`: a path between distinct uncovered vertices that alternates in Mathlib's `IsAlternating` sense;
- `Subgraph.augment M p`: `M ∆ E(p)`;
- `Subgraph.IsMaximumMatching`: a matching with at least as many edges, counted by `Set.encard`, as every matching.

`IsAugmentingPath.isMatching_augment` and `ncard_verts_augment` show that augmenting gives a matching covering two more vertices. `IsMatching.isMaximumMatching_iff_forall_not_isAugmentingPath` is Berge's lemma for finite graphs ([Berge 1957][berge57], Theorem 1; [Schrijver's lecture notes][schrijver17], Theorem 3.2). It is proved from `IsMatching.exists_isAugmentingPath_of_ncard_lt`, using the odd-degree vertices of a component of `M ∆ M'`. `exists_isMaximumMatching` gives a maximum matching of a finite graph, and `IsPerfectMatching.isMaximumMatching` shows that perfect matchings are maximum. The bipartite Berge theorem in `MaxFlow/AugmentingPath` is a separate statement in its own model; neither is derived from the other.

[CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/TutteBerge.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/TutteBerge.lean) proves the Tutte–Berge formula ([Berge 1958][berge58]; [Schrijver's lecture notes][schrijver17], Theorem 5.1) with vertex counts, writing `o(G - U)` as `((⊤ : G.Subgraph).deleteVerts U).coe.oddComponents.ncard`:
- `IsMatching.ncard_verts_add_ncard_oddComponents_le` (weak duality): `|V(M)| + o(G - U) ≤ |V| + |U|`.
- `exists_isMatching_ncard_verts_add_ncard_oddComponents_eq` (strong duality) is derived from Mathlib's `SimpleGraph.tutte` by adjoining `d` universal vertices, where `d` is the maximum deficiency.
- `IsMatching.isMaximumMatching_iff_exists_ncard_verts_add_ncard_oddComponents_eq`: a single `U` certifies that a matching is maximum.
- `IsMaximumMatching.isLeast_two_mul_ncard_edgeSet`: `2ν(G)` is the least value of `|V| + |U| - o(G - U)`.

The [checked instances](../../CslibFrontierTests/GeneralMatching.lean) cover three graphs:
- On the path `0 - 1 - 2 - 3`, an explicit walk is an augmenting path for `{12}`.
- On the `5`-cycle, `U = ∅` certifies the matching `{01, 23}`.
- On the star `K_{1,3}`, `U = {0}` certifies the matching `{01}`.

### Native unweighted perfect and maximum matching

From a bootstrapped repository root, import
`CslibFrontier.Computability.Complexity.Problems.Graph.Matching.Endpoint` and open
`Cslib.Complexity` and `Cslib.Complexity.Graph`. The complete
[checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/NativeMatching.lean) and
[guide chapter](../../guide/CslibFrontierGuide/Matching.lean) show both endpoints and their certificates.
Run `lake build CslibFrontierTests.NativeMatching --wfail --iofail` to check the client.

A `Graph` is a pair of lists: declared binary natural-number names and original endpoint pairs.
`G.maximumMatching` returns a list of selected original pairs; `G.perfectMatching` returns
`some edges` exactly when a perfect matching exists, and `G.decidePerfectMatching` is its Boolean
decision. On the path with edge order `[(1, 2), (0, 1), (2, 3)]`, both searches select
`[(0, 1), (2, 3)]`; a greedy first-edge choice would miss the perfect matching.
`maximumMatching_cardinality G M hM` proves that every actual Mathlib matching `M` has at most
as many edges as the output. `maximumMatchingSubgraph_isMaximumMatching` gives the existing
`Subgraph.IsMaximumMatching` predicate, and `maximumMatchingSubgraph_adj_iff` identifies its
edges with the returned pairs in either orientation. `perfectMatching_spec` certifies successful
search; `decidePerfectMatching_eq_true` gives the full existence equivalence.

All raw graphs follow `G.induced`: repeated declarations are harmless, loops and edges with
undeclared endpoints are discarded, and reversed/parallel duplicates become one edge. The output
keeps the first retained orientation in `G.edgeList`. The empty graph has the empty perfect
matching. Isolates prevent perfect matching and remain unmatched in maximum search. These
normalization rules also apply to disconnected and nonbipartite inputs; callers supply no
canonicality or degree hypothesis. For position-based clients, `maximumMatchingPositions` returns
canonical normalized edge positions and `edgesAtPositions` converts them executably.

The shared `Matching.Positions` API includes `exists_getD_edgeList_of_adj`: actual adjacency
gives a valid retained position in either orientation. Coverage, edge-cardinality and the
legacy perfect-matching counting proof reuse this fact instead of rebuilding the witness.

`computableInPolyTime_maximumMatching`, `computableInPolyTime_perfectMatching` and
`computableInPolyTime_decidePerfectMatching` use ordinary native multitape bit complexity on the
complete framed `encGraph` input, with framed original pairs in `encMatching` and optional output
in `encOption encMatching`. Every retained name bit, explicit one-hot row, scope width,
solver state and output bit is charged by polynomial composition. Enumeration uses actual lists,
never an interval below a raw name; the regressions include the sparse name `2 ^ 250`.
`perfectMatchingProblem_mem_P` is the native P corollary. As with other typed native certificates,
this is a claim on encoded graph values, not a parser for malformed bit strings.

The algorithm specializes the certified Kazda–Kolmogorov–Rolinek even-delta-matroid optimizer
([source and references](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PolyTime/CSP/Edge/Solver/Endpoint.lean)).
Each vertex chooses an incident original edge or a private unmatched port pinned false at its
other end. Mutual original choices form a matching, and
`defects_add_two_mul_positions` proves `defects + 2 * selected edges = distinct vertices`.
Every actual matching supplies a feasible comparison labeling, so minimum defect proves maximum
cardinality without an extra oracle. The native certificates impose no artificial table bound
or finite-language premise. This endpoint does not establish weighted/min-cost matching or a
tight classical cubic or `O(|V|²|E|)` operation count.

Still open ([#3337](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3337)):
- Edmonds' blossom-contraction lemma ([Schrijver's lecture notes][schrijver17], Theorem 5.2);
- an executable blossom algorithm with its `O(|V|²|E|)` bound;
- weighted general matching;
- the Gallai–Edmonds decomposition.

## Matroid intersection

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.Matroid.Intersection` ([CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Matroid/Intersection.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Matroid/Intersection.lean)). It proves Edmonds' matroid intersection theorem for Mathlib matroids `M₁ M₂ : Matroid α` relative to a finite candidate set `E : Finset α`, following the augmenting-path proof of [Schrijver's lecture notes][schrijver17], Section 10.5. The matroids' ground sets need not equal `E`: elements outside `Mᵢ.E` behave as loops. `iSup_card_eq_iInf_eRk_add_eRk` states that the maximum of `I.card` over common independent `I ⊆ E` equals the minimum of `M₁.eRk U + M₂.eRk (E \ U)` over `U ⊆ E`. `iSup_encard_eq_iInf_eRk_add_eRk` gives the same equality for a finite `X : Set α`. Taking `X` to be the common ground set gives the source's Theorem 10.9. `exists_commonIndep_card_eq_eRk_add_eRk` and its `ℕ`-valued companion give a maximum common independent set together with an optimal `U`. Weak duality (`encard_le_eRk_add_eRk`) needs no finiteness.

The algorithmic layer is stated on explicit objects. `IsExchSource`, `IsExchSink` and `IsExchArc` are the source set, sink set and arcs of the exchange digraph `H(M₁, M₂, I)`, displays (23)–(24) of the notes. A walk is a `List α` (`IsExchWalk`, `IsExchAugWalk`), and `IsShortestExchAugWalk` asks for the minimum number of vertices. `IsShortestExchAugWalk.augment` (Theorem 10.6) shows that `I ∆ p.toFinset` is a common independent subset of `E` of size `I.card + 1`; it relies on `indep_exchange_chain`, the chordless-exchange step of Lemma 10.2. `exists_separator_of_not_exists_exchAugWalk` (Theorem 10.7) shows that when no augmenting walk exists, the set of vertices unreachable from the sources attains the bound. The [tests](../../CslibFrontierTests/MatroidIntersection.lean) include a walk that is augmenting but not shortest, along which the exchange breaks `M₁`-independence, so the shortest-walk hypothesis cannot be dropped.

`CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.Matroid.Intersection.Konig` ([CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Matroid/Intersection/Konig.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Matroid/Intersection/Konig.lean)) connects this to the bipartite matching vocabulary above. `unitPartitionMatroid f` is the free matroid pulled back along `f`, in which a set is independent when `f` is injective on it. `isMatching_iff_isCommonIndep` identifies matchings of `G : L × R → Bool` with common independent subsets of `bipartiteEdges G` for the `Prod.fst` and `Prod.snd` partition matroids (Example 10.5a). `exists_maxMatching_separatorCover` derives König's theorem from Edmonds' theorem (Exercise 10.22). The cover is read off from the separator, and its minimality uses the existing matching/cover weak duality. The flow-based König theorem `exists_maxMatching_eq_minVertexCover` remains the primary statement; the tests recover its form from the matroid route.

The executable solver is in `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.Matroid.Intersection.Solver` ([CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Matroid/Intersection/Solver.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Matroid/Intersection/Solver.lean)). Its independence tests are effectful, `Finset α → m Bool` for a monad `m`. One phase (`exchTablesM`) computes the sources, sinks and arcs of the exchange digraph of the current `I` by tests. It then runs a multi-source breadth-first search, `Cslib.Reachability.findWalk` ([CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Reachability/ShortestWalk.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Reachability/ShortestWalk.lean)). The search returns either a shortest source-to-sink walk (`isShortestSrcTgtWalk_of_findWalk_eq_inl`) or the reachable vertices, which form a separator (`isSrcTgtSeparator_of_findWalk_eq_inr`). `maxCommonIndepM` runs at most `n + 1` phases from `∅` on the `n` distinct candidates and returns `(I, U)`, where `U` is the set of unreachable candidates. `maxCommonIndep` is the same program with Boolean tests (`maxCommonIndepM_pure`). For tests that decide `M₁` and `M₂`, `maxCommonIndep_spec` (Theorem 10.8) shows that `I` is a maximum common independent subset of `es.toFinset` and that `U ⊆ es.toFinset` satisfies `I.card = M₁.eRk U + M₂.eRk (es.toFinset \ U)`, so `U` is a checkable optimality certificate. Breadth-first search and list bookkeeping are pure host computation. The tests are issued with core `List.filterM`. The general facts about it are `List.filterM_pure` and `Cslib.IsMonadHom.map_filterM` in [CslibFrontier/Foundations/Control/Monad/FilterM.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Control/Monad/FilterM.lean), and `Cslib.FreeM.countQueries_filterM` in [CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Query/FreeM/FilterM.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Query/FreeM/FilterM.lean).

`CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.Query.Matroid.Intersection` ([CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Query/Matroid/Intersection.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Query/Matroid/Intersection.lean)) runs the solver in `FreeM (IndepPairQuery α)`. Here `IndepPairQuery α = IndepQuery α ⊕ IndepQuery α`, one independence oracle per matroid, and `IndepPairQuery.left`/`right`/`pair` split and join oracles. Only the independence tests are counted. `eval_matroidIntersection_spec` is the correctness theorem for oracles with `ImplementsMatroid (left oracle) M₁` and `ImplementsMatroid (right oracle) M₂`. On `n` distinct candidates a phase from `I` makes exactly `2(n - |I|)(1 + |I|)` queries (`countQueries_exchTablesM_of_nodup`). For every oracle, even one that implements no matroid, `matroidIntersection_upperBound` gives a `Cslib.Query.UpperBound` of `2n(n + 1)²` in the list length. For oracles implementing matroids, `countQueries_matroidIntersection` gives the exact count `∑_{r=0}^{ν} 2(n - r)(1 + r)`, where `ν` is the size of the output, and `countQueries_matroidIntersection_le` bounds it by `2n(ν + 1)²`. These counts describe this implementation, which rebuilds the exchange digraph in every phase. The source states only polynomial running time. The [solver tests](../../CslibFrontierTests/MatroidIntersectionSolver.lean) check:
- shortest-walk and separator outcomes of the search;
- a bipartite matching run that needs a three-vertex augmenting walk, with 20 queries;
- a colourful-forest run (a graphic matroid against a colour partition matroid) that exchanges an edge, with 40 queries;
- the correctness theorem at `Type 1`.

`TimeM` costs for the host computation (the search, the list filters and the symmetric difference) are not charged yet; that remaining work is tracked in [#2018](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/2018). Weighted matroid intersection and infinite ground sets are not treated.

## Stable matching

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.StableMatching.Incentives` (which also brings in `Basic`, `DeferredAcceptance` and `Blocking`) and `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.StableMatching.Lattice`; the declarations live in the namespace `Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.StableMatching`. This is a pure Lean development, not a RAM client. An `Instance n` has `n` proposers and `n` receivers with strict complete preference lists (`Fin n → Equiv.Perm (Fin n)`, rank `0` most preferred); a `Matching n` is a permutation, and `IsStable` means no blocking pair. `Instance.deferredAcceptance` is a total, `decide`-evaluable implementation of proposer-proposing deferred acceptance whose state is the vector of rejection counts. The modules prove stability of the output, the bound `proposals_le : I.proposals ≤ n ^ 2 - n + 1`, proposer-optimality and receiver-pessimality, the distributive lattice `Instance.Stable I` with deferred acceptance as `⊤` and its receiver-proposing dual as `⊥`, the blocking lemma `exists_isBlockingPair_of_lt`, weak Pareto optimality, and coalitional strategy-proofness for proposers (`not_forall_mem_propRank_lt_of_isStable`). Roth's impossibility theorem `MatchingMechanism.not_isStable_and_strategyProof` is proved for three agents on each side (`MatchingMechanism 3`), with the checked witness `MatchingMechanism.rothInstance`. That suffices for the existential impossibility; the statement for every `n ≥ 3` is not formalized.

The cost measure is the number of proposals, a property of the algorithm's trace rather than of a machine model; no Word-RAM or Turing implementation is certified. [CslibFrontierTests/StableMatching.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/StableMatching.lean) contains the checked instances.

### Incomplete lists and the rural hospitals theorem

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.StableMatching.Incomplete.RuralHospitals` and `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.StableMatching.Incomplete.Complete`, which bring in the rest of `Incomplete/`. They model markets with unacceptable partners and possibly unequal sides. An `IncompleteInstance m n` has `m` proposers and `n` receivers. Each agent has a list of the agents it finds acceptable (`propPref : Fin m → List (Fin n)`, most preferred first). Unlisted agents are unacceptable, and only the first occurrence of a repeated entry counts. A `PartialMatching m n` is Mathlib's partial bijection `Fin m ≃. Fin n`, so agents may be unmatched. `propRank p o` ranks an outcome `o : Option (Fin n)` by its position in the list. `none` and unlisted receivers get the list's length, so every acceptable partner beats staying unmatched. `IsStable` means individually rational (`IsIndividuallyRational`) with no blocking pair. A pair `(p, r)` blocks when each strictly prefers the other to its current outcome. These are the definitions of the marriage model as presented in [Roth and Sotomayor][rothsotomayor90], Chapter 2.

`IncompleteInstance.deferredAcceptance` is a `decide`-evaluable implementation of proposer-proposing deferred acceptance. A proposer whose list runs out stays unmatched. The run reaches a fixed point within `totalLength` steps (`step_run_fixed`) and makes at most `totalLength` rejections in total (`sum_finalState_le`), where `totalLength` is the total length of the proposers' lists. The module proves that the output is stable (`isStable_deferredAcceptance`), proposer-optimal (`isProposerOptimal_deferredAcceptance`) and receiver-pessimal. Running the algorithm on the swapped market gives `receiverDeferredAcceptance`, which is receiver-optimal. The rural hospitals theorem in the form of [Roth and Sotomayor][rothsotomayor90], Theorem 2.22, is `unmatched_eq_of_isStable`: any two stable matchings leave the same proposers and the same receivers unmatched. `matchedProposers_eq_of_isStable` and `matchedReceivers_eq_of_isStable` state it for the `Finset`s of matched agents. The proof uses optimality and pessimality of deferred acceptance, together with `PartialMatching.card_matchedReceivers` (a matching matches as many proposers as receivers).

`Instance.toIncompleteInstance` turns a complete-list market into a market in which everyone lists everyone. `isStable_toIncompleteInstance_iff_exists` shows that its stable matchings are exactly the stable perfect matchings `μ.toPEquiv` of the original market. `deferredAcceptance_toIncompleteInstance` shows that the two implementations agree. The complete-list API (lattice, blocking lemma, incentives) has not been generalized to incomplete lists. [CslibFrontierTests/StableMatchingIncomplete.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/StableMatchingIncomplete.lean) checks a market with two stable matchings in which one proposer is unmatched in every stable matching. It also checks a receiver that stays unmatched even though it lists an unmatched proposer, and agreement with the complete-list implementation on Roth's market. Ties, many-to-one markets and the capacitated rural hospitals theorem are not modeled; [#2990](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/2990) tracks these directions.

## Assignment problem: Bertsekas's auction algorithm

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.Assignment.Auction` ([CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Assignment/Auction.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Assignment/Auction.lean)); the declarations live in the namespace `Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.Assignment`. This is a pure Lean development, not a RAM client. The input is an integer cost matrix `c : Fin n × Fin n → ℤ` on the complete bipartite graph, and an assignment is a permutation `σ : Equiv.Perm (Fin n)` of cost `∑ i, c (i, σ i)`, to be minimized. `auction c ε` is the Gauss–Seidel auction of [Bertsekas][bertsekas88] in the cost form of [Bertsekas][bertsekas98], §7.1.1: an unassigned person bids for an object of least reduced cost `c (i, j) + p j`, raising its price by the gap to the second least reduced cost plus `ε`, and evicts the previous owner. Unassigned persons are kept on a stack. Fuel `bidBound n (costSpread c) ε` is computed from the data, so the function is total and `decide`-evaluable.

- **Invariant:** `AuctionInv.bidStep` is Prop. 7.1: each bid preserves `ε`-complementary slackness for the partial assignment (the `AuctionInv.epsCS` field, stated for assigned pairs). The complete-assignment form `IsEpsCompSlack` is recovered for the output by `isEpsCompSlack_auctionPerm`.
- **Termination:** `auction_free` gives termination with every person assigned. `bids_auction_le` bounds the number of bids by `bidBound n C ε = n (C + ε) / ε + 1` whenever every row cost range `c (i, k) - c (i, j)` is at most `C`. The proof uses the explicit measure `n (C + ε) - ∑ j, p j` (`sum_price_bidStep`, `AuctionInv.price_le`) in place of the book's argument by contradiction for Prop. 7.2.
- **Quality:** `auctionPerm c hε` is the output. `sum_auctionPerm_le` shows that it is within `n * ε` of every permutation (Props. 1.4 and 7.2). `sum_auctionPerm_le_rowPotential` turns the final prices into the feasible dual `(rowPotential c p, -p)` certifying this.
- **Exactness:** `scaledAuction c` runs the auction on `(n + 1) * c` with `ε = 1`, and `scaledAuctionPerm c` is its assignment. `sum_scaledAuctionPerm_le` proves it optimal, and `bids_scaledAuction_le` bounds the bids by `n ((n + 1) C + 1) + 1`.

The general layer [CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Assignment/Basic.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Assignment/Basic.lean) states `ε`-complementary slackness, weak duality over permutations and the `n ε` bound for any finite index type and ordered additive group. The cost measure is the number of bids: the `O(n)` scan per bid is not charged and there is no `TimeM` accounting. The bound is pseudo-polynomial, linear in `C / ε`. The `ε`-scaling version with its `O(n A log (n C))` bound (Prop. 7.3), the Jacobi, reverse and asymmetric auctions, rectangular or sparse instances, and a bridge to the potentials of the [Hungarian method](#assignment-problem-and-the-hungarian-method) are open; [#3317](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3317) tracks these directions. [CslibFrontierTests/AuctionAssignment.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/AuctionAssignment.lean) contains the checked instances:

- a `3 × 3` optimum checked against all six permutations, with its dual certificate;
- a `2 × 2` instance on which the unscaled auction is suboptimal, showing that the scaling is needed;
- Bertsekas's price war (Figs. 1.12–1.14), whose bid count grows with `C / ε`;
- the cases `n = 0` and `n = 1`.

## Housing markets and top trading cycles

Import `CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.HousingMarket.Incentives` (which also brings in `Basic` and `TopTradingCycles`); the declarations live in the namespace `Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.HousingMarket`. This is the housing market of [Shapley and Scarf][shapleyscarf74]. An `Instance n` gives each of `n` agents a strict complete preference list over the `n` houses, using the stable-matching conventions above (`pref : Fin n → Equiv.Perm (Fin n)`, rank `0` most preferred). Agent `i` owns house `i`, and an `Allocation n` is a permutation. A coalition `S` may only reallocate its own houses, that is, use permutations `ν` with `ν i ∈ S` for `i ∈ S`. There are two notions of blocking. `Blocks` asks for every member strictly better off, and its unblocked allocations are the core (`InCore`). `WeaklyBlocks` asks for all members weakly better off and one strictly better off, and its unblocked allocations are the strict core (`InStrictCore`). [Schummer and Vohra][schummervohra07], §10.3, call the latter "the core". With strict preferences the two notions still differ: the checked tests contain a core allocation outside the strict core. This contradicts the claim of [Schummer and Vohra][schummervohra07], p. 255, that they coincide for strict preferences. Their argument drops weakly blocking members who are not strictly better off as if they kept their own houses, but such a member may keep a house it received in the trade.

`Instance.ttc` is Gale's top trading cycles algorithm as a total, `decide`-evaluable function. In each round every remaining agent points to the owner of its favourite remaining house (`topChoice`). The agents on cycles (`cycleAgents`, the periodic points of the pointer map) receive the houses they point to and leave. All cycles of a round are removed together, as in [Schummer and Vohra][schummervohra07], Definition 10.5, and `n` rounds suffice. The main results:

- `inStrictCore_iff : I.InStrictCore μ ↔ μ = I.ttc`: the strict core is exactly the TTC allocation ([Roth and Postlewaite][rothpostlewaite77]; [Schummer and Vohra][schummervohra07], Theorem 10.6, whose core is defined by weak blocking).
- `inCore_ttc`, `isIndividuallyRational_ttc`, `isParetoEfficient_ttc`: consequences of strict-core membership. `inCore_ttc` is the existence half of [Roughgarden's CS364A][roughgarden13cs364a], Lecture #9, Theorem 3.2. That lecture defines the core by strict blocking, and for that notion the uniqueness half fails (see the second test market below).
- `isCompetitive_ttc`, `IsCompetitive.inStrictCore` and `exists_isCompetitive_iff`: TTC is competitive (every agent can afford its house and prefers it to every house it can afford) at the prices `ttcPrice`, the number of agents present in the round in which the house leaves. Competitive allocations lie in the strict core, so an allocation is competitive at some `ℕ` prices iff it is the TTC allocation.
- `HousingMechanism.isStrategyProof_topTradingCycles`: the TTC mechanism is strategy-proof ([Roth][roth82]; [Schummer and Vohra][schummervohra07], Theorem 10.7; [CS364A][roughgarden13cs364a], Lecture #9, Theorem 3.1). A misreport by agent `i` can only change the pointers of agent `i`. Before agent `i` leaves, the cycles that do not contain `i` are unchanged, and every house that agent `i` can reach is one it could reach under truthful reporting.

Scope: preferences are strict, every agent owns exactly one house, the market size `n` is fixed by `Fin n`, and only individual (not group) strategy-proofness is proved. No running time is charged: `ttc` is a mathematical function, not a `TimeM` or RAM program, so the `O(n²)` bound for TTC is not stated. House allocation with existing tenants (Abdulkadiroğlu–Sönmez), Ma's characterization of TTC, group strategy-proofness and indifferences are directions recorded in [#3286](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3286). [CslibFrontierTests/HousingMarket.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/HousingMarket.lean) contains the checked instances: a three-cycle, a two-cycle followed by a fixed point, TTC prices, uniqueness by enumeration, and two small mechanisms: keeping the endowment is strategy-proof but not Pareto efficient, and a two-agent mechanism is manipulable.
