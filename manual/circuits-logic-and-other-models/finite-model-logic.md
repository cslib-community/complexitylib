# Finite-model logic and native complexity

The descriptive-complexity development uses Mathlib first-order syntax and semantics, then adds finite encodings, interpretations and scoped relation operators. The main bridges concern **data complexity**: a sentence is fixed while its finite input structure varies. The number of variables and nesting depth of that sentence may affect the polynomial or logspace constants. [cslib-frontier][complexitylib]

## Structures, encodings and formulas

The [closeAll](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Basic.lean#L69) layer supplies finite-variable closure and atomic/order combinators over `FirstOrder.Language`. Finite relational vocabularies enumerate relation symbols of their declared arities. The [Vocabulary](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Encoding.lean#L89) representation is universe size together with a list of relation truth tables. Cardinality is unary; relation tables are framed and their order follows the chosen finite vocabulary enumeration. Tuple coordinate zero is the least significant coordinate of the table index.

The decoder uses a canonical universe `Fin n`. A table for an arity-k relation must contain exactly `n^k` entries, and the relation list must have exactly the expected number of tables. Nullary relations still have one bit when n is zero. Missing raw values may have fallback semantics in the decoder, but the encoded query language requires well-formedness and therefore rejects malformed tables. This separation permits total parsers without weakening the language contract.

The [Structure](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/Query.lean#L48) variant uses canonical `Fin n` with n at least two and fixed numeric symbols for zero, one, maximum, order and BIT. These numeric relations/functions are determined by n rather than supplied as arbitrary input tables. Their BIT convention is least-significant-bit first. The n>=2 restriction avoids degenerate constants; an endpoint over every finite structure must handle the smaller universes separately.

## Interpretations and reductions

[Interpretation](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Interpretation.lean#L98) defines a target structure on a definable subset of source tuples. Target relation symbols are interpreted by formulas and equality is literal tuple equality. Formula pullback relativizes quantifiers to the interpreted domain, proves satisfaction preservation and supports composition. There is no implicit quotient of tuples by a definable equivalence relation.

[TaggedInterpretation](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Interpretation/Tagged.lean#L105) extends the representation to a finite disjoint union of tuple domains, with potentially different dimensions. This makes multi-sorted gadget reductions readable. Equality patterns encode tags into ordinary tuples when sufficient distinct domain elements are available; tiny universes and copy-invariant targets require the explicit treatment supplied by the relevant bridge. SAT, coloring and independent-set reductions are clients.

The projection API is stricter than arbitrary quantifier-free interpretation. [InputLiteral](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ProjectionFormula.lean#L70) has a numeric-case syntax whose leaves are a single signed input atom or a Boolean constant. `Projection` combines that syntax with domain/numeric interpretation. This preserves the intended distinction between a literal selector and a formula such as the conjunction of two independent input relations. Exact classical projection conventions additionally depend on the numeric vocabulary and domain construction. Identify those choices when using the API; arbitrary QF interpretations are not all projections.

Ordered reductions introduce a second subtlety. [DomainInterpretation](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/DomainInterpretation.lean#L81) selects a definable subset of tuples and ranks it lexicographically to obtain a canonical target universe. The rank's BIT relation is not assumed automatically first-order definable. A [numeric certificate](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/DomainInterpretation/Certificate.lean) supplies formulas and their correctness when a reduction needs them. [FullTupleInterpretation](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/FullTuple.lean#L77) specializes to the entire positive-dimensional tuple universe, where canonical arithmetic/order structure is constructed without an arbitrary subset-ranking assumption.

## Scoped operators: LFP and (deterministic) transitive closure

[RelVar](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/Basic.lean#L48) extends syntax with scoped relation variables and general binder shapes. This factors out relabeling, environments, polarity and satisfaction reasoning for operators. The least-fixed-point development deliberately separates raw formulas from checked positive formulas.

The checked syntax [Formula](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/LeastFixedPoint.lean#L72) enforces positivity in the recursively bound relation at every nested binder. Positivity gives monotonicity, so the semantic operator has a least fixed point; over a universe of size n and an arity-k relation, iteration from the empty relation stabilizes by `n^k`. Raw unchecked syntax still has total semantics via the intersection of prefixed points, but that expression need not satisfy a nonmonotone operator's fixed-point equation. The checked/raw distinction is mathematically essential, not just a parser convenience.

[shape](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/TransitiveClosure.lean#L77) uses reflexive transitive closure of a relation on tuples. The binder has two tuples of the same width and no new relation variables; width zero is permitted. This module provides a closure/definability API. [TC.Positive](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/TransitiveClosure/Positive.lean) is a polarity check: a formula passes when every closure occurs under an even number of negations. The formula body of a closure is itself checked at polarity `true`. This is distinct from `Operator.Formula.checkPolarity`, which concerns relation variables. [PosTCDefinable](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/TransitiveClosure.lean) is the corresponding ordered-query notion. [onTCSentence](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/FullTuple/TransitiveClosure.lean) pulls an FO(TC) sentence over the power universe `Fin (n ^ k)` of a full-tuple interpretation back to the input universe `Fin n`, as `onLFPSentence` does for FO(LFP). A closure over pairs of width-`w` target tuples becomes a closure over pairs of width-`w * k` source tuples. `positive_onTCSentence` says that every closure keeps its polarity, and `realize_onTCSentence` that truth is preserved, for positive dimension `k`. The formula-level translation is [TC.Tuple.onFormula](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/TransitiveClosure/Tuple.lean); it shares its term and quantifier-block helpers with the LFP pullback in [Operator/Tuple](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/Tuple.lean). The statement of `realize_onTCSentence` installs the target structure and the `Fact (2 ≤ n ^ k)` instance with `letI`; after rewriting with it, restate both with `let` before applying lemmas on `Fin (n ^ k)`, as the [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/FullTupleTransitiveClosure.lean) do. Only one direction of FO(TC) = NL is proved: FO(TC) ⊆ NL, at every polarity (next section). Do not cite the full capture theorem. Over unordered finite graphs, FO(TC) is strictly more expressive than FO (`TC.exists_formula_not_fo_equivalent`; see Hanf and Gaifman locality below).

[DTC.interpretation](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/DeterministicTransitiveClosure.lean) gives the same `TC.shape` syntax a second meaning, deterministic transitive closure (Immerman 1987, §4): the reflexive transitive closure of the deterministic reduct, which keeps an edge `x → y` only when `y` is the only successor of `x`. FO(DTC) and FO(TC) formulas are therefore the same `Operator.Formula L TC.shape Γ α` trees, built with `TC.tc` and `TC.pair`; only the interpretation passed to `Formula.Realize` says which logic is meant. Use `DTC.realize_tc` and `DTC.realize_tc_pair` to unfold a deterministic closure; `TC.realize_tc`, `TC.realize_tc_of_body` and `TC.Positive` are about `TC.interpretation` and do not apply. The reduct is not monotone in the body: adding edges can remove deterministic paths, so DTC has no `Policy` and may occur at any polarity. `DTC.toTC` replaces every closure body `φ(x, y)` by `φ(x, y) ∧ ¬∃z (φ(x, z) ∧ z ≠ y)`, and `DTC.realize_toTC` shows the result read with `TC.interpretation` means the original read with `DTC.interpretation`. [DTCDefinable](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/DeterministicTransitiveClosure.lean) is the ordered-query notion, with `Definable.dtcDefinable` and `DTCDefinable.tcDefinable` giving FO ⊆ FO(DTC) ⊆ FO(TC). Neither inclusion of FO(DTC) = L is proved yet ([#4084](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4084)). The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/FODeterministicClosure.lean) show that TC reaches `true` from `false` in the complete graph on `Bool` while DTC does not, that DTC does on its one-edge subgraph, that `DTC.toTC` read with `TC.interpretation` also fails on the complete graph, and that the definability chain composes for any query.

## Fagin's theorem

[Sentence](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/SecondOrder/Existential.lean#L124) is a finite collection of existential relation variables, each with an arity, followed by a first-order matrix over the sum vocabulary. The headline equivalence says that a class of finite relational structures is ESO-definable exactly when it is isomorphism-closed and its well-formed encoded query language lies in the library's native NP class. The vocabulary enumeration is fixed and no arbitrary input function symbols are admitted by this encoding theorem.

The reverse direction begins from the native strict verifier formulation. A fixed tuple dimension bounds the polynomial clock and relation tables; an ESO sentence describes a valid computation tableau and guesses an order. Empty and singleton universes are handled by finite truth-table cases rather than an invalid assumption that a positive tuple power always has enough distinct values. The forward direction checks a guessed relation interpretation and the first-order matrix within polynomial bounds. See the [converse implementation](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Fagin/Converse.lean) and, for the survey's tracing, the since-retired [Fagin abstract](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/abstracts/CslibFrontierAbstracts/FaginAbstract.lean) and its [checked bridge](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/abstracts/CslibFrontierAbstracts/FaginBridge.lean).

The source documentation maps its statement to Fagin's finite-model result and textbook formulations by Immerman and Papadimitriou. This author traced the abstract's encoding and quantifiers to the library statement, including malformed, empty-universe and nullary-relation behavior. The original paper correspondence has not been independently exhaustively audited in this research pass.

## MSO over words and the Büchi–Elgot–Trakhtenbrot theorem

ESO allows only one block of existential relation quantifiers, so MSO over words has its own small syntax. [Formula](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/SecondOrder/WordMSO/Basic.lean) `α m n` has `m` free position variables and `n` free set variables, indexed by `Fin m` and `Fin n`. Atoms are `letter a x` (P_a(x)), `lt x y` and `mem x X`. The connectives are `falsum` and `imp`, and the existential quantifiers `ex` and `exSet` bind the *last* index of their sort, as Mathlib's `BoundedFormula` does, so `Realize` extends assignments with `Fin.snoc`. The usual derived connectives, finite `iInf`/`iSup` conjunctions and disjunctions, and the position predicates `first`, `last` and `succ` come with `@[simp]` `realize_*` lemmas. A `Sentence α` (no free variables) defines `Sentence.language`: the words `w` such that the structure on positions `Fin w.length` with letters `w[i]` satisfies it. `Definable` means MSO-definable. `FODefinable` asks for a sentence with `IsFO` (no set quantifiers), so it is FO[<] with letter predicates and no BIT; `isFO_*` simp lemmas compute `IsFO`.

[Definable.isRegular](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/SecondOrder/WordMSO/ToRegular.lean) holds over every alphabet, finite or not. Each formula is read over `ExtLetter α m n`, which adds one bit per free variable, and `isRegular_extLanguage` proves by induction that its language is regular. Regularity of the atoms comes from positional languages; implication uses complement and union, and both quantifiers use the image along the letter map that forgets one bit. The image step needs closure under letter maps, which cslib lacks. [IsRegular.map](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Languages/RegularLanguage.lean) supplies it, together with the positional languages, at the mirrored cslib path and marked `-- upstream: candidate`; it is proved through the NFA characterization `IsRegular.iff_nfa` and [NA.FinAcc.map](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Automata/NA/Map.lean). The converse [definable_of_isRegular](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/SecondOrder/WordMSO/OfRegular.lean) needs a finite alphabet, because a sentence mentions only finitely many letters. Its sentence `dfaSentence M` guesses one set per state through `Fintype.equivFin` and treats the empty word as a separate conjunct. `definable_iff_isRegular` combines the two directions. `evenSentence` defines even length over every alphabet. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/WordMSO.lean) evaluate concrete sentences, the parity DFA and an infinite-alphabet instance.

[wordLanguage](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/SecondOrder/WordMSO/FirstOrder.lean) is the word vocabulary as a Mathlib `FirstOrder.Language`, with symbols `wordLanguage.ltSymb` and `wordLanguage.letterSymb a`, interpreted on `Fin w.length` by `wordStructure w`. `wordStructure w` is not an instance, because `Fin w.length` does not determine `w`, so bring it into scope with `let := wordStructure w`. Build formulas from the typed symbols rather than the raw `WordRel` constructors, whose type is not syntactically `(wordLanguage α).Relations n`, so `simp` and `rw` would fail on them. With the typed symbols, Mathlib's `realize_rel₁`/`realize_rel₂` apply, and the simp lemmas `wordStructure.relMap_ltSymb` and `wordStructure.relMap_letterSymb` evaluate the relations; `mem_someTrue` in the [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/WordMSO.lean) is a worked example. `foDefinable_iff_exists_sentence` identifies `FODefinable` with definability by a Mathlib sentence (`WordFO.language`); `WordFO.toFormula` and `Formula.toWordFO` are the two translations. [not_foDefinable_even](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/SecondOrder/WordMSO/Separation.lean) proves FO[<] ⊊ MSO over every nonempty alphabet (over one letter this is `(aa)*`, the first-order half of Libkin's Corollary 7.27; the star-free half is below). On a word whose letters all equal `c`, `Formula.toOrder c` turns a first-order word formula into a sentence of Mathlib's order language. The Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé bound `realize_iff_of_two_pow_le_card` from [FiniteLinearOrder](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/FiniteLinearOrder.lean) then equates the words of lengths `2 ^ k` and `2 ^ k + 1`. `exists_definable_not_foDefinable`, `exists_isRegular_not_foDefinable` and `not_exists_sentence_language_eq_even` are the packaged forms. Over the empty alphabet every language is first-order definable, which the tests check.

[StarFreeExpression](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Languages/StarFree.lean) has the constructors `zero`, `char`, `plus`, `compl` and `comp`, following Libkin's Definition 7.25. Complement is taken relative to all of `List α`. `IsStarFree L` asks for an expression whose `matches'` is `L`; it lives at the mirrored cslib path, marked `-- upstream: candidate`. `IsStarFree.isRegular` comes from cslib's closure lemmas. `isStarFree_one_iff` shows that `{ε}` is star-free exactly when the alphabet is finite. Over a finite alphabet, `IsStarFree.of_finite` (finite languages) and `IsStarFree.singleton` show that presentations starting from `{ε}` or from all finite languages define the same class. [starFreeSentence](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/SecondOrder/WordMSO/StarFree.lean) translates an expression into a first-order sentence, which gives `IsStarFree.foDefinable` over every alphabet. The translation relativizes to windows `lo < x ≤ hi` between bound variables (a missing bound means an end of the word), and a concatenation guesses the last position of its first factor. A concatenation with an empty first factor has no such position, so it adds the disjunct for the second factor exactly when `matchEpsilon` holds for the first; Libkin's proof sketch leaves this case implicit. `not_isStarFree_even` and `exists_isRegular_not_isStarFree` complete Libkin's Corollary 7.27. `exists_foDefinable_not_isStarFree` records that the converse, FO ⇒ star-free, fails over an infinite alphabet; over a finite alphabet it holds (McNaughton–Papert, below). The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/StarFree.lean) evaluate Libkin's `a*b*` and a concatenation with an empty first factor.

[Composition](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/SecondOrder/WordMSO/Composition.lean) plays Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé games on word structures. `WordDuplicatorWins n u v a b` is `DuplicatorWins` over `wordLanguage α` with `wordStructure u` and `wordStructure v` passed explicitly. Do not use two `let := wordStructure _` instances for this. For concrete words of equal length, such as `[true]` and `[false]`, instance search gives both sides the structure of the later `let`. Its API (`zero_iff`, `succ_iff`, `forth`, `back`, `mono`, `refl`, `symm`, `trans`, `of_eq`, `realize_iff`) restates the generic lemmas with the structures filled in, and `wordDuplicatorWins_iff` and `wordRankType_eq_rankType` unfold the wrappers to `FO.DuplicatorWins` and `FO.rankType`. `wordStructure.partialIso_iff` reduces a partial isomorphism to agreement of the order and the letters on the pebbles. `WordDuplicatorWins.append` is the composition argument of Libkin's Lemma 3.7 and §7.4. Winning positions on `u₁, v₁` and on `u₂, v₂` combine into a winning position on `u₁ ++ u₂, v₁ ++ v₂`: each combined pebble names its factor through `σ : Fin k → Fin k₁ ⊕ Fin k₂`, and [`List.appendIndexEquiv`](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/List/Equiv.lean) identifies `Fin u.length ⊕ Fin v.length` with `Fin (u ++ v).length`. `append_elim0` is the form without pebbles. `append_cons` puts one pebble on an inserted letter `c` in `u₁ ++ c :: u₂`, the decomposition that the converse of McNaughton–Papert, FO ⇒ star-free over a finite alphabet ([#3469](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3469)), performs at the quantified position. `wordRankType` and `wordRankType_eq_iff` connect the game to rank types. `wordCon α n` is rank-`n` equivalence as a `Con (FreeMonoid α)`. Over a finite alphabet it has finite index (`wordCon.finite_quotient`, using the `Finite (Σ l, (wordLanguage α).Relations l)` instance of `WordMSO/FirstOrder`), and `wordCon_preimage_image_language` recognizes the language of every sentence through its quotient, a finite monoid. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/WordMSOComposition.lean) show that `b ≡₁ bb` but not `b ≡₂ bb`, compose the first through `append_elim0`, `append_cons` and `wordCon`, and derive the regularity of the language of a sentence over `Bool` from Myhill–Nerode.

[McNaughtonPapert](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/SecondOrder/WordMSO/McNaughtonPapert.lean) proves the converse over a finite alphabet, `FODefinable.isStarFree`, and with `IsStarFree.foDefinable` the McNaughton–Papert theorem `foDefinable_iff_isStarFree [Finite α]` (Libkin's Theorem 7.26). The induction runs over the classes of rank-`n` equivalence, `rankTypeLanguage n T`, rather than over sentences. A class of rank `0` is empty or everything. By `wordRankType_succ_eq_iff`, a class of rank `n + 1` is a finite Boolean combination of the languages `pointedRankTypeLanguage n τ` of words with a position of one-pebble type `τ`. `pointedRankTypeLanguage_eq` splits such a word as `u₁ ++ c :: u₂` at that position, the adaptation of Libkin's split with a constant `max`, and writes the language as a finite union of products `L(T₁) * {[c]} * L(T₂)` over the triples satisfying `ProducesType`. `WordFO.isStarFree_language` covers the language of a sentence as the finite union of the classes it meets, using `IsStarFree.iSup` and `IsStarFree.iInf` (finite unions and intersections) from `Computability/Languages/StarFree`. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/McNaughtonPapert.lean) apply the theorem to concrete languages and show that the finite alphabet cannot be dropped.

Pitfall: regularity here is Mathlib's `Language.IsRegular`. On `h : L.IsRegular`, dot notation resolves only to Mathlib's `_root_.Language.IsRegular.*` lemmas. Call cslib's closure lemmas by their full names (`Cslib.Language.IsRegular.mul`, `.iInf`, `.map`, ...). Also, `{w | ...}.IsRegular` elaborates the set-builder as a `Set`, so write `Language.IsRegular {w : List α | ...}`. For the same reason, `rw [Language.mem_add]` and `convert` fail on a sum or product that has a set-builder operand. Prove the equality of languages by `ext`, then go through `(Language.mem_add _ _ w).trans` or `change`.

## FO model checking and Immerman–Vardi

Fixed FO formulas have a native logspace model checker on the stated table encoding. The implementation compiles formula evaluation through bounded-register counter programs and validates input shape. A separate circuit compilation expands quantifiers into AND/OR gates, giving constant depth and polynomial size in the universe parameter. That circuit result is an upper direction; it does not by itself claim DLOGTIME-uniform AC0 capture or charge serial encoding length as though it were one relation bit. See [native FO logspace](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/FirstOrder.lean) and [ordered FO circuits](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/FirstOrder/Ordered.lean).

For canonical ordered finite relational structures, [Immerman–Vardi](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/ImmermanVardi.lean) proves `lfpDefinable_iff_encodedLanguage_mem_P`. Positive finite fixed-point iteration supplies the forward native polynomial-time computation. The reverse direction encodes a native polynomial-time machine by a fixed-point tableau in a fixed tuple power. The theorem is a genuine bidirectional model bridge with explicit encoding, not only a semantic containment assumption.

[TCDefinable.encodedLanguage_mem_NL](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/FirstOrder/TransitiveClosure.lean) is the easy inclusion FO(TC) ⊆ NL of Immerman's capture theorem. On the same `encStructure` encoding, it puts every query defined by an FO(TC) sentence in native `NL`, with closures at any polarity; `PosTCDefinable.encodedLanguage_mem_NL` is the positive special case (Immerman 1987, Theorem 3.1). Immerman's numeric vocabulary is `=`, successor, `0` and `max`; ours is `≤`, `BIT`, `0`, `1` and `max`. The decider is a single nondeterministic counter program. The [compiler](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/FirstOrder/TransitiveClosure/Compile.lean) is indexed by polarity, so a negation flips the target polarity rather than complementing a nondeterministic computation. The count of negations restarts inside each closure body. A closure of width `k` at polarity `true` guesses a path one tuple at a time, using at most `n^k` steps. Each guessed edge is checked by a recursive call. A closure at polarity `false` runs the [inductive-counting block](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/FirstOrder/TransitiveClosure/Counting.lean) of Immerman and Szelepcsényi on tuple codes below `n^k`; Immerman 1988, Corollary 3, states the source result NL = FO(positive TC) = FO(TC). Round by round it computes the number of tuples reachable within `i` steps. Each membership test guesses the previous round in increasing order, certifies each guessed tuple by a walk, and certifies edges and non-edges with the body compiled at both polarities. This is a separate in-compiler simulation; the machine-level `NL = coNL` in [complementation](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/Complement.lean) is not reused, because native closure of `NL` under the needed compositions is not yet available. Every register stays at most `(N + 1)^(d + w)`, where `N` is the input length, `d` the maximal input arity and `w` the largest closure width. The converse, NL ⊆ FO(TC), still needs configuration-graph reachability under the ordered encoding. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/TransitiveClosureNL.lean) define s–t reachability from `zero` to `max` in an ordered graph and prove that both it and its complement, defined by a negated closure, lie in `NL`.

## Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé games and nondefinability

[quantifierRank](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/QuantifierRank.lean) measures how deeply the quantifiers of a Mathlib `BoundedFormula` nest. The LogSpace model checker's register depth satisfies [boundDepth_eq_add_quantifierRank](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/FirstOrder/Formula.lean): it equals the number of bound variables in scope plus the quantifier rank. [DuplicatorWins](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/Basic.lean) is the `n`-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game played from a position: a pair of pebble tuples `a : Fin k → M`, `b : Fin k → N`, extended by `Fin.snoc` each round, which are exactly the variable assignments of formulas with `k` bound variables. Duplicator must keep every position a partial isomorphism (`PartialIso`: equality and every relation agree on the pebbles). The game, strategies (`duplicatorWins_iff_exists_strategy`; `Strategy` is exposed data that clients build by recursion and check with `Strategy.isWinning_zero`/`Strategy.isWinning_succ`), back-and-forth systems (`IsBackAndForth.duplicatorWins`) and invariance under isomorphism are available for any language and structures, finite or infinite.

The [Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/Hintikka.lean) has two directions with different hypotheses. `DuplicatorWins.realize_iff` (winning positions satisfy the same formulas of quantifier rank at most `n`) needs `L.IsRelational` and nothing else. The converse `duplicatorWins_of_forall_realize_iff` needs finitely many relation symbols, `[Finite (Σ l, L.Relations l)]`, and is proved with Hintikka formulas of rank-`n` types. `duplicatorWins_iff_forall_sentence` is the combined statement for sentences. `duplicatorWins_iff_forall_formula` and `DuplicatorWins.formula_realize_iff` state it for `L.Formula (Fin k)`, with the free variables naming the pebbles. The rank lemmas `quantifierRank_relabel` and `quantifierRank_toFormula` carry ranks across Mathlib's conversions between the two forms, and `realize_toFormula_relabel` carries truth. As a consequence there are only finitely many formulas of rank at most `n` in `k` variables up to equivalence (`exists_finite_quantifierRank_le_realize_iff`).

To show that a property of finite structures is not first-order definable, import the theorem module and, for each `n`, give two structures on which Duplicator wins `n` rounds, one with the property and one without. Then apply `DuplicatorWins.realize_iff` (or `duplicatorWins_iff_forall_sentence`) to a supposed defining sentence of rank `n`. Duplicator's wins are usually obtained by giving an invariant on positions and checking `IsBackAndForth`. For [finite linear orders](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/FiniteLinearOrder.lean), `duplicatorWins_iff_card` decides the game exactly: equal size, or both sizes at least `2 ^ n - 1` (the characterization in Kolaitis's survey chapter of *Finite Model Theory and Its Applications*). `not_exists_sentence_realize_iff_even` is the resulting nondefinability of EVEN. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/EhrenfeuchtFraisse.lean) cover a rank-`2` sentence separating `Fin 2` from `Fin 3`, the threshold cases, an ODD client, and explicit winning strategies built outside the defining module.

Nondefinability transfers along first-order interpretations. If an interpretation sends each structure of one class to a structure of another, then a defining sentence for a property of the second class pulls back (`Interpretation.realize_onSentence`) to a defining sentence for the corresponding property of the first. [DefinedGraph](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/DefinedGraph.lean) packages this route once for any graph property. A formula `δ` with two free variables defines a graph in every structure (`definedGraph`), interpreted by `graphInterp δ`, so graph sentences pull back (`realize_onSentence_graphInterp`). By `not_exists_sentence_of_even` (or `not_exists_sentence_of_odd`), if the graph defined by `δ` in large finite linear orders has a property exactly when the order has even (or odd) size, then the property is not first-order definable on finite graphs. The module also provides the order formulas used to define such graphs (`covByFormula`, `isBotFormula`, `twoAboveFormula`, ...). This is the reduction from EVEN that Libkin uses for connectivity (Corollary 3.19). [Connectivity](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/Connectivity.lean): the step-two graph (`stepTwoGraph`) of a linear order joins each element to the one two steps above it, the last element to the second, and the penultimate element to the first. It is a defined graph (`stepTwoGraph_eq_definedGraph`), and it is connected exactly when the order has an odd number of elements (`stepTwoGraph_connected_iff_odd`). So `not_exists_sentence_connected` (and `not_exists_sentence_connected_of_le_card`, for graphs with at least `t` vertices) says that no sentence of Mathlib's `Language.graph` defines connectivity on finite simple graphs, in any universe. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/FOConnectivity.lean) evaluate the step-two graph on `Fin 4`, `Fin 5` and `Bool` and pull back a concrete graph sentence. [Bipartiteness and acyclicity](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/GraphProperties.lean) (Libkin, Exercises 3.5(3) and 3.4) use the same transfer. On `Fin m` the cycle formula `cycleArcFormula` defines Mathlib's `cycleGraph m` (`definedGraph_cycleArcFormula_fin`), which is bipartite exactly when `m` is even; a step-two graph closed by one edge (`stepTwoTopBotArcFormula`) is acyclic exactly when the order has even size, for size at least `4`. This gives `not_exists_sentence_isBipartite` and `not_exists_sentence_isAcyclic`. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/FOGraphProperties.lean) include the size bound for acyclicity: on `Fin 3` the closed step-two graph is acyclic although `3` is odd.

Two pitfalls. The game does not support constants or function symbols, so a vocabulary with numeric constants must first replace them by their defining formulas. The order results concern Mathlib's pure order language, FO(≤), not the Immerman convention FO(≤, BIT) used by the ordered capture theorems above. With `BIT`, EVEN is definable (bit zero of `max`), so these results show that `BIT` adds expressive power; they say nothing about FO(≤, BIT). Statements about sentences take an arbitrary empty tuple `a : Fin 0 → M`. Do not rewrite with a lemma stated for `default`: at concrete types such as `Fin 3`, `default` may elaborate through a different `Inhabited` instance and the rewrite fails.

## Hanf and Gaifman locality

[Gaifman](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Locality/Gaifman.lean) defines the Gaifman graph of a structure (`gaifmanGraph L M`), which joins two distinct elements when they occur together in a tuple of some relation. Distances are Mathlib's `SimpleGraph.edist`, valued in `ℕ∞`. `ball L a r` is the set of elements at distance at most `r` from some entry of the tuple `a`. It is a closed ball around a tuple, unlike Mathlib's open `SimpleGraph.ball c r` (`edist < r`) around one vertex. For a simple graph `G`, `gaifmanGraph_structure` identifies the Gaifman graph of `G.structure` with `G`, so balls are graph balls. `IsNbhdIso L r a b h` says that `h : M → N` is an isomorphism of the radius-`r` neighborhoods of `a` and `b`: it is a bijection between the balls (`Set.BijOn`) that sends `a` to `b` and preserves and reflects every relation on tuples from the ball. `NbhdIso` states that such an `h` exists. Libkin's Lemma 4.8 is `IsNbhdIso.restrict` and `IsNbhdIso.mono` (restriction) and `IsNbhdIso.snoc` (combining the neighborhoods of points more than `2r + 1` apart).

[Hanf](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Locality/Hanf.lean) proves Hanf and Gaifman locality with Libkin's radii (Libkin, Theorem 4.12 and Corollary 4.13). `HanfEquiv L d a b` is Libkin's `(M, a) ⇆_d (N, b)`: a bijection `f : M ≃ N` such that adding `c` to `a` and `f c` to `b` gives isomorphic radius-`d` neighborhoods. Over a finite nonempty `M`, `HanfEquiv.duplicatorWins` turns Hanf-equivalence at radius `(3 ^ n - 1) / 2` into a win for Duplicator in the `n`-round game, for any language. For relational languages, `HanfEquiv.realize_iff` and `realize_sentence_iff_of_nbhdIso` give agreement on formulas and sentences of quantifier rank at most `n`. Gaifman locality is `NbhdIso.realize_iff` (and `NbhdIso.formula_realize_iff`): in a finite structure, tuples with isomorphic radius-`(3 ^ (n + 1) - 1) / 2` neighborhoods satisfy the same rank-`n` formulas. The key step is `hanfEquiv_of_nbhdIso` (Libkin, Lemma 4.9).

To show with Hanf's theorem that a property is not first-order definable, give for each `n` two finite structures, one with the property and one without, and a bijection that matches points with isomorphic radius-`(3 ^ n - 1) / 2` neighborhoods. Then apply `realize_sentence_iff_of_nbhdIso`. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/FOLocality.lean) do this at radius `1` for the 8-cycle and two disjoint 4-cycles, using an explicit local isomorphism at each vertex, and check that the two graphs are not isomorphic.

[Locality/GraphProperties](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Locality/GraphProperties.lean) carries out this recipe for cycles and paths at every radius. For connectivity it follows Libkin, §4.1, Figure 4.2 (p. 48), with Theorem 4.12 and the remark following Corollary 4.13 (p. 52). For acyclicity and trees it answers Exercise 4.6 (p. 63). The neighborhoods are described by `SimpleGraph.IsBallPath` from [BallPath](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/BallPath.lean). A ball path is a map `φ : ℤ → V` that parametrizes the radius-`r` ball around `φ 0` as a path over `[lo, hi]`. Then `IsBallPath.edist_le_iff` computes every closed ball of radius `k ≤ r` around the center. In `cycleGraph N` with `N ≥ 2r + 2`, the balls are ball paths over `[-r, r]` (`exists_isBallPath_cycleGraph`). In `pathGraph N` they are ball paths over the interval clipped at the two ends (`exists_isBallPath_pathGraph`). `nbhdIso_of_isBallPath` turns two ball paths over the same interval into a neighborhood isomorphism. This gives `hanfEquiv_cycleGraph` (`C_{a+b}` against `C_a ⊕ C_b` when `a, b ≥ 2r + 2`) and `hanfEquiv_pathGraph` (`P_{a+b}` against `P_a ⊕ C_b` when `a ≥ 2r` and `b ≥ 2r + 2`). At the Hanf radius these become `realize_cycleGraph_iff_sum` (for `a, b > 3 ^ n`) and `realize_pathGraph_iff_sum`. The consequences are quantitative. If a sentence of quantifier rank `q` holds in exactly the connected graphs on a finite type `V`, then `|V| ≤ 2 · 3 ^ q + 1` (`card_le_of_realize_iff_connected`). For trees or acyclic graphs the bound is `|V| ≤ 2 · 3 ^ q` (`card_le_of_realize_iff_isTree`, `card_le_of_realize_iff_isAcyclic`). For `q ≥ 1` all three improve to `|V| < 2 · 3 ^ q` (`card_lt_of_realize_iff_connected`, `card_lt_of_realize_iff_isTree`, `card_lt_of_realize_iff_isAcyclic`). The hypothesis is needed for acyclicity, since `⊤` (rank `0`) defines acyclicity on two vertices. These constants come from Hanf's radius and are not claimed to be sharp. `not_exists_sentence_isTree` is the new unbounded statement. Connectivity and acyclicity already have Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé proofs, `Connectivity.not_exists_sentence_connected` and `GraphProperties.not_exists_sentence_isAcyclic`, so they are not restated. The [locality examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/FOLocalityGraphProperties.lean) rederive both from the bounds without games. They also check the rank-`2`, radius-`4` instance (`C_20` against `C_10 ⊕ C_10`) and show that the triangle has no radius-`1` ball path, so the bound `2r + 2` is needed.

[Foundations/ModelTheory/Graph](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/ModelTheory/Graph.lean) translates graph maps into maps of `Language.graph`-structures: `SimpleGraph.Iso.toLanguageEquiv` and `SimpleGraph.Iso.ofLanguageEquiv` identify `G ≃g H` with equivalences of `G.structure` and `H.structure`, and `SimpleGraph.Embedding.toLanguageEmbedding` and `SimpleGraph.Embedding.ofLanguageEmbedding` do the same for induced embeddings `G ↪g H`. Use the converses to turn a model-theoretic back-and-forth result into a graph map, as `nonempty_iso_radoGraph` does. `SimpleGraph.Iso.realize_formula_iff` and `SimpleGraph.Iso.realize_boundedFormula_iff` move formulas with free variables along an isomorphism `e`, with the assignment composed with `e`, and `SimpleGraph.Iso.realize_sentence_iff` moves sentences. Each side is stated with its own `letI := G.structure`, so rewrite with these lemmas before introducing a structure instance. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Foundations/ModelTheory/Graph.lean) transfer "`x` has a neighbour" along the reversal of a three-vertex path and check the round trips.

[Locality/Reachability](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Locality/Reachability.lean) applies Gaifman locality to binary queries, following Libkin, §4.1, Figure 4.1 (p. 46) and the discussion after Definition 4.7 (p. 49). Directed graphs use the same language: `relStructure R` reads `adj` as an arbitrary binary relation `R` (`relMap_relStructure`), and its Gaifman graph is `SimpleGraph.fromRel R` (`gaifmanGraph_relStructure`). For the covering relation `⋖` of a preorder this is the Hasse diagram (`gaifmanGraph_covBy`). On `Fin N` that is `pathGraph N`, with distances `SimpleGraph.edist_pathGraph` from BallPath. So `(Fin N, ⋖)` is the successor relation. Translation is a neighborhood isomorphism, and `realize_swap_covBy` says that points `a < b` far from each other and from the ends satisfy the same rank-`n` formulas in either order. Mathlib's `le_iff_reflTransGen_covBy` and `lt_iff_transGen_covBy` identify the two closures of `⋖` with `≤` and `<`. This gives `card_le_of_realize_iff_reflTransGen` and `card_le_of_realize_iff_transGen`: a defining formula of rank `q` forces `N ≤ 2 · 3 ^ (q + 1)`. The unbounded forms are `not_exists_formula_reflTransGen` and `not_exists_formula_transGen`, over all finite structures of `Language.graph`, in any universe. For undirected reachability, `nbhdIso_sum` matches a far-apart pair in one copy of `G ⊕g G` with the corresponding pair across the copies. `edist_le_of_realize_iff_reachable` bounds by `3 ^ (q + 1)` the distance of connected vertices of `G` when a rank-`q` formula defines reachability in that one graph. `not_exists_formula_reachable` is the statement for all finite simple graphs. The exponential locality radius of Libkin, §4.3 (p. 54) is `distLE`: `distLE k` has rank `k` and says that the distance is at most `2 ^ k` (`realize_distLE`). It starts from `x = y ∨ E(x, y)`, where Libkin's `d_0` is `E(x, y)`. `exists_nbhdIso_realize_distLE` and `two_pow_le_of_gaifman_radius` show that any radius giving Gaifman locality for rank `n` is at least `2 ^ (n - 1)`. The exact Gaifman rank `2 ^ n - 1` (Exercises 4.10 and 4.11, pp. 63–64) is not formalized. [Operator/TransitiveClosure/Expressiveness](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/TransitiveClosure/Expressiveness.lean) combines this with `fo_definable`. One well-formed FO(TC) formula defines the reflexive–transitive closure of the edge relation (`TC.exists_formula_reflTransGen`), and it is equivalent to no first-order formula on finite structures (`TC.exists_formula_not_fo_equivalent`). This is FO ⊊ FO(TC) on unordered finite graphs. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/FOLocalityReachability.lean) include rank-`1` instances. They also check that the rank-`0` formula `distLE 0` defines `≤` on `Fin 2` but not on `Fin 3`, and that Libkin's radius satisfies the hypothesis of `two_pow_le_of_gaifman_radius`.

Pitfalls. The Hanf statements assume `M` nonempty: two empty structures that disagree on a nullary relation are Hanf-equivalent at every radius, but they disagree on a sentence of rank `0`. The Gaifman forms have no such assumption. `IsNbhdIso.symm` also needs a nonempty domain, because the inverse is built with `Function.invFunOn`. `gaifmanGraph`, `ball`, `NbhdIso` and `HanfEquiv` are not exposed, so in other modules build and take them apart with `gaifmanGraph_adj`, `mem_ball`, `nbhdIso_iff` and `hanfEquiv_iff`. Likewise `relStructure` and `distLE` are not exposed: use `relMap_relStructure`, `realize_distLE_zero`, `realize_distLE_succ` and `realize_distLE`. Gaifman's normal form theorem and the finite-variable pebble games are not formalized.

## Color refinement

[ColorRefinement](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/ColorRefinement.lean) formalizes color refinement (the 1-dimensional Weisfeiler–Leman algorithm) on a `LocallyFinite` simple graph `G` from an initial coloring `χ₀ : V → α`. `refinementColoring G χ₀ n` is the coloring after `n` rounds: each round recolors `v` by `(χ v, (G.neighborFinset v).val.map χ)`. Its colors live in `RefinementColor α n`, which depends only on `α` and `n`, so colorings and color histograms (`colorHistogram G χ₀ n`, the multiset of the vertex colors) of different graphs are directly comparable. Partitions are setoids: a coloring `χ` gives `Setoid.ker χ`, and `Cslib.SetPartition.blocks` counts its classes. `IsEquitable G χ` says that vertices of the same color have the same multiset of neighbor colors; `IsEquitablePartition G r` is the classical neighbor-count definition for a setoid, with counts in `ℕ∞` (`Set.encard`), and `isEquitablePartition_ker_iff` relates the two. It is unrelated to Mathlib's `Finpartition.IsEquipartition`, which is about block sizes.

The partitions of successive rounds refine each other, and the number of classes increases strictly while the partition changes (`blocks_ker_refinementColoring_lt_succ`). So it is stable from round `|V| - k₀` on, where `k₀` is the number of initial classes (`ker_refinementColoring_eq`). `stableColoring G χ₀` is the coloring after `|V|` rounds, and `isGreatest_ker_stableColoring` says that its partition is the coarsest equitable partition refining `χ₀`. A graph isomorphism that preserves the initial colors preserves the colors of every round (`Iso.refinementColoring_apply`), so different histograms in some round rule out an isomorphism (`not_exists_iso_of_colorHistogram_ne`, and `isEmpty_iso_of_colorHistogram_ne` for uncolored graphs). This is the soundness of the color refinement isomorphism test. `forall_colorHistogram_eq_iff` reduces agreement in all rounds to round `|V| + |W|`, by running refinement on the disjoint union (`refinementColoring_sum_inl`, `colorHistogram_sum`). `IsRegularOfDegree.colorHistogram_eq` shows that the test is incomplete: `d`-regular graphs of the same order are never separated. The source, Grohe, Kersting, Mladenov and Selman (ESA 2014, arXiv:1307.5697v2, Section 1 (Introduction), paragraph "Colour Refinement on Matrices"), works with a matrix `A ∈ ℝ^{V×W}`, a weighted bipartite graph with rows `V` and columns `W`, from the trivial partition. It states stabilization within `|V| + |W|` rounds, the number of vertices of that one bipartite graph. The graph statements here, with an arbitrary initial coloring and the `|V| - k₀` bound, are the standard graph form. The `|V| + |W|` of `forall_colorHistogram_eq_iff` is the order of the disjoint union of two graphs and is unrelated to the source's bound. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/ColorRefinement.lean) separate the path `P₄` from the star `K₁,₃` in one round, show that `C₆` and `C₃ ⊕ C₃` have equal histograms in every round but are not isomorphic, and compute equitable partitions and stable colorings of `P₃` and `C₆`, including the exact stable partition of `P₃`.

Pitfalls. The graphs need `LocallyFinite`, not `DecidableRel G.Adj`: Mathlib gives `G ⊕g H` a `LocallyFinite` instance but no decidable adjacency. The definitions are not exposed, so unfold them with `refineColoring_apply`, `refinementColoring_succ`, `stableColoring_eq`, `colorHistogram_eq`, `mem_colorHistogram` and `isEquitable_iff`. Only `RefinementColor` is exposed, so that a color of round `n + 1` is a pair. `decide` does not evaluate refinement colors in other modules, because `refineColoring`/`refinementColoring` are not exposed; compare degrees through `card_refineColoring_snd` instead, as the tests do. Not yet formalized: the correspondence between equitable partitions and fractional automorphisms and isomorphisms (Tinhofer; Ramana, Scheinerman and Ullman), the `O((n + m) log n)` algorithm, `k`-dimensional Weisfeiler–Leman, the Immerman–Lander correspondence with the counting logic C², and Cai–Fürer–Immerman graphs.

## Trakhtenbrot's theorem

Trakhtenbrot's theorem is stated for a separate flat syntax, [RawFormula](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Raw/Basic.lean), rather than Mathlib's `BoundedFormula`, because Mathlib has no `Primcodable` instance for its first-order syntax. The results are transferred to Mathlib's `L.Sentence` at the end of this section. Variables and relation symbols are natural numbers, `rel r args` applies symbol `r` to a variable list (the arity is the list length), and a formula is interpreted in a type `M` by `I : ℕ → List M → Prop` and an assignment `v : ℕ → M`. `FinSat φ` means that `φ` holds in some finite nonempty type under some interpretation and assignment, and `FinValid φ` that it holds in all of them (free variables are thus closed existentially, respectively universally). `RawFormula` is `Denumerable` through `Nat.pair`, so all statements use Mathlib's plain computability vocabulary: `ComputablePred` and `REPred` at the induced `Primcodable` instance.

The easy half is [rePred_finSat](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Raw/FinSat.lean): search over finite witnesses (a size, a list of true atoms, an assignment for the variables below `varBound`), whose satisfaction is primitive recursive. [Undecidability](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Trakhtenbrot/Undecidability.lean) proves `not_computablePred_finSat` (Trakhtenbrot's theorem), `not_rePred_finValid` and `not_rePred_not_finSat`, and two incompleteness corollaries: every r.e. set of finitely valid sentences misses one (`exists_finValid_not_of_rePred`), and a proof system with a decidable proof relation that proves only finitely valid sentences leaves one unprovable (`exists_finValid_unprovable`). The reduction is from Mathlib's halting problem `ComputablePred.halting_problem`: `eval_dom_iff_finSat` says that a code `c` halts on input `0` iff `haltSentence c` has a finite model, and `primrec_haltSentence` says that the reduction is primitive recursive. The sentence has two parts. The [arithmetic axioms](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Trakhtenbrot/Arithmetic.lean) describe order, zero, successor and the `Nat.pair` graph as **relations**, since these are not total functions on a finite initial segment. `arithModel_faithful` shows that every finite model is isomorphic to a standard `Fin N`, and this is where finiteness is used. The [evaln axioms](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Trakhtenbrot/Evaln.lean) unfold `Code.evaln` one step for every code up to `c`, with one ternary graph symbol per code. Only the direction "graph ⊆ true facts" is axiomatized, and `evaln_of_model` shows that it suffices once the arithmetic is standard. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Trakhtenbrot.lean) include an axiom of infinity that holds in `ℕ` but has no finite model, and the reduction sentences of a halting and a diverging code.

Relation symbols in `haltSentence c` have arity at most three, but the sentence uses the symbols up to `encode c + 4`, so `not_computablePred_finSat` is about the union of all finite relational vocabularies. [Binary](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Trakhtenbrot/Binary.lean) proves Libkin's form (Theorem 9.2 and Corollary 9.3). For every vocabulary `σ : Set (ℕ × ℕ)` of pairs (symbol, arity) with `(s, 2) ∈ σ`, no computable `f : RawFormula → Bool` is correct on all sentences over `σ` (`not_exists_computable_finSat_of_mem`), and no r.e. predicate agrees with `FinValid` on them (`not_exists_rePred_finValid_of_mem`). These statements are strictly stronger than the all-formula ones, since the decider only has to be correct on sentences over `σ`. [Sentence](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Raw/Sentence.lean) defines `freeVars`, `IsSentence` (decidable), `IsSentenceOver σ φ` and the existential closure `exClosure`. The primitive recursive reduction `binarize s` has three steps. First, [uniformize](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Raw/Uniformize.lean) merges all symbols into symbol `0`, coding a symbol `r` of arity `k` as arity `Nat.pair r k + 1` padded with a fresh variable. Second, [toGraph](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Raw/Graph.lean) interprets the result in one binary relation. The original elements are the loops, and a tuple is a chain of non-loop vertices that point to its coordinates (`isTupleGadget_tupleGraph_iff`). It uses the generic syntactic [interpretation](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Raw/Interpret.lean) `interpret D θ` (relativize quantifiers to `D`, replace atoms by `θ`). This is a primitive recursive map on raw syntax. The Mathlib-syntax `Cslib.Logic.FO.Interpretation` also pulls formulas back (`onFormula`), but that pullback is noncomputable. Third, `exClosure` closes the formula and `rename [s]` renames the symbol. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/TrakhtenbrotBinary.lean) include sentencehood by `decide`, a tuple gadget, and the reduction of the axiom of infinity.

[Trakhtenbrot/Sentence](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Trakhtenbrot/Sentence.lean) states the same results for Mathlib's `L.Sentence`, for a relational language with finitely many relation symbols (`[L.IsRelational] [Encodable (Σ l, L.Relations l)] [Finite (Σ l, L.Relations l)]`). `Sentence.FinSat φ` means that `M ⊨ φ` for some finite nonempty `M : Type` with an `L.Structure`, and `Sentence.FinValid φ` that `M ⊨ φ` for all of them. `Sentence.rePred_finSat` and `Sentence.rePred_not_finValid` need no binary symbol; for a binary symbol `R : L.Relations 2`, `Sentence.not_computablePred_finSat R`, `Sentence.not_rePred_finValid R`, `Sentence.not_rePred_not_finSat R`, `Sentence.not_computablePred_finValid R`, `Sentence.exists_finValid_not_of_rePred R` and `Sentence.exists_finValid_unprovable R` are Libkin's Theorem 9.2 and Corollary 9.3. The coding is in [Raw/BoundedFormula](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Raw/BoundedFormula.lean). `BoundedFormula.toRaw` renames variables by parity (the bound variable `&i` is `2 * i`, a free variable `a` is `2 * encode a + 1`, and the `∀` at depth `k` binds `2 * k`) and a symbol `R` of arity `l` by `encode ⟨l, R⟩`. `BoundedFormula.fromRaw` is its structural partial inverse, and `isSome_fromRaw` describes its domain with the primitive recursive scoping predicate `RawFormula.WellScoped` of [Raw/Levels](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Raw/Levels.lean). `BoundedFormula.primcodableRaw` codes `φ` by the code of `toRaw φ`; the constructors and `toRaw`, `fromRaw` are primitive recursive for it (`primrec_imp`, `primrec_all`, `primrec_rel`, `primrec_toRaw`, `primrec_fromRaw`, …), and two `Encodable` instances on the symbols give primitive recursively equivalent codings (`primrec_id_primcodableRaw`). `realize_toRaw` says that the translation preserves meaning, so `Sentence.finSat_iff_toRaw` and `Sentence.finValid_iff_toRaw` transfer the r.e. half. For the undecidable half, `Sentence.binarize R ψ` reduces a raw formula: `RawFormula.binarize` makes it a raw sentence over the binary symbol `encode ⟨2, R⟩`, `RawFormula.levels` renames its variables into the level names that `toRaw` produces, and `fromRaw 0` decodes the result, which always succeeds (`Sentence.toRaw_binarize`). The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/TrakhtenbrotSentence.lean) work in `Language.graph`: a computed raw formula and its decoding, rejection of ill-scoped codes, a level-named sentence that decodes, a sentence that is finitely satisfiable but not finitely valid on both sides of the translation, the reduction of the axiom of infinity, and the coding induced by a second symbol encoding.

Scope. The encoded computation is `Code.evaln` rather than a Turing machine run. The `L.Sentence` results need a relational language with finitely many relation symbols, one of them binary: languages with function symbols, infinite vocabularies and vocabularies whose only non-unary symbols have arity at least three are not covered. Mathlib may later give bounded formulas its own coding (a TODO in `Mathlib/ModelTheory/Encoding.lean`), so `primcodableRaw` is a definition with a scoped instance rather than a global instance, which could then form an instance diamond.

To use the results, import `CslibFrontier.Logics.FirstOrder.Trakhtenbrot.Undecidability`, or `CslibFrontier.Logics.FirstOrder.Trakhtenbrot.Binary` for sentences over a fixed vocabulary. For Mathlib sentences, import `CslibFrontier.Logics.FirstOrder.Trakhtenbrot.Sentence` and write `open scoped FirstOrder.Language.BoundedFormula.RawCoding`; without it, `ComputablePred` and `REPred` on `L.Sentence` find no `Primcodable` instance. The theorems take the symbol encoding from the instance in scope; to compare two encodings, pass `primcodableRaw` with explicit instances, as in the checked examples. Mathlib's `realize_*` lemmas reduce `M ⊨ φ` to `RelMap` facts, but `simp` does not unfold a named structure inside an instance argument, so close such goals with `exact` by unfolding. To show that another predicate `p` is undecidable, give a computable `f : RawFormula → α` with `FinSat φ ↔ p (f φ)`, and then apply `not_computablePred_finSat` to `(hp.comp hf).of_eq`. If `f` is only correct on sentences over `{(s, 2)}`, compose it with `binarize s` first, or apply `not_exists_computable_finSat_of_mem`. The name `RawFormula` is also used by the FO(LFP) syntax `Cslib.Logic.FO.LFP.RawFormula`, so qualify it when both namespaces are open.

## The 0-1 law for graphs

The [0-1 law](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw.lean) (Glebskiĭ–Kogan–Liogon'kiĭ–Talanov; Fagin) is stated for the uniform random labelled graph `randomGraph n`, a `PMF` on `SimpleGraph (Fin n)`. `randomGraphProb n P` is the probability of a graph property, that is, the fraction of labelled graphs on `Fin n` with `P`, and `Satisfies G φ` interprets a sentence of Mathlib's `Language.graph` in `G.structure`. `zero_one_law φ` says that `randomGraphProb n (fun G => Satisfies G φ)` tends to `0` or to `1`. `tendsto_randomGraphProb_one_iff` identifies the almost-sure sentences with the consequences of `randomGraphTheory`, the simple-graph axioms together with all [extension axioms](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/ExtensionAxiom.lean).

The proof follows Fagin. An explicit union bound `n (n - 1) ⋯ (n - k + 1) · (1 - 2⁻ᵏ) ^ (n - k)` on the failure probability of each extension axiom is proved by counting in [ExtensionProbability](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/ExtensionProbability.lean). Compactness then gives satisfiability. The back-and-forth step is Mathlib's `IsExtensionPair`, and ℵ₀-categoricity uses `equiv_between_cg`; Mathlib's Łoś–Vaught test `Cardinal.Categorical.isComplete` gives completeness. Non-definability corollaries are stated as "no sentence agrees with `P` on all large `Fin n` graphs": an even number of vertices (also as `¬ FO.Definable`) and an even number of edges, whose probability is exactly `1/2` for `n ≥ 2`. This law is for simple graphs with edge probability `1/2`; the law for every constant edge probability `0 < p < 1` and the law for uniform structures over other relational vocabularies are described below. Unlabelled graphs are not formalized, and no 0-1 law is proved for edge probabilities `p(n)` that depend on `n`; the binomial random graph `G(n, p)` itself and its triangle threshold are described in [random graphs and thresholds](metacomplexity-and-methods.md#random-graphs-and-thresholds).

To show with the 0-1 law that a graph property `P n` (one for each number `n` of vertices) is not first-order definable, show that `randomGraphProb n (P n)` tends neither to `0` nor to `1`, then apply `not_exists_sentence_of_not_tendsto`. An involution of the graphs on `Fin n` that exchanges `P` and `¬P` gives probability exactly `1/2` through `randomGraphProb_eq_half_of_involutive`; toggling one edge is the involution used for an even number of edges ([SimpleGraph.EvenEdges](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/EvenEdges.lean)). One pitfall: `Satisfies G φ` is realization in `G.structure`, which is a definition and not an instance. To use Mathlib's `realize_*` lemmas on it, either rewrite with the characterizations `satisfies_not` and `satisfies_extensionAxiom_iff`, or bring the structure into scope first with `let := G.structure`, as the proofs of those lemmas do. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/ZeroOneLaw.lean) cover exact probabilities `1/2`, an extension axiom holding in the triangle and failing in the empty graph on two vertices, the transfer theorem applied to the sentence "there are no edges", and non-definability of an odd number of vertices.

The [Rado graph](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Rado.lean) `SimpleGraph.radoGraph` is the countable random graph on `ℕ`, given by Ackermann's bit relation: distinct `i` and `j` are adjacent when bit `i` of `j` or bit `j` of `i` is set. `radoGraph_adj` restates adjacency with `Nat.testBit`, and small cases are decidable. `radoGraph_exists_adj` is the extension property for disjoint finite sets `A` and `B`. [ZeroOneLaw/Rado](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/Rado.lean) connects it to the 0-1 law. `tendsto_randomGraphProb_one_iff_radoGraph` says that a sentence holds almost surely in `G(n, 1/2)` exactly when `Satisfies radoGraph φ`, and `tendsto_randomGraphProb_zero_iff_radoGraph` says that it holds almost never otherwise. So to compute the limit probability of a concrete sentence, check it in the Rado graph: only adjacency is decidable, not `Satisfies`, so after `let := radoGraph.structure` unfold the sentence with the `realize_*` lemmas and `decide` the resulting adjacencies, as the triangle test does. `nonempty_iso_radoGraph` (every countable graph with all extension properties is isomorphic to `radoGraph`), `nonempty_embedding_radoGraph` (every countable graph is an induced subgraph) and `nonempty_compl_iso_radoGraph` (self-complementarity) are the classical structure theorems. `satisfies_extensionAxiom_iff_exists_adj` translates between the extension axioms and the finite-set form. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Rado.lean) include a triangle on `0, 1, 3`, which gives the almost-sure existence of triangles.

The [0-1 law for `G(n, p)`](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/BinomialRandomGraph.lean) holds for every constant edge probability `0 < p < 1`, for Mathlib's binomial random graph `G(Fin n, p)` (`SimpleGraph.binomialRandom`, a measure; see [random graphs and thresholds](metacomplexity-and-methods.md#random-graphs-and-thresholds)). Probabilities are written `G(Fin n, p).real {G | Satisfies G φ}`, and the hypotheses are `0 < (p : ℝ)` and `(p : ℝ) < 1`. `binomialRandom_zero_one_law` gives the limit `0` or `1`; `tendsto_binomialRandom_real_one_iff` and `tendsto_binomialRandom_real_zero_iff` characterize it by `randomGraphTheory`, and the `_radoGraph` forms by the Rado graph, so concrete sentences are decided in the Rado graph exactly as for `G(n, 1/2)`. `tendsto_binomialRandom_real_one_iff_tendsto_randomGraphProb` states that the almost-sure sentences of `G(n, p)` are those of `G(n, 1/2)`, so the limit does not depend on `p`, and `not_exists_sentence_of_not_tendsto_binomialRandom` is the non-definability transfer. Only the probabilistic step is new. [Extension](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Probability/Combinatorics/BinomialRandomGraph/Extension.lean) computes the probability `(1 - ∏ i, (if s i then p else 1 - p)) ^ (|V| - k)` that no vertex outside a fixed tuple of `k` distinct vertices realizes the pattern `s` (`binomialRandom_real_forall_not_witness`), from the edge-independence law `binomialRandom_real_edgePattern_of_injective`. The union bound `n (n - 1) ⋯ (n - k + 1) · (1 - min (p, 1 - p) ^ k) ^ (n - k)` is `binomialRandom_real_not_extensionAxiom_le`. Completeness of `randomGraphTheory` and the transfer are reused through `binomialRandomStructure p n`, the pushforward of `G(Fin n, p)` to graph structures. `binomialRandom_real_eq_randomGraphProb` shows that `G(Fin n, p)` at `p = 1/2` is `randomGraph n`. One pitfall: the hypothesis `0 < p < 1` cannot be dropped. `G(n, 0)` is the empty graph and `G(n, 1)` the complete graph, and in them an extension axiom that holds in the Rado graph has probability `0` for every `n ≥ 1`. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/BinomialZeroOneLaw.lean) show this at `p = 0` and `p = 1`, decide a triangle and "no edges" at `p = 1/3` in the Rado graph, and check the bridge at `p = 1/2` on `Fin 2`.

The [0-1 law for finite relational vocabularies](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/Relational.lean) (Glebskiĭ–Kogan–Liogon'kiĭ–Talanov; Fagin) holds for every relational language `L` with finitely many relation symbols, none of them nullary (`[L.IsRelational] [Finite (Σ l, L.Relations l)] [IsEmpty (L.Relations 0)]`). [UniformStructure](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/UniformStructure.lean) defines `uniformStructure n`, a `PMF` on `L.Structure (Fin n)` under which every atomic fact holds independently with probability `1/2`. Atomic facts are defined in [Atom](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/Atom.lean) as `Cslib.Logic.FO.Atom`. `structProb_uniformStructure_eq_card` shows that it is the uniform distribution on structures, Fagin's `μₙ`. `Relational.zero_one_law φ` says that `sentenceProb (uniformStructure n) φ` tends to `0` or to `1`. `Relational.tendsto_sentenceProb_one_iff` identifies the almost-sure sentences with the consequences of `extensionTheory L`, whose [extension axioms](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/Relational/ExtensionAxiom.lean) prescribe the atomic type of a fresh element over `k` given ones. The hypothesis on nullary symbols is not needed for satisfiability (`isSatisfiable_extensionTheory`), but categoricity, completeness and the law fail without it: `not_zero_one_law_of_nullary` shows that a nullary symbol has probability exactly `1/2` for every `n`. The proof follows Fagin.

The measure-independent part of the proof is in [Transfer](../../CslibFrontier/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/Transfer.lean), for any sequence `μ : ∀ n, PMF (L.Structure (Fin n))`. If every sentence of `T` holds with probability tending to `1`, then `T` is satisfiable (`isSatisfiable_of_tendsto_sentenceProb`) and every consequence of `T` holds with probability tending to `1` (`tendsto_sentenceProb_of_models`). If `T` is also complete, `zero_one_law_of_isComplete` gives a 0-1 law and `not_exists_sentence_of_not_tendsto` gives non-definability. A new 0-1 law therefore needs only an almost-sure theory and its completeness. The union bound `n (n - 1) ⋯ (n - k + 1) · (1 - 2⁻ᵃ) ^ (n - k)` uses the counting lemma `Nat.card_subtype_comp_injective_div` (restrictions of uniform functions along injections are uniform) and the limit `tendsto_descFactorial_mul_pow_sub`, which the graph law shares.

One pitfall: for `L = Language.graph`, `uniformStructure` is uniform on directed graphs with loops, not on simple graphs. The irreflexivity axiom has probability `2⁻ⁿ`, and `extensionTheory Language.graph` refutes it, so the `G(n, 1/2)` law above is not a special case. `uniformStructure` and `sentenceProb` are not exposed. Compute with `sentenceProb_def`, `structProb_uniformStructure` and `relMap_atomStructure`, and bring `atomStructure y` into scope with `let` before using Mathlib's `realize_*` lemmas. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/RelationalZeroOneLaw.lean) instantiate the law at `Language.graph`, compute the irreflexivity probability `2⁻ⁿ`, check an extension axiom in a concrete one-element structure and its failure in every one-element structure, and give a vocabulary with a nullary symbol for which the law fails.

## Known Limitations

This survey checks the model definitions and selected endpoint routes rather than every formula-level proof. Source ownership is mixed: Mathlib supplies the underlying first-order logic, while local modules supply finite encodings and complexity-specific operators/reductions. Cardinality, input length and tuple dimension are separate parameters; quoted complexity claims should preserve them and retain the positivity/order/encoding hypotheses.
