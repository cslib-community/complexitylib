# Finite-model logic and native complexity

The descriptive-complexity development uses Mathlib first-order syntax and semantics, then adds finite encodings, interpretations and scoped relation operators. The main bridges concern **data complexity**: a sentence is fixed while its finite input structure varies. The number of variables and nesting depth of that sentence may affect the polynomial or logspace constants. [complexitylib][complexitylib]

## Structures, encodings and formulas

The [closeAll](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Basic.lean#L69) layer supplies finite-variable closure and atomic/order combinators over `FirstOrder.Language`. Finite relational vocabularies enumerate relation symbols of their declared arities. The [Vocabulary](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Encoding.lean#L89) representation is universe size together with a list of relation truth tables. Cardinality is unary; relation tables are framed and their order follows the chosen finite vocabulary enumeration. Tuple coordinate zero is the least significant coordinate of the table index.

The decoder uses a canonical universe `Fin n`. A table for an arity-k relation must contain exactly `n^k` entries, and the relation list must have exactly the expected number of tables. Nullary relations still have one bit when n is zero. Missing raw values may have fallback semantics in the decoder, but the encoded query language requires well-formedness and therefore rejects malformed tables. This separation permits total parsers without weakening the language contract.

The [Structure](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/Query.lean#L48) variant uses canonical `Fin n` with n at least two and fixed numeric symbols for zero, one, maximum, order and BIT. These numeric relations/functions are determined by n rather than supplied as arbitrary input tables. Their BIT convention is least-significant-bit first. The n>=2 restriction avoids degenerate constants; an endpoint over every finite structure must handle the smaller universes separately.

## Interpretations and reductions

[Interpretation](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Interpretation.lean#L98) defines a target structure on a definable subset of source tuples. Target relation symbols are interpreted by formulas and equality is literal tuple equality. Formula pullback relativizes quantifiers to the interpreted domain, proves satisfaction preservation and supports composition. There is no implicit quotient of tuples by a definable equivalence relation.

[TaggedInterpretation](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Interpretation/Tagged.lean#L105) extends the representation to a finite disjoint union of tuple domains, with potentially different dimensions. This makes multi-sorted gadget reductions readable. Equality patterns encode tags into ordinary tuples when sufficient distinct domain elements are available; tiny universes and copy-invariant targets require the explicit treatment supplied by the relevant bridge. SAT, coloring and independent-set reductions are clients.

The projection API is stricter than arbitrary quantifier-free interpretation. [InputLiteral](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ProjectionFormula.lean#L70) has a numeric-case syntax whose leaves are a single signed input atom or a Boolean constant. `Projection` combines that syntax with domain/numeric interpretation. This preserves the intended distinction between a literal selector and a formula such as the conjunction of two independent input relations. Exact classical projection conventions additionally depend on the numeric vocabulary and domain construction. Identify those choices when using the API; arbitrary QF interpretations are not all projections.

Ordered reductions introduce a second subtlety. [DomainInterpretation](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/DomainInterpretation.lean#L81) selects a definable subset of tuples and ranks it lexicographically to obtain a canonical target universe. The rank's BIT relation is not assumed automatically first-order definable. A [numeric certificate](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/DomainInterpretation/Certificate.lean) supplies formulas and their correctness when a reduction needs them. [FullTupleInterpretation](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/FullTuple.lean#L77) specializes to the entire positive-dimensional tuple universe, where canonical arithmetic/order structure is constructed without an arbitrary subset-ranking assumption.

## Scoped operators: LFP and transitive closure

[RelVar](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/Basic.lean#L48) extends syntax with scoped relation variables and general binder shapes. This factors out relabeling, environments, polarity and satisfaction reasoning for operators. The least-fixed-point development deliberately separates raw formulas from checked positive formulas.

The checked syntax [Formula](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/LeastFixedPoint.lean#L72) enforces positivity in the recursively bound relation at every nested binder. Positivity gives monotonicity, so the semantic operator has a least fixed point; over a universe of size n and an arity-k relation, iteration from the empty relation stabilizes by `n^k`. Raw unchecked syntax still has total semantics via the intersection of prefixed points, but that expression need not satisfy a nonmonotone operator's fixed-point equation. The checked/raw distinction is mathematically essential, not just a parser convenience.

[shape](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/TransitiveClosure.lean#L77) uses reflexive transitive closure of a relation on tuples. The binder has two tuples of the same width and no new relation variables; width zero is permitted. This module provides a closure/definability API. [TC.Positive](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/TransitiveClosure/Positive.lean) is a polarity check: a formula passes when every closure occurs under an even number of negations. The formula body of a closure is itself checked at polarity `true`. This is distinct from `Operator.Formula.checkPolarity`, which concerns relation variables. [PosTCDefinable](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/TransitiveClosure.lean) is the corresponding ordered-query notion. Only one direction of FO(TC) = NL is proved: FO(TC) ⊆ NL, at every polarity (next section). Do not cite the full capture theorem.

## Fagin's theorem

[Sentence](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/SecondOrder/Existential.lean#L124) is a finite collection of existential relation variables, each with an arity, followed by a first-order matrix over the sum vocabulary. The headline equivalence says that a class of finite relational structures is ESO-definable exactly when it is isomorphism-closed and its well-formed encoded query language lies in the library's native NP class. The vocabulary enumeration is fixed and no arbitrary input function symbols are admitted by this encoding theorem.

The reverse direction begins from the native strict verifier formulation. A fixed tuple dimension bounds the polynomial clock and relation tables; an ESO sentence describes a valid computation tableau and guesses an order. Empty and singleton universes are handled by finite truth-table cases rather than an invalid assumption that a positive tuple power always has enough distinct values. The forward direction checks a guessed relation interpretation and the first-order matrix within polynomial bounds. See [Fagin abstract](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/abstracts/ComplexityAbstracts/FaginAbstract.lean), [checked bridge](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/abstracts/ComplexityAbstracts/FaginBridge.lean) and [converse implementation](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Fagin/Converse.lean).

The source documentation maps its statement to Fagin's finite-model result and textbook formulations by Immerman and Papadimitriou. This author traced the abstract's encoding and quantifiers to the library statement, including malformed, empty-universe and nullary-relation behavior. The original paper correspondence has not been independently exhaustively audited in this research pass.

## MSO over words and the Büchi–Elgot–Trakhtenbrot theorem

ESO allows only one block of existential relation quantifiers, so MSO over words has its own small syntax. [Formula](../../Complexity/Logics/SecondOrder/WordMSO/Basic.lean) `α m n` has `m` free position variables and `n` free set variables, indexed by `Fin m` and `Fin n`. Atoms are `letter a x` (P_a(x)), `lt x y` and `mem x X`. The connectives are `falsum` and `imp`, and the existential quantifiers `ex` and `exSet` bind the *last* index of their sort, as Mathlib's `BoundedFormula` does, so `Realize` extends assignments with `Fin.snoc`. The usual derived connectives, finite `iInf`/`iSup` conjunctions and disjunctions, and the position predicates `first`, `last` and `succ` come with `@[simp]` `realize_*` lemmas. A `Sentence α` (no free variables) defines `Sentence.language`: the words `w` such that the structure on positions `Fin w.length` with letters `w[i]` satisfies it. `Definable` means MSO-definable. `FODefinable` asks for a sentence with `IsFO` (no set quantifiers), so it is FO[<] with letter predicates and no BIT; `isFO_*` simp lemmas compute `IsFO`.

[Definable.isRegular](../../Complexity/Logics/SecondOrder/WordMSO/ToRegular.lean) holds over every alphabet, finite or not. Each formula is read over `ExtLetter α m n`, which adds one bit per free variable, and `isRegular_extLanguage` proves by induction that its language is regular. Regularity of the atoms comes from positional languages; implication uses complement and union, and both quantifiers use the image along the letter map that forgets one bit. The image step needs closure under letter maps, which cslib lacks. [IsRegular.map](../../Complexity/Computability/Languages/RegularLanguage.lean) supplies it, together with the positional languages, at the mirrored cslib path and marked `-- upstream: candidate`; it is proved through the NFA characterization `IsRegular.iff_nfa` and [NA.FinAcc.map](../../Complexity/Computability/Automata/NA/Map.lean). The converse [definable_of_isRegular](../../Complexity/Logics/SecondOrder/WordMSO/OfRegular.lean) needs a finite alphabet, because a sentence mentions only finitely many letters. Its sentence `dfaSentence M` guesses one set per state through `Fintype.equivFin` and treats the empty word as a separate conjunct. `definable_iff_isRegular` combines the two directions. `evenSentence` defines even length over every alphabet. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/WordMSO.lean) evaluate concrete sentences, the parity DFA and an infinite-alphabet instance.

[wordLanguage](../../Complexity/Logics/SecondOrder/WordMSO/FirstOrder.lean) is the word vocabulary as a Mathlib `FirstOrder.Language`, with symbols `wordLanguage.ltSymb` and `wordLanguage.letterSymb a`, interpreted on `Fin w.length` by `wordStructure w`. `wordStructure w` is not an instance, because `Fin w.length` does not determine `w`, so bring it into scope with `let := wordStructure w`. Build formulas from the typed symbols rather than the raw `WordRel` constructors, whose type is not syntactically `(wordLanguage α).Relations n`, so `simp` and `rw` would fail on them. With the typed symbols, Mathlib's `realize_rel₁`/`realize_rel₂` apply, and the simp lemmas `wordStructure.relMap_ltSymb` and `wordStructure.relMap_letterSymb` evaluate the relations; `mem_someTrue` in the [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/WordMSO.lean) is a worked example. `foDefinable_iff_exists_sentence` identifies `FODefinable` with definability by a Mathlib sentence (`WordFO.language`); `WordFO.toFormula` and `Formula.toWordFO` are the two translations. [not_foDefinable_even](../../Complexity/Logics/SecondOrder/WordMSO/Separation.lean) proves FO[<] ⊊ MSO over every nonempty alphabet (over one letter this is `(aa)*`: the first-order half of Libkin's Corollary 7.27; not star-free also needs star-free ⇒ FO, [#3075](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/issues/3075)). On a word whose letters all equal `c`, `Formula.toOrder c` turns a first-order word formula into a sentence of Mathlib's order language. The Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé bound `realize_iff_of_two_pow_le_card` from [FiniteLinearOrder](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/FiniteLinearOrder.lean) then equates the words of lengths `2 ^ k` and `2 ^ k + 1`. `exists_definable_not_foDefinable`, `exists_isRegular_not_foDefinable` and `not_exists_sentence_language_eq_even` are the packaged forms. Over the empty alphabet every language is first-order definable, which the tests check.

Pitfall: regularity here is Mathlib's `Language.IsRegular`. On `h : L.IsRegular`, dot notation resolves only to Mathlib's `_root_.Language.IsRegular.*` lemmas. Call cslib's closure lemmas by their full names (`Cslib.Language.IsRegular.mul`, `.iInf`, `.map`, ...). Also, `{w | ...}.IsRegular` elaborates the set-builder as a `Set`, so write `Language.IsRegular {w : List α | ...}`.

## FO model checking and Immerman–Vardi

Fixed FO formulas have a native logspace model checker on the stated table encoding. The implementation compiles formula evaluation through bounded-register counter programs and validates input shape. A separate circuit compilation expands quantifiers into AND/OR gates, giving constant depth and polynomial size in the universe parameter. That circuit result is an upper direction; it does not by itself claim DLOGTIME-uniform AC0 capture or charge serial encoding length as though it were one relation bit. See [native FO logspace](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/FirstOrder.lean) and [ordered FO circuits](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/FirstOrder/Ordered.lean).

For canonical ordered finite relational structures, [Immerman–Vardi](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/ImmermanVardi.lean) proves `lfpDefinable_iff_encodedLanguage_mem_P`. Positive finite fixed-point iteration supplies the forward native polynomial-time computation. The reverse direction encodes a native polynomial-time machine by a fixed-point tableau in a fixed tuple power. The theorem is a genuine bidirectional model bridge with explicit encoding, not only a semantic containment assumption.

[TCDefinable.encodedLanguage_mem_NL](../../Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/FirstOrder/TransitiveClosure.lean) is the easy inclusion FO(TC) ⊆ NL of Immerman's capture theorem. On the same `encStructure` encoding, it puts every query defined by an FO(TC) sentence in native `NL`, with closures at any polarity; `PosTCDefinable.encodedLanguage_mem_NL` is the positive special case (Immerman 1987, Theorem 3.1). Immerman's numeric vocabulary is `=`, successor, `0` and `max`; ours is `≤`, `BIT`, `0`, `1` and `max`. The decider is a single nondeterministic counter program. The [compiler](../../Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/FirstOrder/TransitiveClosure/Compile.lean) is indexed by polarity, so a negation flips the target polarity rather than complementing a nondeterministic computation. The count of negations restarts inside each closure body. A closure of width `k` at polarity `true` guesses a path one tuple at a time, using at most `n^k` steps. Each guessed edge is checked by a recursive call. A closure at polarity `false` runs the [inductive-counting block](../../Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/FirstOrder/TransitiveClosure/Counting.lean) of Immerman and Szelepcsényi on tuple codes below `n^k`; Immerman 1988, Corollary 3, states the source result NL = FO(positive TC) = FO(TC). Round by round it computes the number of tuples reachable within `i` steps. Each membership test guesses the previous round in increasing order, certifies each guessed tuple by a walk, and certifies edges and non-edges with the body compiled at both polarities. This is a separate in-compiler simulation; the machine-level `NL = coNL` in [complementation](../../Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/Complement.lean) is not reused, because native closure of `NL` under the needed compositions is not yet available. Every register stays at most `(N + 1)^(d + w)`, where `N` is the input length, `d` the maximal input arity and `w` the largest closure width. The converse, NL ⊆ FO(TC), still needs configuration-graph reachability under the ordered encoding. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/TransitiveClosureNL.lean) define s–t reachability from `zero` to `max` in an ordered graph and prove that both it and its complement, defined by a negated closure, lie in `NL`.

## Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé games and nondefinability

[quantifierRank](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/QuantifierRank.lean) measures how deeply the quantifiers of a Mathlib `BoundedFormula` nest. The LogSpace model checker's register depth satisfies [boundDepth_eq_add_quantifierRank](../../Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/FirstOrder/Formula.lean): it equals the number of bound variables in scope plus the quantifier rank. [DuplicatorWins](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/Basic.lean) is the `n`-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game played from a position: a pair of pebble tuples `a : Fin k → M`, `b : Fin k → N`, extended by `Fin.snoc` each round, which are exactly the variable assignments of formulas with `k` bound variables. Duplicator must keep every position a partial isomorphism (`PartialIso`: equality and every relation agree on the pebbles). The game, strategies (`duplicatorWins_iff_exists_strategy`; `Strategy` is exposed data that clients build by recursion and check with `Strategy.isWinning_zero`/`Strategy.isWinning_succ`), back-and-forth systems (`IsBackAndForth.duplicatorWins`) and invariance under isomorphism are available for any language and structures, finite or infinite.

The [Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/Hintikka.lean) has two directions with different hypotheses. `DuplicatorWins.realize_iff` (winning positions satisfy the same formulas of quantifier rank at most `n`) needs `L.IsRelational` and nothing else. The converse `duplicatorWins_of_forall_realize_iff` needs finitely many relation symbols, `[Finite (Σ l, L.Relations l)]`, and is proved with Hintikka formulas of rank-`n` types. `duplicatorWins_iff_forall_sentence` is the combined statement for sentences. `duplicatorWins_iff_forall_formula` and `DuplicatorWins.formula_realize_iff` state it for `L.Formula (Fin k)`, with the free variables naming the pebbles. The rank lemmas `quantifierRank_relabel` and `quantifierRank_toFormula` carry ranks across Mathlib's conversions between the two forms, and `realize_toFormula_relabel` carries truth. As a consequence there are only finitely many formulas of rank at most `n` in `k` variables up to equivalence (`exists_finite_quantifierRank_le_realize_iff`).

To show that a property of finite structures is not first-order definable, import the theorem module and, for each `n`, give two structures on which Duplicator wins `n` rounds, one with the property and one without. Then apply `DuplicatorWins.realize_iff` (or `duplicatorWins_iff_forall_sentence`) to a supposed defining sentence of rank `n`. Duplicator's wins are usually obtained by giving an invariant on positions and checking `IsBackAndForth`. For [finite linear orders](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/FiniteLinearOrder.lean), `duplicatorWins_iff_card` decides the game exactly: equal size, or both sizes at least `2 ^ n - 1` (the characterization in Kolaitis's survey chapter of *Finite Model Theory and Its Applications*). `not_exists_sentence_realize_iff_even` is the resulting nondefinability of EVEN. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/EhrenfeuchtFraisse.lean) cover a rank-`2` sentence separating `Fin 2` from `Fin 3`, the threshold cases, an ODD client, and explicit winning strategies built outside the defining module.

Nondefinability transfers along first-order interpretations. If an interpretation sends each structure of one class to a structure of another, then a defining sentence for a property of the second class pulls back (`Interpretation.realize_onSentence`) to a defining sentence for the corresponding property of the first. [DefinedGraph](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/DefinedGraph.lean) packages this route once for any graph property. A formula `δ` with two free variables defines a graph in every structure (`definedGraph`), interpreted by `graphInterp δ`, so graph sentences pull back (`realize_onSentence_graphInterp`). By `not_exists_sentence_of_even` (or `not_exists_sentence_of_odd`), if the graph defined by `δ` in large finite linear orders has a property exactly when the order has even (or odd) size, then the property is not first-order definable on finite graphs. The module also provides the order formulas used to define such graphs (`covByFormula`, `isBotFormula`, `twoAboveFormula`, ...). This is the reduction from EVEN that Libkin uses for connectivity (Corollary 3.19). [Connectivity](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/Connectivity.lean): the step-two graph (`stepTwoGraph`) of a linear order joins each element to the one two steps above it, the last element to the second, and the penultimate element to the first. It is a defined graph (`stepTwoGraph_eq_definedGraph`), and it is connected exactly when the order has an odd number of elements (`stepTwoGraph_connected_iff_odd`). So `not_exists_sentence_connected` (and `not_exists_sentence_connected_of_le_card`, for graphs with at least `t` vertices) says that no sentence of Mathlib's `Language.graph` defines connectivity on finite simple graphs, in any universe. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/FOConnectivity.lean) evaluate the step-two graph on `Fin 4`, `Fin 5` and `Bool` and pull back a concrete graph sentence. [Bipartiteness and acyclicity](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/GraphProperties.lean) (Libkin, Exercises 3.5(3) and 3.4) use the same transfer. On `Fin m` the cycle formula `cycleArcFormula` defines Mathlib's `cycleGraph m` (`definedGraph_cycleArcFormula_fin`), which is bipartite exactly when `m` is even; a step-two graph closed by one edge (`stepTwoTopBotArcFormula`) is acyclic exactly when the order has even size, for size at least `4`. This gives `not_exists_sentence_isBipartite` and `not_exists_sentence_isAcyclic`. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/FOGraphProperties.lean) include the size bound for acyclicity: on `Fin 3` the closed step-two graph is acyclic although `3` is odd.

Two pitfalls. The game does not support constants or function symbols, so a vocabulary with numeric constants must first replace them by their defining formulas. The order results concern Mathlib's pure order language, FO(≤), not the Immerman convention FO(≤, BIT) used by the ordered capture theorems above. With `BIT`, EVEN is definable (bit zero of `max`), so these results show that `BIT` adds expressive power; they say nothing about FO(≤, BIT). Statements about sentences take an arbitrary empty tuple `a : Fin 0 → M`. Do not rewrite with a lemma stated for `default`: at concrete types such as `Fin 3`, `default` may elaborate through a different `Inhabited` instance and the rewrite fails.

## Hanf and Gaifman locality

[Gaifman](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Locality/Gaifman.lean) defines the Gaifman graph of a structure (`gaifmanGraph L M`), which joins two distinct elements when they occur together in a tuple of some relation. Distances are Mathlib's `SimpleGraph.edist`, valued in `ℕ∞`. `ball L a r` is the set of elements at distance at most `r` from some entry of the tuple `a`. It is a closed ball around a tuple, unlike Mathlib's open `SimpleGraph.ball c r` (`edist < r`) around one vertex. For a simple graph `G`, `gaifmanGraph_structure` identifies the Gaifman graph of `G.structure` with `G`, so balls are graph balls. `IsNbhdIso L r a b h` says that `h : M → N` is an isomorphism of the radius-`r` neighborhoods of `a` and `b`: it is a bijection between the balls (`Set.BijOn`) that sends `a` to `b` and preserves and reflects every relation on tuples from the ball. `NbhdIso` states that such an `h` exists. Libkin's Lemma 4.8 is `IsNbhdIso.restrict` and `IsNbhdIso.mono` (restriction) and `IsNbhdIso.snoc` (combining the neighborhoods of points more than `2r + 1` apart).

[Hanf](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Locality/Hanf.lean) proves Hanf and Gaifman locality with Libkin's radii (Libkin, Theorem 4.12 and Corollary 4.13). `HanfEquiv L d a b` is Libkin's `(M, a) ⇆_d (N, b)`: a bijection `f : M ≃ N` such that adding `c` to `a` and `f c` to `b` gives isomorphic radius-`d` neighborhoods. Over a finite nonempty `M`, `HanfEquiv.duplicatorWins` turns Hanf-equivalence at radius `(3 ^ n - 1) / 2` into a win for Duplicator in the `n`-round game, for any language. For relational languages, `HanfEquiv.realize_iff` and `realize_sentence_iff_of_nbhdIso` give agreement on formulas and sentences of quantifier rank at most `n`. Gaifman locality is `NbhdIso.realize_iff` (and `NbhdIso.formula_realize_iff`): in a finite structure, tuples with isomorphic radius-`(3 ^ (n + 1) - 1) / 2` neighborhoods satisfy the same rank-`n` formulas. The key step is `hanfEquiv_of_nbhdIso` (Libkin, Lemma 4.9).

To show with Hanf's theorem that a property is not first-order definable, give for each `n` two finite structures, one with the property and one without, and a bijection that matches points with isomorphic radius-`(3 ^ n - 1) / 2` neighborhoods. Then apply `realize_sentence_iff_of_nbhdIso`. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/FOLocality.lean) do this at radius `1` for the 8-cycle and two disjoint 4-cycles, using an explicit local isomorphism at each vertex, and check that the two graphs are not isomorphic.

Pitfalls. The Hanf statements assume `M` nonempty: two empty structures that disagree on a nullary relation are Hanf-equivalent at every radius, but they disagree on a sentence of rank `0`. The Gaifman forms have no such assumption. `IsNbhdIso.symm` also needs a nonempty domain, because the inverse is built with `Function.invFunOn`. `gaifmanGraph`, `ball`, `NbhdIso` and `HanfEquiv` are not exposed, so in other modules build and take them apart with `gaifmanGraph_adj`, `mem_ball`, `nbhdIso_iff` and `hanfEquiv_iff`. Gaifman's normal form theorem and the finite-variable pebble games are not formalized.

## Trakhtenbrot's theorem

Trakhtenbrot's theorem is stated for a separate flat syntax, [RawFormula](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Raw/Basic.lean), rather than Mathlib's `BoundedFormula`, because Mathlib has no `Primcodable` instance for its first-order syntax. Variables and relation symbols are natural numbers, `rel r args` applies symbol `r` to a variable list (the arity is the list length), and a formula is interpreted in a type `M` by `I : ℕ → List M → Prop` and an assignment `v : ℕ → M`. `FinSat φ` means that `φ` holds in some finite nonempty type under some interpretation and assignment, and `FinValid φ` that it holds in all of them (free variables are thus closed existentially, respectively universally). `RawFormula` is `Denumerable` through `Nat.pair`, so all statements use Mathlib's plain computability vocabulary: `ComputablePred` and `REPred` at the induced `Primcodable` instance.

The easy half is [rePred_finSat](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Raw/FinSat.lean): search over finite witnesses (a size, a list of true atoms, an assignment for the variables below `varBound`), whose satisfaction is primitive recursive. [Undecidability](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Trakhtenbrot/Undecidability.lean) proves `not_computablePred_finSat` (Trakhtenbrot's theorem), `not_rePred_finValid` and `not_rePred_not_finSat`, and two incompleteness corollaries: every r.e. set of finitely valid sentences misses one (`exists_finValid_not_of_rePred`), and a proof system with a decidable proof relation that proves only finitely valid sentences leaves one unprovable (`exists_finValid_unprovable`). The reduction is from Mathlib's halting problem `ComputablePred.halting_problem`: `eval_dom_iff_finSat` says that a code `c` halts on input `0` iff `haltSentence c` has a finite model, and `primrec_haltSentence` says that the reduction is primitive recursive. The sentence has two parts. The [arithmetic axioms](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Trakhtenbrot/Arithmetic.lean) describe order, zero, successor and the `Nat.pair` graph as **relations**, since these are not total functions on a finite initial segment. `arithModel_faithful` shows that every finite model is isomorphic to a standard `Fin N`, and this is where finiteness is used. The [evaln axioms](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Trakhtenbrot/Evaln.lean) unfold `Code.evaln` one step for every code up to `c`, with one ternary graph symbol per code. Only the direction "graph ⊆ true facts" is axiomatized, and `evaln_of_model` shows that it suffices once the arithmetic is standard. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/Trakhtenbrot.lean) include an axiom of infinity that holds in `ℕ` but has no finite model, and the reduction sentences of a halting and a diverging code.

Relation symbols in `haltSentence c` have arity at most three, but the sentence uses the symbols up to `encode c + 4`, so `not_computablePred_finSat` is about the union of all finite relational vocabularies. [Binary](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Trakhtenbrot/Binary.lean) proves Libkin's form (Theorem 9.2 and Corollary 9.3). For every vocabulary `σ : Set (ℕ × ℕ)` of pairs (symbol, arity) with `(s, 2) ∈ σ`, no computable `f : RawFormula → Bool` is correct on all sentences over `σ` (`not_exists_computable_finSat_of_mem`), and no r.e. predicate agrees with `FinValid` on them (`not_exists_rePred_finValid_of_mem`). These statements are strictly stronger than the all-formula ones, since the decider only has to be correct on sentences over `σ`. [Sentence](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Raw/Sentence.lean) defines `freeVars`, `IsSentence` (decidable), `IsSentenceOver σ φ` and the existential closure `exClosure`. The primitive recursive reduction `binarize s` has three steps. First, [uniformize](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Raw/Uniformize.lean) merges all symbols into symbol `0`, coding a symbol `r` of arity `k` as arity `Nat.pair r k + 1` padded with a fresh variable. Second, [toGraph](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Raw/Graph.lean) interprets the result in one binary relation. The original elements are the loops, and a tuple is a chain of non-loop vertices that point to its coordinates (`isTupleGadget_tupleGraph_iff`). It uses the generic syntactic [interpretation](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Raw/Interpret.lean) `interpret D θ` (relativize quantifiers to `D`, replace atoms by `θ`). This is a primitive recursive map on raw syntax. The Mathlib-syntax `Cslib.Logic.FO.Interpretation` also pulls formulas back (`onFormula`), but that pullback is noncomputable and Mathlib formulas have no `Primcodable` instance. Third, `exClosure` closes the formula and `rename [s]` renames the symbol. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/TrakhtenbrotBinary.lean) include sentencehood by `decide`, a tuple gadget, and the reduction of the axiom of infinity.

Scope. The encoded computation is `Code.evaln` rather than a Turing machine run. A computable bridge to Mathlib `L.Sentence` remains open ([#2886](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/issues/2886)).

To use the results, import `Complexity.Logics.FirstOrder.Trakhtenbrot.Undecidability`, or `Complexity.Logics.FirstOrder.Trakhtenbrot.Binary` for sentences over a fixed vocabulary. To show that another predicate `p` is undecidable, give a computable `f : RawFormula → α` with `FinSat φ ↔ p (f φ)`, and then apply `not_computablePred_finSat` to `(hp.comp hf).of_eq`. If `f` is only correct on sentences over `{(s, 2)}`, compose it with `binarize s` first, or apply `not_exists_computable_finSat_of_mem`. The name `RawFormula` is also used by the FO(LFP) syntax `Cslib.Logic.FO.LFP.RawFormula`, so qualify it when both namespaces are open.

## The 0-1 law for graphs

The [0-1 law](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw.lean) (Glebskiĭ–Kogan–Liogon'kiĭ–Talanov; Fagin) is stated for the uniform random labelled graph `randomGraph n`, a `PMF` on `SimpleGraph (Fin n)`. `randomGraphProb n P` is the probability of a graph property, that is, the fraction of labelled graphs on `Fin n` with `P`, and `Satisfies G φ` interprets a sentence of Mathlib's `Language.graph` in `G.structure`. `zero_one_law φ` says that `randomGraphProb n (fun G => Satisfies G φ)` tends to `0` or to `1`. `tendsto_randomGraphProb_one_iff` identifies the almost-sure sentences with the consequences of `randomGraphTheory`, the simple-graph axioms together with all [extension axioms](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/ExtensionAxiom.lean).

The proof follows Fagin. An explicit union bound `n (n - 1) ⋯ (n - k + 1) · (1 - 2⁻ᵏ) ^ (n - k)` on the failure probability of each extension axiom is proved by counting in [ExtensionProbability](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/ExtensionProbability.lean). Compactness then gives satisfiability. The back-and-forth step is Mathlib's `IsExtensionPair`, and ℵ₀-categoricity uses `equiv_between_cg`; Mathlib's Łoś–Vaught test `Cardinal.Categorical.isComplete` gives completeness. Non-definability corollaries are stated as "no sentence agrees with `P` on all large `Fin n` graphs": an even number of vertices (also as `¬ FO.Definable`) and an even number of edges, whose probability is exactly `1/2` for `n ≥ 2`. This law is for simple graphs with edge probability `1/2`; the law for uniform structures over other relational vocabularies is described below. Unlabelled graphs and `p(n)`-random graphs are not formalized.

To show with the 0-1 law that a graph property `P n` (one for each number `n` of vertices) is not first-order definable, show that `randomGraphProb n (P n)` tends neither to `0` nor to `1`, then apply `not_exists_sentence_of_not_tendsto`. An involution of the graphs on `Fin n` that exchanges `P` and `¬P` gives probability exactly `1/2` through `randomGraphProb_eq_half_of_involutive`; toggling one edge is the involution used for an even number of edges ([SimpleGraph.EvenEdges](../../Complexity/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/EvenEdges.lean)). One pitfall: `Satisfies G φ` is realization in `G.structure`, which is a definition and not an instance. To use Mathlib's `realize_*` lemmas on it, either rewrite with the characterizations `satisfies_not` and `satisfies_extensionAxiom_iff`, or bring the structure into scope first with `let := G.structure`, as the proofs of those lemmas do. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/ZeroOneLaw.lean) cover exact probabilities `1/2`, an extension axiom holding in the triangle and failing in the empty graph on two vertices, the transfer theorem applied to the sentence "there are no edges", and non-definability of an odd number of vertices.

The [Rado graph](../../Complexity/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Rado.lean) `SimpleGraph.radoGraph` is the countable random graph on `ℕ`, given by Ackermann's bit relation: distinct `i` and `j` are adjacent when bit `i` of `j` or bit `j` of `i` is set. `radoGraph_adj` restates adjacency with `Nat.testBit`, and small cases are decidable. `radoGraph_exists_adj` is the extension property for disjoint finite sets `A` and `B`. [ZeroOneLaw/Rado](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/Rado.lean) connects it to the 0-1 law. `tendsto_randomGraphProb_one_iff_radoGraph` says that a sentence holds almost surely in `G(n, 1/2)` exactly when `Satisfies radoGraph φ`, and `tendsto_randomGraphProb_zero_iff_radoGraph` says that it holds almost never otherwise. So to compute the limit probability of a concrete sentence, check it in the Rado graph: only adjacency is decidable, not `Satisfies`, so after `let := radoGraph.structure` unfold the sentence with the `realize_*` lemmas and `decide` the resulting adjacencies, as the triangle test does. `nonempty_iso_radoGraph` (every countable graph with all extension properties is isomorphic to `radoGraph`), `nonempty_embedding_radoGraph` (every countable graph is an induced subgraph) and `nonempty_compl_iso_radoGraph` (self-complementarity) are the classical structure theorems. `satisfies_extensionAxiom_iff_exists_adj` translates between the extension axioms and the finite-set form. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/Rado.lean) include a triangle on `0, 1, 3`, which gives the almost-sure existence of triangles.

The [0-1 law for finite relational vocabularies](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/Relational.lean) (Glebskiĭ–Kogan–Liogon'kiĭ–Talanov; Fagin) holds for every relational language `L` with finitely many relation symbols, none of them nullary (`[L.IsRelational] [Finite (Σ l, L.Relations l)] [IsEmpty (L.Relations 0)]`). [UniformStructure](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/UniformStructure.lean) defines `uniformStructure n`, a `PMF` on `L.Structure (Fin n)` under which every atomic fact holds independently with probability `1/2`. Atomic facts are the circuit model checker's `ModelChecking.Atom`. `structProb_uniformStructure_eq_card` shows that it is the uniform distribution on structures, Fagin's `μₙ`. `Relational.zero_one_law φ` says that `sentenceProb (uniformStructure n) φ` tends to `0` or to `1`. `Relational.tendsto_sentenceProb_one_iff` identifies the almost-sure sentences with the consequences of `extensionTheory L`, whose [extension axioms](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/Relational/ExtensionAxiom.lean) prescribe the atomic type of a fresh element over `k` given ones. The hypothesis on nullary symbols is not needed for satisfiability (`isSatisfiable_extensionTheory`), but categoricity, completeness and the law fail without it: `not_zero_one_law_of_nullary` shows that a nullary symbol has probability exactly `1/2` for every `n`. The proof follows Fagin.

The measure-independent part of the proof is in [Transfer](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/Transfer.lean), for any sequence `μ : ∀ n, PMF (L.Structure (Fin n))`. If every sentence of `T` holds with probability tending to `1`, then `T` is satisfiable (`isSatisfiable_of_tendsto_sentenceProb`) and every consequence of `T` holds with probability tending to `1` (`tendsto_sentenceProb_of_models`). If `T` is also complete, `zero_one_law_of_isComplete` gives a 0-1 law and `not_exists_sentence_of_not_tendsto` gives non-definability. A new 0-1 law therefore needs only an almost-sure theory and its completeness. The union bound `n (n - 1) ⋯ (n - k + 1) · (1 - 2⁻ᵃ) ^ (n - k)` uses the counting lemma `Nat.card_subtype_comp_injective_div` (restrictions of uniform functions along injections are uniform) and the limit `tendsto_descFactorial_mul_pow_sub`, which the graph law shares.

One pitfall: for `L = Language.graph`, `uniformStructure` is uniform on directed graphs with loops, not on simple graphs. The irreflexivity axiom has probability `2⁻ⁿ`, and `extensionTheory Language.graph` refutes it, so the `G(n, 1/2)` law above is not a special case. `uniformStructure` and `sentenceProb` are not exposed. Compute with `sentenceProb_def`, `structProb_uniformStructure` and `relMap_atomStructure`, and bring `atomStructure y` into scope with `let` before using Mathlib's `realize_*` lemmas. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/RelationalZeroOneLaw.lean) instantiate the law at `Language.graph`, compute the irreflexivity probability `2⁻ⁿ`, check an extension axiom in a concrete one-element structure and its failure in every one-element structure, and give a vocabulary with a nullary symbol for which the law fails.

## Known Limitations

This survey checks the model definitions and selected endpoint routes rather than every formula-level proof. Source ownership is mixed: Mathlib supplies the underlying first-order logic, while local modules supply finite encodings and complexity-specific operators/reductions. Cardinality, input length and tuple dimension are separate parameters; quoted complexity claims should preserve them and retain the positivity/order/encoding hypotheses.
