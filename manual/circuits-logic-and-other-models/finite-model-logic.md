# Finite-model logic and native complexity

The descriptive-complexity development uses Mathlib first-order syntax and semantics, then adds finite encodings, interpretations and scoped relation operators. The main bridges concern **data complexity**: a sentence is fixed while its finite input structure varies. The number of variables and nesting depth of that sentence may affect the polynomial or logspace constants. [complexitylib][complexitylib]

## Structures, encodings and formulas

The [closeAll](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Basic.lean#L69) layer supplies finite-variable closure and atomic/order combinators over `FirstOrder.Language`. Finite relational vocabularies enumerate relation symbols of their declared arities. The [Vocabulary](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Encoding.lean#L89) representation is universe size together with a list of relation truth tables. Cardinality is unary; relation tables are framed and their order follows the chosen finite vocabulary enumeration. Tuple coordinate zero is the least significant coordinate of the table index.

The decoder uses a canonical universe `Fin n`. A table for an arity-k relation must contain exactly `n^k` entries, and the relation list must have exactly the expected number of tables. Nullary relations still have one bit when n is zero. Missing raw values may have fallback semantics in the decoder, but the encoded query language requires well-formedness and therefore rejects malformed tables. This separation permits total parsers without weakening the language contract.

The [Structure](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/Query.lean#L48) variant uses canonical `Fin n` with n at least two and fixed numeric symbols for zero, one, maximum, order and BIT. These numeric relations/functions are determined by n rather than supplied as arbitrary input tables. Their BIT convention is least-significant-bit first. The n>=2 restriction avoids degenerate constants; an endpoint over every finite structure must handle the smaller universes separately.

## Interpretations and reductions

[Interpretation](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Interpretation.lean#L98) defines a target structure on a definable subset of source tuples. Target relation symbols are interpreted by formulas and equality is literal tuple equality. Formula pullback relativizes quantifiers to the interpreted domain, proves satisfaction preservation and supports composition. There is no implicit quotient of tuples by a definable equivalence relation.

[TaggedInterpretation](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Interpretation/Tagged.lean#L105) extends the representation to a finite disjoint union of tuple domains, with potentially different dimensions. This makes multi-sorted gadget reductions readable. Equality patterns encode tags into ordinary tuples when sufficient distinct domain elements are available; tiny universes and copy-invariant targets require the explicit treatment supplied by the relevant bridge. SAT, coloring and independent-set reductions are clients.

The projection API is stricter than arbitrary quantifier-free interpretation. [InputLiteral](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ProjectionFormula.lean#L70) has a numeric-case syntax whose leaves are a single signed input atom or a Boolean constant. `Projection` combines that syntax with domain/numeric interpretation. This preserves the intended distinction between a literal selector and a formula such as the conjunction of two independent input relations. Exact classical projection conventions additionally depend on the numeric vocabulary and domain construction. Identify those choices when using the API; arbitrary QF interpretations are not all projections.

Ordered reductions introduce a second subtlety. [DomainInterpretation](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/DomainInterpretation.lean#L81) selects a definable subset of tuples and ranks it lexicographically to obtain a canonical target universe. The rank's BIT relation is not assumed automatically first-order definable. A [numeric certificate](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/DomainInterpretation/Certificate.lean) supplies formulas and their correctness when a reduction needs them. [FullTupleInterpretation](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Ordered/FullTuple.lean#L77) specializes to the entire positive-dimensional tuple universe, where canonical arithmetic/order structure is constructed without an arbitrary subset-ranking assumption.

## Scoped operators: LFP and transitive closure

[RelVar](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/Basic.lean#L48) extends syntax with scoped relation variables and general binder shapes. This factors out relabeling, environments, polarity and satisfaction reasoning for operators. The least-fixed-point development deliberately separates raw formulas from checked positive formulas.

The checked syntax [Formula](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/LeastFixedPoint.lean#L72) enforces positivity in the recursively bound relation at every nested binder. Positivity gives monotonicity, so the semantic operator has a least fixed point; over a universe of size n and an arity-k relation, iteration from the empty relation stabilizes by `n^k`. Raw unchecked syntax still has total semantics via the intersection of prefixed points, but that expression need not satisfy a nonmonotone operator's fixed-point equation. The checked/raw distinction is mathematically essential, not just a parser convenience.

[shape](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/Operator/TransitiveClosure.lean#L77) uses reflexive transitive closure of a relation on tuples. The binder has two tuples of the same width and no new relation variables; width zero is permitted. This module provides a closure/definability API. The survey did not establish a complete FO(TC)-captures-NL endpoint, so it should not be listed as proved merely because transitive closure syntax exists.

## Fagin's theorem

[Sentence](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Logics/SecondOrder/Existential.lean#L124) is a finite collection of existential relation variables, each with an arity, followed by a first-order matrix over the sum vocabulary. The headline equivalence says that a class of finite relational structures is ESO-definable exactly when it is isomorphism-closed and its well-formed encoded query language lies in the library's native NP class. The vocabulary enumeration is fixed and no arbitrary input function symbols are admitted by this encoding theorem.

The reverse direction begins from the native strict verifier formulation. A fixed tuple dimension bounds the polynomial clock and relation tables; an ESO sentence describes a valid computation tableau and guesses an order. Empty and singleton universes are handled by finite truth-table cases rather than an invalid assumption that a positive tuple power always has enough distinct values. The forward direction checks a guessed relation interpretation and the first-order matrix within polynomial bounds. See [Fagin abstract](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/abstracts/ComplexityAbstracts/FaginAbstract.lean), [checked bridge](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/abstracts/ComplexityAbstracts/FaginBridge.lean) and [converse implementation](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Fagin/Converse.lean).

The source documentation maps its statement to Fagin's finite-model result and textbook formulations by Immerman and Papadimitriou. This author traced the abstract's encoding and quantifiers to the library statement, including malformed, empty-universe and nullary-relation behavior. The original paper correspondence has not been independently exhaustively audited in this research pass.

## FO model checking and Immerman–Vardi

Fixed FO formulas have a native logspace model checker on the stated table encoding. The implementation compiles formula evaluation through bounded-register counter programs and validates input shape. A separate circuit compilation expands quantifiers into AND/OR gates, giving constant depth and polynomial size in the universe parameter. That circuit result is an upper direction; it does not by itself claim DLOGTIME-uniform AC0 capture or charge serial encoding length as though it were one relation bit. See [native FO logspace](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/FirstOrder.lean) and [ordered FO circuits](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/FirstOrder/Ordered.lean).

For canonical ordered finite relational structures, [Immerman–Vardi](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/ImmermanVardi.lean) proves `lfpDefinable_iff_encodedLanguage_mem_P`. Positive finite fixed-point iteration supplies the forward native polynomial-time computation. The reverse direction encodes a native polynomial-time machine by a fixed-point tableau in a fixed tuple power. The theorem is a genuine bidirectional model bridge with explicit encoding, not only a semantic containment assumption.

## Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé games and nondefinability

[quantifierRank](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/QuantifierRank.lean) measures how deeply the quantifiers of a Mathlib `BoundedFormula` nest. The LogSpace model checker's register depth satisfies `boundDepth_eq_add_quantifierRank`: it equals the number of bound variables in scope plus the quantifier rank. [DuplicatorWins](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/Basic.lean) is the `n`-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game played from a position: a pair of pebble tuples `a : Fin k → M`, `b : Fin k → N`, extended by `Fin.snoc` each round, which are exactly the variable assignments of formulas with `k` bound variables. Duplicator must keep every position a partial isomorphism (`PartialIso`: equality and every relation agree on the pebbles). The game, strategies (`duplicatorWins_iff_exists_strategy`; `Strategy` is exposed data that clients build by recursion and check with `Strategy.isWinning_zero`/`Strategy.isWinning_succ`), back-and-forth systems (`IsBackAndForth.duplicatorWins`) and invariance under isomorphism are available for any language and structures, finite or infinite.

The [Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/Hintikka.lean) has two directions with different hypotheses. `DuplicatorWins.realize_iff` (winning positions satisfy the same formulas of quantifier rank at most `n`) needs `L.IsRelational` and nothing else. The converse `duplicatorWins_of_forall_realize_iff` needs finitely many relation symbols, `[Finite (Σ l, L.Relations l)]`, and is proved with Hintikka formulas of rank-`n` types. `duplicatorWins_iff_forall_sentence` is the combined statement for sentences. `duplicatorWins_iff_forall_formula` and `DuplicatorWins.formula_realize_iff` state it for `L.Formula (Fin k)`, with the free variables naming the pebbles. The rank lemmas `quantifierRank_relabel` and `quantifierRank_toFormula` carry ranks across Mathlib's conversions between the two forms. As a consequence there are only finitely many formulas of rank at most `n` in `k` variables up to equivalence (`exists_finite_quantifierRank_le_realize_iff`).

To show that a property of finite structures is not first-order definable, import the theorem module and, for each `n`, give two structures on which Duplicator wins `n` rounds, one with the property and one without. Then apply `DuplicatorWins.realize_iff` (or `duplicatorWins_iff_forall_sentence`) to a supposed defining sentence of rank `n`. Duplicator's wins are usually obtained by giving an invariant on positions and checking `IsBackAndForth`. For [finite linear orders](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/EhrenfeuchtFraisse/FiniteLinearOrder.lean), `duplicatorWins_iff_card` decides the game exactly: equal size, or both sizes at least `2 ^ n - 1` (the characterization in Kolaitis's survey chapter of *Finite Model Theory and Its Applications*). `not_exists_sentence_realize_iff_even` is the resulting nondefinability of EVEN. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/EhrenfeuchtFraisse.lean) cover a rank-`2` sentence separating `Fin 2` from `Fin 3`, the threshold cases, an ODD client, and explicit winning strategies built outside the defining module.

Two pitfalls. The game does not support constants or function symbols, so a vocabulary with numeric constants must first replace them by their defining formulas. The order results concern Mathlib's pure order language, FO(≤), not the Immerman convention FO(≤, BIT) used by the ordered capture theorems above. With `BIT`, EVEN is definable (bit zero of `max`), so these results show that `BIT` adds expressive power; they say nothing about FO(≤, BIT). Statements about sentences take an arbitrary empty tuple `a : Fin 0 → M`. Do not rewrite with a lemma stated for `default`: at concrete types such as `Fin 3`, `default` may elaborate through a different `Inhabited` instance and the rewrite fails.

## The 0-1 law for graphs

The [0-1 law](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw.lean) (Glebskiĭ–Kogan–Liogon'kiĭ–Talanov; Fagin) is stated for the uniform random labelled graph `randomGraph n`, a `PMF` on `SimpleGraph (Fin n)`. `randomGraphProb n P` is the probability of a graph property, that is, the fraction of labelled graphs on `Fin n` with `P`, and `Satisfies G φ` interprets a sentence of Mathlib's `Language.graph` in `G.structure`. `zero_one_law φ` says that `randomGraphProb n (fun G => Satisfies G φ)` tends to `0` or to `1`. `tendsto_randomGraphProb_one_iff` identifies the almost-sure sentences with the consequences of `randomGraphTheory`, the simple-graph axioms together with all [extension axioms](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/ExtensionAxiom.lean).

The proof follows Fagin. An explicit union bound `n (n - 1) ⋯ (n - k + 1) · (1 - 2⁻ᵏ) ^ (n - k)` on the failure probability of each extension axiom is proved by counting in [ExtensionProbability](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/ExtensionProbability.lean). Compactness then gives satisfiability. The back-and-forth step is Mathlib's `IsExtensionPair`, and ℵ₀-categoricity uses `equiv_between_cg`; Mathlib's Łoś–Vaught test `Cardinal.Categorical.isComplete` gives completeness. Non-definability corollaries are stated as "no sentence agrees with `P` on all large `Fin n` graphs": an even number of vertices (also as `¬ FO.Definable`) and an even number of edges, whose probability is exactly `1/2` for `n ≥ 2`. The law covers only the graph vocabulary and edge probability `1/2`. Unlabelled graphs, other relational vocabularies and `p(n)`-random graphs are not formalized.

To show with the 0-1 law that a graph property `P n` (one for each number `n` of vertices) is not first-order definable, show that `randomGraphProb n (P n)` tends neither to `0` nor to `1`, then apply `not_exists_sentence_of_not_tendsto`. An involution of the graphs on `Fin n` that exchanges `P` and `¬P` gives probability exactly `1/2` through `randomGraphProb_eq_half_of_involutive`; toggling one edge is the involution used for an even number of edges ([SimpleGraph.EvenEdges](../../Complexity/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/EvenEdges.lean)). One pitfall: `Satisfies G φ` is realization in `G.structure`, which is a definition and not an instance. To use Mathlib's `realize_*` lemmas on it, either rewrite with the characterizations `satisfies_not` and `satisfies_extensionAxiom_iff`, or bring the structure into scope first with `let := G.structure`, as the proofs of those lemmas do. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/ZeroOneLaw.lean) cover exact probabilities `1/2`, an extension axiom holding in the triangle and failing in the empty graph on two vertices, the transfer theorem applied to the sentence "there are no edges", and non-definability of an odd number of vertices.

The [Rado graph](../../Complexity/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Rado.lean) `SimpleGraph.radoGraph` is the countable random graph on `ℕ`, given by Ackermann's bit relation: distinct `i` and `j` are adjacent when bit `i` of `j` or bit `j` of `i` is set. `radoGraph_adj` restates adjacency with `Nat.testBit`, and small cases are decidable. `radoGraph_exists_adj` is the extension property for disjoint finite sets `A` and `B`. [ZeroOneLaw/Rado](../../Complexity/Logics/FirstOrder/ZeroOneLaw/Rado.lean) connects it to the 0-1 law. `tendsto_randomGraphProb_one_iff_radoGraph` says that a sentence holds almost surely in `G(n, 1/2)` exactly when `Satisfies radoGraph φ`, and `tendsto_randomGraphProb_zero_iff_radoGraph` says that it holds almost never otherwise. So to compute the limit probability of a concrete sentence, check it in the Rado graph. `nonempty_iso_radoGraph` (every countable graph with all extension properties is isomorphic to `radoGraph`), `nonempty_embedding_radoGraph` (every countable graph is an induced subgraph) and `nonempty_compl_iso_radoGraph` (self-complementarity) are the classical structure theorems. `satisfies_extensionAxiom_iff_exists_adj` translates between the extension axioms and the finite-set form. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/Rado.lean) include a triangle on `0, 1, 3`, which gives the almost-sure existence of triangles.

## Known Limitations

This survey checks the model definitions and selected endpoint routes rather than every formula-level proof. Source ownership is mixed: Mathlib supplies the underlying first-order logic, while local modules supply finite encodings and complexity-specific operators/reductions. Cardinality, input length and tuple dimension are separate parameters; quoted complexity claims should preserve them and retain the positivity/order/encoding hypotheses.
