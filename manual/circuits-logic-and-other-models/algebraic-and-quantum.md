# Algebraic and quantum models

Algebraic and quantum developments reuse the generic circuit architecture while changing both semantics and what resources are free. Formal polynomial equality, exact rational matrix evaluation and unitary-state evolution are distinct notions of correctness. Their bridges to encoded native computations are explicit developments rather than consequences of using Lean's executable definitions. [complexitylib][complexitylib]

## Arithmetic, affine and full bases

[ArithOp](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic.lean#L82) uses constants, binary addition and multiplication. Interpretation over a polynomial ring produces a formal polynomial, and evaluation homomorphisms transport it to values in another carrier. Formal polynomial equality is the appropriate algebraic identity notion; over a finite field, different formal polynomials can define the same function. Separate addition/multiplication weights permit statements about multiplication complexity. Scalar multiplication is still multiplication when represented as such.

[affineSignature](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic.lean#L67) makes an entire arbitrary-arity affine combination one gate. The [FullOp](https://github.com/leanprover/cslib/blob/254e30e6d4299c51323aaeaf5f51a4763b375d4a/Cslib/Computability/Circuit/Signature.lean#L37) model is broader still: any operation depending on at most a fixed number of values is primitive. Over the full binary basis, tests and selections can cost one gate, and `Synthesis.full_sum`, `full_dot` and `full_chain` bound counts of these operations. An algebraic operation count in that model is not automatically a bound in the restricted addition/multiplication basis, nor a bound on binary integer/rational computation. [cslib][cslib] See [full-basis synthesis](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/FullArithmetic.lean).

[SigmaPiOp](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/SigmaPi.lean#L109) permits unbounded sums with edge coefficients and unbounded products. Product depth counts multiplication layers only; weighted sums contribute zero to that depth. Set-multilinearity records that products combine disjoint variable groups. [SPCircuit](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/SPCircuit.lean#L89) provides shared DAGs in this basis. Its `IsSimple` condition requires distinct input wires at product gates; the unfolding bound used by the constant-product-depth lower-bound development assumes this condition.

The [constant-depth arithmetic abstract](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/abstracts/ComplexityAbstracts/ConstantDepthArithmeticAbstract.lean) states an iterated-matrix-multiplication lower bound over any field for the specified simple Sigma–Pi circuits and product-depth convention. Its finite exponent involves the matrix-chain parameter and `4^Delta-1`; the asymptotic regime chooses the chain length roughly `log log n`. Source annotations credit the Limaye–Srinivasan–Tavenas development and the stated reduction through set-multilinearization. This survey does not remove the simplicity hypothesis or identify the bound with unrestricted arbitrary repeated-wire product syntax.

## Valiant classes and term models

[PolynomialFamily](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/VP.lean#L188) consists of families whose numbers of variables, formal total degrees and circuit sizes are all polynomially bounded in the family index. This is nonuniform and includes degree as an independent restriction. The library's repeated-squaring example has small circuits but exponential degree and is therefore excluded from VP. A p-projection substitutes variables/constants and permits a polynomially bounded family reindexing.

[VNP](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/VNP.lean#L112) is defined by a Boolean sum of a VP verifier family over auxiliary variables. It is meaningful over a commutative semiring, while particular membership theorems may require a ring or field. The library proves VP inclusion, p-projection closure, permanent membership and the usual consequence that a VNP-complete family lies in VP exactly when VNP is contained in VP. The pinned VNP module explicitly states that permanent VNP-completeness is not proved there. This boundary matters to a paper cataloguing achieved endpoints rather than familiar theorem names.

[Op](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/SumOfTerms/Basic.lean#L47) counts dictionary terms while additions are free; [Term](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Waring/Basic.lean#L39) specializes the terms to scalar multiples of powers of homogeneous linear forms. These term counts have their own lower-bound and compilation APIs. They must be translated before being compared with arithmetic gate counts.

## Reverse-mode differentiation and lower-bound transfer

[Circuit.withGradient](../../Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Gradient.lean) is the Baur–Strassen construction. It keeps every output of an `arithSignature K` circuit and appends the partial derivatives of one chosen output, scaled by a seed constant. The size is exactly `s + 2 + 2·#additions + 4·#multiplications`, so at most `5s + 2`; the two extra gates are the seed and zero constants. The multiplication count is exactly three times the original. That count includes multiplications by constants, which Baur and Strassen's nonscalar measure does not count. There is no subtraction, so correctness (`Circuit.polynomial_withGradient_natAdd`) holds formally over every commutative semiring. `Circuit.exists_gradient` packages the result as a circuit computing `f` and all `∂f/∂xᵢ`. To transfer a lower bound for the gradient of `f` to `f` itself, use `Circuit.le_five_mul_size_add_two_of_gradient` or `Circuit.le_three_mul_mulCost_of_gradient`. For the power sum `∑ X i ^ (d + 1)` with `d + 1` invertible, `Circuit.le_three_mul_mulCost_of_psum` reduces to the power map. Strassen's degree bound, which would supply the `n log d` hypothesis there, is not formalized. In [the adjugate module](../../Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Adjugate.lean), `pderiv_det_genericMatrix` identifies each partial derivative of the generic determinant with an adjugate entry, and `exists_circuit_adjugate_of_det` turns determinant circuits into adjugate circuits with the same `5s + 2` bound; `exists_circuit_adjugate` gives adjugate circuits with at most `15n⁴ + 22` gates over every commutative ring. Executable checks are in [the gradient tests](../../ComplexityTests/ArithmeticGradient.lean).

## Matrix multiplication exponent

Import [NineQuarters.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/MatrixMultiplication/NineQuarters.lean) for the endpoint and [Exponent.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/MatrixMultiplication/Exponent.lean) for the definitions. `ComputesMatMul K C` says that a circuit `C` over `arithSignature K` with `2 n ^ 2` inputs and `n ^ 2` outputs, fed the row-major entries of `A` followed by those of `B`, outputs the entries of `A * B` for **every** pair of `n × n` matrices over `K`. Correctness is exact matrix equality in the field, not formal polynomial identity, and the model is nonuniform: one circuit per `n`, with no generator. `AdmissibleExponent K τ` requires, for every `ε > 0`, a constant `c` and circuits of size at most `c * n ^ (τ + ε)` for every `n ≥ 1`; `omega K` is the infimum of the admissible exponents. Size is cslib's `Circuit.size`, the total number of gates: additions, multiplications **and** constants cost one each, while inputs and outputs are free. Every admissible exponent is at least `2`, one gate per output, so the infimum is over a set bounded below; the trivial algorithm gives `3`.

The endpoint [omega_le_nine_quarters](../../Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/MatrixMultiplication/NineQuarters.lean) states `omega K ≤ 9 / 4` for every `[Field K] [CharZero K]`, and `admissibleExponent_nine_quarters` supplies circuits of size `O(n ^ (9/4 + γ))` for every `γ > 0`, following [Pass's constructive proof](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28627) of OpenAI's bound. Characteristic zero is a genuine hypothesis: the interpolation nodes are the integers `0, 1, 2, …`, which must be distinct in `K`; the paper works over `ℚ`. The statement depends only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

**Rank is not a gate count.** The tensor layer in [Tensor/Basic.lean](../../Complexity/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Tensor/Basic.lean) states bilinear algorithms: `Tensor.Rank r T` means that `T` is a restriction of the unit tensor `unit r`, a bilinear algorithm with `r` scalar multiplications and arbitrary, uncounted linear encodings of the inputs and decoding of the outputs. `Tensor.mm p q r` is the matrix multiplication tensor, `rank_mm` its trivial bound `p q r`, and `Rank 7 (mm 2 2 2)` is Strassen's algorithm. A rank bound therefore counts only the multiplications of the bilinear step and is not a bound on any circuit's gate count. The bridge is `admissibleExponent_of_rank`: from `Rank r (mm v v v)` with `2 ≤ v`, `r ≤ v ^ τ` and `2 < τ`, block recursion gives total-gate bounds `O(n ^ (τ + ε))` for every `ε > 0`, through the recursion `S (j + 1) ≤ r S j + 9 r (v ^ (j + 1)) ^ 2 + 1` and zero padding to the next power of `v`. Degenerations over `K[ε]` (`Tensor.IsDegen`, in [Tensor/Degeneration.lean](../../Complexity/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Tensor/Degeneration.lean)) and Bini's conversion `IsDegen.exists_rank_tpow` turn border-rank bounds into rank bounds on tensor powers; the `9/4` identity is obtained this way, so its side `v` and its multiplication count `r` are existential.

Complete clients: [MatrixMultiplicationExponent.lean](../../ComplexityTests/MatrixMultiplicationExponent.lean) checks `omega ℚ ≤ 9 / 4`, `omega ℝ ≤ 9 / 4`, `AdmissibleExponent ℚ (9 / 4 + 1 / 100)`, the trivial exponent `3` both directly and through block recursion from `Rank 8 (mm 2 2 2)`, and `2 ≤ omega ℚ`; [TensorBasic.lean](../../ComplexityTests/TensorBasic.lean) checks the bilinear maps of `mm` and of the polynomial product, Strassen's witness of `Rank 7 (mm 2 2 2)`, the interpolation bound `Rank 3 (full 2 2)` and an instance of the primal packing. Build them with

```bash
lake build Complexity.Computability.Circuit.Arithmetic.MatrixMultiplication.NineQuarters \
  ComplexityTests.MatrixMultiplicationExponent ComplexityTests.TensorBasic
```

**Not certified here.** The paper's Theorem 1.1 is an integer algorithm with bit cost `O(n^(9/4+γ) M(B + log n))` on `B`-bit entries ([§4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28627v2#page=20)); the formalization proves the field-circuit statement only and says nothing about integer arithmetic, division by the cleared denominators or bit complexity. The paper's "computable" side `v` and count `r` are obtained here as existentials (`Tensor.exists_rank_mm_lt_rpow`), with no extraction procedure and no explicit constants; the parameters are galactic. Degenerations compose at the order `(h + 1) (k + 1) - 1` rather than the paper's additive bookkeeping, the gap parameter is `⌊w₀ / c ^ d⌋ ^ 4` rather than `⌊w / u⌋ ^ 2`, and the lengths of the rounds are bounded explicitly rather than by a limit argument; none of this changes the exponent. Positive characteristic, the rectangular and dual exponents and any lower bound beyond `2 ≤ omega K` are open; see [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md).

## Karatsuba polynomial multiplication

[Karatsuba/Defs.lean](../../Complexity/Algorithms/Lean/Query/Arith/Karatsuba/Defs.lean) writes `Cslib.Query.karatsuba` in cslib's arithmetic query model `FreeM (ArithQuery α)`. The model is a program, not a circuit: every coefficient addition, subtraction and multiplication is an oracle query, and list splitting, zero padding and concatenation are free. The program is generic in `α`, assuming only `[Zero α]` for padding, so it can compute on coefficients only through queries. Its branching depends only on lengths, so the cost theorems hold under **every** oracle. Correctness ([Correctness.lean](../../Complexity/Algorithms/Lean/Query/Arith/Karatsuba/Correctness.lean)) uses the honest oracle of a commutative ring: `ofCoeffList_eval_karatsuba` identifies the output, via `Polynomial.ofCoeffList`, with the product of the input polynomials. The output has `2 max(|p|, |q|) - 1` coefficients, so it can end in zeros.

[Cost.lean](../../Complexity/Algorithms/Lean/Query/Arith/Karatsuba/Cost.lean) gives the exact cost (`cost_karatsuba`). With `N = max(|p|, |q|)`, there are `mulCount N` multiplications and `addCount N` additions and subtractions, given by `M(n) = M(⌊n/2⌋) + 2 M(⌈n/2⌉)` and `A(n) = A(⌊n/2⌋) + 2 A(⌈n/2⌉) + 4n - 3`. The proved bounds are:

- the closed forms `mulCount (2^k) = 3^k` and `2 addCount (2^k) + 16·2^k = 13·3^k + 3`;
- the total `mulCount n + addCount n ≤ 9·3^⌈log₂ n⌉ ≤ 27 n^log₂ 3`, packaged as `karatsuba_upperBound` for `Cslib.Query.UpperBound`;
- a lower bound `n^log₂ 3 ≤ 3 mulCount n`, with `karatsuba_lowerBound` recording for `Cslib.Query.LowerBound` that every length attains the exact count;
- `mulCount n < n²` for `n ≥ 2`.

The upper bounds come from a reusable halving-recurrence lemma, [HalvingRecurrence.lean](../../Complexity/Foundations/Data/Nat/HalvingRecurrence.lean) (`Nat.add_mul_two_pow_clog_le_of_halving`). It proves the recurrence by substitution with a subtracted linear term and needs no monotonicity. [LogTwoRpow.lean](../../Complexity/Foundations/Data/Nat/LogTwoRpow.lean) converts `a^⌈log₂ n⌉` and `a^⌊log₂ n⌋` to `n^log₂ a`.

These are operation counts, not bit complexity. One recursion level is a bilinear algorithm with three products, valid over any commutative ring. It gives the same bound as the tensor layer's `Rank 3 (full 2 2)`, which `Tensor.rank_full` proves differently, by interpolation over a field of characteristic zero; no bridge between the two is formalized. A tensor rank counts only products, while this program also counts every addition and subtraction of the recursion.

Complete client: [Karatsuba.lean](../../ComplexityTests/Karatsuba.lean). It checks concrete integer products, including unequal lengths, an uneven split and empty operands, and the symbolic three-multiplication formula. It also checks exact counts against the closed forms, and that the count is strictly below `n²` except at `n = 1`. It confirms the same query count under a dishonest oracle and applies the packaged bound at a concrete length. Build it with

```bash
lake build Complexity.Algorithms.Lean.Query.Arith.Karatsuba.Cost \
  Complexity.Algorithms.Lean.Query.Arith.Karatsuba.Correctness ComplexityTests.Karatsuba
```

**Not certified here.** Integer Karatsuba as Boolean circuits is a separate development, described in [Integer multiplication circuits](circuits-and-formulas.md#integer-multiplication-circuits). Also not certified here: a native or word-RAM implementation, Toom–Cook (FFT multiplication is in [the next section](#fast-fourier-transform)), and lower bounds on the number of multiplications for polynomial products; see [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md).

## Fast Fourier transform

[DFT.lean](../../Complexity/Foundations/Algebra/DFT.lean) defines `Cslib.dft ω a`, the evaluation of the coefficient vector `a : Fin n → R` at `1, ω, …, ω ^ (n - 1)` over any commutative ring. Mathlib's `ZMod.dft` is complex-valued, built on `ZMod.stdAddChar`. The module proves the cyclic convolution theorem `dft_cyclicConv` (only `ω ^ n = 1` is needed), the inversion formula `dft_dft_neg`, the Cooley–Tukey splitting `dft_eq_add_of_lt` / `dft_eq_sub_of_le`, and `cyclicConv_coeff`, which identifies cyclic convolution with polynomial multiplication when `natDegree p + natDegree q < n`. Inversion needs a *principal* root of unity ([Principal.lean](../../Complexity/Foundations/RingTheory/RootsOfUnity/Principal.lean)): the geometric sums of `ω ^ l` must vanish for `0 < l < n`. In an integral domain primitive roots are principal (`IsPrimitiveRoot.isPrincipalRoot`), and a principal root is primitive whenever `(n : R) ≠ 0` (`IsPrincipalRoot.isPrimitiveRoot`). In general neither implies the other: `4` is a primitive square root of unity in `ZMod 15` but not principal (`1 + 4 ≠ 0`), and `1` is a principal square root of unity in `ZMod 2` but not primitive. The radix-2 hypothesis `ω ^ (2 ^ k) = -1` makes `ω` principal of order `2 ^ (k + 1)` in every commutative ring, with no unit or domain assumption (`IsPrincipalRoot.of_pow_two_pow_eq_neg_one`).

[FFT/Defs.lean](../../Complexity/Algorithms/Lean/Query/Arith/FFT/Defs.lean) writes the recursive radix-2 transform `Cslib.Query.fft` in the arithmetic query model `FreeM (ArithQuery α)`. Ring operations are queries, while index arithmetic, the even/odd split and reading a supplied twiddle table `w : ℕ → α` are free. Correctness ([FFT/Correctness.lean](../../Complexity/Algorithms/Lean/Query/Arith/FFT/Correctness.lean)) assumes `w j = ω ^ j` on the needed range. `Cslib.Query.fftMul` instead builds its table with `2 ^ k - 1` charged multiplications from `ω` and `nInv = (2 ^ (k + 1))⁻¹`. The program's branching depends only on lengths, so the costs ([FFT/Cost.lean](../../Complexity/Algorithms/Lean/Query/Arith/FFT/Cost.lean)) are exact under every oracle:

- the transform of length `n = 2 ^ k` makes exactly `(n / 2) log₂ n` multiplications and `n log₂ n` additions and subtractions (`cost_fft`, `cost_fft_two_pow`);
- FFT multiplication of length `N = 2 ^ (k + 1)` makes `(3 / 2) N log₂ N + (5 / 2) N - 1` multiplications and `3 N log₂ N` additive operations (`cost_fftMul`), at most `7 N log₂ N` queries (`countQueries_fftMul_le`, packaged as `fftMul_upperBound`).

Under `ω ^ (2 ^ k) = -1` and `nInv * 2 ^ (k + 1) = 1`, `eval_fftMul` computes the cyclic convolution. `eval_fftMul_coeff` and `eval_fftMul_eq_mul` give the coefficients of `p * q`, and the polynomial `p * q` itself, when `natDegree p + natDegree q < N`. In an integral domain a primitive `2 ^ (k + 1)`-th root of unity satisfies the hypothesis (`eval_fft_of_isPrimitiveRoot`).

Complete client: [FFT.lean](../../ComplexityTests/FFT.lean). It checks number-theoretic transforms and products over `ZMod 17` at lengths `4` and `8`, a wrap-around product, and the `Complex.I` instance. It also has a counterexample over `ZMod 15` showing that `ω ^ (2 ^ k) = -1` cannot be dropped, and exact query counts under honest and dishonest oracles. Build it with

```bash
lake build Complexity.Algorithms.Lean.Query.Arith.FFT.Cost \
  Complexity.Algorithms.Lean.Query.Arith.FFT.Correctness ComplexityTests.FFT
```

**Not certified here.** These are operation counts in the algebraic query model, not bit complexity and not a circuit size or depth bound. Lengths are fixed powers of two, with no list-level padding wrapper. Rings without suitable roots of unity (Schönhage–Strassen), other radices and FFT lower bounds are open; see [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md).

## Rational formulas and hitting lists

[RatOp](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Rational/Defs.lean#L69) uses rational constants, addition, multiplication and inverse. Evaluation is a relation: an inverse node is defined only when both products with the proposed inverse are one. This supports noncommutative matrix substitution without silently assigning a value at poles. A formula is admissible if some positive-dimensional rational matrix substitution makes it defined, and nonzero if some defined value is nonzero.

The main [hitting-list endpoint](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Rational.lean) produces an explicit executable list of matrix substitutions, with dimension and encoding length polynomial in the variable count plus formula-node bound. Every admissible nonzero formula within the bound has an invertible value at some listed substitution. The source credits [OpenAI's rational-hitting formalization at its pinned revision](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/OAI/Computability/RationalHitting) and records both its source revision and the intermediate Complexitylib port revision. The executable nonzero/admissibility tests are deductions from that result, with no new-priority claim.

The [native generator endpoint](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Rational/Runtime.lean) separately proves polynomial time for producing the encoded list from unary parameters. It specifies a standalone parser: valid positive n and s have input `true^n ++ [false] ++ true^s`, and malformed inputs produce the empty output word. Output encodes matrix dimension and tuple count self-delimitingly, followed by normalized rational data. The statement does **not** assert polynomial bit complexity for evaluating an arbitrary input rational formula on every generated substitution. Generator complexity and identity-test evaluation complexity must remain separate in a paper.

## Quantum circuits, finite codes and uniformity

[Qubits](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/Basic.lean#L92) represents an n-qubit state as complex Euclidean space indexed by n-bit strings. A gate is a unitary on a finite set of qubits, together with an injective ordered placement. A circuit is a list whose semantics is ordered matrix composition. The primitive model is pure unitary evolution with final basis measurement; reversible classical gates, output support and path-sum identities are derived over it.

Arbitrary unitary matrices are semantic objects. The [GateCode](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/Code.lean#L98) interface supplies a finite basis of Hadamard, NOT, controlled-NOT and Toffoli codes, with explicit target/control indices and well-formedness. The [UniformCircuitFamily](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/Uniform.lean#L79) structure requires native polynomial-time generation of both the well-formed gate list and the positive register width from unary input length. The width is at least the input length. Unary wire/width encodings prevent an uncharged exponentially large register address. Inputs are followed by zero ancillas; acceptance measures qubit zero, and postselection conditions on qubit one.

[BQP](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP.lean#L69) uses one such family for a language under a supplied injective instance encoding: every encoded member is accepted with probability at least two thirds, and every encoded nonmember with probability at most one third. Polynomial circuit size follows from the native polynomial-time generator. The model and its gate-set convention are owned here.

The parameterized time classes add an explicit gate bound. In each row below there exist natural constants `c1,c2` and one uniform family whose circuit at **every** length n has at most `c1*t(n)+c2` gates. Acceptance conditions apply to encoded instances; they do not impose a language specification on bitstrings outside the encoding's image.

| Class | Acceptance on an encoded instance |
|---|---|
| [BQTIME](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Time.lean#L86) | Probability at least `2/3` on members and at most `1/3` on nonmembers. |
| [NQTIME](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Time.lean#L95) | Membership is equivalent to strictly positive acceptance probability; no explicit positive gap is required. |
| [EQTIME](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Time.lean#L103) | Acceptance probability is exactly one on members and exactly zero on nonmembers. |
| [PostBQTIME](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Time.lean#L114) | Qubit one reads one with strictly positive probability on every instance, including nonmembers. Conditioned on that event, qubit zero reads one with probability at least `2/3` on members and at most `1/3` on nonmembers. |

The postselected probability is the joint probability for qubits zero and one divided by the probability for qubit one. The raw ratio is totalized to zero when its denominator is zero; the class's separate positivity condition excludes that case. There is no explicit lower bound on the postselection probability beyond strict positivity.

All four definitions retain the **polynomial-time generator ceiling**, whatever t is. Consequently [BQTIME is contained in BQP for every t](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Time.lean#L142), and [BQP equals the union over bounds `t(n)=n^k`](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Time.lean#L147). A superpolynomial t therefore does not turn these definitions into general quantum time classes. Exact acceptance gives both positive acceptance and bounded error with the same gate bound. The exact and positive-acceptance conventions concern this particular finite gate set; approximate gate simulation would not preserve exact zero probabilities. The cited quantum-Turing-machine results `NQP = coC_=P` and `PostBQP = PP` are not proved by this time-class module.

These models connect to tensor networks through [the path-network construction](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/PathNetwork.lean). Their machine-class bridge expresses acceptance as a GapP function divided by `2^h`, with h the number of Hadamard gates, and proves [BQP contained in native AWPP and hence PP](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Counting.lean#L149). The [machine counting account](../machines-and-classes/verifiers-counting-interaction.md) owns those target classes. The library cites the bounded-error equivalence with broader elementary gate sets but does not formalize the required universality and Solovay–Kitaev simulation in this interface.

## Quantum verifiers and QMA

A [UniformVerifierFamily](../../Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/Verifier.lean) is a uniform circuit family together with a witness length, itself computed in polynomial time from the unary input length. On an input word of length n, the circuit starts with the input on qubits `0, …, n-1`, an arbitrary pure witness state on the next `witnessLength n` qubits, and zeros elsewhere. It accepts when the final measurement finds qubit zero in state one. The acceptance probability is a quadratic form in the witness, and on a basis witness it is the ordinary circuit run on the input word followed by the witness bits. [PromiseQMA c s](../../Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/QMA.lean) asks that some unit witness reach at least `c n` on every encoded yes-instance and that every unit witness stay at most `s n` on every encoded no-instance. `QMA` is the `(2/3, 1/3)` decision version.

Witnesses are pure states. The equivalence with mixed witnesses, by convexity of the acceptance probability, is stated in the docstrings but not formalized.

The [NP containment](../../Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/QMA/NP.lean) writes the pair encoding of the input and each basis witness on a fresh block with classical gates, runs a uniform circuit family for the `NP` relation there, and swaps its answer into qubit zero. A certificate is accepted with certainty. When no certificate exists, every basis witness is rejected with certainty, and therefore so is every witness state. Hence promise `NP` lies in `QMA(1, 0)`. `BQP ⊆ QMA` uses verifiers that read no witness.

[Weak error reduction](../../Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/QMA/Amplification.lean) follows Kitaev's parallel repetition (Kitaev–Shen–Vyalyi, Section 14.2; Marriott–Watrous, Theorem 3.2, at the gap `(2/3, 1/3)`): `2r(n)` copies of the verifier read `2r(n)` witness blocks, and the strict majority of their answers is measured. For `r = 6q` this gives completeness `1 - 2^{-q(n)}` and soundness `2^{-q(n)}` for every polynomial q, with the witness length multiplied by `2r(n)`.

Soundness must hold for witnesses entangled across the blocks. The [tensor-power bound](../../Complexity/Foundations/Quantum/TensorPower.lean) proves it by bounding, one copy at a time, the weight of the outcomes with many accepting copies.

[QMA ⊆ PP](../../Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/QMA/Counting.lean) (`QMA_subset_PP`, noted by Kitaev–Watrous, proved by Vyalyi; the proof here follows Marriott–Watrous Theorem 3.4) holds more generally for every constant gap: `PromiseQMA_subset_promiseClass_PP` places `QMA(c, s)` in promise `PP` whenever `s < c`, with no error reduction. Completeness and soundness may also depend on the input length. `PromiseQMA_subset_promiseClass_PP_of_gap` handles an inverse-polynomial gap `c(n) − s(n) ≥ 1/q(n)` when s is computable to precision `1/(2q(n))`. It is a special case of `PromiseQMA_subset_promiseClass_PP_of_grid`, which only asks for a grid point `σ(n)/D(n)` of polynomial mesh with `s(n) ≤ σ(n)/D(n)` and `(σ(n)+1)/D(n) ≤ c(n)`. The proof works with the [acceptance operator](../../Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/AcceptOperator.lean) `Q = B†B`, where B restricts the final state to the accepting outcomes. Q is positive and its quadratic form is the acceptance probability.

A [trace-power bound](../../Complexity/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/TracePower.lean) applies to any positive operator T. If its quadratic form reaches `c ≥ 0` on some unit vector, then `c^j ≤ tr(T^j)`. If the form is at most s on every unit vector, then `tr(T^j) ≤ dim · s^j`. For the verifier's operator Q, `dim = 2^m`, where m is the witness length. Choose naturals B and D with `s ≤ B/D` and `(B+1)/D ≤ c`, computed in polynomial time when c and s depend on the input length, and take a power `d = B(m+1)`. Bernoulli's inequality then makes `((B+1)/D)^d` at least twice `2^m (B/D)^d`. So `tr(Q^d)` lies on opposite sides of `((B+1)/D)^d / 2` on yes-instances and on no-instances.

In the computational basis, `tr(Q^d)` is a sum over closed paths of products of matrix entries (`LinearMap.trace_pow_eq_sum`). Each entry is a [projected pair gap](../../Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/PathSum.lean) divided by `2^h`. Such a gap counts signed pairs of Hadamard–Toffoli paths from two input registers and is a `GapP` function of the instance. Reindexing the closed paths as polynomially long words shows that `2^{hd} tr(Q^d)` is `GapP` (`traceGap_mem_GapP`). Comparing it with the threshold yields a `GapP` function that is positive exactly on the separating set.

Error reduction from an inverse-polynomial gap and Marriott–Watrous strong error reduction that keeps the witness length are not formalized ([#2876](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/issues/2876)). [Checked clients](../../ComplexityTests/QMA.lean) include a verifier whose answer depends on its witness, a language it places in `QMA` with perfect completeness and soundness zero, and the amplified verifier evaluated on basis witnesses. Build them with `lake build ComplexityTests.QMA`. The [trace-gap clients](../../ComplexityTests/QMACounting.lean) compute trace gaps of that verifier by `decide`, apply both `PP` containments, and apply the gap version to completeness 1 and soundness `1 − 1/(n+1)`. Build them with `lake build ComplexityTests.QMACounting`.

## Quantum query algorithms

The quantum query model is separate from uniform circuits. [State](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/Search.lean#L83) has a query-position register, a control bit and finite workspace. A controlled phase oracle multiplies a basis amplitude by the sign determined by the selected input bit and control; arbitrary input-independent linear isometries alternate with oracle calls. Only oracle calls are charged. Workspace size and implementation of those isometries are free in the query count.

The [BBBV theorem](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/Search.lean) gives `N-2 <= 144 T^2` for success at least two thirds on every uniquely marked input. The [polynomial method](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/Polynomial.lean) bounds amplitude degree by T and acceptance-probability degree by 2T, yielding approximate-degree and block-sensitivity consequences. The [spectral adversary method](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/Adversary.lean) gives the corresponding error-dependent adversary lower bound; the derived deterministic-query simulations include a fourth-power bound through the stated sensitivity parameters.

**Ambainis's adversary method.** [AmbainisAdversary.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/AmbainisAdversary.lean) proves the relational adversary bound of Ambainis (STOC 2000; JCSS 2002), Theorem 6, for bit queries. Take a nonempty finite set `R` of input pairs on which one fixed `T`-query algorithm ends with overlaps at most `δ`. Suppose every first input has at least `m` partners and every second input at least `m'`, and `l_{x,i} l_{y,i} ≤ L` on every related pair that differs at `i`. Then `one_sub_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_mul_sqrt` gives `(1 - δ) √(m m') ≤ 2T √L`. Lemma 1 (`norm_inner_le_two_mul_sqrt_of_measureProb`, in [Overlap.lean](../../Complexity/Foundations/Quantum/Measurement/Overlap.lean)) turns error `ε ≤ 1/2` into `δ = 2√(ε(1-ε))`. That gives Theorem 2, `one_sub_two_mul_sqrt_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_mul_sqrt`, and the single-bit-flip case `one_sub_two_mul_sqrt_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_of_neighbors` (`l = l' = 1`). Only inputs occurring in `R` are constrained, so the bounds apply to promise problems. Both inputs of every pair are run on the same algorithm `U, ψ₀`. [Symmetric.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/AmbainisAdversary/Symmetric.lean) derives the bounded-error bounds `N ≤ 36 T` for `PARITY_N` (`card_le_thirty_six_mul_of_parity`) and `N ≤ 72 T` for `MAJ_N` (`card_le_seventy_two_mul_of_majority`). It also proves `(1 - 2√(ε(1-ε))) √(k (N + 1 - k)) ≤ 2T` for algorithms that need only separate weight `k` from weight `k - 1` (`one_sub_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_of_weight`), and for `Th^N_k`. [Tribes.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/AmbainisAdversary/Tribes.lean) proves Theorem 4 for the `AND` of `s` `OR`s of `w` bits, `(1 - 2√(ε(1-ε))) √(w s) ≤ 2T` (`one_sub_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_of_forall_exists`), and its De Morgan dual for `tribes` (`one_sub_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_of_tribes`). Both need correctness only when every block has at most one true (respectively false) bit, and both give `w s ≤ 1296 T²` at error `1/3`. For total functions, the parity and threshold bounds would also follow from the spectral adversary above, since `λ(PARITY_N) = N` and `λ(Th^N_k) = √(k (N - k + 1))`, but the library does not prove these values. The promise forms would not follow. The constants are those of this proof; the source states `Ω(·)`. Not yet formalized: non-Boolean input alphabets (Theorem 7, permutation inversion), the weighted, spectral and negative-weight adversaries for general relations, and the Nayak–Wu promise-threshold bound ([#3203](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/issues/3203)). [Checked clients](../../ComplexityTests/QuantumAmbainisAdversary.lean) verify the counting hypotheses by `decide` on a concrete nonempty relation. They also apply the bounds to `readBit` and to the `OR_N` algorithm `orSearch`, recovering `Ω(√N)` for search. Build them with `lake build ComplexityTests.QuantumAmbainisAdversary`.

[Grover search](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/Grover.lean) proves the exact sine-squared success formula and the square-root query upper bound for a unique marked item. [unknown-count search](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/UnknownSearch.lean) uses a fixed query budget and a purified random iteration count to obtain constant success whenever at least one item is marked, and a bounded-error OR algorithm. Its packaging is not the adaptive expected-runtime BBHT theorem; it is a fixed-budget query result.

**Bit queries and Hadamard transforms.** [BitQuery.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/BitQuery.lean) packages algorithms written with bit queries `|i, b, w⟩ ↦ |i, b ⊕ x_i, w⟩` inside the phase-oracle model, without a second oracle. A Hadamard gate on the control bit before and after a phase query is exactly the basis permutation `bitFlip x` (`hadCtrl_phaseOracle_hadCtrl_eq_permute`). `bitQueryAlg T π prep post` is a preparation isometry, `T` charged queries separated by input-independent basis permutations, and a final isometry. By `run_bitQueryAlg`, its state after the last query is `post` applied to the prepared state permuted by the composite classical permutation `bitQueryPerm π x T`. Each bit query costs exactly one phase query. [HadamardTransform.lean](../../Complexity/Foundations/Quantum/HadamardTransform.lean) gives the isometry `hadamardTransform e`, which applies a Hadamard gate to every qubit of a register `K → Bool`, for a basis split as `e : ι ≃ (K → Bool) × R`.

**Bernstein–Vazirani.** In [BernsteinVazirani.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/BernsteinVazirani.lean) the input is the truth table `linearTable e s` of `a ↦ s ⬝ a`, with positions identified with `{0,1}^n` by `e`. One phase query between two Hadamard transforms leaves the algorithm in the basis state `|s, 1⟩` (`run_bernsteinVazirani`), so the query register gives `s` with probability one. The classical side is in [QueryComplexity/BernsteinVazirani.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/BernsteinVazirani.lean), on the same truth table. Querying the `n` unit vectors recovers `s` (`linearDecodeTree`). The truth table of `a ↦ s ⬝ a` is the Hadamard codeword of `s` read through `e` (`linearTable_eq_hadamardCode_comp`). Because the `2^n` inputs have distinct answers and a tree of depth `q` takes at most `2^q` values, every deterministic tree has depth `≥ n` (`card_le_depth_of_linearTable`). Every randomized tree with error `ε` on each input (on the promise inputs, for any total `F` extending the answer) satisfies `2^n (1 − ε) ≤ 2^q` (`linearTable_randomized_lower_bound`), so `q ≥ n` for `ε < 1/2`. `complexity_linearDecode` and `randomizedComplexity_linearDecode` give `D = R_ε = n` for `0 ≤ ε < 1/2`. `bernsteinVazirani_separation` combines the two sides. The lower bounds use the generic counting lemmas `DecisionTree.card_le_two_pow_depth_of_injOn` and `RandomizedDecisionTree.card_mul_one_sub_le_two_pow_depth` ([Randomized/Counting.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Randomized/Counting.lean)), which apply to any search problem with distinct answers. [ComplexityTests/BernsteinVazirani.lean](../../ComplexityTests/BernsteinVazirani.lean) shows the threshold `1/2` is sharp: at `n = 1`, uniform guessing has depth `0` and error exactly `1/2`.

**Simon's problem and the exponential separation.** Both sides use the same input: the truth table `X : Fin N → Bool` of the `N = n 2^n` output bits of `f : {0,1}^n → {0,1}^n`, indexed by `E : Fin N ≃ {0,1}^n × Fin n`. One value query costs `n` bit queries. [QueryComplexity/Simon.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Simon.lean) states Simon's promise `SimonPromise f s` (`f a = f b` exactly when `b ∈ {a, a ⊕ s}`) and the decision task "is `s ≠ 0`". The library has no promise-problem type for query complexity, so the classical theorem takes a total `F` that agrees with that answer on promise inputs and a randomized decision tree whose error is at most `ε` on every promise input. `simon_randomized_lower_bound` proves `2 (2^n − 1)(1 − 2ε) ≤ (q² − q)(1 − ε)` for depth `q`. At `ε = 1/3` this gives `2^n ≤ q²` (`simon_two_pow_le_depth_sq`) and `2^n ≤ R_{1/3}(F)²` for every such total `F`. The proof is a coupling over the hard inputs `π ∘ rep_s`, in the spirit of de Wolf's birthday argument. [Quantum/Simon.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/Simon.lean) runs `m` rounds of Simon's subroutine side by side as one bit-query algorithm, `simonAlg m E i₀`. It accepts when the measured rows have a common nonzero orthogonal vector, a rank test needing no further queries. It accepts with certainty when `s ≠ 0`, and with probability at most `(2^n − 1)/2^m` when `f` is injective. With `m = n + 2`, `simonAlg_spec` gives `n (n + 2)` queries with one-sided error below `1/4`. `simon_separation` combines the two bounds. `simon_separation_inputLength` states the separation in the input length: the same algorithm is correct with `n (n + 2) ≤ 3 log₂² N` queries, against `N ≤ q² log₂ N`, that is `q ≥ N^{1/2 − o(1)}`. Both sides count bit queries, so the comparison is like for like; the quantum side pays a factor `n` over de Wolf's `n + 2` value queries. Simulating value queries by bit queries transfers the classical bound to value queries only up to that factor (`q_v ≥ 2^{n/2}/n`). The coupling argument uses only that `q` queries touch at most `q` inputs `a`, so it would give `2^n ≤ q_v²` directly, but that is not formalized. [Quantum/SimonSearch.lean](../../Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/SimonSearch.lean) proves the search version. The classical post-processing `simonSolve` outputs the unique nonzero string orthogonal to all measured rows, or `0`, and outputs `s` whenever the strings orthogonal to the rows are exactly `0` and `s` (`SimonDetermines`, `simonSolve_eq`). For `s ≠ 0`, the general bound `measureProb_simonAlg_le` with fibers of size at most `2^m` (`card_simonG_fiber_le`) and the pair count `BitString.card_kernel_pair` bound the failure probability by `(2^n − 1)/2^m`; for `s = 0` failure is acceptance by the decision version. So `le_measureProb_simonFound` gives success probability at least `1 − (2^n − 1)/2^m` on every promise input, including `s = 0` (injective `f`, answer `0`), and `simonAlg_search_spec` gives `n (n + 2)` queries with success probability above `3/4`. de Wolf §3.1 states the search problem with the promise `s ≠ 0`. On the classical side, `simon_search_ne_zero_two_pow_le_sq` proves `2^n ≤ (q + 2n)²` for trees that find `s` with error `1/3` on the promise inputs with `s ≠ 0`, with no requirement when `s = 0`. Each leaf `s'` is extended by the `2n` bit queries of `f(0)` and `f(s')`, answering whether `s' ≠ 0` and `f(0) = f(s')` (`RandomizedDecisionTree.mapTree`, `depth_mapTree_le`, `errorProb_mapTree_le`). This answers `false` on every injective input, so it decides Simon's problem with no larger error. `simon_search_separation` combines this bound with the quantum one. The variant that also requires the output `0` when `s = 0` has the cleaner bound `2^n ≤ q²` (`simon_search_two_pow_le_depth_sq`, by post-processing the output with `s ↦ (s ≠ 0)` through `RandomizedDecisionTree.mapOutput`). Since that variant constrains more inputs, its lower bound is weaker. `simonSolve` searches all `2^n` strings; it makes no queries, but polynomial-time post-processing by Gaussian elimination is not formalized. Complete clients: [QuantumSimon.lean](../../ComplexityTests/QuantumSimon.lean), [QuantumSimonSearch.lean](../../ComplexityTests/QuantumSimonSearch.lean) and [QuantumBernsteinVazirani.lean](../../ComplexityTests/QuantumBernsteinVazirani.lean). Build them with

## Known Limitations

The selected definitions and headline statements have been source-inspected; the full algebraic hitting, contraction, and quantum proof chains have not been independently rederived or rebuilt in this worker pass. In particular, arbitrary field/real/unitary parameters are not finite encodings, semantic operations are not native instructions, a query upper bound is not a gate or bit-time upper bound, and a tensor rank or field-circuit gate bound is not an integer bit-cost bound.
