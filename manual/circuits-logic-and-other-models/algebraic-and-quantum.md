# Algebraic and quantum models

Algebraic and quantum developments reuse the generic circuit architecture while changing both semantics and what resources are free. Formal polynomial equality, exact rational matrix evaluation and unitary-state evolution are distinct notions of correctness. Their bridges to encoded native computations are explicit developments rather than consequences of using Lean's executable definitions. [cslib-frontier][complexitylib]

## Arithmetic, affine and full bases

[ArithOp](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic.lean#L82) uses constants, binary addition and multiplication. Interpretation over a polynomial ring produces a formal polynomial, and evaluation homomorphisms transport it to values in another carrier. Formal polynomial equality is the appropriate algebraic identity notion; over a finite field, different formal polynomials can define the same function. Separate addition/multiplication weights permit statements about multiplication complexity. Scalar multiplication is still multiplication when represented as such.

[affineSignature](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic.lean#L67) makes an entire arbitrary-arity affine combination one gate. The [FullOp](https://github.com/leanprover/cslib/blob/254e30e6d4299c51323aaeaf5f51a4763b375d4a/Cslib/Computability/Circuit/Signature.lean#L37) model is broader still: any operation depending on at most a fixed number of values is primitive. Over the full binary basis, tests and selections can cost one gate, and `Synthesis.full_sum`, `full_dot` and `full_chain` bound counts of these operations. An algebraic operation count in that model is not automatically a bound in the restricted addition/multiplication basis, nor a bound on binary integer/rational computation. [cslib][cslib] See [full-basis synthesis](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/FullArithmetic.lean).

[SigmaPiOp](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/SigmaPi.lean#L109) permits unbounded sums with edge coefficients and unbounded products. Product depth counts multiplication layers only; weighted sums contribute zero to that depth. Set-multilinearity records that products combine disjoint variable groups. [SPCircuit](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/SPCircuit.lean#L89) provides shared DAGs in this basis. Its `IsSimple` condition requires distinct input wires at product gates; the unfolding bound used by the constant-product-depth lower-bound development assumes this condition.

The [constant-depth arithmetic abstract](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/abstracts/CslibFrontierAbstracts/ConstantDepthArithmeticAbstract.lean), since retired, states an iterated-matrix-multiplication lower bound over any field for the specified simple Sigma–Pi circuits and product-depth convention. Its finite exponent involves the matrix-chain parameter and `4^Delta-1`; the asymptotic regime chooses the chain length roughly `log log n`. Source annotations credit the Limaye–Srinivasan–Tavenas development and the stated reduction through set-multilinearization. This survey does not remove the simplicity hypothesis or identify the bound with unrestricted arbitrary repeated-wire product syntax.

## Valiant classes and term models

[PolynomialFamily](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/VP.lean#L188) stores a variable-count function and a polynomial at each family index, with no bounds. `PolynomialFamily.IsPFamily` requires the variable count and actual total degree to be polynomially bounded in the family index. `VP` additionally requires circuits of polynomially bounded size computing the family's formal polynomials. The class is nonuniform and includes degree as an independent restriction. The library's repeated-squaring example has small circuits but exponential degree and is therefore excluded from VP. A p-projection substitutes variables/constants and permits a polynomially bounded family reindexing.

[VNP](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/VNP.lean#L112) is defined by a Boolean sum of a VP verifier family over auxiliary variables. It is meaningful over a commutative semiring, while particular membership theorems may require a ring or field. The library proves VP inclusion, p-projection closure, permanent membership and the usual consequence that a VNP-complete family lies in VP exactly when VNP is contained in VP. The pinned VNP module explicitly states that permanent VNP-completeness is not proved there. This boundary matters to a paper cataloguing achieved endpoints rather than familiar theorem names.

[Op](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/SumOfTerms/Basic.lean#L47) counts dictionary terms while additions are free; [Term](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Waring/Basic.lean#L39) specializes the terms to scalar multiples of powers of homogeneous linear forms. These term counts have their own lower-bound and compilation APIs. They must be translated before being compared with arithmetic gate counts.

## Reverse-mode differentiation and lower-bound transfer

[Circuit.withGradient](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Gradient.lean) is the Baur–Strassen construction. It keeps every output of an `arithSignature K` circuit and appends the partial derivatives of one chosen output, scaled by a seed constant. The size is exactly `s + 2 + 2·#additions + 4·#multiplications`, so at most `5s + 2`; the two extra gates are the seed and zero constants. The multiplication count is exactly three times the original. That count includes multiplications by constants, which Baur and Strassen's nonscalar measure does not count. There is no subtraction, so correctness (`Circuit.polynomial_withGradient_natAdd`) holds formally over every commutative semiring. `Circuit.exists_gradient` packages the result as a circuit computing `f` and all `∂f/∂xᵢ`. To transfer a lower bound for the gradient of `f` to `f` itself, use `Circuit.le_five_mul_size_add_two_of_gradient` or `Circuit.le_three_mul_mulCost_of_gradient`. For the power sum `∑ X i ^ (d + 1)` with `d + 1` invertible, `Circuit.le_three_mul_mulCost_of_psum` reduces to the power map. Strassen's degree bound, which would supply the `n log d` hypothesis there, is not formalized. In [the adjugate module](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Adjugate.lean), `pderiv_det_genericMatrix` identifies each partial derivative of the generic determinant with an adjugate entry, and `exists_circuit_adjugate_of_det` turns determinant circuits into adjugate circuits with the same `5s + 2` bound; `exists_circuit_adjugate` gives adjugate circuits with at most `15n⁴ + 22` gates over every commutative ring. Executable checks are in [the gradient tests](../../CslibFrontierTests/ArithmeticGradient.lean).

## Determinantal complexity

[HasDeterminantalRepresentation](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/DeterminantalComplexity.lean) `f m` says that `f` is the determinant of an `m × m` matrix whose entries have total degree at most one. `determinantalComplexity f : ℕ∞` is the least such `m`. `⊤` means that no representation exists. Valiant's universality theorem, which makes the determinantal complexity of every polynomial finite, is not formalized, so finiteness is known here only for specific polynomials such as the permanent. A projection of the generic `m × m` determinant has a representation of size `m` (`hasDeterminantalRepresentation_of_isProjection`), and the determinant of a matrix of variables and constants is such a projection (`isProjection_det_of_forall_exists_eq`). Grenet's theorem [grenet11][grenet11] is `isProjection_permanent_det_two_pow_sub_one`: over every commutative ring, the generic `n × n` permanent is a projection of the generic `(2ⁿ - 1) × (2ⁿ - 1)` determinant, so `determinantalComplexity_permanent_le` gives `dc(perm_n) ≤ 2ⁿ - 1`. Grenet states characteristic other than two. In [the Grenet module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/GrenetPermanent.lean), `Matrix.permanent_eq_det_grenetMatrix` proves `A.permanent = A.grenetMatrix.det` for every square matrix over a commutative ring. Grenet's branching program on the subsets of `{0, …, n - 1}`, numbered in colex order, is turned into a determinant by the adjugate identity of [the branching-program module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/BranchingProgramDeterminant.lean), `Matrix.eq_det_submatrix_sub_one_of_eq_single_add_vecMul`. The construction is Grenet's with a different sign normalization: `-1` on the whole diagonal instead of loops of weight `1` with a parity fix. The Mignon–Ressayre theorem [mignonressayre04][mignonressayre04] is `sq_le_two_mul_determinantalComplexity_permanent_of_charZero`: `n² ≤ 2 · dc(perm_n)` over every field of characteristic zero, so `n² / 2 ≤ dc(perm_n) ≤ 2ⁿ - 1`. The proof compares Hessian ranks at a zero of the permanent. In [the determinant Hessian module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/HessianDeterminant.lean), `Matrix.rank_hessian_det_le` bounds the Hessian of the determinant of any `m × m` matrix of affine polynomials by rank `2m` at every zero of the determinant, over every field. It reduces to a singular matrix with a zero column through the affine chain rule `MvPolynomial.rank_hessian_aeval_le`. In [the permanent Hessian module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/HessianPermanent.lean), `Matrix.rank_hessian_permanent_mignonRessayrePoint` shows that the permanent's Hessian has full rank `n²` at the all-ones matrix with `1 - n` in one corner, for `n ≥ 2` and `(n - 1)! ≠ 0` in the field. Its invertibility is proved by a sandwich identity in the matrix algebra, not by eigenvalues. **Hypotheses:** the general statement `sq_le_two_mul_determinantalComplexity_permanent` only assumes `(n - 1)! ≠ 0`, that is, characteristic zero or a characteristic at least `n`. Mignon and Ressayre state characteristic zero. To bound a concrete matrix, apply the representation forms `sq_le_two_mul_of_hasDeterminantalRepresentation_permanent` or `Matrix.card_mul_card_le_two_mul_card_of_permanent_eq_det`. The Cai–Chen–Li extension to every characteristic other than two, Yabe's bound `(n - 1)² + 1` over the reals, and border determinantal complexity are not formalized. In characteristic two the permanent is the determinant, so the bound fails at `n = 3`, where `(n - 1)! = 0`; the factorial hypothesis cannot be dropped. [The tests](../../CslibFrontierTests/ArithmeticDeterminantalComplexity.lean) check this, show `dc(perm₂) = 2` and `5 ≤ dc(perm₃) ≤ 7` over `ℚ`, evaluate the `3 × 3` Hessian, of rank `9`, and evaluate the determinant of Grenet's matrix of a `2 × 2` integer matrix.

## Non-commutative branching programs

[NoncommABP](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Noncommutative/ABP.lean) is a homogeneous layered non-commutative algebraic branching program of degree `d`. It has layers `0, …, d` of `width k` vertices, one source and one sink, and each edge carries a homogeneous linear form `∑ x, weight k x i j • x`. The computed polynomial lies in Mathlib's `FreeAlgebra K X`, read through the word coefficients `FreeAlgebra.coeff` of [the free-algebra coefficient module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/FreeAlgebra.lean). Nisan's matrix `nisanMatrix k m f` has rows indexed by the words of length `k`, columns by the words of length `m`, and entry the coefficient of `u v` in `f`. `NoncommABP.nisanMatrix_eq_mul` factors it through layer `k`, so `NoncommABP.rank_nisanMatrix_le_width` gives width at least `rank M_k(f)`. In [the construction module](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Noncommutative/Nisan.lean), `NoncommABP.exists_width_eq_rank` builds, for every nonzero homogeneous `f` of degree `d ≥ 1`, an ABP with width exactly `rank M_k(f)` in every layer, using bases of the spans of the rows. `NoncommABP.isLeast_size` is Nisan's theorem: the least size is `∑_{k ≤ d} rank M_k(f)`. **Size convention:** the size counts every vertex, source and sink included, and the sum runs over `0 ≤ k ≤ d`, as in Nisan's Definition 1 and Theorem 1. The displayed statement of Theorem 11.2 in Saptharishi's survey leaves out the final term `rank M_d(f) = 1`. The model is homogeneous and layered. `f = 0` is excluded from exactness because its Nisan matrices have rank `0`, while every ABP of positive degree has a source and a sink.

[The palindrome module](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Noncommutative/Palindrome.lean) gives Nisan's explicit hard polynomials, the palindrome `Pal_{2m} = ∑_w w · reverse(w)` and the double `Doub_{2m} = ∑_w w · w`. Both are instances of `pairedWordPoly σ = ∑_u u · σ(u)` (`palindrome_eq_pairedWordPoly`, `doubled_eq_pairedWordPoly`), with coefficient, homogeneity and nonvanishing lemmas for each. Their middle Nisan matrix is a permutation of the identity, so its rank is `n ^ m` (`rank_nisanMatrix_pairedWordPoly`). Hence `NoncommABP.pow_le_width_of_polynomial_eq_palindrome` and `NoncommABP.pow_le_width_of_polynomial_eq_doubled` give at least `n ^ m` vertices in the middle layer. `Circuit.noncommPolynomial` in [the circuit module](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Noncommutative/Circuit.lean) reads an `arithSignature K` circuit as a non-commutative polynomial by evaluating it in `FreeAlgebra K (Fin n)`. Evaluation in any `K`-algebra substitutes into it (`Circuit.lift_noncommPolynomial`), and substituting commuting variables gives `Circuit.polynomial` (`Circuit.lift_X_noncommPolynomial`). `palCircuit K n m` computes `Pal_{2m}` with exactly `(3n + 1) m + 1` gates through the recursion `Pal_{2m+2} = ∑_x x · Pal_{2m} · x`, which is the recursion of Nisan's Theorem 4 extended from two variables to `n`. `palindrome_separation` packages the separation between non-commutative circuits and homogeneous layered non-commutative ABPs, which is exponential in `m` for `n ≥ 2` variables. The survey states the ABP bound as `n^Ω(d)`; here it is the explicit `n ^ (d / 2)`. Not formalized: the reduction from general non-commutative ABPs (survey Exercise 11.1), the non-commutative formula lower bounds, and ROABPs. Tests are in [the ABP tests](../../CslibFrontierTests/ArithmeticNoncommutativeABP.lean), where the least ABP size is exactly `3` for `x₀ x₁` and exactly `4` for `x₀ x₀ + x₁ x₁`, which needs a middle layer of width `2`, and in [the palindrome tests](../../CslibFrontierTests/ArithmeticNoncommutativePalindrome.lean), where `Pal_2` over two variables is `x₀ x₀ + x₁ x₁`, `rank M_2(Pal_4) = 9` over three variables, and the separation is shown at `n = m = 10`.

## Matrix multiplication exponent

Import [NineQuarters.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/MatrixMultiplication/NineQuarters.lean) for the endpoint and [Exponent.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/MatrixMultiplication/Exponent.lean) for the definitions. `ComputesMatMul K C` says that a circuit `C` over `arithSignature K` with `2 n ^ 2` inputs and `n ^ 2` outputs, fed the row-major entries of `A` followed by those of `B`, outputs the entries of `A * B` for **every** pair of `n × n` matrices over `K`. Correctness is exact matrix equality in the field, not formal polynomial identity, and the model is nonuniform: one circuit per `n`, with no generator. `AdmissibleExponent K τ` requires, for every `ε > 0`, a constant `c` and circuits of size at most `c * n ^ (τ + ε)` for every `n ≥ 1`; `omega K` is the infimum of the admissible exponents. Size is cslib's `Circuit.size`, the total number of gates: additions, multiplications **and** constants cost one each, while inputs and outputs are free. Every admissible exponent is at least `2`, one gate per output, so the infimum is over a set bounded below; the trivial algorithm gives `3`.

The endpoint [omega_le_nine_quarters](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/MatrixMultiplication/NineQuarters.lean) states `omega K ≤ 9 / 4` for every `[Field K] [CharZero K]`, and `admissibleExponent_nine_quarters` supplies circuits of size `O(n ^ (9/4 + γ))` for every `γ > 0`, following [Pass's constructive proof](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28627) of OpenAI's bound. Characteristic zero is a genuine hypothesis: the interpolation nodes are the integers `0, 1, 2, …`, which must be distinct in `K`; the paper works over `ℚ`. The statement depends only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

**Rank is not a gate count.** The tensor layer in [Tensor/Basic.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Tensor/Basic.lean) states bilinear algorithms: `Tensor.Rank r T` means that `T` is a restriction of the unit tensor `unit r`, a bilinear algorithm with `r` scalar multiplications and arbitrary, uncounted linear encodings of the inputs and decoding of the outputs. `Tensor.mm p q r` is the matrix multiplication tensor, `rank_mm` its trivial bound `p q r`, and `Rank 7 (mm 2 2 2)` is Strassen's algorithm. A rank bound therefore counts only the multiplications of the bilinear step and is not a bound on any circuit's gate count. The bridge is `admissibleExponent_of_rank`: from `Rank r (mm v v v)` with `2 ≤ v`, `r ≤ v ^ τ` and `2 < τ`, block recursion gives total-gate bounds `O(n ^ (τ + ε))` for every `ε > 0`, through the recursion `S (j + 1) ≤ r S j + 9 r (v ^ (j + 1)) ^ 2 + 1` and zero padding to the next power of `v`. Degenerations over `K[ε]` (`Tensor.IsDegen`, in [Tensor/Degeneration.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Tensor/Degeneration.lean)) and Bini's conversion `IsDegen.exists_rank_tpow` turn border-rank bounds into rank bounds on tensor powers; the `9/4` identity is obtained this way, so its side `v` and its multiplication count `r` are existential.

Complete clients: [MatrixMultiplicationExponent.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/MatrixMultiplicationExponent.lean) checks `omega ℚ ≤ 9 / 4`, `omega ℝ ≤ 9 / 4`, `AdmissibleExponent ℚ (9 / 4 + 1 / 100)`, the trivial exponent `3` both directly and through block recursion from `Rank 8 (mm 2 2 2)`, and `2 ≤ omega ℚ`; [TensorBasic.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/TensorBasic.lean) checks the bilinear maps of `mm` and of the polynomial product, Strassen's witness of `Rank 7 (mm 2 2 2)`, the interpolation bound `Rank 3 (full 2 2)` and an instance of the primal packing. Build them with

```bash
lake build CslibFrontier.Computability.Circuit.Arithmetic.MatrixMultiplication.NineQuarters \
  CslibFrontierTests.MatrixMultiplicationExponent CslibFrontierTests.TensorBasic
```

**Not certified here.** The paper's Theorem 1.1 is an integer algorithm with bit cost `O(n^(9/4+γ) M(B + log n))` on `B`-bit entries ([§4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28627v2#page=20)); the formalization proves the field-circuit statement only and says nothing about integer arithmetic, division by the cleared denominators or bit complexity. The paper's "computable" side `v` and count `r` are obtained here as existentials (`Tensor.exists_rank_mm_lt_rpow`), with no extraction procedure and no explicit constants; the parameters are galactic. Degenerations compose at the order `(h + 1) (k + 1) - 1` rather than the paper's additive bookkeeping, the gap parameter is `⌊w₀ / c ^ d⌋ ^ 4` rather than `⌊w / u⌋ ^ 2`, and the lengths of the rounds are bounded explicitly rather than by a limit argument; none of this changes the exponent. Positive characteristic, the rectangular and dual exponents and any lower bound beyond `2 ≤ omega K` are open; see [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md).

Until the formal abstracts were retired on 2026-11-08, the
[matrix multiplication abstract](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/f65cf6aabf60f13270367fea7d080141c7a910f5/abstracts/CslibFrontierAbstracts/MatrixMultiplicationAbstract.lean)
restated the exact circuit bound and infimum with visible quantifiers. The current statements are
those of [NineQuarters.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/MatrixMultiplication/NineQuarters.lean),
built by the command above.

## Homogeneous depth-five circuits for iterated matrix multiplication

Import [LowerBound.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/DepthFive/LowerBound.lean) for the endpoint and [Model.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/DepthFive/Model.lean) for the circuit model. `Cslib.Circuits.DepthFive.Circuit K σ` is a syntactically homogeneous `ΣΠΣΠΣ` circuit in normalized form: a *lower product gate* multiplies homogeneous linear forms in the variables `σ`, a *middle sum gate* is a linear combination of lower product gates of one degree, an *upper product gate* multiplies middle sum gates with repetition allowed, and the output is a linear combination of upper product gates of one degree. Gates are shared freely. `Circuit.polynomial` is the formal polynomial computed, and `Circuit.size` counts the lower product, middle sum and upper product gates. The bottom sum gates, variable leaves and output gate of a layered circuit are not counted, so `size` is at most the gate count of the source and a lower bound on it is a lower bound there.

The endpoint [exists_forall_rpow_le_size](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/DepthFive/LowerBound.lean) states that there is an absolute `n₀` such that, for every `[Field K] [CharZero K]` and every `n ≥ n₀`, every `c : Circuit K (Fin n × Fin n × Fin n)` with `c.polynomial = imm K n n` satisfies `n ^ (√n / 400) ≤ c.size`. Here `imm K n n` is the entry `(0, 0)` of the product of `n` generic `n × n` matrices, from [WordPolynomial.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/WordPolynomial.lean), and correctness is equality of formal polynomials. `rpow_le_size` is the same bound with the explicit threshold `n ≥ exp 29200`. The matching construction is [exists_circuit_size_le_rpow](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/DepthFive/UpperBound.lean): over every commutative semiring and for every `n ≥ 2`, some `Circuit` with `polynomial = imm K n n` has `size ≤ n ^ (√n + 4)`, so over characteristic zero the size is `n ^ Θ(√n)`. The statements depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. They follow an unrefereed [OpenAI preprint](https://github.com/openai/math); the proofs here are independent of its Lean development.

**How the proof is organized.** The measure `MvPolynomial.rankMeasure s km ab g` in [RankMeasure.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/DepthFive/RankMeasure.lean) is the rank, on the polynomials of bidegree `ab`, of the operator that differentiates in the variables `s` and multiplies by the others according to the component of `g` of bidegree `km` ([MixedOperator.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/MvPolynomial/MixedOperator.lean), [Bihomogeneous.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/MvPolynomial/Bihomogeneous.lean)). `rankMeasure_circuit_le` bounds it for circuits and `rankMeasure_imm_ge` bounds it from below for `imm`, through the inequality `(tr AᵀA)² ≤ rank A · tr (AᵀA)²` over `ℝ` and a transfer of rank to any field of characteristic zero. Both are proved for every parameter choice satisfying `Params.Good`, a list of explicit inequalities in [Params.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/DepthFive/Params.lean); `good_paramsOf` proves them for the source's parameters when `n ≥ 2 ^ 20`. To reuse the method for another polynomial, prove a lower bound on `rankMeasure` for it and combine it with `rankMeasure_circuit_le` as `rpow_le_size_of_good` does.

**Building circuits.** Write a construction with named gates and let [Circuit.ofFintype](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/DepthFive/OfFintype.lean) number them: it takes gate data indexed by any finite types, and `polynomial_ofFintype` and `size_ofFintype` give the polynomial and the size in terms of those names. [exists_circuit_list_prod](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/DepthFive/MatrixProduct.lean) turns entry `(0, 0)` of a product of `r` matrices of size `ν`, whose entries are combinations of monomials of one degree indexed by `Λ`, into a circuit of size `r * |Λ| + r * ν ^ 2 + ν ^ (r + 1)`. The upper bound applies it to blocks of `Nat.sqrt n + 1` consecutive layers of `IMM`; `exists_circuit_imm_of_le_mul` gives the exact size for any block length.

Complete clients: [DepthFive.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/DepthFive.lean) builds a six-gate circuit for `X 0 * X 1 + X 1 ^ 2` that repeats a gate inside a product, and applies the lower bound over `ℚ` and `ℝ`; [DepthFiveUpper.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/DepthFiveUpper.lean) gives a sixteen-gate circuit for `IMM_{2,2}` and both bounds over `ℚ` with one threshold. Build them with

```bash
lake build CslibFrontier.Computability.Circuit.Arithmetic.DepthFive.LowerBound \
  CslibFrontier.Computability.Circuit.Arithmetic.DepthFive.UpperBound \
  CslibFrontierTests.Computability.Circuit.Arithmetic.DepthFive \
  CslibFrontierTests.Computability.Circuit.Arithmetic.DepthFiveUpper
```

**Not certified here.** The upper bound counts `n ^ (r + 1)` upper product gates where the source has `n ^ (r - 1)`, and does not count leaves or bottom sums; both counts are below `n ^ (√n + 4)`. There is no conversion theorem from a layered circuit with explicit bottom sum gates to `Circuit`; the correspondence is described in the module documentation of the model. Positive characteristic is open: the trace argument uses characteristic zero. The threshold `exp 29200` and the constant `400` are not optimized.

## Karatsuba polynomial multiplication

[Karatsuba/Defs.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Query/Arith/Karatsuba/Defs.lean) writes `Cslib.Query.karatsuba` in cslib's arithmetic query model `FreeM (ArithQuery α)`. The model is a program, not a circuit: every coefficient addition, subtraction and multiplication is an oracle query, and list splitting, zero padding and concatenation are free. The program is generic in `α`, assuming only `[Zero α]` for padding, so it can compute on coefficients only through queries. Its branching depends only on lengths, so the cost theorems hold under **every** oracle. Correctness ([Correctness.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Query/Arith/Karatsuba/Correctness.lean)) uses the honest oracle of a commutative ring: `ofCoeffList_eval_karatsuba` identifies the output, via `Polynomial.ofCoeffList`, with the product of the input polynomials. The output has `2 max(|p|, |q|) - 1` coefficients, so it can end in zeros.

[Cost.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Query/Arith/Karatsuba/Cost.lean) gives the exact cost (`cost_karatsuba`). With `N = max(|p|, |q|)`, there are `mulCount N` multiplications and `addCount N` additions and subtractions, given by `M(n) = M(⌊n/2⌋) + 2 M(⌈n/2⌉)` and `A(n) = A(⌊n/2⌋) + 2 A(⌈n/2⌉) + 4n - 3`. The proved bounds are:

- the closed forms `mulCount (2^k) = 3^k` and `2 addCount (2^k) + 16·2^k = 13·3^k + 3`;
- the total `mulCount n + addCount n ≤ 9·3^⌈log₂ n⌉ ≤ 27 n^log₂ 3`, packaged as `karatsuba_upperBound` for `Cslib.Query.UpperBound`;
- a lower bound `n^log₂ 3 ≤ 3 mulCount n`, with `karatsuba_lowerBound` recording for `Cslib.Query.LowerBound` that every length attains the exact count;
- `mulCount n < n²` for `n ≥ 2`.

The upper bounds come from a reusable halving-recurrence lemma, [HalvingRecurrence.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/Nat/HalvingRecurrence.lean) (`Nat.add_mul_two_pow_clog_le_of_halving`). It proves the recurrence by substitution with a subtracted linear term and needs no monotonicity. [LogTwoRpow.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/Nat/LogTwoRpow.lean) converts `a^⌈log₂ n⌉` and `a^⌊log₂ n⌋` to `n^log₂ a`, as base-`2` cases of the general base-`b` lemmas used by the [master theorem](../algorithms-and-memory/sorting-and-recurrences.md#divide-and-conquer-recurrences).

These are operation counts, not bit complexity. One recursion level is a bilinear algorithm with three products, valid over any commutative ring. It gives the same bound as the tensor layer's `Rank 3 (full 2 2)`, which `Tensor.rank_full` proves differently, by interpolation over a field of characteristic zero; no bridge between the two is formalized. A tensor rank counts only products, while this program also counts every addition and subtraction of the recursion.

Complete client: [Karatsuba.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/Karatsuba.lean). It checks concrete integer products, including unequal lengths, an uneven split and empty operands, and the symbolic three-multiplication formula. It also checks exact counts against the closed forms, and that the count is strictly below `n²` except at `n = 1`. ## Fast Fourier transform

[DFT.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Algebra/DFT.lean) defines `Cslib.dft ω a`, the evaluation of the coefficient vector `a : Fin n → R` at `1, ω, …, ω ^ (n - 1)` over any commutative ring. Mathlib's `ZMod.dft` is complex-valued, built on `ZMod.stdAddChar`. The module proves the cyclic convolution theorem `dft_cyclicConv` (only `ω ^ n = 1` is needed), the inversion formula `dft_dft_neg`, the Cooley–Tukey splitting `dft_eq_add_of_lt` / `dft_eq_sub_of_le`, and `cyclicConv_coeff`, which identifies cyclic convolution with polynomial multiplication when `natDegree p + natDegree q < n`. Inversion needs a *principal* root of unity ([Principal.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/RingTheory/RootsOfUnity/Principal.lean)): the geometric sums of `ω ^ l` must vanish for `0 < l < n`. In an integral domain primitive roots are principal (`IsPrimitiveRoot.isPrincipalRoot`), and a principal root is primitive whenever `(n : R) ≠ 0` (`IsPrincipalRoot.isPrimitiveRoot`). In general neither implies the other: `4` is a primitive square root of unity in `ZMod 15` but not principal (`1 + 4 ≠ 0`), and `1` is a principal square root of unity in `ZMod 2` but not primitive. The radix-2 hypothesis `ω ^ (2 ^ k) = -1` makes `ω` principal of order `2 ^ (k + 1)` in every commutative ring, with no unit or domain assumption (`IsPrincipalRoot.of_pow_two_pow_eq_neg_one`).

[FFT/Defs.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Query/Arith/FFT/Defs.lean) writes the recursive radix-2 transform `Cslib.Query.fft` in the arithmetic query model `FreeM (ArithQuery α)`. Ring operations are queries, while index arithmetic, the even/odd split and reading a supplied twiddle table `w : ℕ → α` are free. Correctness ([FFT/Correctness.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Query/Arith/FFT/Correctness.lean)) assumes `w j = ω ^ j` on the needed range. `Cslib.Query.fftMul` instead builds its table with `2 ^ k - 1` charged multiplications from `ω` and `nInv = (2 ^ (k + 1))⁻¹`. The program's branching depends only on lengths, so the costs ([FFT/Cost.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Lean/Query/Arith/FFT/Cost.lean)) are exact under every oracle:

- the transform of length `n = 2 ^ k` makes exactly `(n / 2) log₂ n` multiplications and `n log₂ n` additions and subtractions (`cost_fft`, `cost_fft_two_pow`);
- FFT multiplication of length `N = 2 ^ (k + 1)` makes `(3 / 2) N log₂ N + (5 / 2) N - 1` multiplications and `3 N log₂ N` additive operations (`cost_fftMul`), at most `7 N log₂ N` queries (`countQueries_fftMul_le`, packaged as `fftMul_upperBound`).

Under `ω ^ (2 ^ k) = -1` and `nInv * 2 ^ (k + 1) = 1`, `eval_fftMul` computes the cyclic convolution. `eval_fftMul_coeff` and `eval_fftMul_eq_mul` give the coefficients of `p * q`, and the polynomial `p * q` itself, when `natDegree p + natDegree q < N`. In an integral domain a primitive `2 ^ (k + 1)`-th root of unity satisfies the hypothesis (`eval_fft_of_isPrimitiveRoot`).

Complete client: [FFT.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/FFT.lean). It checks number-theoretic transforms and products over `ZMod 17` at lengths `4` and `8`, a wrap-around product, and the `Complex.I` instance. It also has a counterexample over `ZMod 15` showing that `ω ^ (2 ^ k) = -1` cannot be dropped, and exact query counts under honest and dishonest oracles. Build it with

```bash

  CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Lean.Query.Arith.FFT.Correctness CslibFrontierTests.FFT
```

## Kronecker powers, group circulants and linear measurements

[KroneckerRank.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/KroneckerRank.lean) proves the nonsingular-minor characterization `Matrix.exists_submatrix_det_ne_zero`, exact Kronecker rank multiplicativity `Matrix.rank_kronecker` (`(A ⊗ₖ B).rank = A.rank * B.rank` over any field), scalar-homomorphism eigenvector transfer `Matrix.mulVec_map_eq_smul`, and the flat `(Fin k → m)`-indexed Kronecker power `Matrix.kroneckerPow A k`. ## Matrix rigidity and linear circuits

[Matrix/Rigidity.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Rigidity.lean) defines `Matrix.diffCount A B`, the number of entries where `A` and `B` differ, and `Matrix.rigidity A r`, the least `diffCount A B` over matrices `B` of rank **at most** `r`. This is the convention of Lokam's survey (Definition 2.1). Some sources write "rank below `r`", which shifts the index by one. Over any field the module proves `rigidity_antitone`, `rigidity_le_card`, `rigidity_eq_zero_iff` (`rigidity A r = 0 ↔ A.rank ≤ r`), `rank_sub_le_rigidity` (`A.rank - r ≤ A.rigidity r`, because changing one entry lowers the rank by at most one) and the exact value `rigidity 1 r = n - r` for the identity. Columns are always finite. Rows may be infinite, but then `diffCount` uses `Set.ncard`, which is `0` on infinite sets, so rigidity takes the junk value `0`. The results relating rigidity to rank therefore assume finitely many rows.

[Arithmetic/Rigidity.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Rigidity.lean) proves Valiant's criterion, which says that a linear map with a small, shallow linear circuit is close to a low-rank map. The model is a `Circuit (affineSignature K) n m` interpreted by `affineInterpretation K` that computes `x ↦ A *ᵥ x`, with fan-in at most `2` (`FanInAtMost 2`). Gates of arity `0` and `1`, zero coefficients and arbitrary constant terms are allowed. Size is the number of **gates**, and depth is the largest number of gates on an input-to-output path. Lokam's linear circuits (Definition 2.2) have nonzero edge labels and no constants, and they count **edges**. In the logarithmic-depth regime of Valiant's criterion they also have fan-in exactly `2`. Each such circuit is a circuit of this model with the same depth and with at most as many gates as edges, so the bounds here apply to them as well. The results are:

- `Circuit.Computes.rigidity_le`, the quantitative form. If `c.depth < 2 ^ D` and `t ≤ D`, then `A.rigidity (2 * t * c.size / D) ≤ m * 2 ^ (2 ^ (D - t))`. This is Lokam's Theorem 2.1, `R_A(s log t / log d) ≤ 2 ^ O(d / t) n`, with explicit constants, under the dictionary `t ↦ 2 ^ t`, `d ↦ 2 ^ D` and `s ↦ 2 · size`. The proof grades the wires read by gates by the gate depths. It then applies the library's Valiant depth reduction, `Cslib.IsGrading.exists_sdiff` in [DepthReduction.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/DepthReduction.lean) (Lokam, Lemma 2.2, credited to Erdős, Graham and Szemerédi). That step removes at most a `t / D` fraction of the wires and leaves a grading with `2 ^ (D - t)` levels; the tails of the removed wires span the low-rank part.
- `Circuit.Computes.mul_log_log_lt_size`, the asymptotic form, with explicit constants. Let `ℓ = Nat.log 2 n` and `C, k, e > 0`, and assume `2 * k ≤ ℓ` and `n * 2 ^ (ℓ / k) < A.rigidity (n / e)`. Then every such circuit of depth at most `C * ℓ` satisfies `n * (log₂ ℓ + 1) < 2 * e * (log₂ (2 * C * k) + 1) * c.size`. For fixed constants this is `Ω(n log log n)` gates. For large enough `n`, an `(εn, n^(1+δ))`-rigid matrix satisfies the hypothesis for every `e ≥ 1/ε` and every `k > 1/δ`.
- `Circuit.Computes.rigidity_le_of_isPolynomial`, the quantitative form for any polynomial interpretation over an **infinite** field, such as the `+, ×` basis. It goes through linearization: `Circuit.Computes.exists_affine` preserves size, fan-in and depth.

Complete client: [MatrixRigidity.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/MatrixRigidity.lean). It checks the rigidity of the identity, of the zero matrix and of the rank-one all-ones matrix. It applies the quantitative bound to a two-input sum gate (with `t = D` and with `t < D`), to the identity circuit in depth `0` and to an arithmetic `+` circuit over `ℚ`. It also shows that the asymptotic hypotheses are satisfiable and applies the asymptotic corollary. Build it with

```bash

```

The test's witness is therefore degenerate: its rank target is `0`. The criterion does not cover nonlinear gates over finite fields, or fan-in above `2`. The size here counts gates, not Lokam's edges.

### Explicit lower bounds: totally regular and Cauchy matrices

[Matrix/Rigidity/TotallyRegular.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Rigidity/TotallyRegular.lean) proves the explicit rigidity bound of Friedman and of Shokrollahi, Spielman and Stemann, the best known explicit bound as surveyed by Lokam (2009), in the form of Lokam's Theorem 2.5. `IsTotallyRegular.le_rigidity_of_le_two_pow` says that an `n × n` totally regular matrix (every square submatrix is nonsingular, `Matrix.IsTotallyRegular`) needs at least `n² / (4 (r + 1)) · log₂ (n / r)` changes to bring its rank down to at most `r`, for `n ≤ 2 ^ r` and `2 r ≤ n`. Lokam states the theorem for `log² n ≤ r ≤ n / 2`, with the square of the logarithm. With base-`2` logarithms, `log² n ≤ r` implies `n ≤ 2 ^ r`, so the range proved here is wider; for example, `n = 64`, `r = 6` is inside it but outside Lokam's. The proof is the same, so this is not a new result. The logarithm in the bound is to base `2`; Lokam leaves the base of `log (n / r)` unstated. The proof has two steps:

- `Cslib.kovari_sos_turan` in [KovariSosTuran.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/KovariSosTuran.lean) is the Kővári–Sós–Turán bound (Lokam, Lemma 2.3, from Bollobás, *Modern Graph Theory*, §IV.2, Theorem 10). It is stated for an `m × n` grid with natural-number powers and truncated subtraction: a set `S` with no `s × s` rectangle `I ×ˢ J` satisfies `n (|S| - (s - 1) n) ^ s ≤ (s - 1) (n (m + 1 - s)) ^ s`. For `1 ≤ s ≤ m + 1`, taking `s`-th roots gives Lokam's real-root form; outside that range the real-root form can fail (a full `1 × 1` grid with `s = 3`), but the natural-number form still holds.
- `Cslib.exists_disjoint_product_of_card_lt` is the untouched-minor lemma (Lokam, Lemma 2.4). If fewer than `n (m - r) / (2 (r + 1)) · log₂ (n / r)` cells of an `m × n` grid are changed, with `n ≤ r 2 ^ (r + 1)`, then some `(r + 1) × (r + 1)` rectangle is untouched. For `r ≥ m` the bound is `0`, so there is nothing to prove. In a totally regular matrix that submatrix is nonsingular, so the changed matrix keeps rank at least `r + 1`.

`IsTotallyRegular.le_rigidity` is the rectangular statement the proof gives: `n (m - r) / (2 (r + 1)) · log₂ (n / r) ≤ A.rigidity r` for an `m × n` totally regular matrix, under `n ≤ r 2 ^ (r + 1)`. For square matrices, Lokam's hypothesis `log² n ≤ r` implies `n ≤ 2 ^ r ≤ r 2 ^ (r + 1)` when `r ≥ 1`. `le_rigidity_cauchy` applies the bound to Cauchy matrices. The library writes them as `1 / (x i - y j)`; Lokam's `1 / (x i + y j)` is `cauchy x (-y)`. `le_rigidity_cauchyNat` and `le_rigidity_cauchyNat_zmod` apply it to the explicit family `cauchyNat` over fields of characteristic zero and over `ZMod q` for primes `q ≥ 2 n`.

Complete client: [MatrixRigidityCauchy.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/MatrixRigidityCauchy.lean). It checks that the Kővári–Sós–Turán bound is attained on a `2 × 2` grid and derives the bound `22` for rectangle-free subsets of a `7 × 7` grid. It checks that every `5` cells of a `4 × 4` grid miss some `2 × 2` rectangle, and that a rectangular `4 × 8` Cauchy matrix satisfies every hypothesis of the general bound. Finally it shows that the `64 × 64` explicit Cauchy matrix over `ℚ` and over `ZMod 131` needs at least `438` changes to reach rank at most `6`. These parameters satisfy `n ≤ 2 ^ r` but lie outside Lokam's `log² n ≤ r`. Build it with

```bash

```

**Not certified here.** At `r = ε n` the bound is only `Ω(n)`, far below what Valiant's criterion needs (Lokam, p. 16). Shparlinsky's Vandermonde bound, the Sylvester–Hadamard bounds, the counting bounds `R_A(r) ≤ (n - r)²` and near-maximal rigidity of random matrices, and Alman–Williams non-rigidity of the Walsh–Hadamard matrices are open; see [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md).

## Hitting sets for small partial-derivative spaces

Import [HittingSet/Basic.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/HittingSet/Basic.lean). `IsHittingSet H 𝒞` says that the finite set of points `H : Finset (σ → F)` hits the class `𝒞 : Set (MvPolynomial σ F)`: every nonzero member of `𝒞` is nonzero at some point of `H`. It is a statement about formal polynomials and their values at field points, with no model of black-box access, running time or bit length.

The endpoint [isHittingSet_logHittingSet](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/HittingSet/Basic.lean) is for `[Field F] [CharZero F]`: `logHittingSet F n r d` hits every polynomial `f` in `n` variables with `f.totalDegree ≤ d` that lies in some submodule `V` with `Module.Finite F V`, `Module.finrank F V ≤ r` and `pderiv i p ∈ V` for all `i` and `p ∈ V`; `card_logHittingSet_le` bounds its size by `d * (n * r ^ 2 + r) + 1`. The hypothesis is a derivative-closed space containing `f`, which covers the space of all partial derivatives of `f` without defining it. `aeval_logCurve_ne_zero` is the underlying statement, Theorem 1.1 of [Bhardwaj et al.](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/217/): `f` does not vanish on the curve `logCurve F n (n * r ^ 2 + r)`, whose `i`-th coordinate is the series `ln (1 + (i + 1) t)` truncated below that degree. `isHittingSet_sum_pow s d` specializes to sums of `s` `d`-th powers of linear forms, with `r = s * (d + 1)`. The statements depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. The source is an unrefereed report whose authors disclose that the proof originated with an AI model; the proofs here are independent of any other formalization.

**To use it for another class**, exhibit a derivative-closed space: a submodule spanned by a finite family that the partial derivatives map into its span, as `isHittingSet_sum_pow` does with the powers `ℓₖ ^ e`, `e ≤ d`. The reusable pieces are [Derivation.wronskian_ne_zero](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/RingTheory/Derivation/Wronskian.lean) (the Wronskian of a derivation on a domain whose constants are the base field is nonzero on independent families), [PowerSeries.exists_eq_smul_of_mul_derivative_eq](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/RingTheory/PowerSeries/Constants.lean) (constants of the derivative of power series) and [algebraicIndependent_logSeries](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/HittingSet/AlgebraicIndependent.lean) (the series `ln (1 + (i + 1) t)` are algebraically independent over `F[X]`).

**Sparse polynomials and lower bounds.** `IsHittingSet` is defined in [HittingSet/Defs.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/HittingSet/Defs.lean), with `IsHittingSet.mono`. [isHittingSet_primePowers](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/HittingSet/Sparse.lean) gives, over a domain of characteristic zero, a hitting set of `s` points for the `n`-variate polynomials with at most `s` monomials (`f.support.card ≤ s`): the points `(p₀ ^ j, …, pₙ₋₁ ^ j)`, `j < s`, of powers of the first `n` primes, as in Ben-Or and Tiwari's zero test. `isHittingSet_powerHittingSet` is the general form, for any point `x` over a domain whose monomials `∏ᵢ x i ^ m i` are pairwise distinct; use it with another such point when primes are not available. In the other direction, [IsHittingSet.exists_notMem](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/HittingSet/LowerBound.lean) turns a hitting set `H` for a class into a lower bound: over a field, for every finite set `T` of monomials with `H.card < T.card`, some nonzero polynomial with monomials in `T` is outside the class. It is an existence statement from a dimension count. The checked client is [HittingSetSparse.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/HittingSetSparse.lean).

Complete client: [HittingSet.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/HittingSet.lean) gives a hitting set of `157` points for sums of two squares of linear forms in two rational variables. Build it with

```bash
lake build CslibFrontier.Computability.Circuit.Arithmetic.HittingSet.Basic \
  CslibFrontierTests.Computability.Circuit.Arithmetic.HittingSet
```

**Not certified here.** Characteristic zero is a genuine hypothesis: the Wronskian argument fails in positive characteristic. The source's extension to noncommutative algebraic branching programs is not formalized. Nothing is proved about the bit length of the points or the cost of computing them, and the bound `n * r ^ 2 + r` is the source's, not optimized. This is unrelated to the rational-formula hitting list of the next section, which concerns noncommutative rational formulas.

## Rational formulas and hitting lists

[RatOp](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Rational/Defs.lean#L69) uses rational constants, addition, multiplication and inverse. Evaluation is a relation: an inverse node is defined only when both products with the proposed inverse are one. This supports noncommutative matrix substitution without silently assigning a value at poles. A formula is admissible if some positive-dimensional rational matrix substitution makes it defined, and nonzero if some defined value is nonzero.

The main [hitting-list endpoint](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Rational.lean) produces an explicit executable list of matrix substitutions, with dimension and encoding length polynomial in the variable count plus formula-node bound. Every admissible nonzero formula within the bound has an invertible value at some listed substitution. The source credits [OpenAI's rational-hitting formalization at its pinned revision](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/OAI/Computability/RationalHitting) and records both its source revision and the intermediate Complexitylib port revision. The executable nonzero/admissibility tests are deductions from that result, with no new-priority claim.

The [native generator endpoint](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Rational/Runtime.lean) separately proves polynomial time for producing the encoded list from unary parameters. It specifies a standalone parser: valid positive n and s have input `true^n ++ [false] ++ true^s`, and malformed inputs produce the empty output word. Output encodes matrix dimension and tuple count self-delimitingly, followed by normalized rational data. The statement does **not** assert polynomial bit complexity for evaluating an arbitrary input rational formula on every generated substitution. Generator complexity and identity-test evaluation complexity must remain separate in a paper.

## Quantum circuits, finite codes and uniformity

[Qubits](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/Basic.lean#L92) represents an n-qubit state as complex Euclidean space indexed by n-bit strings. A gate is a unitary on a finite set of qubits, together with an injective ordered placement. A circuit is a list whose semantics is ordered matrix composition. The primitive model is pure unitary evolution with final basis measurement; reversible classical gates, output support and path-sum identities are derived over it.

Arbitrary unitary matrices are semantic objects. The [GateCode](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/Code.lean#L98) interface supplies a finite basis of Hadamard, NOT, controlled-NOT and Toffoli codes, with explicit target/control indices and well-formedness. The [UniformCircuitFamily](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/Uniform.lean#L79) structure requires native polynomial-time generation of both the well-formed gate list and the positive register width from unary input length. The width is at least the input length. Unary wire/width encodings prevent an uncharged exponentially large register address. Inputs are followed by zero ancillas; acceptance measures qubit zero, and postselection conditions on qubit one.

[BQP](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP.lean#L69) uses one such family for a language under a supplied injective instance encoding: every encoded member is accepted with probability at least two thirds, and every encoded nonmember with probability at most one third. Polynomial circuit size follows from the native polynomial-time generator. The model and its gate-set convention are owned here.

**Controlled phase gates.** [PhaseCode.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/PhaseCode.lean) adds the phase gate `S = diag(1, i)`, the controlled phase gate `Λ(S)` and their inverses. A code `(true, (inv, t, cs))` names the gate on qubits `t :: cs`, with at most one control; `(false, g)` is an ordinary gate code. A `UniformPhaseCircuitFamily` over the extended set has the same uniformity, width, input and acceptance conventions as a `UniformCircuitFamily`. [`mem_BQP_iff_phase`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/PhaseGates.lean) shows that both kinds of families define the same `BQP`.

The proof is an exact simulation, not an approximation. [The real encoding](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/RealEncoding.lean) adds one last qubit and stores the amplitude `a + ib` of `|x⟩` as `a` on `|x 0⟩` and `b` on `|x 1⟩`. Real gates act on such states unchanged. Each controlled phase gate becomes four gates on the extra qubit: `H`, then a CNOT or Toffoli gate controlled by the phase gate's qubits, then `H` and the same flip again. The inverse uses the reversed sequence.

`UniformPhaseCircuitFamily.realify` performs this translation in polynomial time, with at most four gates per gate. `acceptProb_realify` gives equal acceptance probabilities on every input. More generally, `PhaseGateCode.onesProb_toRealCodes` preserves the probability of every event on the original qubits. The time classes and `QMA` have not yet been restated for the extended set.

This is the reduction step in Aharonov's proof that Toffoli and Hadamard are computationally universal (arXiv:quant-ph/0301040, Theorem 2). Its other inputs, Kitaev's universality of `{Λ(S), H}` and the Solovay–Kitaev theorem, are tracked in [#3350](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3350).

To place a language in `BQP` with phase gates:

1. Define a `UniformPhaseCircuitFamily`.
2. Compute its acceptance probability. `PhaseGateCode.exists_toPlacedGate?_eq_ctrlPhase` decodes a well-formed phase code into a `PlacedGate.ctrlPhase`, and `PlacedGate.eval_ctrlPhase_ket` applies it to a basis state.
3. Apply `mem_BQP_iff_phase.mpr`.

The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/QuantumRealEncoding.lean) does this for the first-bit language. It also checks that the real simulation maps `|1 0⟩` to `|1 1⟩`, which records `S|1⟩ = i|1⟩`. Build it with `lake build CslibFrontierTests.QuantumRealEncoding`.

The parameterized time classes add an explicit gate bound. In each row below there exist natural constants `c1,c2` and one uniform family whose circuit at **every** length n has at most `c1*t(n)+c2` gates. Acceptance conditions apply to encoded instances; they do not impose a language specification on bitstrings outside the encoding's image.

| Class | Acceptance on an encoded instance |
|---|---|
| [BQTIME](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Time.lean#L86) | Probability at least `2/3` on members and at most `1/3` on nonmembers. |
| [NQTIME](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Time.lean#L95) | Membership is equivalent to strictly positive acceptance probability; no explicit positive gap is required. |
| [EQTIME](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Time.lean#L103) | Acceptance probability is exactly one on members and exactly zero on nonmembers. |
| [PostBQTIME](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Time.lean#L114) | Qubit one reads one with strictly positive probability on every instance, including nonmembers. Conditioned on that event, qubit zero reads one with probability at least `2/3` on members and at most `1/3` on nonmembers. |

The postselected probability is the joint probability for qubits zero and one divided by the probability for qubit one. The raw ratio is totalized to zero when its denominator is zero; the class's separate positivity condition excludes that case. There is no explicit lower bound on the postselection probability beyond strict positivity.

All four definitions retain the **polynomial-time generator ceiling**, whatever t is. Consequently [BQTIME is contained in BQP for every t](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Time.lean#L142), and [BQP equals the union over bounds `t(n)=n^k`](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Time.lean#L147). A superpolynomial t therefore does not turn these definitions into general quantum time classes. Exact acceptance gives both positive acceptance and bounded error with the same gate bound. The exact and positive-acceptance conventions concern this particular finite gate set; approximate gate simulation would not preserve exact zero probabilities. The cited quantum-Turing-machine results `NQP = coC_=P` and `PostBQP = PP` are not proved by this time-class module.

These models connect to tensor networks through [the path-network construction](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/PathNetwork.lean). Their machine-class bridge expresses acceptance as a GapP function divided by `2^h`, with h the number of Hadamard gates, and proves [BQP contained in native AWPP and hence PP](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Counting.lean#L149). The [machine counting account](../machines-and-classes/verifiers-counting-interaction.md) owns those target classes. Beyond the controlled phase gates above, the library cites the bounded-error equivalence with arbitrary elementary gate sets but does not formalize the required universality and Solovay–Kitaev simulation ([#3350](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3350)).

**Error reduction.** [Error reduction](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/Amplification.lean) runs `k` copies of a family's circuit on disjoint blocks, followed by a reversible counter that writes into qubit zero whether more than `t` copies accept (`UniformCircuitFamily.threshold`). Its acceptance probability is a sum over the accepting vote patterns (`acceptProb_threshold`). In closed form it is the binomial tail `P[Bin(k, p) > t]`, where p is the original acceptance probability (`acceptProb_threshold_eq_sum_choose`). The strict majority `k = 2r`, `t = r` gives error `2^{-q(n)}` for every polynomial q (`mem_BQP_iff_forall_errorExponent`). With Hoeffding's inequality, [any constant gap](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BQP/ConstantGap.lean) suffices. If a family accepts members with probability at least c and nonmembers with probability at most s, for real constants `s < c`, the problem is in BQP (`mem_BQP_of_gap`, `mem_BQP_iff_exists_gap`). This holds even when both constants lie on the same side of `1/2`; the vote uses `⌈1/δ²⌉` copies, where `2δ = c - s`. Gaps that shrink with the input length are not treated.

**Gate approximation.** [Gate approximation](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/Approximation.lean) bounds the cost of replacing gates. The matrix norm is Mathlib's scoped `ℓ²` operator norm. A client must write `open scoped Matrix.Norms.L2Operator` to state or use the matrix bounds. For circuits of equal length, `‖U_m⋯U_1 - V_m⋯V_1‖ ≤ Σ‖U_i - V_i‖` (`Circuit.norm_matrix_sub_matrix_le`), and the same holds block by block for subcircuits (`Circuit.norm_matrix_flatten_sub_matrix_flatten_le`). The probability of every set of outcomes moves by at most the distance between the unitaries, by `|P_ψ(S) - P_φ(S)| ≤ ‖ψ - φ‖` for unit states (`abs_measureProb_sub_le_norm_sub`). This is the bookkeeping step in comparing BQP with Arora–Barak's elementary gates ([#3350](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3350)). Density of a universal gate set and Solovay–Kitaev synthesis remain open. The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/BQPApproximation.lean) computes exact threshold tails for fair coins and compares the Hoeffding bound with the exact tail `37/256` for eight coins. It also decides the first-bit language from the gap `(17/20, 9/10)` and shows that distinct basis states are at distance at least one. Build it with `lake build CslibFrontierTests.BQPApproximation`.

**Controlled circuits and reflections.** These modules are infrastructure for Shi's constructive synthesis of real rotations from the Hadamard and Toffoli gates [shi03][shi03], the route chosen for [#3350](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3350). In [the controlled-circuit module](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/Controlled.lean), a circuit with no gate on qubit `j` never changes it (`Circuit.matrix_eq_zero_of_ne`), so it commutes with the projection `projOutcomes` onto a value of `j` (`Circuit.eval_projOutcomes_bit`). Well-formed gate codes run backwards undo themselves (`Circuit.eval_ofCodes_reverse`). `Circuit.IsControlled c C D` says that `C` does nothing when qubit `c` is zero and runs `D` when it is one. Adding the control `c` to classical codes gives such a circuit (`Circuit.isControlled_addControl`). So does conjugating a controlled circuit by codes that avoid `c` (`Circuit.IsControlled.conj`), and controlled circuits compose (`Circuit.IsControlled.append`). Together these build `Λ(T)` from a Toffoli gate for `T = H[a] Λ(σ_x)[a, b] H[a]`. This `T` is a variant of Shi's `T_{π/4} = σ_x[b] T`, without the final `σ_x` on `b`; it is a real involution with the same entry `⟨00|T|00⟩ = 1/2`. In [the phase-oracle module](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/PhaseOracle.lean), `H X H = Z` holds exactly (`Gate.hadamard_mul_not_mul_hadamard`). Computing a bit classically, flipping its phase and uncomputing therefore negates exactly the amplitudes on which the bit is one (`Circuit.eval_ofCodes_phaseOracleCodes`). With a Toffoli-gate AND on zeroed scratch qubits, this gives the reflection about the all-zero string of a set of qubits (`Circuit.eval_ofCodes_reflectZeroCodes`). [The plane-rotation module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/PlaneRotation.lean) proves the Grover geometry for linear maps. Write `v(φ) = cos φ • a + sin φ • e`. If `R_a` negates `a` and fixes `e`, and `R_b` negates `b = v(π/2 - γ)` and fixes `v(-γ)`, then `R_b R_a` maps `v(φ)` to `-v(φ - 2γ)`, so `T` rounds map it to `(-1)^T v(φ - 2Tγ)` (`LinearAlgebra.iterate_reflection_comp_reflection`). Shi's reflection `R` along `1̃ = b` fixes `b`, so on the plane `R = -R_b`, and his Grover iterate `(R Λ̄(σ_z))^T` maps `v(φ)` to `v(φ - 2Tγ)` without the sign. Shi's approximation of a real rotation (his Lemma 4.5 and Theorem 4.6) and the class equality are not yet formalized. The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/QuantumReflection.lean) proves that the Toffoli codes are the controlled `T`. It also checks that the reflection negates `|00⟩` and fixes `|10⟩`, and that two reflections at angle `π/4` turn `ℝ²` by a quarter. Build it with `lake build CslibFrontierTests.QuantumReflection`.

## Quantum verifiers and QMA

A [UniformVerifierFamily](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/Verifier.lean) is a uniform circuit family together with a witness length, itself computed in polynomial time from the unary input length. On an input word of length n, the circuit starts with the input on qubits `0, …, n-1`, an arbitrary pure witness state on the next `witnessLength n` qubits, and zeros elsewhere. It accepts when the final measurement finds qubit zero in state one. The acceptance probability is a quadratic form in the witness, and on a basis witness it is the ordinary circuit run on the input word followed by the witness bits. [PromiseQMA c s](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/QMA.lean) asks that some unit witness reach at least `c n` on every encoded yes-instance and that every unit witness stay at most `s n` on every encoded no-instance. `QMA` is the `(2/3, 1/3)` decision version.

Witnesses are pure states. The equivalence with mixed witnesses, by convexity of the acceptance probability, is stated in the docstrings but not formalized.

The [NP containment](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/QMA/NP.lean) writes the pair encoding of the input and each basis witness on a fresh block with classical gates, runs a uniform circuit family for the `NP` relation there, and swaps its answer into qubit zero. A certificate is accepted with certainty. When no certificate exists, every basis witness is rejected with certainty, and therefore so is every witness state. Hence promise `NP` lies in `QMA(1, 0)`. `BQP ⊆ QMA` uses verifiers that read no witness.

The [Merlin–Arthur containment](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/QMA/MA.lean) builds its verifier in three steps. It reads Merlin's proof as the witness and prepares Arthur's coins with a [layer of Hadamard gates](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/HadamardLayer.lean) on fresh zero qubits. It then runs the NP verifier above on proofs of length `q(n) + p(n)`. The classical part maps basis states to basis states, so on a witness `∑_y ψ_y |y⟩` the acceptance probability is `∑_y |ψ_y|² · #{r | V(x, y ++ r)} / 2^{p(n)}`, with no cross terms (`acceptProb_ofMA`). A good proof's basis state gives completeness. Soundness holds for every witness state, because the acceptance probability is an average of classical acceptance probabilities that are at most `1/3`. This proves `MA ⊆ QMA` (`MA_subset_QMA`). With no proof the same circuit is a `BQP` family, which gives `BPP ⊆ BQP` (`BPP_subset_BQP`), the randomized simulation of Watrous, Section IV.3. [Checked clients](../../CslibFrontierTests/QMAMA.lean) compute the acceptance probabilities `1/2` and `0` of a one-coin verifier on its two basis proofs and `1/4` on their equal superposition, and bound every witness state by `1/2`. Build them with `lake build CslibFrontierTests.QMAMA`. `MA ⊆ QMA` is stated for decision problems only, since the library has no promise version of `MA`.

[Weak error reduction](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/QMA/Amplification.lean) follows Kitaev's parallel repetition (Kitaev–Shen–Vyalyi, Section 14.2; Marriott–Watrous, Theorem 3.2, at the gap `(2/3, 1/3)`): `2r(n)` copies of the verifier read `2r(n)` witness blocks, and the strict majority of their answers is measured. For `r = 6q` this gives completeness `1 - 2^{-q(n)}` and soundness `2^{-q(n)}` for every polynomial q, with the witness length multiplied by `2r(n)`.

Soundness must hold for witnesses entangled across the blocks. The [tensor-power bound](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/TensorPower.lean) proves it by bounding, one copy at a time, the weight of the outcomes with many accepting copies.

[QMA ⊆ PP](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Classes/QMA/Counting.lean) (`QMA_subset_PP`, noted by Kitaev–Watrous, proved by Vyalyi; the proof here follows Marriott–Watrous Theorem 3.4) holds more generally for every constant gap: `PromiseQMA_subset_promiseClass_PP` places `QMA(c, s)` in promise `PP` whenever `s < c`, with no error reduction. Completeness and soundness may also depend on the input length. `PromiseQMA_subset_promiseClass_PP_of_gap` handles an inverse-polynomial gap `c(n) − s(n) ≥ 1/q(n)` when s is computable to precision `1/(2q(n))`. It is a special case of `PromiseQMA_subset_promiseClass_PP_of_grid`, which only asks for a grid point `σ(n)/D(n)` of polynomial mesh with `s(n) ≤ σ(n)/D(n)` and `(σ(n)+1)/D(n) ≤ c(n)`. The proof works with the [acceptance operator](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/AcceptOperator.lean) `Q = B†B`, where B restricts the final state to the accepting outcomes. Q is positive and its quadratic form is the acceptance probability.

A [trace-power bound](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/TracePower.lean) applies to any positive operator T. If its quadratic form reaches `c ≥ 0` on some unit vector, then `c^j ≤ tr(T^j)`. If the form is at most s on every unit vector, then `tr(T^j) ≤ dim · s^j`. For the verifier's operator Q, `dim = 2^m`, where m is the witness length. Choose naturals B and D with `s ≤ B/D` and `(B+1)/D ≤ c`, computed in polynomial time when c and s depend on the input length, and take a power `d = B(m+1)`. Bernoulli's inequality then makes `((B+1)/D)^d` at least twice `2^m (B/D)^d`. So `tr(Q^d)` lies on opposite sides of `((B+1)/D)^d / 2` on yes-instances and on no-instances.

In the computational basis, `tr(Q^d)` is a sum over closed paths of products of matrix entries (`LinearMap.trace_pow_eq_sum`). Each entry is a [projected pair gap](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/PathSum.lean) divided by `2^h`. Such a gap counts signed pairs of Hadamard–Toffoli paths from two input registers and is a `GapP` function of the instance. Reindexing the closed paths as polynomially long words shows that `2^{hd} tr(Q^d)` is `GapP` (`traceGap_mem_GapP`). Comparing it with the threshold yields a `GapP` function that is positive exactly on the separating set.

Error reduction from an inverse-polynomial gap and Marriott–Watrous strong error reduction that keeps the witness length are not formalized ([#2876](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/2876)). [Checked clients](../../CslibFrontierTests/QMA.lean) include a verifier whose answer depends on its witness, a language it places in `QMA` with perfect completeness and soundness zero, and the amplified verifier evaluated on basis witnesses. Build them with `lake build CslibFrontierTests.QMA`. ## Quantum information primitives

These modules use states `EuclideanSpace ℂ ι` over an arbitrary finite index type, not the `Qubits n` registers of the circuit model. A qubit is indexed by `Bool`, and a pair of systems by a product type. Operators act by Kronecker products. [Pauli.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/Pauli.lean) defines the Pauli matrices `X` and `Z`. [Bipartite.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/Bipartite.lean) defines the product state `ψ ⊗ φ` (`prodState`), the unnormalized state of the second system after outcome `i` on the first (`condState`) and its normalization (`postMeasure`). It proves that the computational-basis measurement gives outcome `i` on the first system with probability `‖condState i Ψ‖²` (`measure_map_fst_apply`). [Bell.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/Bell.lean) defines the maximally entangled state `d^{-1/2} ∑_i |i, i⟩` (`maxEntangled`) and the Bell state `|Φ⁺⟩` (`bell`). It proves `⟨Φ| A ⊗ B |Φ⟩ = tr(Aᵀ B)/d` (`star_maxEntangled_dotProduct_kronecker_mulVec_eq_trace`). This generalizes de Wolf, Chapter 1, Exercise 15(a), which is the case `B = I`, `d = 2`.

[NoCloning.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/NoCloning.lean) proves the **ancilla-free** no-cloning theorem, the form of de Wolf, Chapter 1, Exercise 12. The copying map acts on the two registers `E ⊗ E` alone, input and blank. By the standard inner-product argument, if an isometry of `E ⊗ E` clones two unit vectors from the same blank state, then `⟨ψ, φ⟩ = ⟨ψ, φ⟩²`, so the vectors are orthogonal or equal (`inner_eq_zero_or_eq_of_clone`, and `inner_eq_zero_or_eq_of_unitary_clone` for unitary matrices). By the linearity and superposition argument of Wootters–Zurek and Dieks, in dimension at least 2 no linear map clones every unit vector, even up to a nonzero state-dependent factor (`not_exists_linearMap_clone_smul`, `not_exists_linearMap_clone`). In dimension 1, for every nonzero blank state, the identity is such a cloner up to a nonzero factor, so the dimension hypothesis is needed for the factor form. The matrix forms `not_exists_matrix_clone_smul` and `not_exists_matrix_clone` assume no unitarity. All statements concern pure states. Wootters and Zurek also allow an apparatus register whose final state may depend on the input, `|ψ⟩|e⟩|A⟩ ↦ |ψ⟩|ψ⟩|A_ψ⟩`. That form follows from the same linearity argument but is not formalized here. No-cloning for mixed states and no-broadcasting are not formalized either, since they need channel semantics; density matrices are in the next section.

[GateMatrix.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/GateMatrix.lean) gives the CNOT matrix on `Bool × Bool` and the actions of CNOT and Hadamard on vectors. It relates the register gates `Gate.cnot` and `Gate.not` to `cnotMatrix` and `X` by reindexing (`cnot_eq_submatrix_cnotMatrix`, `not_eq_submatrix_pauliX`). [Teleportation.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/Teleportation.lean) follows Bennett et al. (1993) and de Wolf, Section 1.5. Alice applies CNOT and then Hadamard to `ψ ⊗ Φ⁺` and measures her two qubits. `teleportation` proves that the outcome `(a, b)` is uniform on `Bool × Bool` and that Bob's correction `Z^a X^b` turns his post-measurement qubit into `ψ`. The circuit model has no intermediate measurement, so Alice's measurement is the marginal `PMF.map Prod.fst` of the full computational-basis measurement, and Bob's state is `postMeasure`. This is the partial-measurement postulate of de Wolf, Section 1.3. Two-register unitaries are built with `onFirst` from [Register.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/Register.lean). Teleportation of one half of an entangled state, as in entanglement swapping, is a pure-state extension of `teleportState_apply` that is not formalized yet. [SuperdenseCoding.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Quantum/SuperdenseCoding.lean) follows Bennett–Wiesner (1992). Alice applies `X^b Z^a` to her half of `Φ⁺`, and Bob applies CNOT, then Hadamard, and measures. `superdense_coding` proves that Bob obtains `(a, b)` with probability 1. De Wolf's Chapter 1, Exercise 14 applies `X` for the first bit and then `Z` for the second. With this decoder (CNOT, then Hadamard), that encoding yields the swapped pair, so the encoding here is ordered to return `(a, b)`. Holevo's bound, that two classical bits per qubit is optimal, and Nayak's bound for quantum random access codes are not formalized ([#3329](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3329)).

[Checked clients](../../CslibFrontierTests/QuantumProtocols.lean) teleport `|0⟩`, `|1⟩` and `|+⟩` and decode all four superdense messages. They also show that CNOT clones both basis states but not `|+⟩`, as the orthogonal-or-equal dichotomy requires, and that in dimension 1 a cloner up to a nonzero factor exists for a nonzero blank. Build them with `lake build CslibFrontierTests.QuantumProtocols`.

## Density matrices, no-signalling and the quantum one-time pad

[DensityMatrix.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/DensityMatrix.lean) represents mixed states as matrices, following [de Wolf][dewolf19], §15.1 and §18.3. `IsDensityMatrix ρ` means positive semidefinite with trace one. `pureState ψ` is `|ψ⟩⟨ψ|`, a density matrix for a unit vector (`isDensityMatrix_pureState`). `partialTraceLeft` and `partialTraceRight` give the reduced state of the second and of the first system of a matrix on `ι × κ`. Either half of a maximally entangled state is maximally mixed (`partialTraceLeft_pureState_maxEntangled`, `partialTraceRight_pureState_maxEntangled`). Each is a sum of principal submatrices, so it preserves positive semidefiniteness, and it preserves the trace; reduced states of density matrices are therefore density matrices. On a Kronecker product, `Tr_A (A ⊗ₖ B) = tr A • B`. Mathlib's `Matrix.trace_kronecker` and `Matrix.PosSemidef.kronecker` cover the full trace and products.

An operator on the traced-out system moves cyclically under `Tr_A` (`partialTraceLeft_kronecker_one_mul`). This gives **no-signalling** for a Kraus family on Alice's system: `Tr_A (∑ⱼ (Kⱼ ⊗ 1) ρ (Kⱼ ⊗ 1)ᴴ) = Tr_A ρ` whenever `∑ⱼ Kⱼᴴ Kⱼ = 1` (`partialTraceLeft_kraus`). The special cases are a local unitary (`partialTraceLeft_conj_unitary`; [de Wolf][dewolf19], Chapter 18, Exercise 7, here for mixed states) and a computational-basis measurement whose outcome is discarded (`partialTraceLeft_measure`). The Kraus form is a standard generalization of the exercise, not a new result. General channels (CPTP maps, Stinespring and Choi representations), von Neumann entropy and trace distance are not formalized.

[PauliTwirl.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/PauliTwirl.lean) works on the bit strings `ι → Bool`. It defines the bit flip `X^a |z⟩ = |z + a⟩` (`pauliXPow`), the phase flip `Z^b |z⟩ = (-1)^{b · z} |z⟩` (`pauliZPow`) and the Pauli string `X^a Z^b` (`pauliString`), all unitary. On one qubit they are the matrices of Pauli.lean after reindexing, which the tests check. The **Pauli twirl** is completely depolarizing: `∑_{a,b} X^a Z^b ρ (X^a Z^b)ᴴ = 2ⁿ tr ρ • 1` (`sum_pauliTwirl`). The proof sums over `b` by character orthogonality (`BitString.sum_signVal_dotProduct_mul_signVal_dotProduct`) and then over `a` by a translation. With a reference system, the twirl gives `2ⁿ 1 ⊗ Tr_A ρ` (`sum_pauliTwirl_kronecker_one`).

[OneTimePad.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Crypto/Protocols/Quantum/OneTimePad.lean) formalizes the **quantum one-time pad** of [Ambainis, Mosca, Tapp and de Wolf][ambainismoscatappdewolf00], Theorem 4.1, without ancilla; [de Wolf][dewolf19], Chapter 18, Exercise 5 is the one-qubit case. A uniformly random `2n`-bit key `(a, b)` encrypts `ρ` as `X^a Z^b ρ (X^a Z^b)ᴴ` (`encrypt`). Decryption is exact (`decrypt_encrypt`), and ciphertexts of states are states (`isDensityMatrix_encrypt`). The key-averaged ciphertext of every state is `1 / 2ⁿ` (`averageCiphertext_eq`), so it is the same for all plaintexts (`averageCiphertext_eq_averageCiphertext`). Suppose the eavesdropper also holds a reference system entangled with the plaintext. The key-averaged joint state is then `1 / 2ⁿ ⊗ Tr_A ρ` (`averageCiphertextWith_eq`), which depends only on the reference system's own state; this product form is a standard strengthening of the remark in the proof of Theorem 4.1. AMTW apply random tensor products of `I, X, Y, Z`; since `Y = iXZ`, these agree with `X^a Z^b` up to a phase that cancels in the conjugation. Secrecy is stated as equality of the eavesdropper's averaged state. This is the quantum analogue of the ciphertext-distribution form of cslib's `PerfectlySecret`, not an instance of that classical interface. The matching lower bound, that `2n` bits of key entropy are necessary (Corollary 5.4 there), is not formalized.

[Checked clients](../../CslibFrontierTests/QuantumDensity.lean) check the following:

- a basis state is a density matrix, and the trace-one condition is not vacuous;
- either half of a maximally entangled state is maximally mixed, and stays so under Alice's unitaries and measurements;
- the `n`-qubit Pauli powers restrict to `X` and `Z` on one qubit;
- encrypting `|z⟩` gives `|z + a⟩`;
- `|0⟩` and every unit state have the same average ciphertext `1 / 2`, and a traceless matrix twirls to zero;
- encrypting half of an EPR pair leaves Eve's joint state `1 / 2 ⊗ 1 / 2`.

Build them with `lake build CslibFrontierTests.QuantumDensity`.

## Quantum query algorithms

The quantum query model is separate from uniform circuits. [State](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/Search.lean#L83) has a query-position register, a control bit and finite workspace. A controlled phase oracle multiplies a basis amplitude by the sign determined by the selected input bit and control; arbitrary input-independent linear isometries alternate with oracle calls. Only oracle calls are charged. Workspace size and implementation of those isometries are free in the query count.

The [BBBV theorem](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/Search.lean) gives `N-2 <= 144 T^2` for success at least two thirds on every uniquely marked input. The [polynomial method](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/Polynomial.lean) bounds amplitude degree by T and acceptance-probability degree by 2T, yielding approximate-degree and block-sensitivity consequences. The [spectral adversary method](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/Adversary.lean) gives the corresponding error-dependent adversary lower bound; the derived deterministic-query simulations include a fourth-power bound through the stated sensitivity parameters.

**Ambainis's adversary method.** [AmbainisAdversary.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/AmbainisAdversary.lean) proves the relational adversary bound of Ambainis (STOC 2000; JCSS 2002), Theorem 6, for bit queries. Both it and the sensitivity-graph bound in [Adversary.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/Adversary.lean) share the weighted progress measure `weightedProgress` (`one_sub_mul_sum_le_of_sq_le`, `inner_phaseOracle_phaseOracle`) in [Progress.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/Progress.lean). Take a nonempty finite set `R` of input pairs on which one fixed `T`-query algorithm ends with overlaps at most `δ`. Suppose every first input has at least `m` partners and every second input at least `m'`, and `l_{x,i} l_{y,i} ≤ L` on every related pair that differs at `i`. Then `one_sub_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_mul_sqrt` gives `(1 - δ) √(m m') ≤ 2T √L`. Lemma 1 (`norm_inner_le_two_mul_sqrt_of_measureProb`, in [Overlap.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/Measurement/Overlap.lean)) turns `1 - ε` correctness with `2 * ε ≤ 1` into `δ = 2√(ε(1-ε))`. That gives Theorem 2, `one_sub_two_mul_sqrt_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_mul_sqrt`, and the single-bit-flip case `one_sub_two_mul_sqrt_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_of_neighbors` (`l = l' = 1`). Only inputs occurring in `R` are constrained, so the bounds apply to promise problems. Both inputs of every pair are run on the same algorithm `U, ψ₀`. [Symmetric.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/AmbainisAdversary/Symmetric.lean) derives the bounded-error bounds `N ≤ 36 T` for `PARITY_N` (`card_le_thirty_six_mul_of_parity`) and `N ≤ 72 T` for `MAJ_N` (`card_le_seventy_two_mul_of_majority`). It also proves `(1 - 2√(ε(1-ε))) √(k (N + 1 - k)) ≤ 2T` for algorithms that need only separate weight `k` from weight `k - 1` (`one_sub_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_of_weight`), and for `Th^N_k`. [Tribes.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/AmbainisAdversary/Tribes.lean) proves Theorem 4 for the `AND` of `s` `OR`s of `w` bits, `(1 - 2√(ε(1-ε))) √(w s) ≤ 2T` (`one_sub_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_of_forall_exists`), and its De Morgan dual for `tribes` (`one_sub_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_of_tribes`). Both need correctness only when every block has at most one true (respectively false) bit, and both give `w s ≤ 1296 T²` at error `1/3`. For total functions, the parity and threshold bounds would also follow from the spectral adversary above, since `λ(PARITY_N) = N` and `λ(Th^N_k) = √(k (N - k + 1))`, but the library does not prove these values. The promise forms would not follow. The constants are those of this proof; the source states `Ω(·)`. [WeightLayers.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/AmbainisAdversary/WeightLayers.lean) relates every input of weight `a` to every superset of weight `b > a`, the relation of Ambainis's Theorem 5. The partner counts are binomial coefficients ([WeightInterval.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/BitString/WeightInterval.lean): `card_filter_le_and_weight_eq`, `card_filter_ge_and_weight_eq`). With overlap bound `δ` it gives `(1 - δ) √((N - a) b) ≤ 2T (b - a)` (`one_sub_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_mul_sub_of_le`). For an algorithm that accepts weight `k` and rejects weight `k' ≠ k` with error `ε ≤ 1/2`, and need not be correct elsewhere, `one_sub_mul_add_le_four_mul_mul_abs_sub_of_weight` gives the Nayak–Wu bound `(1 - 2√(ε(1-ε))) (√(N |k - k'|) + √(m (N - m))) ≤ 4T |k - k'|` for every `m` between `k` and `k'`. Nayak and Wu prove this `Ω(√(N/Δ) + √(m(N-m))/Δ)` with polynomials; here it comes from the adversary method. When `k` and `k'` lie on both sides of `N/2`, `one_sub_mul_card_le_four_mul_mul_abs_sub_of_weight` gives `(1 - 2√(ε(1-ε))) N ≤ 4T |k - k'|`, the `Ω(1/ε)` bound of Ambainis's Theorem 5. Not yet formalized either: the weighted, spectral and negative-weight adversaries for general relations and oracle-overlap deduplication ([#3239](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3239)). [Checked clients](../../CslibFrontierTests/QuantumAmbainisAdversary.lean) verify the counting hypotheses by `decide` on a concrete nonempty relation. They also apply the bounds to `readBit` and to the `OR_N` algorithm `orSearch`, recovering `Ω(√N)` for search. Build them with `lake build CslibFrontierTests.QuantumAmbainisAdversary`.

**Multi-outcome readouts, value queries, and Nayak–Wu consequences.** [Readout.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/Measurement/Readout.lean) defines `measureOut`, pushing computational-basis measurement `Cslib.Quantum.measure` forward along any classical readout `out : ι → β` and relating event probabilities to `measureProb` (`measureOut_toOuterMeasure_apply`). [ValueQuery.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/ValueQuery.lean) formalizes quantum query algorithms over arbitrary finite index types `ι` and input alphabets `α`: `ValueState ι V W` (`EuclideanSpace ℂ (ι × V × W)`), unitary step oracle `valueOracle` (including permutation/additive steps `permStep` and `addStep`), and `runValue`. Two-value promise algorithms reduce to controlled bit-query algorithms on `State N (V × W)`: by `exists_sim_value` with at most `2T` queries via unitary diagonalization of `(step a₀)⁻¹ ∘ step a₁` ([UnitarySpectrum.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/UnitarySpectrum.lean), `LinearIsometryEquiv.exists_orthonormalBasis_eigenvectors`), tightening to `T` queries when that relative step is an involution (`exists_sim_involution`, covering bitwise-XOR `addStep_rel_involutive`). It also embeds `T`-query index-returning algorithms into `(T + 1)`-query value-returning ones (`exists_sim_index_to_value`, `exists_sim_index_to_value_perm`). Using these reductions, [NayakWuConsequences.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/NayakWuConsequences.lean) derives the statistical lower bounds of [Nayak and Wu][nayakwu99] directly from `one_sub_mul_add_le_four_mul_mul_abs_sub_of_weight` rather than the paper's polynomial-method proof: for `Δ`-approximate counting (`one_sub_mul_approxCount_le`, `approxCount_lower_bound`; Theorem 1.11, `1/2 ≤ Δ` and `4Δ ≤ n`, giving `16T` and `288T` at `ε = 1/3`, plus `4T` in `one_sub_mul_exactCount_le` for `0 < Δ ≤ 1/2`), for `Δ`-approximate `k`-th smallest (`IsApproxKthSmallest`, `IsApproxKthSmallestRelaxed`, Theorem 1.5, `1 ≤ k ≤ n`, `1 ≤ Δ`, `4Δ ≤ n`; `one_sub_mul_kthSmallest_le` giving `32T` / `576T` generally, `16T` / `288T` for involutive steps, and `T + 1` bounds for index-returning algorithms in `kthSmallest_index_lower_bound`), and for `δ`-approximate median (`IsApproxMedian`, Corollary 1.6, `1 ≤ n`, `0 < δ < 1`; `one_sub_mul_median_le` giving `min (1/δ) n ≤ 432T` at `ε = 1/3` and `1/δ ≤ 432T` when `1 ≤ δ n` in `median_inv_delta_lower_bound`, tightening to `12T` / `216T` for involutive steps, plus `T + 1` bounds in `median_index_lower_bound`). At `n = 3` and `δ = 1/3`, returning a uniform index in `0` queries already yields an exact median with probability at least `2/3`, so the `T + 1` shift is necessary for index-returning algorithms. Comparison-tree oracles, approximate mean (Corollary 1.12), and relative counting (Theorem 1.13) remain unformalized ([#3239](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3239)). [Checked clients](../../CslibFrontierTests/QuantumNayakWu.lean) instantiate `measureOut`, unitary eigenvectors on Pauli swap, `exists_sim_involution`, `count2Alg`, the `4`-query sorter `readAll4` witnessing Theorem 1.5 and Corollary 1.6, and the `0`-query random-index witness for `median_index_lower_bound`. Build them with `lake build CslibFrontierTests.QuantumNayakWu`.

[Grover search](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/Grover.lean) proves the exact sine-squared success formula and the square-root query upper bound for a unique marked item. [unknown-count search](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/UnknownSearch.lean) uses a fixed query budget and a purified random iteration count to obtain constant success whenever at least one item is marked, and a bounded-error OR algorithm. Its packaging is not the adaptive expected-runtime BBHT theorem; it is a fixed-budget query result.

**Non-Boolean value-query adversaries and permutation inversion.** [ReadoutOverlap.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/Measurement/ReadoutOverlap.lean) lifts [Ambainis's][ambainis02] Lemma 1 (`2√(ε(1-ε))` overlap bound) to disjoint outcome sets (`norm_inner_le_two_mul_sqrt_of_disjoint`) and multi-outcome classical readouts (`norm_inner_le_two_mul_sqrt_of_readout_ne`, `norm_inner_le_two_mul_sqrt_of_measureOut_ne`, `measureOut_eq_ofReal_measureProb`). [ValueAdversary.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/ValueAdversary.lean) proves Theorem 6 of [Ambainis (2002)][ambainis02] (§6.1) directly on `ValueState ι V W` for arbitrary non-Boolean input alphabets `α` and arbitrary unitary value-oracle families `step : α → (ℂ^V ≃ₗᵢ[ℂ] ℂ^V)`, decomposing states across query slices `querySlice i` (`sum_sq_norm_querySlice`, `norm_inner_valueOracle_valueOracle_ge`) to preserve the factor `2` without two-value diagonalization (`one_sub_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_mul_sqrt_value`) and, when every relation endpoint has pointwise success at least `1 - ε` and `2 * ε ≤ 1`, the error-dependent readout bounds (`one_sub_two_mul_sqrt_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_mul_sqrt_value`, `one_sub_two_mul_sqrt_mul_sqrt_le_two_mul_mul_sqrt_value_measureOut`). [PermutationInversion.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/PermutationInversion.lean) applies this to inverting a permutation `π : Equiv.Perm ι` at `target : ι` (`π.symm target`), formalizing Theorem 7 of [Ambainis (2002)][ambainis02] (§6.2) via the transposition relation `permInvPermRel` across disjoint index sets `I₀, I₁` (`card_permInvPermRel_fst`, `card_permInvPermRel_snd`, `card_permInvPermRel_diff_mul_le`): when every permutation is inverted with pointwise success at least `1 - ε` and `2 * ε ≤ 1`, `(1 - 2√(ε(1-ε))) √⌊|ι|/2⌋ ≤ 2T` (`one_sub_two_mul_sqrt_mul_sqrt_div_two_le_of_permInversion`), `(1 - 2√(ε(1-ε))) √(|ι| - 1) ≤ 2√2 T` (`one_sub_two_mul_sqrt_mul_sqrt_sub_one_le_of_permInversion`), and `(1 - 2√(ε(1-ε))) √|ι| ≤ 2√3 T` for `2 ≤ |ι|` (`one_sub_two_mul_sqrt_mul_sqrt_card_le_of_permInversion`), giving `|ι| / 2 ≤ 1296 T²` (`permInversion_div_two_lower_bound`) and `|ι| ≤ 3888 T²` (`permInversion_lower_bound`, `permInversion_measureOut_lower_bound`) at error `1/3`. [Checked clients](../../CslibFrontierTests/QuantumPermutationInversion.lean) verify `permInvPermRel` counts and the `l_max = 2 < 4 = l l'` separation on `Fin 3` and `Fin 4` by `decide`, check sharpness at `|ι| = 1` (`T = 0`) and `0`-query impossibility for `|ι| ≥ 2`, and instantiate the bounds on a `1`-query inverter for `Fin 2` and a `2`-query inverter over the ternary alphabet `Fin 3`. Build them with `lake build CslibFrontierTests.QuantumPermutationInversion`.

**Bit queries and Hadamard transforms.** [BitQuery.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/BitQuery.lean) packages algorithms written with bit queries `|i, b, w⟩ ↦ |i, b ⊕ x_i, w⟩` inside the phase-oracle model, without a second oracle. A Hadamard gate on the control bit before and after a phase query is exactly the basis permutation `bitFlip x` (`hadCtrl_phaseOracle_hadCtrl_eq_permute`). `bitQueryAlg T π prep post` is a preparation isometry, `T` charged queries separated by input-independent basis permutations, and a final isometry. By `run_bitQueryAlg`, its state after the last query is `post` applied to the prepared state permuted by the composite classical permutation `bitQueryPerm π x T`. Each bit query costs exactly one phase query. [HadamardTransform.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/HadamardTransform.lean) gives the isometry `hadamardTransform e`, which applies a Hadamard gate to every qubit of a register `K → Bool`, for a basis split as `e : ι ≃ (K → Bool) × R`.

**Bernstein–Vazirani.** In [BernsteinVazirani.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/BernsteinVazirani.lean) the input is the truth table `linearTable e s` of `a ↦ s ⬝ a`, with positions identified with `{0,1}^n` by `e`. One phase query between two Hadamard transforms leaves the algorithm in the basis state `|s, 1⟩` (`run_bernsteinVazirani`), so the query register gives `s` with probability one. The classical side is in [QueryComplexity/BernsteinVazirani.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/BernsteinVazirani.lean), on the same truth table. Querying the `n` unit vectors recovers `s` (`linearDecodeTree`). The truth table of `a ↦ s ⬝ a` is the Hadamard codeword of `s` read through `e` (`linearTable_eq_hadamardCode_comp`). Because the `2^n` inputs have distinct answers and a tree of depth `q` takes at most `2^q` values, every deterministic tree has depth `≥ n` (`card_le_depth_of_linearTable`). Every randomized tree with error `ε` on each input (on the promise inputs, for any total `F` extending the answer) satisfies `2^n (1 − ε) ≤ 2^q` (`linearTable_randomized_lower_bound`), so `q ≥ n` for `ε < 1/2`. `complexity_linearDecode` and `randomizedComplexity_linearDecode` give `D = R_ε = n` for `0 ≤ ε < 1/2`. `bernsteinVazirani_separation` combines the two sides. The lower bounds use the generic counting lemmas `DecisionTree.card_le_two_pow_depth_of_injOn` and `RandomizedDecisionTree.card_mul_one_sub_le_two_pow_depth` ([Randomized/Counting.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Randomized/Counting.lean)), which apply to any search problem with distinct answers. [CslibFrontierTests/BernsteinVazirani.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/BernsteinVazirani.lean) shows the threshold `1/2` is sharp: at `n = 1`, uniform guessing has depth `0` and error exactly `1/2`.

**Deutsch–Jozsa.** The promise is that the input `x : Fin N → Bool` is constant or balanced, with exactly `N/2` true bits (`BitString.IsBalanced`, in [Balanced.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/BitString/Balanced.lean)). As for Bernstein–Vazirani and Simon, there is no promise-problem type: `SolvesDeutschJozsa F` asks a total `F` to answer `true` on constant inputs and `false` on balanced ones. The quantum side, [Quantum/DeutschJozsa.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/DeutschJozsa.lean), reuses the Bernstein–Vazirani circuit unchanged, as de Wolf §2.4.2 notes. On any input its final amplitudes are the Walsh–Hadamard coefficients of `(-1)^x` (`run_bernsteinVazirani_apply`). So the query register reads `0` with probability `(2^{-n} ∑_i (-1)^{x_i})²` (`regProb_run_bernsteinVazirani_zero`). That is `1` on constant inputs and `0` on balanced ones, so one query decides the promise problem exactly (`deutschJozsa_regProb_eq_bitVal`). The deterministic side, [QueryComplexity/DeutschJozsa.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/DeutschJozsa.lean), proves that over `N = 2m` positions every solving tree has depth `≥ m + 1` (`depth_ge_of_solvesDeutschJozsa`). The adversary answers `false`; after `≤ m` queries the balanced input that is true exactly on `m` unqueried positions reaches the same leaf as the all-false input. Querying any `m + 1` distinct positions and comparing the bits suffices (`deutschJozsaTree_solves`), so `m + 1` is the least complexity of a solving total function (`isLeast_complexity_solvesDeutschJozsa`). `deutschJozsa_separation` combines one exact quantum query with the deterministic bound `2^{n-1} + 1`. The separation holds only for exact algorithms. [Randomized/DeutschJozsa.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Randomized/DeutschJozsa.lean) draws `k` positions uniformly with replacement through `uniformMixture`, and answers whether the bits read agree (`randomDeutschJozsa`). It never errs on constant inputs, and errs with probability exactly `2/2^k` on balanced ones (`errorProb_randomDeutschJozsa_of_isBalanced`, via the generic `RandomizedDecisionTree.errorProb_uniformMixture`). For `k = 2` this is de Wolf's error `1/2`. `randomDeutschJozsa_separation` sets three random queries with error `≤ 1/4` on the promise against `m + 1` deterministic ones. The error is bounded on the promise inputs only, since `randomizedComplexity` requires every input. [CslibFrontierTests/DeutschJozsa.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/DeutschJozsa.lean), [CslibFrontierTests/QuantumDeutschJozsa.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/QuantumDeutschJozsa.lean) and [CslibFrontierTests/RandomizedDeutschJozsa.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/RandomizedDeutschJozsa.lean) check the following: `N = 2` (Deutsch's problem) and `N = 4`, with depths `2` and `3` attained; two queries fail at `N = 4`; `N = 0` is contradictory; the out-of-promise input `1000` reads `0` with probability `1/4`; and the randomized errors are `1`, `1/2` and `1/4` for `k = 1, 2, 3`. Exact quantum lower bounds for promise problems via polynomial degree, and recursive Fourier sampling, are not formalized.

**Simon's problem and the exponential separation.** Both sides use the same input: the truth table `X : Fin N → Bool` of the `N = n 2^n` output bits of `f : {0,1}^n → {0,1}^n`, indexed by `E : Fin N ≃ {0,1}^n × Fin n`. One value query costs `n` bit queries. [QueryComplexity/Simon.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Simon.lean) states Simon's promise `SimonPromise f s` (`f a = f b` exactly when `b ∈ {a, a ⊕ s}`) and the decision task "is `s ≠ 0`". The library has no promise-problem type for query complexity, so the classical theorem takes a total `F` that agrees with that answer on promise inputs and a randomized decision tree whose error is at most `ε` on every promise input. `simon_randomized_lower_bound` proves `2 (2^n − 1)(1 − 2ε) ≤ (q² − q)(1 − ε)` for depth `q`. At `ε = 1/3` this gives `2^n ≤ q²` (`simon_two_pow_le_depth_sq`) and `2^n ≤ R_{1/3}(F)²` for every such total `F`. The proof is a coupling over the hard inputs `π ∘ rep_s`, in the spirit of de Wolf's birthday argument. [Quantum/Simon.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/Simon.lean) runs `m` rounds of Simon's subroutine side by side as one bit-query algorithm, `simonAlg m E i₀`. It accepts when the measured rows have a common nonzero orthogonal vector, a rank test needing no further queries. It accepts with certainty when `s ≠ 0`, and with probability at most `(2^n − 1)/2^m` when `f` is injective. With `m = n + 2`, `simonAlg_spec` gives `n (n + 2)` queries with one-sided error below `1/4`. `simon_separation` combines the two bounds. `simon_separation_inputLength` states the separation in the input length: the same algorithm is correct with `n (n + 2) ≤ 3 log₂² N` queries, against `N ≤ q² log₂ N`, that is `q ≥ N^{1/2 − o(1)}`. Both sides count bit queries, so the comparison is like for like; the quantum side pays a factor `n` over de Wolf's `n + 2` value queries. Simulating value queries by bit queries transfers the classical bound to value queries only up to that factor (`q_v ≥ 2^{n/2}/n`). The coupling argument uses only that `q` queries touch at most `q` inputs `a`, so it would give `2^n ≤ q_v²` directly, but that is not formalized. [Quantum/SimonSearch.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/QueryComplexity/Quantum/SimonSearch.lean) proves the search version. The classical post-processing `simonSolve` outputs the unique nonzero string orthogonal to all measured rows, or `0`, and outputs `s` whenever the strings orthogonal to the rows are exactly `0` and `s` (`SimonDetermines`, `simonSolve_eq`). For `s ≠ 0`, the general bound `measureProb_simonAlg_le` with fibers of size at most `2^m` (`card_simonG_fiber_le`) and the pair count `BitString.card_kernel_pair` bound the failure probability by `(2^n − 1)/2^m`; for `s = 0` failure is acceptance by the decision version. So `le_measureProb_simonFound` gives success probability at least `1 − (2^n − 1)/2^m` on every promise input, including `s = 0` (injective `f`, answer `0`), and `simonAlg_search_spec` gives `n (n + 2)` queries with success probability above `3/4`. de Wolf §3.1 states the search problem with the promise `s ≠ 0`. On the classical side, `simon_search_ne_zero_two_pow_le_sq` proves `2^n ≤ (q + 2n)²` for trees that find `s` with error `1/3` on the promise inputs with `s ≠ 0`, with no requirement when `s = 0`. Each leaf `s'` is extended by the `2n` bit queries of `f(0)` and `f(s')`, answering whether `s' ≠ 0` and `f(0) = f(s')` (`RandomizedDecisionTree.mapTree`, `depth_mapTree_le`, `errorProb_mapTree_le`). This answers `false` on every injective input, so it decides Simon's problem with no larger error. `simon_search_separation` combines this bound with the quantum one. The variant that also requires the output `0` when `s = 0` has the cleaner bound `2^n ≤ q²` (`simon_search_two_pow_le_depth_sq`, by post-processing the output with `s ↦ (s ≠ 0)` through `RandomizedDecisionTree.mapOutput`). Since that variant constrains more inputs, its lower bound is weaker. `simonSolve` searches all `2^n` strings; it makes no queries, but polynomial-time post-processing by Gaussian elimination is not formalized. Complete clients: [QuantumSimon.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/QuantumSimon.lean), [QuantumSimonSearch.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/QuantumSimonSearch.lean), [QuantumBernsteinVazirani.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/QuantumBernsteinVazirani.lean) and the Deutsch–Jozsa clients above. ## Quantum Fourier transform and exact period finding

This is the quantum core of Shor's algorithm in the case where the period divides the register size. It works at the level of states and unitary matrices, using `unitaryEquiv`, `ket` and `measure`; it is not phrased in the query model of the previous section or in the uniform circuits of `BQP`.

[Fourier.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/Fourier.lean) defines `Cslib.Quantum.qft N : unitaryGroup (ZMod N) ℂ`, the matrix `F_N(j, k) = ω^{jk}/√N` with `ω = e^{2πi/N}`, for `[NeZero N]`. `qft_apply` gives its entries as `ZMod.stdAddChar (j * k) / √N` and `qft_apply_eq_exp` as `exp(2πi · j.val · k.val / N) / √N`. `qft_mulVec` identifies its action on amplitude vectors with `√N • 𝓕⁻`, Mathlib's inverse DFT `ZMod.dft.symm`, so the sign convention is Shor's eq. (4.1) and Mathlib's inverse transform. Unitarity is character orthogonality (`AddChar.sum_mulShift`). For a register pair `ι × κ`, [Register.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/Register.lean) gives `onFirst U = U ⊗ I` (`onFirst_apply`).

[PeriodFinding.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Algorithms/Quantum/PeriodFinding.lean) states the promise `HasExactPeriod f r` for `f : ZMod N → S`: `r ∣ N`, and `f x = f x' ↔ x.val ≡ x'.val [MOD r]`. Order finding is the main instance: `hasExactPeriod_pow` gives `a ↦ x ^ a.val` exact period `orderOf x` in any left-cancellative monoid when `orderOf x ∣ N`, for example `(ZMod M)ˣ`. The algorithm has three steps:

- **State preparation.** `unitaryEquiv_oracle_qft_ket_zero` takes any unitary `U` with `U|x, s₀⟩ = |x, f x⟩`. Applying `onFirst (qft N)` and then `U` to `|0, s₀⟩` gives `oracleState f = N^{-1/2} ∑_x |x⟩|f x⟩`. `addOracle f`, the permutation unitary `|x, s⟩ ↦ |x, s + f x⟩` for an additive group `S`, is one such oracle (`unitaryEquiv_addOracle_qft_ket_zero`).
- **Fourier transform.** `periodState f` applies `onFirst (qft N)` to `oracleState f`. Under the promise, the amplitude of `|y, f x₀⟩` is `ω^{x₀ y}/r` when `N / r ∣ y.val` and `0` otherwise (`HasExactPeriod.periodState_apply`). The proof uses orthogonality over the subgroup of multiples of `r` (`ZMod.sum_dvd_val_stdAddChar_mul` in [ZModMultiples.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Analysis/Fourier/ZModMultiples.lean)).
- **Measurement.** `periodFindingOutcome f` is the first-register marginal of measuring both registers. `HasExactPeriod.periodFindingOutcome_apply` gives probability `1/r` on each multiple of `N / r` and `0` elsewhere. `HasExactPeriod.periodFindingOutcome_eq` states this as equality with `Cslib.Probability.PMF.uniformOfFinset` on `{y | N / r ∣ y.val}`. `HasExactPeriod.measure_period_circuit` states the same end to end: for any oracle `U` as above, the first-register marginal of measuring `(F_N ⊗ I) U (F_N ⊗ I)|0, s₀⟩` is that uniform distribution.

Classical recovery takes the reduced denominator `N / gcd(N, y.val)` of `y / N`. Since `y = c · N / r`, this is `r` exactly when `gcd(c, r) = 1`, equivalently when `addOrderOf y = r` (`ZMod.addOrderOf_eq_div_gcd_val`). `HasExactPeriod.periodFindingOutcome_recover` gives success probability exactly `φ(r) / r`. The elementary bound `Nat.le_totient_mul_log_add_one` ([Totient.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/Nat/Totient.lean)), `n ≤ φ(n) (log₂ n + 1)`, gives at least `1 / (log₂ r + 1)` (`HasExactPeriod.inv_log_add_one_le_periodFindingOutcome_recover`). The sharper `Ω(1 / log log r)` of Shor and de Wolf rests on Hardy–Wright, Theorem 328, and is not formalized.

Complete client: [QuantumPeriodFinding.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/QuantumPeriodFinding.lean). It checks the promise for reduction modulo `3` on `ZMod 6` and fails it for periods `2` and `6`. It checks order finding for the unit `2` of order `4` in `(ZMod 5)ˣ` on `ZMod 8`, and that `qft 2` is the Hadamard matrix `Gate.hadamardMatrix` (all four entries). It computes outcome probabilities and recovery probabilities `2/3` and `1/2`, and checks that the totient bound is tight at `n = 2`. Build it with

```bash
lake build CslibFrontier.Algorithms.Quantum.PeriodFinding CslibFrontierTests.QuantumPeriodFinding
```

**Not certified here.** These are statements about unitary matrices, not gate-level circuits. There is no QFT circuit, no reversible modular exponentiation and no gate count, uniformity or approximation cost. Shor's general case `r ∤ N` with continued-fraction recovery, phase estimation, the reductions from factoring and discrete logarithm to order finding, and factoring in `BQP` are open. See the Shor's algorithm entry in [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md) and [#3252](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3252).

## Nonlocal games and Tsirelson's bound

[POVM.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/POVM.lean) adds general measurements. A `POVM A ι` is a family of positive semidefinite matrices `E_a` on `ℂ^ι` summing to the identity. Measuring a unit state `ψ` gives outcome `a` with probability `⟨ψ|E_a|ψ⟩` (`POVM.prob`, `POVM.measure`), and the computational-basis measurement `Cslib.Quantum.measure` is the POVM of the projections `|x⟩⟨x|` (`POVM.measure_basis`). Two parties measuring the halves of a bipartite state jointly perform the Kronecker product POVM (`POVM.kronecker`). A `±1` observable (Hermitian, `O² = 1`) is the projective measurement with effects `(1 ± O)/2`, outcome `false` for `+1` (`POVM.ofObservable`). Conversely, every two-outcome POVM has the observable `E_false − E_true` (`POVM.observable`), which satisfies `O² ≤ 1` even when the POVM is not projective (`POVM.posSemidef_one_sub_observable_mul_self`).

[TwoProverGame/Quantum.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PCP/TwoProverGame/Quantum.lean) gives classical two-prover games (`TwoProverGame`) entangled strategies, in the finite-dimensional tensor-product model of Cleve, Høyer, Toner and Watrous (CCC 2004, §2). A `QuantumStrategy X Y A B ι κ` is a pure unit state on `ℂ^ι ⊗ ℂ^κ` with a POVM for each question of each player. `quantumWinProb` is `∑ π(x,y) ∑ V(a,b|x,y) ⟨ψ|X_x^a ⊗ Y_y^b|ψ⟩` (`quantumWinProb_eq_sum`). `quantumValue` is the supremum over the dimensions `Fin n` and `Fin m`; the source gives both players the same `ℂⁿ`, which gives the same value after padding (not formalized; the CHSH value is unaffected, since its bound covers all dimensions and the EPR strategy has equal dimensions). Relabelling the bases (`QuantumStrategy.reindex`) shows that every strategy on finite index types is bounded by `quantumValue` (`quantumWinProb_le_quantumValue`). A deterministic strategy is a strategy on one-dimensional systems (`QuantumStrategy.ofDeterministic`), so `value_le_quantumValue`.

[TwoProverGame/CHSH.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PCP/TwoProverGame/CHSH.lean) defines the CHSH game (`chsh`: uniform bits, accept when `a ⊕ b = x ∧ y`) and proves that its classical value is `3/4` (`value_chsh`), by case analysis over the 16 deterministic strategies. By `winProb_shared_le_value` the bound also covers shared randomness. [TwoProverGame/Tsirelson.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PCP/TwoProverGame/Tsirelson.lean) proves Tsirelson's inequality (Cirel'son 1980). For any strategy with binary answers and arbitrary POVMs, the correlations `E_{xy} = ⟨ψ|A_x ⊗ B_y|ψ⟩` satisfy `E₀₀ + E₀₁ + E₁₀ − E₁₁ ≤ 2√2` (`QuantumStrategy.chsh_correlation_le`). The proof is the vector argument through Cauchy–Schwarz and the parallelogram law (`chsh_re_inner_le` in [InnerProductSpace/CHSH.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/CHSH.lean), a fact about any inner product space). The CHSH winning probability is `1/2 + (E₀₀ + E₀₁ + E₁₀ − E₁₁)/8` (`quantumWinProb_chsh`), so `quantumValue_chsh_le` bounds the entangled value by `cos²(π/8) = 1/2 + √2/4` (`Real.cos_sq_pi_div_eight`). The EPR strategy of CHTW §3.1 attains it (`chshEPR`): the state `(|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2` (`bell` from [Bell.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/Bell.lean)), Alice measuring `Z` or `X`, Bob measuring `(Z ± X)/√2` (Pauli matrices from [Pauli.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Quantum/Pauli.lean)). Its correlations `±√2/2` (`correlation_chshEPR`) come from `⟨Φ|A ⊗ B|Φ⟩ = ∑ A_ij B_ij / 2`. So `quantumValue_chsh` gives `quantumValue chsh = cos²(π/8)`, and `value_chsh_lt_quantumValue_chsh` gives the strict separation from `3/4`.

Not formalized: the mixed-state and commuting-operator models, general XOR games with Tsirelson's vector characterization and its semidefinite program (CHTW Theorem 4, Proposition 5), parallel repetition of entangled games, `MIP*`, and rigidity (self-testing) of the CHSH strategy ([#3313](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3313)). [Checked clients](../../CslibFrontierTests/Tsirelson.lean) compute winning probabilities of specific deterministic strategies, check that a `±1` observable is unitary and is recovered from its measurement, recover the classical value from one-dimensional quantum strategies, check that the EPR strategy attains Tsirelson's inequality with equality, and show that no quantum strategy wins CHSH with certainty while the entangled value exceeds `0.85`. Build them with `lake build CslibFrontierTests.Tsirelson CslibFrontierTests.TwoProverGame`.

## Truncated Polynomial Algebras and Rational Reconstruction

Five algebraic modules support truncated polynomial calculations, bridges to `JetTable` and `B_{P,ℓ}`, and exact reconstruction from modular or evaluated values:

- `Polynomial.Truncated R r` in [TruncatedAlgebra.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/Polynomial/TruncatedAlgebra.lean) realizes `R[X] / (X ^ r)` over an arbitrary commutative ring `R`, with coefficient extraction (`coeff`), canonical representatives (`toPoly`, `coeffs`), Cauchy product formulas (`coeff_mul`, `coeff_mul_range`, `mk_trunc_mul_trunc`), geometric unit inversion (`geomInv`, `unitInv`, `isUnit_iff`), nilpotent substitution (`subst`, `substZeroConst`, `subst_comp`, `mk_comp_eq_subst`, `mk_trunc_subst_eq_subst`), projection and ring-homomorphism functoriality (`proj`, `map`), and equivalence with `AdjoinRoot (X ^ r)` (`equivAdjoinRoot`).
- [TruncatedJetBridge.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/Polynomial/TruncatedJetBridge.lean) relates `Polynomial.Truncated R r` by ring isomorphism (`equivJetTableR1`, `equivJetTable1R`) to the bivariate truncated polynomial representation `Cslib.JetTable r 1 R` and `Cslib.JetTable 1 r R` ([StagedJetArithmetic.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/Polynomial/StagedJetArithmetic.lean)). [TruncatedAlgebraBridge.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Frontier/TensorNetwork/TruncatedAlgebraBridge.lean) bridges `Polynomial.Truncated (ZMod (2 ^ P)) ℓ` to `truncHom P ℓ`, `truncRingHom P ℓ`, and the tensor-network ring `Ring P (truncFl ℓ)` (`B_{P,ℓ}`).
- `Rat.reconstruct` and `Rat.reconstructPi` in [Reconstruction.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/Rat/Reconstruction.lean) formalize Wang's extended-Euclidean rational reconstruction modulo `M` ([Wang 1981][wang81]; [von zur Gathen and Gerhard][vzg13], §5.10), `Rat.height` bounds, uniqueness below `2 * N * D < M`, soundness and completeness, and Chinese-remainder multi-modulus recovery from pairwise coprime moduli (`ZMod (m i)`).
- `Polynomial.PolyRat.reconstruct` and `Polynomial.PolyRat.cauchy` in [RationalReconstruction.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/Polynomial/RationalReconstruction.lean) formalize rational-function reconstruction modulo `M : F[X]` when `N + D < M.natDegree` ([von zur Gathen and Gerhard][vzg13], §5.7, p. 115), modular uniqueness (`PolyRat.height`, `eq_of_dvd_sub_of_bounds`), Cauchy interpolation from evaluations on a finset via `Lagrange.interpolate` ([von zur Gathen and Gerhard][vzg13], §5.8, p. 118), and specialization to `(ZMod p)[X]`.

Not formalized: subquadratic fast Euclidean or half-GCD algorithms, native word-RAM bit-cost bounds for modular arithmetic or Chinese remaindering, and machine complexity embeddings of rational reconstruction ([#3672](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3672)). [Checked clients](../../CslibFrontierTests/TruncatedAndReconstruction.lean) test `Truncated ℤ 4` multiplication and `T ^ 4 = 0`, `geomInv`/`unitInv` and the `var ℚ 3` non-unit boundary, `substZeroConst`, `JetTable` and `B_{P,ℓ}` bridges, Wang reconstruction (`-3/4` and `5/3` mod `101`), CRT recovery over `[5, 7, 11]`, the `2 * N * D ≥ M` failure boundary, and `(ZMod 5)[X]` / `(ZMod 7)[X]` rational-function and Cauchy reconstruction. Build them with `lake build CslibFrontierTests.TruncatedAndReconstruction`.

## Known Limitations

The selected definitions and headline statements have been source-inspected; the full algebraic hitting, contraction, and quantum proof chains have not been independently rederived or rebuilt in this worker pass. In particular, arbitrary field/real/unitary parameters are not finite encodings, semantic operations are not native instructions, a query upper bound is not a gate or bit-time upper bound, and a tensor rank or field-circuit gate bound is not an integer bit-cost bound.
