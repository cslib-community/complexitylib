# Learning and property testing

The learning development makes a useful distinction between statistical/sample complexity, membership-query complexity and native running time. Its main local algorithms prove the first two. They should not be described as computational PAC learners merely because their sample or query bounds are polynomial or quasi-polynomial. [cslib-frontier][complexitylib]

## Upstream PAC and local uniform learning

Upstream [ConceptClass](https://github.com/leanprover/cslib/blob/254e30e6d4299c51323aaeaf5f51a4763b375d4a/Cslib/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Defs.lean#L121) defines a concept class as a set of functions and a learner as a function from an indexed labelled sample to a hypothesis. Success probabilities and excess error are measured over an explicit family of input/label distributions. It provides deterministic and internally randomized PAC notions and sample-complexity operators. The `R` in `IsRPACLearnerFor` denotes randomized, not realizable; realizability is imposed through the chosen distribution family. A learner is not required to be an encoded algorithm in this definition. [cslib][cslib]

Local [uniformExamples](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/Basic.lean#L91) specializes to Boolean functions on a finite cube and independent uniform examples. `uniformError` is the fraction of cube inputs on which the hypothesis differs from the target. `Learns` bounds this error with failure probability delta after a prescribed number of labelled samples. Its bridge to upstream PAC instantiates the uniform realizable family; it does not establish distribution-free learning.

The [low-degree Fourier learner](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/LowDegree.lean) estimates low-degree coefficients from examples and thresholds the resulting polynomial. With N retained coefficients, its sample condition is of the form `m >= (4N/epsilon) log(2N/delta)` together with a Fourier-tail assumption. The [AC0 learning theorem](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/AC0.lean) combines this learner with Linial–Mansour–Nisan concentration, giving quasi-polynomial sample bounds for the stated bounded-depth/size circuit class. The local exponent is the implementation's quantitative version, with its circuit-depth convention; native Fourier arithmetic and hypothesis-evaluation time are not part of this theorem.

The upstream randomized variant, [IsRPACLearnerFor](https://github.com/leanprover/cslib/blob/254e30e6d4299c51323aaeaf5f51a4763b375d4a/Cslib/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Defs.lean#L181), chooses a sample-to-hypothesis learner using an internal probability space and bounds the expected sample-failure probability, with the stated almost-everywhere measurability condition. This is a separate semantic randomization layer from random training examples.

## Distribution-free realizable PAC learning

Realizable PAC learning over arbitrary distributions uses the upstream deterministic notion `IsPACLearnerFor` with the family [realizableDistributions](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Realizable.lean) of joint distributions on which some concept of the class has zero error. That module is a copy of the open CSLib pull request #858. [RealizableSample](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/RealizableSample.lean) shows that i.i.d. samples from such a distribution are almost surely realizable. It follows that a learner which is consistent in the upstream `VersionSpace` sense almost surely outputs a concept agreeing with the whole sample. [ERM](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/ERM.lean) defines empirical risk minimization through the upstream `empiricalMiscount` and proves that every ERM learner is consistent on realizable samples.

[FiniteClass](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/FiniteClass.lean) proves the Occam bound for a finite class `H`, following Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Corollary 2.3. Every consistent learner, in particular every ERM learner, fails to reach error `ε` with probability at most `|H| (1 - ε)^m`, hence at most `δ` once `log |H| + log (1/δ) ≤ ε m`. The corollaries give `IsPACLearnerFor` over `realizableDistributions H` and the sample-complexity bound `⌈(log |H| + log (1/δ))/ε⌉`. The probability statements assume that each hypothesis has a measurable misclassification set. `measurableSet_misclassified` supplies this when the hypotheses are measurable and the label type has measurable equality, for example `Bool`. The learners are semantic maps: no running time or hypothesis representation is claimed.

The bad event of that bound needs no learner: `measure_pi_exists_consistent_lt_error_le_card_mul_pow` bounds the probability that some `h ∈ H` with error above `ε` is consistent with the sample, and `natCast_mul_one_sub_ofReal_pow_le` turns `|H| (1 - ε)^m` into `δ`. [CryptoHardness/Inversion](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/CryptoHardness/Inversion.lean) applies them to the easy direction of the correspondence between average-case learning and one-way functions. [blumfurstkearnslipton93][blumfurstkearnslipton93] [impagliazzolevin90][impagliazzolevin90] For a finite keyed family `F : κ → (ι → Bool) → Bool`, a key consistent with a labelled sample is a preimage of that sample under the map from a key and points to `examples (F K) x`. `labelInversion F I K` runs a randomized inverter `I` on `m` uniform examples of `F K`. `labelInversion_lt_uniformError_le` bounds the probability that the returned key has uniform error above `ε` by the probability that it disagrees with some example plus `|κ| (1 - ε)^m`; the Occam form and the average over a prior on the key follow. The inverter is an arbitrary randomized map, so no running time is counted; the polynomial-time statement through `WeakOneWayAt` remains open on [#4927](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4927). Complete client: [LearningInversion.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/LearningInversion.lean) reads the key of a constant function off its first label.

[GrowthFunction](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/GrowthFunction.lean) defines `restriction`, the set of labelings a binary class induces on a sequence of `m` points, and the growth function, the maximum size of that set. It proves Sauer's lemma, `∑_{i ≤ d} (m choose i)`, for any class whose shattered finite sets have at most `d` points; the combinatorial core is Mathlib's Sauer–Shelah lemma for set families. It also proves the forms `(m + 1)^d`, `(e m / d)^d` for `d ≤ m`, and `exp (m / T) T^d` for every real `T ≥ 1` (`growthFunction_le_exp_mul_pow`, which both explicit VC sample sizes below use), and `2^m` on a shattered set. The general binomial bound `∑_{i ≤ d} (n choose i) ≤ (n + 1)^d` lives in [Foundations/Data/Nat/Choose/Sum](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/Nat/Choose/Sum.lean), which the low-degree Boolean analysis also uses. [PACLearning/VCDimension](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/VCDimension.lean) adds two facts about the upstream `vcDim`: a shattered finite set has at most `vcDim C` points when `HasFiniteVCDim C` (`SetShatters.card_le_vcDim`), and every class on a finite domain has finite VC dimension (`hasFiniteVCDim_of_finite`). [PACLearning/DualVCDimension](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/DualVCDimension.lean) defines the dual class `dualClass C`, whose concepts are the evaluations `c ↦ c x` on the concepts of `C`, and proves Assouad's dual VC bound. If every finite set shattered by `C` has at most `d` points, then every finite family shattered by the dual class has at most `2 ^ (d + 1) - 1` members (`card_le_of_setShatters_dualClass`, and `vcDim_dualClass_le` for `vcDim`). The proof reduces to Mathlib's finset-level bitstring-coding lemma by tracing the concepts on finitely many witness points. Precomposition preserves the bounds as well: if `{g ∘ φ | g ∈ G}` shatters `W`, then `φ` is injective on `W` and `G` shatters `φ '' W` (`SetShatters.card_le_of_comp`). This is the form the Moran–Yehudayoff compression scheme uses. [DualVCDimension.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/DualVCDimension.lean) checks the bound for the thresholds on `ℕ`.

[PACLearning/Halfspaces](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Halfspaces.lean) computes the VC dimension of halfspaces (Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Section 9.1.3). For a linear map `Φ : V →ₗ[ℝ] α → ℝ` from a real parameter space, `positivityClass Φ` is the class `{x ↦ decide (0 < Φ v x)}`. When `V` is finite-dimensional it shatters no finite set of more than `finrank ℝ V` points (`card_le_finrank_of_setShatters_positivityClass`, `vcDim_positivityClass_le`): the evaluation functionals at such points are linearly dependent, and labelling the points with positive coefficients `true` gives a contradiction. Points at which the parameters realize every real vector of values are shattered (`setShatters_positivityClass_range`), so `vcDim_positivityClass_eq` gives equality when there are `finrank ℝ V` such points. The instances are `homogeneousHalfspaces d`, `x ↦ decide (0 < w ⬝ᵥ x)` on `Fin d → ℝ`, with VC dimension `d` (`vcDim_homogeneousHalfspaces`, Theorem 9.2), and `affineHalfspaces d`, `x ↦ decide (0 < w ⬝ᵥ x + b)`, with VC dimension `d + 1` (`vcDim_affineHalfspaces`, Theorem 9.3). `homogeneousHalfspaces_eq_positivityClass` and `affineHalfspaces_eq_positivityClass` rewrite a halfspace class into the general form. The source labels by `sign (⟪w, x⟫ + b)`; here a point is `true` exactly when the value is strictly positive, as in the perceptron. [LinearPredictors.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/LinearPredictors.lean) checks that the standard basis of `ℝ²` is shattered, that two points on one open ray are not, and that no three points of the plane are.

[PACLearning/NatarajanDimension](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/NatarajanDimension.lean) extends shattering to multiclass classes `C : ConceptClass α β` (Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Section 29.1). `NatarajanShatters C W` asks for two labelings `f₀ f₁ : α → β` that differ at every point of `W` such that every `B ⊆ W` is realized by some concept that agrees with `f₀` on `B` and with `f₁` on `W \ B` (Definition 29.1). `natarajanDim C` and `HasFiniteNatarajanDim C` mirror `vcDim` and `HasFiniteVCDim`. With `Bool` labels N-shattering is shattering (`natarajanShatters_iff_setShatters`), so `natarajanDim C = vcDim C` (`natarajanDim_eq_vcDim`). Natarajan's lemma replaces Sauer's lemma. With `k` labels, a set of labelings of `n` points whose N-shattered sets have at most `d` points has at most `natarajanSum n k d = ∑_{i ≤ d} (n choose i) (k choose 2)^i` elements (`ncard_le_natarajanSum`). The proof is the induction of Exercise 29.3, counting unordered rather than ordered pairs of labels. `ncard_restriction_le_natarajanSum` bounds the labelings a class realizes on sample points, with repetitions allowed; `NatarajanShatters.of_restrict` transfers N-shattering from those labelings back to the points. For `k ≥ 1` the sum is at most `(n + 1)^d k^(2d)` (`natarajanSum_le`). On a finite domain, `ncard_le_card_pow_natarajanDim` gives the form of Lemma 29.4, `|C| ≤ |α|^Ndim(C) k^(2 Ndim(C))`. The multiclass sample-complexity bounds of Theorem 29.3 remain open in #5084. [NatarajanDimension.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/NatarajanDimension.lean) shows that the class of all functions N-shatters every set and has Natarajan dimension `|α|`, and that with `Bool` labels on three points it attains the bound `8`. It also shows that the constant functions N-shatter one point but no two, which makes their dimension `1`. A class on four points with three labels and dimension at most `1` therefore has at most `13` of the `81` functions. Finally, homogeneous halfspaces in the plane have Natarajan dimension `2`.

[EpsilonNet](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/EpsilonNet.lean) proves the ε-net theorem by double-sample symmetrization. The measure-theoretic steps are reusable and live in [DoubleSample](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/DoubleSample.lean). `swapSamples σ` exchanges chosen coordinates between two samples and preserves `μ^m × μ^m` on any measurable space with σ-finite `μ` (`measurePreserving_swapSamples`). If at most `2^m K` of the `2^m` swaps of every double sample land in a measurable set `J`, then `J` has probability at most `K` (`measure_prod_le_of_card_filter_swapSamples_mem_le`). The ghost-sample bound `ν B ≤ 2 (ν × ν') J` holds when every `S ∈ B` extends into `J` with probability at least `1/2` (`measure_le_two_mul_measure_prod`). A sample is an ε-net for a class `G` under `μ` if it meets every `g ∈ G` of mass at least `ε` (`IsEpsNet`). For `εm ≥ 8`, `m` i.i.d. samples fail to be an ε-net with probability at most `2 Π_G(2m) / 2^⌈εm/2⌉` (`measure_pi_not_isEpsNet_mul_two_pow_le`). With Sauer's lemma, `vcSampleSize d ε δ = (8/ε)(d log (48/ε) + log (4/δ))` samples bring this below `δ` (`measure_pi_not_isEpsNet_le_of_vcSampleSize_le`). Here `d` bounds the size of every finite set shattered by the class; `hasFiniteVCDim_iff` supplies such a `d` from `HasFiniteVCDim`. [VCUpperBound](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/VCUpperBound.lean) applies the theorem to the loss class of `C`. Every consistent learner, in particular every ERM learner, then reaches error `ε` with confidence `1 - δ` from `vcSampleSize d ε δ` examples over every distribution realizable by `C` (`IsConsistent.measure_pi_lt_error_le_of_vcSampleSize_le`). This is the VC upper bound of Blumer, Ehrenfeucht, Haussler and Warmuth (Theorem 2.1) with different constants of the same form. The corollaries are `isPACLearnerFor_realizableDistributions_of_vcSampleSize_le`, the realizable sample-complexity bound `⌈vcSampleSize (vcDim C) ε δ⌉` (`sampleComplexity_realizableDistributions_le_of_hasFiniteVCDim`), and `isPACLearnable_realizableDistributions_of_hasFiniteVCDim` for every class of finite VC dimension. These results assume a countable domain with measurable singletons and `Bool` labels, so that every set of samples is measurable. General domains need a measurability ("well-behaved") hypothesis on the class; issue #2868 tracks that case. The matching EHKV lower bound `Ω(d/ε)` and the resulting characterization of learnability are described in [PAC lower bounds and the fundamental theorem](#pac-lower-bounds-and-the-fundamental-theorem). A mistake bound gives another route, with sample size `⌈2 M / (ε δ)⌉`, through [online-to-batch conversion](#online-learning-in-the-mistake-bound-model).

[Compression/Basic](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Compression/Basic.lean) defines `SampleCompressionScheme C k`. On every sample realized by `C`, `compress` keeps a list of at most `k` indices and `reconstruct`, applied to the kept examples, labels the whole sample correctly; the reconstructed hypothesis need not lie in `C`. The compression bound `measure_pi_lt_error_learner_le` (Littlestone and Warmuth; Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Chapter 30) says that on `m` i.i.d. examples from a distribution realizable by `C`, the scheme's learner has error above `ε` with probability at most `(m + 1)^k (1 - ε)^(m - k)`. Each of the at most `(m + 1)^k` index lists leaves at least `m - k` held-out examples, which a hypothesis of error above `ε` labels correctly with probability at most `(1 - ε)^(m - k)`. Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David count `m^k` sequences of exactly `k` indices; keeping at most `k` costs the extra `+ 1`. `SampleCompressionScheme.isPACLearnerFor` (condition `(m + 1)^k (1 - ε)^(m - k) ≤ δ`), `SampleCompressionScheme.isPACLearnerFor_of_log_le` (condition `k log (m + 1) + log (1/δ) ≤ ε (m - k)`) and `SampleCompressionScheme.isPACLearnable` give the realizable PAC conclusions. `SampleCompressionScheme.ofSubset` restricts a scheme to a subclass. To apply them to a new class, build a scheme and prove `MeasurableReconstruction` for its reconstruction map: the misclassification set must be measurable jointly in the kept examples and the test example. On countable domains `measurableReconstruction_of_countable _` discharges it; for jointly measurable reconstructions use `measurableReconstruction_of_measurable`. The compression map needs no measurability. Conversely, [Compression/Shattering](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Compression/Shattering.lean) shows that a scheme of size `k` bounds the shattered sets: if `C` shatters a finite set `W`, the `2 ^ |W|` labelings of `W` inject into the lists of at most `k` labeled points of `W`, so `2 ^ |W| ≤ (2 |W| + 1) ^ k` (`SampleCompressionScheme.two_pow_card_le_of_setShatters`) and `C` has finite VC dimension (`SampleCompressionScheme.hasFiniteVCDim`). [CompressionShattering.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/CompressionShattering.lean) derives from the size-1 lower-set scheme that lower sets of `ℕ` shatter at most two points.

[Compression/Order](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Compression/Order.lean) gives schemes of size 1 for `lowerSetClass α` (thresholds: keep the largest positive example) and size 2 for `ordConnectedClass α` (intervals: keep the smallest and largest positive examples) on a linear order. On `ℝ` or `ℕ` these classes are exactly the thresholds and the intervals; on an order such as `ℚ` they also contain cuts at irrational points. Their reconstructions are measurable on second-countable order topologies such as `ℝ`. [Compression/Thresholds](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Compression/Thresholds.lean) places the thresholds `x < θ` of the mistake-bound development, whose Littlestone dimension is infinite, in `lowerSetClass ℕ` (`thresholdClass_subset_lowerSetClass`), so they are PAC learnable (`isPACLearnable_thresholdClass`). [Compression/MistakeBound](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Compression/MistakeBound.lean) turns a deterministic online learner with mistake bound `M` into the size-`M` scheme `ofMistakeBound`, which reconstructs with the learner itself (Floyd and Warmuth). Repeated passes over the sample keep the examples that the learner, fed the kept examples, gets wrong. On a realizable sample the kept examples are a mistake sequence, so at most `M` are kept and within `M + 1` passes a pass keeps nothing new. For a learner with `IsMeasurableLearner A` (see the online-to-batch module), `IsMeasurableLearner.measurableReconstruction` supplies the measurability hypothesis, and `isPACLearnerFor_of_mistakeBound_le` gives the realizable sample size `(m + 1)^M (1 - ε)^(m - M) ≤ δ`. That is logarithmic in `1/δ`, whereas `isPACLearnerFor_prefixMajority` needs `2 M ≤ ε δ (m + 1)`, linear in `1/δ` but with no `log m` factor. With SOA, `ofLittlestoneDim` has size `d` when `littlestoneDim C ≤ d`, and `isPACLearnerFor_of_littlestoneDim_le` gives the sample size `(m + 1)^d (1 - ε)^(m - d) ≤ δ` on countable domains. [SampleCompression.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/SampleCompression.lean) runs the threshold, interval and halving schemes on concrete samples by `decide`, including a non-realizable sample on which the threshold reconstruction errs. It also checks explicit sample sizes for `ε = δ = 1/2`: 5 examples for thresholds and 10 for intervals on `ℝ`; 5 for a three-concept class from the halving algorithm's mistake bound; 10 for the thresholds `θ ≤ 3` on `ℕ` from their Littlestone dimension `2`; and 5 for all thresholds on `ℕ`, which have infinite Littlestone dimension, from the threshold scheme.

[Compression/VCDimension](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Compression/VCDimension.lean) begins the Moran–Yehudayoff construction of a compression scheme of size `exp(O(d))` for every class of VC dimension `d`. Fix a consistent learner `A` reading `s ≥ vcSampleSize d ε δ` examples with `δ < 1` and a sample `S` of `m` examples that `C` realizes. For every distribution on the positions of `S`, some sequence `b` of `s` positions has `A (S ∘ b)` wrong with probability at most `ε` (`IsConsistent.exists_subsample_toOuterMeasure_le`). This is the VC upper bound on the finite domain `Fin m`, so it needs no measurability hypothesis. The minimax theorem then gives one distribution on such sequences that is wrong at each example of `S` with probability at most `ε` (`IsConsistent.exists_pmf_subsample_toOuterMeasure_le`, for `0 < m`). The remaining step, a small set of subsamples whose majority vote is right everywhere, needs ε-approximations for the dual class. [VCCompression.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/VCCompression.lean) applies the result to the thresholds on `ℕ` at `ε = 1/3`, `δ = 1/2`.

Complete clients: [RealizablePAC.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/RealizablePAC.lean) discharges the measurability side condition with `measurableSet_misclassified (measurable_of_countable _)`. It also checks the realizable and non-realizable distributions, ERM and non-consistent learners, Occam and VC sample-complexity bounds for small parameters, a tight Sauer bound for thresholds, and the swap-counting and ghost-sample bounds on one-point samples.

## Agnostic PAC learning

The upstream `IsPACLearnerFor` already measures success against `optimalError D C`, the best error in the class, so agnostic PAC learning is `IsPACLearnerFor` over the family `Set.univ` of all distributions; no realizability is assumed. [UniformConvergence](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/UniformConvergence.lean) defines `IsRepresentative D C ε S`: the empirical error of every `h ∈ C` on `S` is within `ε` of its true error (Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Definition 4.1). Hoeffding's inequality for one hypothesis (`measure_pi_le_abs_empiricalMiscount_div_sub_error`) and a union bound give uniform convergence for a finite class: `m` i.i.d. examples fail to be `ε`-representative with probability at most `|H| · 2 exp (-2 m ε²)` (`measure_pi_not_isRepresentative_le`). On an `ε / 2`-representative sample every empirical risk minimizer has error at most `optimalError D C + ε` (`IsRepresentative.error_le_optimalError_add`, Lemma 4.2, for any class). [AgnosticFiniteClass](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/AgnosticFiniteClass.lean) combines them into Corollary 4.6. Under every distribution, an ERM learner for a finite class `H` misses `optimalError D H + ε` with probability at most `δ` once `2 log (2 |H| / δ) ≤ ε² m` (`IsERM.measure_pi_lt_error_le_of_log_le`). The corollaries are `isPACLearnerFor_univ_of_log_le`, the agnostic sample-complexity bound `⌈2 log (2 |H| / δ) / ε²⌉` (`sampleComplexity_univ_le_of_finite`) and `isPACLearnable_univ_of_finite`. The first two assume `[Nonempty (α → β)]`: for empty `H` the sample-size condition already holds at `m = 0`, and a learner from zero examples exists only if some hypothesis `α → β` does. The domain and labels are arbitrary measurable spaces; as in the realizable case, each hypothesis needs a measurable misclassification set, and the learners are semantic maps. The Hoeffding step is stated for the relative frequency of any measurable event (`measure_pi_le_abs_card_filter_div_sub_measure`).

[VCUniformConvergence](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/VCUniformConvergence.lean) proves the uniform convergence theorem of Vapnik and Chervonenkis by double-sample symmetrization, reusing [DoubleSample](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/DoubleSample.lean). For a Boolean class `G` on a countable domain with measurable singletons, `m` i.i.d. samples make the relative frequency of some `{g = true}` deviate from its probability by at least `ε` with probability at most `4 Π_G(2m) exp (-m ε² / 8)` (`measure_pi_exists_le_abs_card_filter_div_sub_measure_le`; Anthony and Bartlett, Theorem 4.3, state the strict form). The ghost-sample step uses that Hoeffding bound at `ε / 2`; the swap step uses Hoeffding's inequality for random bit strings (`Cslib.BitString.card_filter_le_abs_sum_mul_sub_half_le`) once for each of the at most `Π_G(2m)` labelings of the double sample. Through the loss class, a binary class `C` fails to be `ε`-representative with probability at most `4 Π_C(2m) exp (-m ε² / 8)` (`measure_pi_not_isRepresentative_le_growthFunction`). With Sauer's lemma at `T = (10/ε)²`, `uniformConvergenceSampleSize d ε δ = (16/ε²)(2d log (10/ε) + log (4/δ))` examples bring this below `δ` for `0 < ε ≤ 10` and `0 < δ` (`measure_pi_not_isRepresentative_le_of_uniformConvergenceSampleSize_le`). [AgnosticVC](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/AgnosticVC.lean) combines this with Lemma 4.2. Under every distribution, an ERM learner for `C` misses `optimalError D C + ε` with probability at most `δ` from `uniformConvergenceSampleSize d (ε/2) δ` examples (`IsERM.measure_pi_lt_error_le_of_uniformConvergenceSampleSize_le`). The corollaries are `isPACLearnerFor_univ_of_uniformConvergenceSampleSize_le`, the agnostic sample-complexity bound `⌈uniformConvergenceSampleSize (vcDim C) (ε/2) δ⌉` (`sampleComplexity_univ_le_of_hasFiniteVCDim`), and `isPACLearnable_univ_of_hasFiniteVCDim`. The rate is `O((d log (1/ε) + log (1/δ))/ε²)`. The sharp rate `O((d + log (1/δ))/ε²)` of Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Theorem 6.8, needs chaining; [AgnosticVCSharp](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/AgnosticVCSharp.lean) below proves it. Uncountable domains need a measurability hypothesis on the class, which issue #3005 tracks.

Complete client: [AgnosticPAC.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/AgnosticPAC.lean) uses a distribution that labels one point both ways. No class realizes it, and the two constant hypotheses have optimal error `1 / 2` under it. The file checks a representative sample and a non-representative one. It also places the agnostic sample complexity of the constants at `(1/2, 1/2)` in `[1, 17]`.

Complete client: [AgnosticVC.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/AgnosticVC.lean) uses a distribution on `ℕ × Bool` that labels the point `1` both ways. No class realizes it, and the optimal error of thresholds under it is `1 / 4`. The file checks uniform convergence and the ERM tail bound for thresholds under it, an explicit agnostic sample size of `97600` at `ε = δ = 1/10`, and that the constant-`true` learner fails.

[EpsilonApproximation](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/EpsilonApproximation.lean) defines ε-approximations: `IsEpsApprox μ G ε S` says that for every `g ∈ G` the relative frequency of `{g = true}` among the points of `S` is within `ε` of `μ {g = true}`. From the uniform convergence theorem, `m` i.i.d. samples fail to be an ε-approximation with probability at most `δ` once `uniformConvergenceSampleSize d ε δ ≤ m` (`measure_pi_not_isEpsApprox_le_of_uniformConvergenceSampleSize_le`). So for `δ < 1` every probability measure on a countable domain has an ε-approximation of that size (`exists_isEpsApprox_of_uniformConvergenceSampleSize_le`; Moran and Yehudayoff, Theorem 2.1, with the weaker size `O(d log (1/ε)/ε²)`). A distribution given as a `PMF` on a countable type enters through `PMF.toMeasure`. Applied to the dual class, this is the sampling step of the Moran–Yehudayoff compression scheme (#4914). [EpsilonApproximation.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/EpsilonApproximation.lean) checks it for all Boolean functions on `Bool`.

[Packing](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Packing.lean) is the first step toward the sharp rate. Fix `m > 0` points `x`. A finite set `P` of labelings in `restriction C x` is `ε`-separated if any two of them differ on at least `ε m` points. Subsampling bounds such a set by the growth function. If `2 log |P| < ε k`, then some `k` of the points, possibly repeated, keep the labelings of `P` distinct (`exists_injOn_comp_of_two_mul_log_card_lt`), so `|P| ≤ Π_C(k)` (`card_le_growthFunction_of_two_mul_log_card_lt`). With Sauer's lemma, `|P| ≤ (8/ε)^(2d)` for `0 < ε ≤ 1` (`card_le_div_pow_of_le_hammingDist`). Unlike `Π_C(m)`, this bound does not grow with `m`. It is Dudley's packing bound (Vershynin, Theorem 8.3.18), not Haussler's sharper `e (d + 1) (2e/ε)^d`. A separated subset of maximal size is a net at the same scale (`Cslib.Code.exists_subset_pairwise_lt_hammingDist_forall_exists_le`, with a natural radius), so the bound also limits nets; chaining needs such a net at each scale. Complete client: [Packing.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/Packing.lean) bounds half-separated threshold labelings by `256` and gives a net of at most `1024` threshold labelings at Hamming scale `m / 4`.

[AgnosticVCSharp](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/AgnosticVCSharp.lean) proves the sharp rate on countable domains with measurable singletons. The ghost-sample step of VCUniformConvergence is stated for any bound on the fraction of bad swaps of a double sample (`measure_pi_exists_le_abs_card_filter_div_sub_measure_le_of_card_filter_swapSamples_le`). Here that fraction is bounded by chaining rather than by a union over the `Π_G(2m)` labelings. Under a swap, the count difference of `g` is a correlation of `a_g i = g (z.1 i) - g (z.2 i)` with random signs. The maximum over `g ∈ G` changes by at most `2` when one sign changes, so McDiarmid's inequality for bit strings (`Cslib.BitString.card_filter_expect_add_le_le`) controls the fraction of swaps on which it exceeds its mean. The mean is at most `32 √(m d)` by `expect_ciSup_sum_mul_signVal_le_of_hammingDist`, since `a_g` depends only on the labeling of the double sample by `g`. If every shattered set has at most `d` points and `16384 d ≤ m ε²`, the bound is `4 exp (-m ε² / 32)` (`measure_pi_exists_le_abs_card_filter_div_sub_measure_le_exp`), with no growth-function factor. The loss class shatters no larger sets than `C` (`card_le_of_setShatters_lossClass`), which gives `measure_pi_not_isRepresentative_le_exp`, the ERM tail `IsERM.measure_pi_lt_error_le_exp`, and, with `chainingSampleSize d ε δ = (16384 d + 32 log (4/δ)) / ε²`, the corollaries `isPACLearnerFor_univ_of_chainingSampleSize_le` and `sampleComplexity_univ_le_chainingSampleSize`, an agnostic sample complexity of at most `⌈chainingSampleSize (vcDim C) (ε/2) δ⌉`. The constants are not optimized: at `d = 1` and `ε = δ = 1/10` this size is about `6.6 · 10⁶`, against `97600` from `uniformConvergenceSampleSize`. The chaining size wins only for very small `ε`. Complete client: [AgnosticVCSharp.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/AgnosticVCSharp.lean) checks uniform convergence and the ERM tail bound for thresholds under the non-realizable distribution of AgnosticVC.lean, and the explicit sample size `6607000` at `ε = δ = 1/10`, with its sample-complexity bound.

## PAC lower bounds and the fundamental theorem

[SampleComplexityLower](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/SampleComplexityLower.lean) and its `SampleComplexityLower/` submodules vendor Ehrenfeucht, Haussler, Kearns and Valiant's lower bound (Theorem 1) from Samuel Schlesinger's unsubmitted CSLib branch `feat/pac-learning-ehkv-headline`; the adaptations to the current CSLib and Mathlib pins are marked `-- vendored:` in the sources. If a class `C` shatters a finite set `W` with `|W| ≥ 2`, every randomized `(ε, δ)`-PAC learner with `ε ≤ 1/8` and `δ < 1/14` over a family containing the distributions `C` realizes uses `m ≥ (|W| - 1)/(32 ε)` examples (`sample_complexity_lower_bound_randomized`, with deterministic, agnostic and `vcDim` corollaries). The source's Theorem 1 (§3) assumes `δ ≤ 1/100`; the wider range `δ < 1/14` is the vendored proof's adaptation, and the Lemma 3 label in `EHKVProof` carries the same qualification. These statements assume that every concept of `C` is measurable. [VCLowerBound](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/VCLowerBound.lean) removes that hypothesis on every domain with measurable singletons. `restrictDomain C s` restricts the concepts to a subdomain `s`; on a countable `s` the inclusion `s × Bool → α × Bool` is a measurable embedding (`MeasurableEmbedding.of_countable` in [MeasurableSpace/Embedding](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/MeasureTheory/MeasurableSpace/Embedding.lean): an injection out of a countable space with measurable singletons), so a distribution and its pushforward give every hypothesis the same error and the class the same optimal error. Hence a learner for `C`, composed with the inclusion, learns the restricted class (`IsPACLearnerFor.restrictDomain`, `IsRPACLearnerFor.restrictDomain`), and realizable distributions push forward to realizable ones (`isRealizableBy_map_inclusion`). On the finite set `W` every concept is measurable, so the vendored bound gives `|W| ≤ 32 ε m + 1` for any class (`card_le_of_isRPACLearnerFor`, `card_le_of_isPACLearnerFor`) and `vcDim C ≤ 32 ε m + 1` (`vcDim_le_of_isRPACLearnerFor`). Consequently PAC learnability, deterministic or randomized, over any family containing the realizable distributions implies finite VC dimension (`IsPACLearnable.hasFiniteVCDim`, `IsRPACLearnable.hasFiniteVCDim`). Measurable singletons cannot simply be dropped: under the trivial σ-algebra a nonconstant hypothesis has error one under every distribution, so a majority vote learns the class of all Boolean functions agnostically although it shatters every finite set.

[UniformConvergenceProperty](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/UniformConvergenceProperty.lean) defines the two remaining statements of Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Theorem 6.7, over a family of distributions. `HasUniformConvergence C` is the uniform convergence property over the family (their Definition 4.3): for `ε, δ ∈ (0, 1)`, all sufficiently large samples from any distribution in the family are `ε`-representative with probability at least `1 - δ`. `IsERMLearnable C` says that for every `ε` and `δ` one sample size `m₀` makes every ERM on `m ≥ m₀` examples an `(ε, δ)`-learner over the family. The source lets the sample size depend on the ERM rule; uniform convergence supplies the uniform version, so the characterization is unchanged. Uniform convergence at `ε / 2` gives learnability by every ERM (`HasUniformConvergence.isERMLearnable`, Corollary 4.4), and learnability by every ERM gives PAC learnability (`IsERMLearnable.isPACLearnable`), because every nonempty class has an ERM. In [AgnosticVC](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/AgnosticVC.lean), a class of finite VC dimension on a countable domain has the uniform convergence property over all distributions (`hasUniformConvergence_univ_of_hasFiniteVCDim`).

[FundamentalTheorem](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/FundamentalTheorem.lean) proves Theorem 6.7 on countable domains with measurable singletons (`tfae_hasFiniteVCDim`): uniform convergence over all distributions, learnability by every ERM over all distributions, agnostic PAC learnability, PAC learnability over the realizable distributions, learnability by every ERM over the realizable distributions, and finite VC dimension are equivalent. The cycle runs from uniform convergence through ERM to agnostic and then realizable learnability, from there to finite VC dimension by the lower bound above, and back to uniform convergence by the growth-function bound. The named corollaries are `isPACLearnable_univ_iff_hasFiniteVCDim` and `isPACLearnable_realizableDistributions_iff_hasFiniteVCDim`, with `isRPACLearnable_univ_iff_hasFiniteVCDim` and `isRPACLearnable_realizableDistributions_iff_hasFiniteVCDim` for randomized learners. On every domain with measurable singletons, agnostic learnability (`IsPACLearnable C Set.univ`) implies finite VC dimension (`IsPACLearnable.hasFiniteVCDim_of_univ`). Issue #4912 tracks the rest: the quantitative bounds of their Theorem 6.8 and the upper bounds on uncountable domains (#2868).

Complete client: [FundamentalTheoremPAC.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/FundamentalTheoremPAC.lean) shows that thresholds on `ℕ` have the uniform convergence property, are learned by every ERM, and are realizably and agnostically learnable, through the characterization. The class of all Boolean functions has none of these properties on `ℕ` and is not learnable on `ℝ`, where most of its concepts are not Borel measurable. It also shows that ten examples do not learn all Boolean functions on `ℕ` to accuracy `1/8` with confidence `14/15`, and that zero examples do not learn them on `Fin 3`.

## Nonuniform learning, SRM and MDL

[SRM](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/SRM.lean) follows Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Section 7.2. A `Weighting ι` has positive weights that sum to at most `1`; `Weighting.geometric` on `ℕ` and `Weighting.comap` along an injection are provided. `HasUniformConvergenceRate H 𝒟 ε` says that for `m > 0` and `δ > 0`, `m` examples fail to be `ε m δ`-representative with probability at most `δ`. With `ε m δ → 0` as `m → ∞` this is the uniform convergence property (Definition 4.3); without that limit, any rate `ε ≥ 1` qualifies. The Hoeffding rates for finite classes and singletons are `hasUniformConvergenceRate_finset` and `hasUniformConvergenceRate_singleton`; the sample-size form of the finite-class bound is `measure_pi_not_isRepresentative_le_of_log_le`. The SRM bound (Theorem 7.4) is `Weighting.measure_pi_exists_not_isRepresentative_le`, with one-sided form `Weighting.measure_pi_exists_lt_error_le`. It gives confidence `w i · δ` to class `i`; with probability at least `1 - δ`, every `h ∈ H i` then has empirical and true error within `ε i m (w i · δ)` of each other. `IsSRM A H pen` says that `A` outputs a minimizer of `L_S(h) + pen i` over all pairs `(i, h)` with `h ∈ H i`. The source instead charges `h` the bound of the first class that contains it. `exists_isSRM` gives a minimizer when the penalties tend to infinity along the indices. `IsSRM.measure_pi_exists_lt_error_le` is the excess-risk guarantee: if `pen i` dominates the rates, then with probability at least `1 - δ` the output's error is at most `error D h + 2 pen i` for all `i` and `h ∈ H i`.

[Nonuniform](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Nonuniform.lean) defines `IsNonuniformLearnable C 𝒟` (Definition 7.1). For every accuracy `ε` and confidence `δ` in `(0, 1)` there are learners, one per sample size, such that for every `h ∈ C` there is a sample size `m₀` beyond which, under every distribution of `𝒟`, the output's error exceeds `error D h + ε` with probability at most `δ`. Unlike PAC learning, `m₀` may depend on `h`. As in the upstream `IsPACLearnable`, the learners may depend on `(ε, δ)`; the source fixes one algorithm. `isNonuniformLearnable_iUnion` (Theorems 7.3 and 7.5) proves that a countable union of classes with vanishing rates is nonuniformly learnable by SRM. Its penalty adds `e i / m` for an injection `e` into `ℕ`, so that a minimizer always exists. Theorem 7.5's explicit sample complexity is not stated. `isNonuniformLearnable_of_countable` applies the theorem to countable classes, viewed as unions of singletons (Section 7.3).

[MDL](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/MDL.lean) defines `IsPrefixFreeDescription C d`: binary descriptions `d : (α → β) → List Bool`, none a prefix of another within `C`. Its Kraft weighting `h ↦ 2^-|d h|` comes from Mathlib's Kraft inequality `InformationTheory.IsPrefixFree.tsum_one_div_card_pow_length_le_one` (Lemma 7.6); the finite Kraft converse in the Huffman development is not needed. `IsPrefixFreeDescription.mdl_bound` is the MDL bound (Theorem 7.7): with probability at least `1 - δ`, every `h ∈ C` has `L_D(h) ≤ L_S(h) + √((|d h| log 2 + log (2 / δ)) / (2 m))`. The source has the weaker `|h|` in place of `|d h| log 2`. All these bounds hold for arbitrary measurable domains and labels, provided each hypothesis has a measurable misclassification set. Three results remain open, tracked by issue #4915: the converse of the characterization (Theorem 7.2), that a nonuniformly learnable class is a countable union of finite-VC classes; that PAC learnability implies nonuniform learnability; and consistency (Section 7.4).

Complete client: [NonuniformPAC.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/NonuniformPAC.lean) uses the thresholds on `ℕ`. With penalty `t` for `{threshold t}`, the constant learner `threshold 0` satisfies `IsSRM` on one-example samples. It fails `IsSRM` under equal penalties on the example `(0, false)`. The geometric weighting gives a nonvacuous SRM bound: with `m = 10000` and `δ = 1/20`, `threshold 0` has gap at most `1/50`. The unary code `1^t 0` is a prefix-free description of the thresholds; its Kraft weights are the geometric weights, and its MDL bound has gap `√(((t + 1) log 2 + log (2 / δ)) / (2 m))`. A description that is injective but not prefix-free is rejected. The thresholds and the indicators of finite sets of naturals are nonuniformly learnable; the latter class shatters every finite set (`not_hasFiniteVCDim_finsetIndicators`).

## PAC-Bayes bounds

[PACBayes/Basic](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACBayes/Basic.lean) proves McAllester's PAC-Bayes bound for a countable hypothesis index `ι` with measurable singletons, following Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Theorem 31.1. Losses are functions `ℓ : ι → Z → ℝ` with values in `[0, 1]`, each measurable. The risks are `trueRisk ℓ D i` and `empiricalRisk ℓ S i` of [LossMinimization/Basic](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/LossMinimization/Basic.lean) (see [Learning with general losses](#learning-with-general-losses)), on samples `S : Fin m → Z` drawn from `Measure.pi`. For a prior `P` fixed before the sample, `m ≥ 2` and `δ > 0`, the samples on which some probability measure `Q` on `ι` with `klDiv Q P ≠ ⊤` has `𝔼_Q L_S + √((KL(Q ‖ P) + log (m / δ)) / (2 (m - 1))) < 𝔼_Q L_D` have probability at most `δ` (`measure_pi_exists_integral_risk_gt_log_div`). The posterior is quantified inside the event, so it may depend on the sample. The proof uses `log (√m / δ)` (`measure_pi_exists_integral_risk_gt`), because Hoeffding's lemma gives the exponential moment `√m` where the source's Exercise 31.1 gives `m`. Point-mass posteriors give the Occam bound of Exercise 31.2: every `i` with `P {i} > 0` has `L_D(i) ≤ L_S(i) + √((log (1 / P {i}) + log (m / δ)) / (2 (m - 1)))` (`measure_pi_exists_risk_gt`). The probabilistic inputs are general. [Probability/ChangeOfMeasure](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/ChangeOfMeasure.lean) proves the change-of-measure inequality `∫ f ∂μ ≤ KL(μ ‖ ν) + log ∫ exp f ∂ν` from Mathlib's tilted measures and evaluates `KL(δ_a ‖ ν) = log (1 / ν {a})`. [Probability/SubGaussian](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/SubGaussian.lean) proves `𝔼 exp (s X²) ≤ 1 / √(1 - 2 s c)` for Mathlib's `HasSubgaussianMGF X c` and shows that the empirical mean of `m` bounded i.i.d. samples is sub-Gaussian with variance proxy `1 / (4 m)`. For a countable class of classifiers under the 0-1 loss, the weighted SRM bound of [Nonuniform learning, SRM and MDL](#nonuniform-learning-srm-and-mdl) applied to singleton classes gives the sharper Occam form `√((log (1 / w h) + log (2 / δ)) / (2 m))`; the PAC-Bayes bound covers general `[0, 1]` losses and posteriors that average over hypotheses. The `kl` form is in the next paragraph. Measurable (uncountable) hypothesis spaces, Catoni's bound and margin applications remain open (#4916).

[PACBayes/KL](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACBayes/KL.lean) proves the PAC-Bayes-kl bound of Seeger (Theorem 1, stated for the 0-1 loss) for losses in `[0, 1]`, following Maurer. In the same setting with `m ≥ 1`, the samples on which some posterior `Q` with `klDiv Q P ≠ ⊤` has `(KL(Q ‖ P) + log ((m + 1) / δ)) / m < binaryKL (𝔼_Q L_S) (𝔼_Q L_D)` have probability at most `δ` (`measure_pi_exists_binaryKL_integral_risk_gt`); point masses give the Occam form with `log (1 / P {i})` (`measure_pi_exists_binaryKL_risk_gt`). The bound is implicit in the true risk and is much sharper than McAllester's when the empirical risk is near `0`. [Probability/BinaryKL](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/BinaryKL.lean) defines the binary relative entropy `binaryKL q p = p klFun (q / p) + (1 - p) klFun ((1 - q) / (1 - p))` from Mathlib's `klFun`, proves convexity in `q`, the binomial bound `C(m, k) p^k (1 - p)^(m - k) exp (m binaryKL (k / m) p) ≤ 1`, the log-sum inequality in integral form and Jensen's inequality `binaryKL (∫ q) (∫ p) ≤ ∫ binaryKL q p`. At `p ∈ {0, 1}` with `q ≠ p` the true divergence is infinite while `binaryKL` takes finite junk values, so Jensen's inequality assumes `q = p` wherever `p ∈ {0, 1}`; the theorem gets this for every hypothesis on almost every sample. [Probability/BernoulliComparison](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/BernoulliComparison.lean) proves Maurer's comparison (Lemma 3): for `φ ≥ 0` convex on `[0, 1]`, `𝔼 φ` of the mean of `n` i.i.d. `[0, 1]` variables with mean `p` is at most `∑ₖ C(n, k) p^k (1 - p)^(n - k) φ (k / n)` (`lintegral_ofReal_comp_sum_div_le`), which with the binomial bound gives `𝔼 exp (n binaryKL (mean) p) ≤ n + 1` (`lintegral_exp_mul_binaryKL_sum_div_le`). The proof also uses `integrable_of_lintegral_exp_ne_top` of Probability/ChangeOfMeasure, since `binaryKL` of the risks can be unbounded under `Q`, and Markov's inequality over the prior, `measure_ofReal_div_le_lintegral_le` of PACBayes/Basic, shared with McAllester's bound. Maurer's sharper `log (2√m / δ)` for `m ≥ 8` and the square-root form through a binary Pinsker inequality remain open.

Complete client: [PACBayes.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/PACBayes.lean) reuses the noisy distribution of `AgnosticPAC.lean` with the 0-1 losses of the two constant classifiers and a uniform prior. With `m = 1024` and `δ = 1 / 16` it derives from the Occam corollary that both constants have empirical risk at least `2 / 5`, and from the posterior bound at `Q = P` that the prior-averaged empirical risk is at least `2 / 5`; both true risks are `1 / 2`. It also checks empirical risks on a concrete sample, a posterior of infinite divergence, and why `m ≥ 2` is needed: at `m = 1` the margin vanishes and a one-example sample has empirical risk `0` against true risk `1 / 2`.

Complete client: [PACBayesKL.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/PACBayesKL.lean) applies the Occam form of the `kl` bound to the same example with `m = 1023` and `δ = 1 / 16`: with probability at least `15 / 16` neither constant has empirical risk `0`, since the margin `15 log 2 / 1023` is below `binaryKL 0 (1 / 2) = log 2`. It also checks why `0 < m` is needed, the junk value `binaryKL 1 0 = 1`, the failure of Jensen's inequality for junk values, and an instance of the binomial bound.

## Rademacher complexity

[Rademacher/Basic](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Rademacher/Basic.lean) follows Mohri, Rostamizadeh and Talwalkar, Definitions 3.1 and 3.2. `rademacherAverage a` is `𝔼_σ ⨆ j, 𝔼 i, signVal (σ i) * a j i` for a family of vectors `a : ι → Fin m → ℝ`, with uniform bits turned into signs by `signVal`. `empiricalRademacherComplexity F S` is the Rademacher average of the restrictions of the functions `f ∈ F` to the sample `S : Fin m → Z`. `rademacherComplexity D F m` averages it over `Probability.PMF.pi` of `m` independent draws from `D : PMF Z` on a finite type `Z`, which is the setting of the finite McDiarmid inequality. The supremum is the real `⨆`, so results assume a class bounded on the sample. For a sign vector `σ` whose correlations are unbounded above, the supremum inside the expectation takes the junk value `0`; no theorem reaches that case. The definitions are not exposed: rewrite with `rademacherAverage_def`, `empiricalRademacherComplexity_def` and `rademacherComplexity_def`. Suprema over a bounded class need `BddAbove` side conditions, supplied by `Fintype.bddAbove_range_expect` and `Cslib.BitString.bddAbove_range_expect_signVal_mul`. Massart's lemma is `Cslib.BitString.expect_ciSup_sum_mul_signVal_le` in [BitString/Massart](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/BitString/Massart.lean): for `N` vectors of Euclidean norm at most `r`, the expected maximal signed sum is at most `r √(2 log N)` (Massart 2000, Lemma 5.2; MRT Theorem 3.7). Its proof uses the sign moment-generating bound `expect_exp_mul_sum_mul_signVal_le` of [BitString/Rademacher](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/BitString/Rademacher.lean) and Jensen's inequality. `rademacherAverage_le` and `rademacherAverage_le_mul_sqrt` are the normalized forms. For a class bounded by `B`, `empiricalRademacherComplexity_le_of_finite` bounds the empirical complexity by `B √(2 log P / m)`, where `P` is the number of distinct restrictions to the sample. That is MRT Corollary 3.8 for bounded real classes; the source states it for `{-1, +1}`-valued classes. The finite-class forms are `empiricalRademacherComplexity_le_of_finset` and `rademacherComplexity_le_of_finset`. A single function has complexity `0` (`empiricalRademacherComplexity_singleton`, `rademacherComplexity_singleton`), and the complexity is monotone in the class (`empiricalRademacherComplexity_mono`). Two bounded families realizing the same vectors have the same average (`rademacherAverage_eq_of_forall_exists`). `rademacherAverage_add_mul_signVal_mul` is affine invariance: translating every vector by a fixed `c`, flipping fixed coordinates and scaling by `β ≥ 0` multiplies the average by `β`.

[Rademacher/Generalization](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Rademacher/Generalization.lean) proves MRT Theorem 3.3 for classes with values in `[a, b]`, where the source has `[0, 1]`. Bartlett and Mendelson, Theorem 8, is a variant for loss classes with a different normalization of the Rademacher complexity and different constants. Symmetrization, `sum_pi_mul_ciSup_sub_le_two_mul_rademacherComplexity`, bounds the expected worst-case gap `𝔼_S sup_f (E_D f - Ê_S f)` by `2 R_m(F)` using a ghost sample and coordinate swaps. McDiarmid's inequality then applies through the closure lemmas `HasBoundedDifferences.ciSup` and `HasBoundedDifferences.expect` in [BoundedDifferences](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/BoundedDifferences.lean). They show that the worst-case gap and the empirical complexity (`hasBoundedDifferences_empiricalRademacherComplexity`) change by at most `(b - a) / m` when one sample point changes. `rademacherComplexity_generalization_bound` (MRT (3.3)) states that with probability at least `1 - δ` every `f ∈ F` has `E_D f ≤ Ê_S f + 2 R_m(F) + (b - a) √(log (1/δ) / (2m))`. `empiricalRademacherComplexity_generalization_bound` (MRT (3.4)) is the data-dependent form, with `2 R̂_S(F) + 3 (b - a) √(log (2/δ) / (2m))`. Both are stated as outer-measure bounds `≤ δ` on the failure event, for `m > 0`, `a < b` and `δ > 0`. `rademacher_generalization_bound_of_finset` combines (3.3) with Massart for a finite `[0, 1]`-valued class. It illustrates the method; Hoeffding's inequality with a union bound gives the sharper `√(log (|F|/δ) / (2m))`.

[Rademacher/GrowthFunction](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Rademacher/GrowthFunction.lean) handles binary classes. `signClass C` is the set of `{-1, +1}`-valued functions `signVal ∘ c` for `c ∈ C`. Its empirical complexity on `m` points is at most `√(2 log Π_C(m) / m)` (`empiricalRademacherComplexity_signClass_le`, MRT Corollary 3.8). With Sauer's lemma from [GrowthFunction](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/GrowthFunction.lean) it is at most `√(2 d log (e m / d) / m)` for `d ≤ m` (`empiricalRademacherComplexity_signClass_le_vc`, with MRT Corollary 3.18), and at most `√(2 d log (m + 1) / m)` for all `m` (`empiricalRademacherComplexity_signClass_le_add_one`). Here `d` bounds every finite set shattered by `C`. The Rademacher-complexity forms are `rademacherComplexity_signClass_le` and `rademacherComplexity_signClass_le_vc`. [Rademacher/Chaining](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Rademacher/Chaining.lean) removes the logarithm. `empiricalRademacherComplexity_signClass_le_mul_sqrt` bounds the empirical complexity by `32 √(d / m)` for every `m`, and `rademacherComplexity_signClass_le_mul_sqrt` is the expected form. The proof applies Dudley's chaining bound `Cslib.BitString.expect_ciSup_sum_mul_signVal_le_chaining` from [BitString/Chaining](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/BitString/Chaining.lean) (SSBD Lemma 27.4). It uses Hamming nets of the realized labelings at scales `m / 4^(n+1)`, whose sizes the packing bound of [Packing](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Packing.lean) controls. The general form, `expect_ciSup_sum_mul_signVal_le_of_hammingDist`, accepts any vectors of norm at most `r` whose squared distances are at most `4 r² / n` times the Hamming distance of the realized labelings they come from, and bounds their expected maximal correlation with random signs by `32 r √d`. The constant is far from optimal, so the logarithmic bounds are smaller at realistic sample sizes; the chaining bound has the better rate.

[Rademacher/Classification](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Rademacher/Classification.lean) gives the binary-classification bounds of MRT Section 3.1 to 3.3 on a finite domain `α`, for a distribution `D : PMF (α × Bool)` on labeled examples. The source states them for an arbitrary input space. The true error of `h` is `(D.toOuterMeasure {p | h p.1 ≠ p.2}).toReal` and its empirical error is `empiricalMiscount h S / m`. The bridges to the general theorems are `sum_mul_bitVal_decide_ne` and `expect_bitVal_decide_ne`. `bitClass C` is the set of `{0, 1}`-valued functions `bitVal ℝ ∘ c`, so `bitClass (lossClass C)` is the class of 0-1 losses `(x, y) ↦ 𝟙[h x ≠ y]`; `lossClass` and `mem_lossClass` live in [VCUpperBound](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/VCUpperBound.lean). MRT Lemma 3.4 is `empiricalRademacherComplexity_bitClass_lossClass`: on a labeled sample the loss class has half the empirical complexity of `signClass C` on the unlabeled points. The proof writes `𝟙[a ≠ y] = 1/2 + (1/2) signVal (!y) signVal a` and applies affine invariance. Its expected form `rademacherComplexity_bitClass_lossClass` uses the marginal `D.map Prod.fst`. MRT Theorem 3.5 is `rademacherComplexity_classification_bound` for (3.17) and `empiricalRademacherComplexity_classification_bound` for (3.18). With probability at least `1 - δ`, every `h ∈ C` has `err_D(h) ≤ err_S(h) + R_m(signClass C) + √(log (1/δ) / (2m))`, or the data-dependent form with `R̂_{S_X}(signClass C) + 3 √(log (2/δ) / (2m))`. `growthFunction_classification_bound` is MRT Corollary 3.9, with `√(2 log Π_C(m) / m)`. `vc_classification_bound` is MRT Corollary 3.19, with `√(2 d log (e m / d) / m)`, for `m ≥ d`, which the source assumes implicitly through Corollary 3.18. For empirical risk minimization, `IsERM.rademacherComplexity_agnostic_bound` combines (3.17) at confidence `δ / 2` with Hoeffding's upper tail for a best concept at confidence `δ / 2`. The tail comes from the generalization bound for a singleton class, whose complexity is `0`. With probability at least `1 - δ`, an ERM output satisfies `err_D(A S) ≤ err_D(c) + R_m(signClass C) + √(2 log (2/δ) / m)` for every `c ∈ C`. `IsERM.vc_agnostic_bound` is the VC form, with excess error `√(2 d log (e m / d) / m) + √(2 log (2/δ) / m)`. `IsERM.vc_agnostic_bound_mul_sqrt`, in Rademacher/Chaining, has excess error `32 √(d / m) + √(2 log (2/δ) / m)`. That is the rate `O(√((d + log (1/δ)) / m))` of SSBD Theorem 6.8, here on a finite domain. These are derived results whose constants differ from SSBD Chapter 26. On countable domains, [AgnosticVC](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/PACLearning/AgnosticVC.lean) gives the corresponding `IsPACLearnerFor` packaging through growth-function uniform convergence.

[Rademacher/Contraction](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Rademacher/Contraction.lean) proves Talagrand's contraction lemma in the uncentered form of [Mohri, Rostamizadeh and Talwalkar][mohrirostamizadehtalwalkar18], Section 5.4, Lemma 5.7 (cf. [Ledoux and Talagrand][ledouxtalagrand91], Theorem 4.12). For a uniformly bounded family `a : ι → Fin m → ℝ` and per-coordinate `K`-Lipschitz maps `φ i : ℝ → ℝ` (`K : ℝ≥0`), `rademacherAverage_comp_le` gives `rademacherAverage (fun j i => φ i (a j i)) ≤ K * rademacherAverage a` without requiring `φ i 0 = 0`, by coordinate-by-coordinate sign-flip symmetrization. The class forms bound empirical and population Rademacher complexities both for `(φ ∘ ·) '' F` (`empiricalRademacherComplexity_image_comp_le`, `rademacherComplexity_image_comp_le`) and for example-dependent Lipschitz losses `(fun f z => φ z (f (g z))) '' F` against `g ∘ S` and `D.map g` (`empiricalRademacherComplexity_image_comp_le_of_map`, `rademacherComplexity_image_comp_le_of_map`), as well as per-index compositions (`rademacherAverage_comp_le_empiricalRademacherComplexity`).

[Rademacher/Margin](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Rademacher/Margin.lean) formalizes the fixed-`ρ` margin bounds of [Mohri, Rostamizadeh and Talwalkar][mohrirostamizadehtalwalkar18], Section 5.4 (Definitions 5.5–5.6 and Theorem 5.8). `marginLoss ρ u = min 1 (max 0 (1 - u / ρ))` takes values in `[0, 1]` (`marginLoss_mem_Icc`), sandwiches `𝟙[u ≤ 0] ≤ marginLoss ρ u ≤ 𝟙[u ≤ ρ]` for `ρ > 0` (`boole_le_marginLoss`, `marginLoss_le_boole`), and is `(1 / ρ)`-Lipschitz (`lipschitzWith_marginLoss`, `lipschitzWith_marginLoss_nnreal`). For a distribution `D : PMF (α × Bool)` on a finite domain `α` (reading labels via `signVal y ∈ {-1, +1}`) and a uniformly bounded real hypothesis class `H : Set (α → ℝ)`, `empiricalMarginLoss` and `marginRisk` define the empirical and population `ρ`-margin losses, `marginLossClass ρ H` contracts by `1 / ρ`, and `rademacherComplexity_margin_bound` (MRT (5.39)) and `empiricalRademacherComplexity_margin_bound` (MRT (5.40)) bound the event that some `h ∈ H` exceeds `empiricalMarginLoss ρ h S + (2 / ρ) R_m(H) + √(log (1/δ) / (2m))` (resp. `(2 / ρ) R̂_{S_X}(H) + 3 √(log (2/δ) / (2m))`) in true error `(D.toOuterMeasure {p | signVal p.2 * h p.1 ≤ 0}).toReal` by `δ`. `margin_bound_of_finset` specializes (5.39) to finite classes via Massart's lemma. Margin bounds uniform in `ρ` (MRT Theorem 5.9), local Rademacher complexity and measurable (non-finite) domains remain open ([#3297](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3297)).

[Rademacher/Linear](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Rademacher/Linear.lean) bounds the complexity of bounded-norm linear predictors. For a feature map `φ : Z → E` into a real inner product space, `linearPredictors φ B` is the class `z ↦ ⟪w, φ z⟫` with `‖w‖ ≤ B`; `φ = id` gives the class `{x ↦ ⟪w, x⟫ : ‖w‖ ≤ B}` of the sources. On a sample `S : Fin m → Z` its empirical complexity is at most `B √(∑ i, ‖φ (S i)‖²) / m` (`empiricalRademacherComplexity_linearPredictors_le_sqrt`), hence at most `B R / √m` when every sample point has `‖φ (S i)‖ ≤ R` (`empiricalRademacherComplexity_linearPredictors_le`; [Mohri, Rostamizadeh and Talwalkar][mohrirostamizadehtalwalkar18], Theorem 5.10; Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Lemma 26.10). The bound does not depend on the dimension of `E`. The proof applies Cauchy–Schwarz for each sign vector, then Jensen's inequality, then `Cslib.BitString.expect_norm_sum_signVal_smul_sq` in [BitString/Rademacher](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/BitString/Rademacher.lean): `𝔼 ‖∑ i, σ_i v_i‖² = ∑ i, ‖v_i‖²` for vectors in any real inner product space. `rademacherComplexity_linearPredictors_le` is the population form on a finite `Z`. `linearPredictors_margin_bound` is MRT Corollary 5.11, (5.44): with probability at least `1 - δ`, every `‖w‖ ≤ B` has true error `(D.toOuterMeasure {p | signVal p.2 * ⟪w, φ p.1⟫ ≤ 0}).toReal` at most `empiricalMarginLoss ρ (fun z => ⟪w, φ z⟫) S + 2 B R / (ρ √m) + √(log (1/δ) / (2m))`, for features of norm at most `R` on a finite domain. `linearPredictors_empirical_margin_bound` is the data-dependent form, with `2 B √(∑ i, ‖φ (S i).1‖²) / (ρ m) + 3 √(log (2/δ) / (2m))` and no radius hypothesis. The sources allow any distribution on the ball of radius `R`; kernel classes remain open in [#4919](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4919). [LinearPredictors.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/LinearPredictors.lean) evaluates the trace bound `5/2` on the sample `(3 e₀, 4 e₁)` in `ℝ²`, below the uniform bound `4 / √2`, and instantiates the margin bound for unit features with `m = 10000`, `ρ = 1/2` and `δ = 1/20`, where the complexity term is `1/25`.

[Rademacher/LinearL1](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Rademacher/LinearL1.lean) treats the ℓ₁ ball. For a feature map `φ : Z → Fin n → ℝ`, `l1LinearPredictors φ B` is the class of predictors `z ↦ ∑ j, w j * φ z j` with `∑ j, |w j| ≤ B`. On a sample whose feature coordinates are at most `R` in absolute value, the empirical complexity is at most `B R √(2 log (2 n) / m)` (`empiricalRademacherComplexity_l1LinearPredictors_le`; Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Lemma 26.11), and `rademacherComplexity_l1LinearPredictors_le` is the population form on a finite `Z`. For each sign vector, Hölder's inequality reduces the supremum over the ball to the `2 n` vertices `± B eⱼ`, and `rademacherAverage_le_mul_sqrt` (Massart) bounds those. Compared with the ℓ₂ bound, the constraint on the weights is stronger and the assumption on the features weaker (ℓ∞ instead of ℓ₂), at the cost of a `√(log n)` factor. [LinearL1Predictors.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/LinearL1Predictors.lean) checks a concrete member of the class, bounds the complexity on any `100` points of `[-1, 1]²` below `1/5`, and gives a population bound below `1/25` for `±1` features with `B = 2` and `m = 10000`.

Complete clients: [RademacherComplexity.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/RademacherComplexity.lean) computes complexity `0` for a single function and an empty sample. On a one-point sample, the two constant functions `±1` have empirical complexity exactly `1`, which Massart bounds by `√(2 log 2)`. It instantiates the finite-class bound with `m = 100` and `δ = 1/20` at a gap of `61/100`, the data-dependent bound (3.4) with `m = 10000` and `δ = 1/20` at a gap of `1/5`, and the VC bounds with `d = 2` on `Bool`, including `R̂_S ≤ 1/2` on `100` points. For classification, the 0-1 losses of the two constant classifiers on one labeled point have complexity exactly `1/2`, a single classifier gives Hoeffding's tail, and with `m = 65536` and `δ = 1/20` the VC bound gives a gap of `0.06` and ERM over all classifiers on `Bool` is within `0.08` of a Bayes-optimal one. [RademacherMargin.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/RademacherMargin.lean) checks an exact equality case (`u ↦ 5 - 3u` on `{±1}`, attaining `3`) and a strict collapse case (`abs`) of Talagrand contraction, boundary and interior values of `marginLoss (1/2)` and `empiricalMarginLoss`, and nonvacuous `m = 10000`, `ρ = 1/2`, `δ = 1/20` instances of both `margin_bound_of_finset` (gap `0.12`) and `empiricalRademacherComplexity_margin_bound` (gap `0.23`). [RademacherChaining.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/RademacherChaining.lean) bounds the empirical complexity of thresholds on `ℕ` by `32 / √m` for every `m`, which is `1/32` on `2^20` points. With `2^20` examples and `δ = 1/20`, it shows that ERM over all classifiers on `Bool` is within `0.05` of the best classifier.

## Learning with general losses

Classification measures hypotheses by the 0-1 error; convex learning problems measure them by a real loss. [LossMinimization/Basic](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/LossMinimization/Basic.lean) follows Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Section 3.2.2: hypotheses form an arbitrary type `W`, examples an arbitrary type `Z`, and a loss is any `ℓ : W → Z → ℝ`. `trueRisk ℓ D w` is the expected loss `∫ ℓ w z ∂D` under a distribution `D` on examples (equation (3.3)), and `empiricalRisk ℓ S w` is the average loss `𝔼 i, ℓ w (S i)` on a sample `S : Fin m → Z` (equation (3.4)). The source asks for nonnegative losses; these definitions do not. The true risk is a Bochner integral, so it is `0` for a non-integrable loss, and the results that integrate assume integrability. `empiricalRisk_update` computes the effect of replacing one example. `integral_empiricalRisk_comp` writes the expected empirical risk of a sample-dependent hypothesis `A S` as the average over `i` of the expected loss of `A S` on `S i`. `integral_empiricalRisk` shows that for a fixed hypothesis the empirical risk on `m > 0` i.i.d. examples is an unbiased estimate of the true risk. `convexOn_empiricalRisk` and `convexOn_trueRisk` show that losses convex in the hypothesis have convex risks; the true risk needs an integrable loss at each point of the convex set. `continuousOn_empiricalRisk` and `integrable_empiricalRisk_comp` pass continuity in the hypothesis and integrability over samples from the losses to the empirical risk, and `measurable_empiricalRisk` passes measurability. For a loss with values in `[0, 1]`, `trueRisk_mem_Icc` and `empiricalRisk_mem_Icc` place both risks in `[0, 1]`. [Checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/LossMinimization.lean) compute the absolute loss on a three-point sample before and after replacing an example, check that its empirical risk is convex and continuous, and compute the true risk `(w² + (w - 1)²) / 2` of a squared loss under the uniform distribution on `Bool`, together with its unbiased estimate on five examples.

## Stability and regularized loss minimization

[LossMinimization/Stability](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/LossMinimization/Stability.lean) follows Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Section 13.2. A learning rule on `m` examples is a function `A : (Fin m → Z) → W`. `replaceOneGap ℓ D A` is the expected increase `𝔼_{(S, z'), i} [ℓ(A(S⁽ⁱ⁾), z_i) - ℓ(A S, z_i)]` of the loss on a training example when that example is replaced by an independent one. The sample and the fresh example are drawn from `(Measure.pi fun _ => D).prod D`, and `S⁽ⁱ⁾` is `Function.update S i z'`. `integral_trueRisk_sub_empiricalRisk` is Theorem 13.2: for `m > 0` the expected generalization gap `𝔼_S [L_D(A S) - L_S(A S)]` equals the replace-one gap. Its integrability hypotheses hold for bounded losses that are jointly measurable with a measurable rule, and on finite example spaces with measurable singletons. The proof shows that replacing one example preserves the joint distribution, as a special case of the double-sample swap `PACLearning.measurePreserving_swapSamples`; `integral_update_eval` states this preservation for any integrand. `integral_trueRisk_le_of_replaceOneGap_le` bounds the expected generalization gap under one distribution by a bound on the replace-one gap under that distribution. `IsReplaceOneStable ℓ A ε` (Definition 13.3, without the source's unused monotonicity in `m`) bounds the replace-one gap by `ε` under every distribution. `IsReplaceOneStable.integral_trueRisk_le` turns stability into `𝔼_S [L_D(A S)] ≤ 𝔼_S [L_S(A S)] + ε`. `isReplaceOneStable_of_forall_le` derives stability from a pointwise (uniform) bound and needs no measurability.

[LossMinimization/RLM](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/LossMinimization/RLM.lean) defines `IsRLM ℓ R K A`: on every sample, `A` returns a minimizer over `K` of `L_S(w) + R(w)`. `exists_isRLM` constructs such a rule on compact sets. `exists_isRLM_tikhonov` constructs one for `R(w) = r‖w‖²` on closed subsets of proper spaces, including the unconstrained Soft-SVM. For a `σ`-strongly convex regularizer and convex losses that are `ρ`-Lipschitz on `K` (Mathlib's `LipschitzOnWith`), `IsRLM.norm_sub_le` bounds the movement of the minimizer by `2ρ / (σ m)`, and `IsRLM.sub_le` and `IsRLM.isReplaceOneStable` give uniform and replace-one stability `2ρ² / (σ m)`. The common step is `IsRLM.mul_norm_sub_sq_le`, which adds the quadratic growth of both regularized objectives: `σ m ‖v - u‖²` is at most the sum of the loss changes on the two differing examples. With `strongConvexOn_mul_norm_sq`, which says that `r‖w‖²` is `2r`-strongly convex, `IsRLM.isReplaceOneStable_tikhonov` gives the rate `ρ² / (r m)`. This is half of Corollary 13.6's `2ρ² / (r m)`, because the proof uses quadratic growth at both minimizers. `IsRLM.integral_trueRisk_le` is the oracle inequality for any stable rule with a nonnegative regularizer; its fitting term `IsRLM.integral_empiricalRisk_le` is equation (13.16), `𝔼_S [L_S(A S)] ≤ L_D(w) + R(w)`. Its Tikhonov form `IsRLM.integral_trueRisk_le_tikhonov` (Corollary 13.8) and `IsRLM.integral_trueRisk_le_add_sqrt` (Corollary 13.9, excess `ρ B √(8 / m)` for `r = √(2ρ² / (B² m))`) keep the source's constants, with the comparator ranging over `K`. [LossMinimization/Hinge](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/LossMinimization/Hinge.lean) proves that the hinge loss `max 0 (1 - y ⟪w, x⟫)` is convex and `|y| ‖x‖`-Lipschitz (Claim 15.6), and gives the Soft-SVM oracle inequality (Corollary 15.7) for feature maps of norm at most `ρ` and labels in `[-1, 1]`. [Checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/RegularizedLossMinimization.lean) compute a replace-one gap of `1 / 2` for a rule that memorizes its only example, so it is not stable at any smaller rate. They bound the expected hinge risk of every Soft-SVM rule on a two-point problem with `m = 100` by `3 / 10`, construct such a rule, and evaluate Corollary 13.9 at `m = 800` on the unit ball (excess `1 / 10`). 

[LossMinimization/Smooth](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/LossMinimization/Smooth.lean) follows Section 13.3.2 for losses that are convex, nonnegative and `β`-smooth. Smoothness is a pair of hypotheses rather than a definition: every loss is `Differentiable` on the whole space and its `fderiv` is `LipschitzWith β`. [LipschitzDeriv](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Analysis/Calculus/LipschitzDeriv.lean) proves the two facts the argument uses: the quadratic upper bound `f y ≤ f x + f' x (y - x) + β/2 ‖y - x‖²` (`image_le_of_lipschitzWith_hasFDerivAt`, equation (12.5)) and self-boundedness `‖f' x‖² ≤ 2β f x` for nonnegative `f` (`norm_sq_le_of_lipschitzWith_hasFDerivAt`, equation (12.6)). Self-boundedness evaluates `f` away from `x`, so smoothness is assumed on all of `E`, not only on `K`. For a `σ`-strongly convex regularizer and `4β ≤ σ m`, `IsRLM.sub_le_of_lipschitzWith_fderiv` bounds each replace-one change by `5β / (σ m) (ℓ(A S, z_i) + ℓ(A S⁽ⁱ⁾, z'))`, and `IsRLM.replaceOneGap_le_of_lipschitzWith_fderiv` takes expectations. The rate is relative to the fit `𝔼_S [L_S(A S)]` rather than uniform, so these results do not produce `IsReplaceOneStable`. For Tikhonov regularization with `2β ≤ r m`, `IsRLM.replaceOneGap_le_tikhonov_of_lipschitzWith_fderiv` proves Corollary 13.7 with the factor `5β / (r m)` in place of the source's `48β / (r m)`. `IsRLM.integral_trueRisk_le_tikhonov_of_lipschitzWith_fderiv` is Corollary 13.10 and `IsRLM.integral_trueRisk_le_add_of_lipschitzWith_fderiv` is Corollary 13.11. The smaller factor comes from using both growth terms of `IsRLM.mul_norm_sub_sq_le`. It matters for Corollary 13.11: with its parameters `m ≥ 150 β B² / ε²` and `r = ε / (3 B²)`, the factor `48` leaves an excess of about `ε / 3 + 0.96 ε`, while `5` gives `ε / 3 + ε / 10`. [LossMinimization/Squared](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/LossMinimization/Squared.lean) defines the squared loss `(⟪w, x⟫ - y)² / 2` of equation (13.3). It proves the loss convex, with derivative `(⟪w, x⟫ - y) ⟪x, ·⟫`, which is `‖x‖²`-Lipschitz (Example 12.6). `IsRLM.integral_trueRisk_squaredLoss_le_add` is Theorem 13.1 for ridge regression with features of norm at most `1` and labels in `[-1, 1]`. [Checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/SmoothLossMinimization.lean) derive the source's factor `48` in Corollaries 13.7 and 13.10 and construct ridge regression rules. They bound the expected squared risk of every ridge rule on a two-point problem with `m = 100` and `r = 1 / 10` by `3 / 20`, and evaluate Theorem 13.1 at `ε = 1 / 10` on `15000` examples. High-probability bounds from uniform stability (Bousquet and Elisseeff, Theorem 12), measurability of the minimizer for general example spaces and the stability of SGD remain open ([#4917](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4917)).

## Membership-query learners

For the large-Fourier-coefficient algorithm, the [2007 draft Chapter 19 audit](../../results/arora-barak-2007.md#chapter-19-more-pcp-theorems-and-the-fourier-transform-technique) separates the bucket-search and query guarantees from the uncertified polynomial machine runtime of Theorem 19.18.

[QueryLearner](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/MembershipQuery.lean#L76) takes a random seed to a decision tree. A queried coordinate is itself an input point for the unknown target function, and a leaf is a hypothesis function. `QueriesAtMost` bounds tree depth for every seed. This represents adaptive membership queries directly, while nonadaptive point lists are a specialization.

A learner can also be measured by its own circuit size. [`QueryLearner.HasCircuit A q S`](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/NonadaptiveCircuit.lean) says that on every seed the learner queries `q` points fixed by the seed, and a De Morgan circuit with at most `S` gates computes its hypothesis at `x` from the `q` answers followed by `x`. This is the learner-level form of learning by circuits with non-adaptive queries in Chen, Hirahara, Oliveira, Pich, Rajgopal and Santhanam (Definition 3, p. 12). [chenhiraharaoliveirapichrajgopalsanthanam2020][chenhiraharaoliveirapichrajgopalsanthanam2020] It differs from the source in four ways. The circuit is chosen per seed, while the source has one randomized circuit; Lemma 34 of the same paper fixes the seed first, so it needs only this form. The circuit evaluates the hypothesis instead of printing a description of it. Size is circuit complexity in the De Morgan basis. The predicate constrains the hypothesis as a function of the target, not the decision tree, so it does not replace `QueriesAtMost`. `HasCircuit.complexity_eval_le` bounds every hypothesis by `S + 2` gates, `mono` and `pad` raise `S` and `q`, and `hasCircuit_ofNonadaptive` covers learners built by `ofNonadaptive`. [`HasCircuit.exists_oracleCircuit`](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/NonadaptiveCircuit/Oracle.lean) gives, on every seed, an oracle circuit with at most `S + q + 2` gates, `q` of them oracle gates with at most one on any path, which computes the hypothesis under any oracle whose slice at length `n` is the target. It is built by `Boolean.fixedQueryCircuit` ([oracle circuits](circuits-and-formulas.md#boolean-bases-and-related-models)). The [checked client](../../CslibFrontierTests/NonadaptiveCircuit.lean) applies each lemma to a learner with one query and no gates. [#4634](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4634) tracks the proof that the CIKK learner has such a circuit of size `2^{O(ℓ+g+e)}`.

Adaptive learners have the same measure. [`QueryLearner.ofAdaptive Q H`](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/AdaptiveCircuit.lean) asks, on the seed `ω`, the query `Q ω i` computed from the answers to the first `i` queries, and outputs `H ω` of all `q` answers. It is a decision tree of depth `q` (`queriesAtMost_ofAdaptive`), and run on `f` it outputs `H` of the answers `answersUpTo` of `f` (`eval_ofAdaptive`). `QueryLearner.HasAdaptiveCircuit A q S` says that on every seed each query is computed from the earlier answers by a De Morgan circuit, and the hypothesis at `x` from all answers followed by `x`, with at most `S` gates in all; the queries do not depend on `x`, since the learner outputs a hypothesis. Gates shared between the circuits are counted once for each. `HasAdaptiveCircuit.exists_queriesAtMost` replaces such a learner by one making at most `q` queries with the same hypotheses, and `HasCircuit.hasAdaptiveCircuit` turns `HasCircuit A q S` into `HasAdaptiveCircuit A q (S + 2q)`, each fixed query being a constant of two gates. `complexity_append_answers_le` reads the answers from a truth table of `2^n` entries with the gates of the queries plus `2^n (2n + 3) + 1` gates per query, which select the entry at the computed address (`complexity_select_le` in [Lookup](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/Lookup.lean)).

Uniform random examples can be simulated by querying independently sampled points, with exact distributional correctness. The [Kushilevitz–Mansour development](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/KushilevitzMansour.lean) reuses a nonadaptive sample set across stages, with the explicit query bound `m(2n+3)` and a sample schedule depending on spectral norm, error and confidence. Bounded-leaf decision trees supply a concrete learnable class through a spectral-norm bound. These statements charge oracle queries only; they do not bound the time or finite representation needed for all Fourier operations and output hypotheses.

The [Nisan–Wigderson learner](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/NisanWigderson.lean) runs the reconstruction of the Nisan–Wigderson generator as a non-adaptive membership-query learner, following Carmosino, Impagliazzo, Kabanets and Kolokolova. [carmosinoimpagliazzokabanetskolokolova2016][carmosinoimpagliazzokabanetskolokolova2016] `nwLearner D test` takes a design `D` and a test on `m`-bit strings; its seeds are the trials of the [sampled reconstruction](../../CslibFrontier/Crypto/Primitives/PRG/NisanWigderson/Sampling.lean). On a trial it queries the at most `m 2^a` points of [`trialPoints`](../../CslibFrontier/Crypto/Primitives/PRG/NisanWigderson/Queries.lean) (`nwLearner_queriesAtMost`) and outputs the trial's guess (`eval_nwLearner`), which `trialGuess_congr` shows reads the target only there. Every hypothesis has De Morgan circuit size at most that of the test plus `m (2^a (2a + 3) + 1) + 1` (`complexity_eval_nwLearner_le`). The same bound holds for one circuit per trial that reads the answers to the queries from input wires instead of fixing them (`complexity_trialGuess_answers_le`); each earlier output bit selects its answer by at most `a` input bits (`complexity_select_le` in [Lookup.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/Lookup.lean)). `nwLearner_learns` turns a distinguisher with advantage `ε` against the generator of every target in `C` into learning with error `1/2 - ε/(2m)` and success probability `ε/(2m)`, and `nwLearner_learns_of_dens` takes a test accepting a `δ` fraction of all strings but no generator output, the natural-property form, through its negation. `QueryLearner.learns_uniformOfFintype_of_le_dens` is the bridge: with uniform seeds from a finite type, a density lower bound on the good seeds gives `Learns`, and `uniformError_eq_one_sub_dens` writes the error as one minus the agreement density. This is one run of their Theorem 2.11 with `δ` in place of `1/5` and advantage `δ/(2m)` in place of `1/L`. [Selecting the best of `k` runs](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/NisanWigderson/BestOf.lean) gives Theorem 2.11 in a library form: `bestOf_nwLearner_learns` turns a distinguisher with advantage `ε > 0` against the generator of every target in `C` into learning `C` with error `1/2 - ε/(4m)` and confidence `1 - η` when `ε/(2m) ≤ 1`, `log (2/η) ≤ (ε/(2m)) k` and `8 log (4k/η) ≤ t (ε/(2m))²`, and `bestOf_nwLearner_learns_of_dens` is its natural-property case with `δ` in place of `ε`. The learner makes at most `k m 2^a + t` queries (`bestOf_nwLearner_queriesAtMost`) and keeps the single-run circuit bound (`complexity_eval_bestOf_nwLearner_le`); the source takes advantage above `1/5`, has error `1/2 - 1/L` and uses `poly(L)` runs. Complete client: [NisanWigdersonBestOf.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/NisanWigdersonBestOf.lean) uses `8` runs and `1000` sample points for error `1/2 - 3/32` with confidence `1/2`. Only queries and hypothesis size are certified, not the running time.

[Best-of-`k` selection](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/BestOf.lean) turns a learner that succeeds with small probability into one with confidence `1 - η`. `bestOf A k t` runs `A` on `k` independent seeds, queries the target at `t` fresh uniform points and outputs a hypothesis with the most agreements there; `bestOfSeed μ k t` is its seed distribution. It makes at most `k * q + t` queries when every run makes at most `q` (`queriesAtMost_bestOf`), and it outputs the hypothesis of one of the runs (`exists_eval_bestOf_eq`), so any complexity bound on the runs' hypotheses carries over (`le_sup_eval_bestOf`). `learns_bestOf` turns error `ε` with confidence `1 - δ` into error `ε + 2θ` with confidence `1 - δ ^ k - 2k exp (-2tθ²)`, by Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound over the runs. `learns_bestOf_of_success` solves for the parameters: error `ε` with probability `p` becomes error `ε + 2θ` with confidence `1 - η` when `log (2/η) ≤ p k` and `log (4k/η) ≤ 2tθ²`. `learns_bestOf_of_weak`, its case `θ = γ/4`, is the weak-learner form used by Carmosino, Impagliazzo, Kabanets and Kolokolova (CCC 2016, Theorem 2.11): agreement `1/2 + γ` with probability `p` becomes agreement `1/2 + γ/2` with probability `1 - η` when `log (2/η) ≤ p k` and `8 log (4k/η) ≤ t γ²`. Only queries are certified, not running time. The Nisan–Wigderson instance of Theorem 2.11 is above. Complete client: [QueryLearnerBestOf.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/QueryLearnerBestOf.lean) boosts a learner that outputs the target's negation half of the time, with `6` runs and `1504` sample points, to error `1/4` with confidence `1/2`.

[The parameters of the CIKK learner](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/CIKK/Parameters.lean) fix, as explicit numerals in exponents `e`, `g` and `h` (error `2^{-e}`, advantage `2^{-g}`, confidence `1 - 2^{-h}`), the direct-product length `cikkK e g`, a power of two, the Goldreich–Levin mask count `cikkMasks`, the decoder's fixed-set size `cikkFixed` and trial count `cikkTrials`, and the runs `cikkRuns` and sample points `cikkSamples` of best-of selection. Their lemmas discharge, in the forms the stages take and after `log x ≤ N` for `0 < x ≤ 2^N` (`Real.log_le_of_le_two_pow`), the Goldreich–Levin list condition and the direct-product conditions of Carmosino, Impagliazzo, Kabanets and Kolokolova's Theorem 5.1, and the conditions of one outer best-of selection. That outer selection is the library's: the source composes the Nisan–Wigderson and amplification reconstructions, each boosting its own confidence (proof of Theorem 3.2, p. 10:11), and one final boost replaces them at the cost of constants. Taking `k` a power of two solves the fixed point `2s ≤ k` (`two_mul_cikkFixed_le_cikkK`) in place of the source's `k = ⌈3c/ε · ln(1/γ)⌉ + 1` (proof of Theorem 4.3, step 1, p. 10:15), and `k ≤ 2^{e+10} (6g + 3e + 36)` (`cikkK_le`). The closed forms `cikkLogK_le`, `cikkK_le_two_pow`, `cikkMasks_le`, `cikkFixed_le`, `cikkTrials_le`, `cikkRuns_le` and `cikkSamples_le` bound every parameter by a power of two with exponent linear in `e`, `g` and `h`, so bounds on the learner need not unfold the definitions. Complete client: [CIKKParameters.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/CIKKParameters.lean) boosts a learner with the amplification stage's success probability through `learns_bestOf_of_success`.

[Black-box reductions](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/Reduction.lean) learn a class `C` from a learner `A` for a transformed class `amp '' C`. `reduce A s post` runs `A`, answers each query `amp f y` with a decision tree `s y` that queries `f` (`DecisionTree.substitute`), and post-processes the hypothesis of `A` with an extra seed. Run on `f`, it outputs the post-processed hypothesis of `A` run on `amp f` (`eval_reduce`). It makes at most `q * d` queries when `A` makes at most `q` and every `s y` has depth at most `d` (`queriesAtMost_reduce`). `learns_reduce` multiplies success probabilities: suppose `A` succeeds with probability `p` and, for every `f ∈ C`, post-processing turns every hypothesis with error `ε'` against `amp f` into one with error `ε` against `f` for a `ρ` fraction of the extra seeds. Then the reduction, run on `reduceSeed μ T`, succeeds with probability `p * ρ`. This is the reconstruction property of black-box amplification (Carmosino, Impagliazzo, Kabanets and Kolokolova, CCC 2016, Definition 3.4), with the oracle given by the trees `s y`. Only queries are certified, not running time. Complete client: [AmpReconstruction.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/AmpReconstruction.lean) runs the trivial reduction, which asks each query directly.

The [reconstruction for `Amp(f) = (f^k)^GL`](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/CIKK/Amplification.lean) is a black-box reduction (Carmosino, Impagliazzo, Kabanets and Kolokolova, Theorem 4.3). `ampLearner A s m K` answers each query of `A` to `glAmp f k` with the `k` queries of `glAmpQuery`, so it makes at most `q * k` queries (`queriesAtMost_ampLearner`). It decodes the hypothesis of `A` first with the Goldreich–Levin candidate `glAmpCandidate` for `K` masks and a parity guess, then with the direct-product decoder `dpDecode`; its seed distribution is `ampSeed μ k s m K`. `learns_ampLearner` is the reconstruction in a library form (registry ID `cikk-amp-reconstruction`). If `A` learns `{glAmp f k | f ∈ C}` with error `1/2 - γ` and success probability `p`, then `ampLearner A s m K` learns `C` with error `ε` and success probability `p δ₀²/8`, where `δ₀ = γ/2^(K+3)`. The factor `δ₀²/8` is a `δ₀` fraction of good Goldreich–Levin seeds times a `δ₀/8` fraction of good direct-product seeds for each of them, combined by the two-stage sampling bound [`mul_le_dens_filter_prod`](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Probability/TwoStage.lean). It needs four conditions: `k ≤ 2(γ/2)²(2^K - 1)`, `2s ≤ k`, `s ≥ (64/ε) log(512/(ε δ₀²))` and `m ≥ (16/δ₀) log(8/ε)`. The module docstring labels where this departs from the source: the Goldreich–Levin list has `2^K = O(k/γ²)` entries instead of `O(1/γ²)`, each seed gives a deterministic decoder, the success probability and the constants are ours, and the parameter conditions are hypotheses rather than a choice of `k`. If the hypothesis of `A` has a De Morgan circuit with `c` gates, the reconstructed hypothesis has one with at most `m (s + 1) ((2^K - 1)(c + 1) + 8 + 2(2^K - 1)(2^K + 1)) + 3m + 1` (`complexity_eval_ampLearner_le` in [CIKK/Complexity.lean](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/CIKK/Complexity.lean)); running time is not certified. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/CIKKAmplification.lean) evaluate `glAmpQuery` and state the reconstruction for a single function.

[The CIKK learner](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/CIKK/Learner.lean) composes the three stages at these parameters, for `p = 2` (Carmosino, Impagliazzo, Kabanets and Kolokolova, Theorem 5.1, in a per-function black-box form). `cikkLearner n ℓ e g h test` is best-of selection over the reconstruction for `Amp(f) = glAmp f (cikkK e g)` of the Nisan–Wigderson learner built from the negated test, for the design `Design.binaryPolynomial (k n + k) ℓ`; its seed distribution is `cikkSeed n ℓ e g h`. `learns_cikkLearner` is the learning theorem (registry ID `cikk-learning-from-dense-test`). Let the test, on strings of `2^ℓ` bits, accept at least `2^{2^ℓ} 2^{ℓ+1-g}` of them and no output of the generator of `glAmp f k` for any `f ∈ C`. Then the learner learns `C` with error `2^{-e}` and confidence `1 - 2^{-h}`. It makes at most `(h + 1) 2^{2ℓ + 10g + 6e + 59}` queries (`queriesAtMost_cikkLearner_two_pow`; exact count `queriesAtMost_cikkLearner`). The single outer best-of selection replaces the source's per-stage boosts, at the cost of constants. The test may have any density, not only the largeness `1/5` of a natural property, and the deviations of `learns_ampLearner` carry over; the module docstring labels them. Running time is not certified. Complete client: [CIKKLearner.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/CIKKLearner.lean) specializes the theorem to one target and evaluates the query bound at small parameters. For AC⁰[2], [CIKK/AC0Mod.lean](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/CIKK/AC0Mod.lean) instantiates the test with the rank property (`learns_cikkLearner_rankTest`, registry ID `cikk-learning-ac0-2`), giving quasi-polynomially many queries and hypotheses of quasi-polynomial De Morgan circuit size for fixed target depth `d` (`complexity_eval_cikkLearner_rankTest_le_two_pow`; only size is certified, not depth); see [natural proofs](metacomplexity-and-methods.md#natural-proofs-and-cryptographic-assumptions). The step from a dense test that rejects small circuits to the learner's hypothesis is stated at one input length for constant-depth bases containing AC⁰[2] ([`learns_cikkLearner_of_hom`](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/CIKK/DenseTest.lean)) and for De Morgan circuit complexity ([`learns_cikkLearner_of_lt_complexity`](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/CIKK/PPoly.lean)); [CIKKDenseTest.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/CIKKDenseTest.lean) applies the De Morgan form to the functions of high circuit complexity.

[The size of the CIKK learner's hypotheses](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/CIKK/Complexity.lean) is bounded relative to the test (registry ID `cikk-learner-hypothesis-size`). On every target and seed, `complexity_eval_cikkLearner_le` bounds the hypothesis's De Morgan circuit size by `T (F + 1) ((2^K - 1)(c + N + 3) + 8 + 2(2^K - 1)(2^K + 1)) + 3T + 1`, where `c` is the test's circuit size, `N = 2^ℓ (2^{ℓ-1} (2(ℓ-1) + 3) + 1)` is the Nisan–Wigderson overhead, and `T`, `F` and `K` are `cikkTrials`, `cikkFixed` and `cikkMasks`. The closed form `complexity_eval_cikkLearner_le_two_pow` is `2^{11g + 9e + 3ℓ + 80} (c + 1)`, with this library's constants. The proof follows the output of `bestOf` to one run (`le_sup_eval_bestOf`), unfolds the learner with `cikkLearner_def`, and composes the bounds of the Nisan–Wigderson hypothesis, the Goldreich–Levin candidate and the direct-product decoder. The bound is affine in `c`, with `T (F + 1)(2^K - 1)` copies of the test. With unit-cost test gates it is the size that the source's oracle circuit would have, but that oracle circuit is not built. Only size is certified, not depth or running time. Complete client: [CIKKComplexity.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/CIKKComplexity.lean) applies the closed form to a test of bounded size and composes one run's bounds.

[Learners distinguish pseudorandom functions](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/CryptoHardness.lean), the converse of learning from natural proofs. [valiant84][valiant84] [ggm86][ggm86] `QueryLearner.agrees A ω z g` runs `A` with seed `ω` on the oracle `g` and accepts when the hypothesis agrees with `g` at the point `z`. On a uniformly random function the value at an unqueried point is a fair coin, so for a learner with at most `q` queries on `N` coordinates and any seed the test accepts with probability at most `1/2 + q/2^(N+1)` over a uniform point (`expect_dens_agrees_le`). A learner with error `ε ≤ 1` and confidence `1 - δ` for a class containing every member of a keyed family `F` accepts the members with probability at least `(1 - δ)(1 - ε)` for some seed (`exists_le_expect_dens_agrees`). `exists_dens_agrees_sub_dens_agrees` fixes a seed and a point at which the test accepts a uniformly random member more often than a uniformly random function by at least `(1 - δ)(1 - ε) - 1/2 - q/2^(N+1)`; it applies to adaptive learners. [CryptoHardness/Circuit.lean](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/CryptoHardness/Circuit.lean) states the circuit form through `HasCircuitTests A q t`: `A` agrees with a learner making at most `q` queries, and for every seed and point its test, read on truth tables, is a circuit with at most `t` gates. A non-adaptive learner with a circuit of `S` gates has tests of `S + 7` gates (`HasCircuit.isCircuitTest_agrees`, `HasCircuit.hasCircuitTests`), and an adaptive one with circuits of `S` gates in all has tests of `S + q (2^n (2n + 3) + 1) + 7` gates, reading its answers from the table (`HasAdaptiveCircuit.isCircuitTest_agrees`, `HasAdaptiveCircuit.hasCircuitTests`). `Learns.le_of_secure` bounds the accuracy of a learner with tests of `t` gates by the security of `F` against such circuits: `(1 - δ)(1 - ε) ≤ 1/2 + q/2^(n+1) + η`; `eventually_le_of_isExponentiallySecure` is the form for exponentially secure families. [CryptoHardness/GGM.lean](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/CryptoHardness/GGM.lean) instantiates the GGM functions ([natural proofs](metacomplexity-and-methods.md#natural-proofs-and-cryptographic-assumptions)). `Learns.le_of_secure_generator` charges a learner with tests of `t` gates for the functions of circuit size `n (C + 4k) + 2` against a generator secure against circuits with `t + 2^n (n C + 1)` gates, with advantage `(2^n - 1) η`. From a subexponentially secure family of generators of polynomial circuit complexity, `exists_eventually_not_learns_of_isSubexponentiallySecure` (registry ID `learning-hardness-from-generators`) gives a polynomial `s` such that for every `c` and every `ε ≤ 1`, `δ ≥ 0` with `(1 - δ)(1 - ε) > 3/4`, for large `n` no learner with at most `2^(n-1)` adaptive queries and circuits with `S ≤ 2^(c n)` gates in all (`HasAdaptiveCircuit`) learns the functions of circuit size `s n`; non-adaptive learners are covered through `HasCircuit.hasAdaptiveCircuit`. The tests are nonuniform circuits with the learner's seed and comparison point fixed by averaging, and the learners are circuits; running time is not modelled. The uniform pseudorandom-function model and the cryptographic hardness of learning restricted classes remain open ([#4927](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4927)). Complete client: [CryptoHardnessLearning.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/CryptoHardnessLearning.lean) learns the dictator `x ↦ x 0` with no queries and no gates, so the test accepts at most half of all functions at some point, and the family whose only member is the dictator is not `1/4`-secure against circuits with seven gates. An adaptive learner asks `0` and then the point all of whose bits are the first answer; its queries are substitutions, so it has adaptive circuits of four gates and circuit tests. The client also instantiates the unlearnability theorem at `ε = δ = 1/10`, for adaptive and for non-adaptive learners.

[Learners yield natural properties](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/CryptoHardness/NaturalProperty.lean), the direction of Oliveira and Santhanam (Theorem 8, (1) ⇒ (5)) that closes the loop with the CIKK learner above. [oliveirasanthanam2017][oliveirasanthanam2017] For a learner `A` with seeds uniform on a finite `Ω`, `QueryLearner.lowAgreement A θ` is the set of functions on which the agreement test, averaged over all seeds and points, accepts with probability below `θ`. With `θ = (1 - δ)(1 - ε)`, a learner with error `ε ≤ 1` and confidence `1 - δ` for `C` puts no member of `C` in it (`notMem_lowAgreement`, through the shared averaging lemma `le_expect_dens_eq`). With at most `q` queries, at least a `θ - (1/2 + q/2^(N+1))` fraction of all functions lie in it for `θ ≤ 1` (`sub_le_dens_lowAgreement`, the fair-coin bound averaged over seeds). Its truth-table test is a negated threshold of the `M = |Ω| 2^n` agreement tests, so tests of `t` gates give at most `M t + 2 + M (2 (M + 2)) + 1` gates (`complexity_truthTableTest_lowAgreement_le`). `isNatural_PPoly_lowAgreement` makes the property `P/poly`-natural when the seed sets and tests have size `2^(c(n+1))` and the accuracy gap is at least `2^(-cn)`, using `truthTableLanguage_mem_PPoly_of_complexity_le` in [NaturalProof.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/NaturalProof.lean); `isUseful_lowAgreement` makes it useful against every class whose languages eventually have their slices in `C`, and `isNatural_PPoly_and_isUseful_SIZE` (registry ID `learning-natural-property`) combines the two for `SIZE s`. The accuracy gap restricts the theorem to strong or confidence-amplified learners: the non-trivial learners of the source's item (1), with error `1/2 - 1/n^k` and confidence `1 - 1/n`, have `(1 - δ)(1 - ε) = (1 - 1/n)(1/2 + 1/n^k)`, which is below `1/2` for `k ≥ 2` and `n ≥ 3`; those learners are not covered as stated. The property averages exactly over a small uniform seed set rather than sampling, so arbitrary seed distributions and the source's `BPP`-natural form with running time are not covered. Complete client: [LearningNaturalProperty.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/LearningNaturalProperty.lean) uses the learner that outputs the constant `false` with no queries; its property excludes the constant, holds for at least half of all functions, and is `P/poly`-natural and useful against every class whose slices are eventually `false`.

## Statistical queries

The [statistical query model](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/StatisticalQuery/Basic.lean) follows Kearns's Definition 3 on a finite domain `X` with a fixed distribution `D : PMF X`. A `Query X` is a function `X → Bool → ℝ` with values in `[-1, 1]`, so Kearns's `{0, 1}` predicates and Reyzin's `±1` queries are special cases. A learner is a deterministic `OracleComp (spec X) (X → Bool)` program whose calls are queries answered by reals. `OracleComp.IsQueryBounded` bounds its query count. `Learns A D C τ ε` requires error at most `ε` for every target in `C` and every answer sequence within `τ` of the true expectations, including adaptive answers (`OracleComp.supportWith`). `LearnsStateless` only quantifies over fixed answer functions. It is the weaker notion (`Learns.learnsStateless`), so lower bounds are stated for it. There is a single tolerance and no confidence parameter. A learner whose per-query tolerances are all at least `τ` is covered by the single tolerance `τ`.

The [lower bounds](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/StatisticalQuery/LowerBound.lean) use Szörényi's argument as presented by Reyzin. `LearnsStateless.card_le_of_abs_corr_le` takes concepts `f i ∈ C` with pairwise `|corr D (f i) (f j)| ≤ γ`, a learner using at most `q` queries, `γ < τ²` and `γ < (1 - 2ε)²`. It bounds the number of concepts by `q (1-γ)/(τ²-γ) + (1-γ)/((1-2ε)²-γ)`. `card_le_of_corr_le` assumes only a one-sided correlation bound and doubles the first term. With `γ = 1/d`, so that the one-sided statistical query dimension is at least `d`, `card_mul_sq_sub_one_le_two_mul_add_one` takes Reyzin's Theorem 12 hypothesis of accuracy `τ` (`1 - 2ε ≥ τ`) and gives `d τ² - 1 ≤ 2q + 1`. Its exact-learning special case `ε = 0`, `card_mul_sq_sub_one_le_two_mul`, gives Reyzin's query count `d τ² - 1 ≤ 2q`. The [parity corollary](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/StatisticalQuery/Parity.lean) `LearnsStateless.two_pow_card_le_of_parityOn_mem` gives `2^n ≤ q/τ² + 1/(1-2ε)²` for any class containing all parities on `n` bits under the uniform distribution. [CslibFrontierTests/StatisticalQuery.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/StatisticalQuery.lean) has a concrete one-query learner, an edge case where tolerance `1` fails, and parity instances.

The [simulation](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/StatisticalQuery/Simulation.lean) runs a learner on random examples in the block form of Reyzin's Section 2.1. `simulate n blocks A` answers the `i`-th call `φ` by the empirical mean `empirical φ (blocks i)` of `φ` on the `i`-th block of `m` labelled examples. `toOuterMeasure_simulate_notMem_le` is the union bound along an adaptive run: each call is answered from a block independent of the earlier ones, whatever the earlier answers were. It applies to any program with a query budget, any per-query set of acceptable answers and a uniform per-query failure bound `β`, giving `n β` for `n` queries. Hoeffding's inequality for an empirical average under an arbitrary finite distribution (`PMF.pi_abs_sum_div_sub_sum_mul_ge_le` in [Concentration](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/Concentration.lean)) gives `toOuterMeasure_empirical_notMem_valid_le`. Together they give `Learns.toOuterMeasure_lt_disagreement_simulate_le`: a learner with tolerance `τ ≥ 0`, error `ε` and at most `q` queries, run on `q` independent blocks of `m` examples of a target in `C`, has error above `ε` with probability at most `2 q exp(-m τ² / 2)`. `Learns.isPACLearnerFor` packages the learner `simulateLearner A q m` on `q m` examples, split into blocks along `finProdFinEquiv` (`PMF.pi_map_finProdFinEquiv` in [Product/Reindex](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/Product/Reindex.lean)). It is an `IsPACLearnerFor (q * m) ε δ C (realizable D C)` witness whenever `2 q exp(-m τ² / 2) ≤ δ`. This condition needs a positive tolerance. For `τ > 0` it holds once `m ≥ 2 log(2q/δ) / τ²`. At `τ = 0` it reads `2 q ≤ δ < 1`, so it applies only to learners making no queries. `realizable D C` contains the distributions of a `D`-random point labelled by a concept of `C`, so this is fixed-distribution PAC learning. Under the uniform distribution it is `UniformLearning.uniformRealizable C` by definition.

The [low-degree algorithm](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/StatisticalQuery/LowDegree.lean) `lowDegree ι t` is a non-adaptive learner. Through `askAll` it asks `charQuery S`, `(x, y) ↦ signVal y · χ_S(x)`, for each of the `N` sets `S` of at most `t` coordinates, so it makes `N` queries (`isQueryBounded_lowDegree`), and returns the sign of `∑ c_S χ_S` for the answers `c_S`. Under the uniform distribution, `expect` of `charQuery S` is the Fourier coefficient on `S`, and `disagreement` is `UniformLearning.uniformError`. `learns_lowDegree` gives error `N τ² + κ` for every class whose Fourier weight above degree `t` is at most `κ`. `learns_lowDegree_parityOn` gives error `N τ²` for the parities on at most `t` coordinates, in contrast to the lower bound for all parities. [CslibFrontierTests/StatisticalQuerySimulation.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/StatisticalQuerySimulation.lean) runs the one-query learner on two concrete blocks, giving different hypotheses. It checks a 40-example PAC instance and the low-degree learner on three bits, including the PAC learner it yields.

The [statistical query dimension](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/StatisticalQuery/Dimension.lean) `sqDim D C` is Reyzin's Definition 11 for a fixed distribution: the largest `d` such that `C` contains `d` distinct concepts with pairwise `|corr D f g| ≤ 1/d`. Blum, Furst, Jackson, Kearns, Mansour and Rudich (BFJKMR) use the threshold `1/d³` in their Definition 2. Their dimension `d₃` and `d₁ = sqDim` satisfy `d₃ ≤ d₁ < (d₃+1)³`, so polynomial boundedness does not depend on the normalization; this relation is not formalized. The variant `sqDimOneSided D C` drops the absolute value and asks only `corr D f g ≤ 1/d`. This is the hypothesis Szörényi's lower-bound argument uses. `sqDim_le_sqDimOneSided` and `sqDimOneSided_le` give `sqDim ≤ sqDimOneSided ≤ 18 (sqDim + 1)³`, so the two are polynomially related. On the identity and negation of a bit, which have correlation `-1`, they are `1` and `2`. The dimension characterizes weak learning (Section 5 of BFJKMR). `LearnsStateless.sqDimOneSided_mul_sq_le` gives the lower bound `sqDimOneSided D C · τ² ≤ 2q + 2` when `1 - 2ε ≥ τ`, and hence `LearnsStateless.sqDim_mul_sq_le`. This is one weaker than Reyzin's Theorem 12 count `dτ² ≤ 2q + 1`, because a hypothesis with error `ε > 0` may correlate with one more concept. The exact-learning case is `card_mul_sq_sub_one_le_two_mul`. For the upper bound, `exists_cover_sqDim` takes a largest family `G ⊆ C` whose pairwise correlations have absolute value at most `1/(d+1)`, where `d = sqDim D C`. Then `|G| ≤ d`, and every concept of `C` has correlation of absolute value more than `1/(d+1)` with a member of `G`. Reyzin's Observation 23 sketch uses the family at threshold `1/d`; we choose the family at threshold `1/(d+1)`. The deterministic `corrLearner` asks the correlational query `corrQuery g` (`E_D[±g(x) · ±y]`) for each member of `G`. It takes the member whose answer has the largest absolute value and returns it, or its negation if that answer is negative, so its hypothesis need not lie in `C`. So `exists_learns_sqDim` (Reyzin's Observation 23, the analogue of BFJKMR's Theorem 13) makes at most `d` correlational queries and has error `1/2 - 1/(2(d+1)) + τ` against adaptive answers of every tolerance `τ`. Asymptotically, `tfae_polynomiallyBounded_sqDim` shows that four conditions are equivalent: a polynomially bounded `sqDim`, a polynomially bounded `sqDimOneSided`, weak learnability with polynomially many correlational queries of tolerance `1/(p n+1)` and advantage `1/(p n+1)` against adaptive answers, and the same with arbitrary queries against fixed answers. [CslibFrontierTests/StatisticalQueryDimension.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/StatisticalQueryDimension.lean) computes the dimensions of small classes, separates `sqDim` from `sqDimOneSided`, runs the weak learner on the identity and negation of a bit, and derives that parities are not weakly SQ-learnable from `2^n ≤ sqDim`.

These are information-theoretic bounds on query count, tolerance and sample size. They do not charge running time, and the learners are deterministic. Simulation under random classification noise (Kearns's Lemma 2 and Theorem 3), the single-sample form of Kearns's Theorem 1 (with its finite-query-space and finite-VC-dimension sample bounds), and a distribution-free form are not yet formalized.

## Online learning with experts

The [multiplicative-update core](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MultiplicativeUpdate.lean) fixes the experts-setting conventions. Experts form a finite type, losses are an arbitrary real sequence `ℓ : ℕ → ι → ℝ`, and an update factor `φ` gives expert `i` the weight `∏ s < t, φ (ℓ s i)`; the learner plays `prob φ ℓ t`, the normalized weights, and pays `∑ i, prob φ ℓ t i * ℓ t i`. The distribution in round `t` depends only on earlier losses (`prob_congr`), and `adaptiveLoss_eq` shows the bounds cover adversaries that see the current distribution. Every analysis passes through the single potential inequality `sum_log_le_log_card_add`.

[Multiplicative weights](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MultiplicativeWeights.lean) (`mwFactor η x = 1 - η x`) has the Arora–Hazan–Kale regret bound `mw_regret_le` with its exact constants, the mixed-strategy form, a second-order variant and the tuned `2 √(T ln n)` bound. [Hedge](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/Hedge.lean) (`exp (-η x)`) has the bound with the learner's expected squared loss and its tuned `2 √(T ln n)` form. [Weighted majority](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/WeightedMajority.lean) proves `M(T) ≤ 2 (1 + η) mᵢ(T) + 2 ln n / η` for every tie-breaking rule, stated as a hypothesis on the predictions; the concrete rule `weightedMajority` satisfies it.

The hypotheses decide which statement applies. Multiplicative weights needs `0 < η ≤ 1 / 2` and losses in `[-1, 1]` (the second-order variant needs only `η ℓ ≤ 1 / 2`), and its tuned bound needs `n ≥ 2` and `T ≥ 4 ln n`. Hedge needs `0 < η ≤ 1` and losses in `[-1, 1]` (or only `η ℓ ≥ -1`), and its tuned bound needs `n ≥ 2` and `T ≥ ln n`. Weighted majority needs `0 < η ≤ 1 / 2`. The Blum–Mansour learner below needs the same `η` and losses as Hedge, and its tuned bound needs `n ≥ 2` and `T ≥ n ln n`. The zero-sum theorems need entries in `[0, 1]`, `0 < ε ≤ 1`, `T > 0` with `ε ^ 2 T ≥ 4 ln n`, and columns that best respond to the learner's distribution in every round. The declarations live in the namespace `Cslib.MachineLearning.OnlineLearning.Experts`.

[Zero-sum games](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/ZeroSum.lean) run multiplicative weights on the rows against best-responding columns. After `T ≥ 4 ln n / ε ^ 2` rounds, the average row distribution and the empirical column distribution are `ε`-optimal, and the average payoff lies in `[v, v + ε]`. The value `v` is [IsMinimaxValue](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/Minimax/Value.lean), whose existence comes from the exact Sion-based minimax theorem in `Probability/Minimax`; `sum_averageProb_mul_le_add_of_forall_exists_sum_le` is the explicit `ε`-approximate counterpart of that theorem. The best-response property is a hypothesis, so any oracle can be used; `bestResponsePlay` (defined by choice) shows it is satisfiable. [Checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/OnlineLearning.lean) compute distributions, solve matching pennies, and show the mistake bound failing without the weighted-majority hypothesis.

[Swap regret](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/SwapRegret.lean) compares the learner with every *swapping function* `δ : ι → ι`, which replaces each play of `i` by `δ i`: the learner has swap regret at most `R` if `∑ t, ∑ i, p t i * ℓ t i ≤ ∑ t, ∑ i, p t i * ℓ t (δ i) + R` for every `δ`. Constant `δ` gives external regret, the benchmark above; swap regret is strictly stronger. `swap_regret_le_sum_regret` is the Blum–Mansour reduction ([blummansour07][blummansour07], Theorem 5; [roughgarden13cs364a][roughgarden13cs364a], Lecture #18, Theorem 2.1). It runs one external-regret algorithm per action; row `j` of the matrix `q t` is copy `j`'s distribution. The learner plays a stationary vector `p t ᵥ* q t = p t`, and copy `j` sees the losses `p t j * ℓ t`. If copy `j` has regret at most `R j` against every action, the learner has swap regret at most `∑ j, R j`. The theorem takes stationarity and the copies' regret bounds as hypotheses, so any external-regret algorithm can be plugged in. Stationary vectors exist by [`Matrix.exists_vecMul_eq_self_of_mem_rowStochastic`](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Stochastic.lean): every real row-stochastic matrix over a finite nonempty type has one, and `Matrix.stationaryVec` chooses one. `blumMansourProb η ℓ` instantiates the reduction with Hedge copies (`blumMansourWeight`, with `blumMansourWeight_eq_weight` identifying copy `j` with Hedge on `blumMansourLoss η ℓ j`). It is online (`blumMansourProb_congr`), and `blumMansour_swap_regret_le` bounds its swap regret by `η T + n ln n / η`, because the copies' second-order Hedge terms sum to at most `η T`. `blumMansour_swap_regret_le_sqrt` takes `η = √(n ln n / T)` and gives `2 √(T n ln n)`, the order of Blum and Mansour's Corollary 7. [Checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/SwapRegret.lean) give a play sequence with zero external regret and swap regret `3`, compute a stationary vector, and show that the learner plays the uniform distribution against zero losses. The game-theoretic consequence, that no-swap-regret dynamics converge to approximate correlated equilibria (Blum and Mansour's Theorem 3), is not yet formalized; [#4201](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4201) tracks it.

All of these are real-valued statements about the defined update rules. They do not charge arithmetic, sampling or oracle calls, the Blum–Mansour learner's stationary vectors are chosen classically rather than computed, and randomized weighted majority is not yet modelled as a probabilistic computation.

## Boosting

[AdaBoost](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Boosting/AdaBoost.lean) (Freund and Schapire) runs on a finite labelled sample `S : Fin m → α × Bool` with a deterministic weak learner `A : (Fin m → ℝ) → α → Bool`, which maps weights on the sample to a hypothesis. `rounds S A t` is a `Round` record of the weights and the hypothesis of round `t`; `Round.error` is its weighted error `εₜ` and `Round.vote` its vote weight `½ log ((1 - εₜ) / εₜ)`. The weights are the distribution of the [multiplicative-update core](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MultiplicativeUpdate.lean) with the sample points as experts, update factor `exp`, and the adaptive loss sequence chosen by the weak learner, so the proofs reuse its potential identity `potential_eq_card_mul_prod`; `rounds_weights_succ` recovers the textbook update, multiplying each weight by `exp (-wₜ yᵢ hₜ(xᵢ))` (signs from `BitString.signVal`) and renormalizing. `adaBoost S A T` returns the hypothesis of the first round before `T` with `εₜ = 0`, where the sources' vote weight would be infinite, and otherwise the sign of the weighted vote `score S A T`, with ties predicting `false`.

`sum_mul_exp_pointLoss` computes each round's normalizer as `2 √(εₜ (1 - εₜ))`. `empiricalMiscount_adaBoost_le_prod` bounds the training error by `∏ₜ 2 √(εₜ (1 - εₜ))` when every `εₜ < 1`, hence by `exp (-2 ∑ₜ (1/2 - εₜ)²)` (`empiricalMiscount_adaBoost_le_exp_sum`), and `empiricalMiscount_adaBoost_le_exp` by `exp (-2 γ² T)` when every `εₜ ≤ 1/2 - γ` with `γ ≥ 0` ([shalevshwartzbendavid14][shalevshwartzbendavid14], Theorem 10.2; [mohrirostamizadehtalwalkar18][mohrirostamizadehtalwalkar18], Theorem 7.2). `IsWeakLearner S A γ` asks for error at most `1/2 - γ` on every positive distribution on `S`; `IsWeakLearner.adaBoost_eq` then gives an output consistent with `S` once `log m < 2 γ² T`. [Checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/AdaBoost.lean) run AdaBoost with decision stumps on the sample `0 ↦ true, 1 ↦ false, 2 ↦ true`: the stump that errs on the lightest point has edge `1/6`, AdaBoost is consistent after `40` rounds, and the learner that always returns the constant stump lacks edge `1/4`. Generalization of the boosted class (SSBD Lemma 10.3, the ensemble margin bound of MRT Corollary 7.5) and weak-to-strong PAC learnability are not yet formalized ([#4913](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4913)); nothing here charges the weak learner's running time.

## Online learning in the mistake-bound model

The [mistake-bound core](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Basic.lean) formalizes Littlestone's realizable model. A deterministic `OnlineLearner α` predicts a `Bool` label from the labelled history and the current instance. `mistakes A S` counts its wrong predictions on a finite labelled sequence `S`. `Realizable C S` asks that some concept of the upstream `ConceptClass` agree with all of `S`. `mistakeBound A C : ℕ∞` is the supremum of the mistakes over realizable sequences, and `optimalMistakeBound C` is the infimum over learners. `mistakeBound_eq_iSup_adversary` shows that adaptive adversaries choosing instances and labels from the history add nothing for deterministic learners, and `mistakeBound_eq_iSup_concept` gives Littlestone's form with a fixed target. This model has no experts and no real-valued losses: the learner pays for each mistake against the realizability requirement. The non-realizable extension, with randomized learners and regret against the best concept, is described below under randomized learners and agnostic online learning.

[Mistake trees](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Littlestone.lean) are defined by recursion on their height (`HasMistakeTree`); `hasMistakeTree_iff_exists_tree` relates them to explicit instance trees. `littlestoneDim` takes values in `ℕ∞` and is `0` for the empty class (Littlestone uses `-1`). The adversary lower bound is `littlestoneDim_le_mistakeBound`. The [standard optimal algorithm](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/SOA.lean) matches it, so `optimalMistakeBound_eq_littlestoneDim` holds for every class, finite or not. [Halving](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Halving.lean) bounds finite classes by `⌊log₂ |C|⌋`. [VC dimension](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/VCDimension.lean) is at most the Littlestone dimension (`vcDim_le_littlestoneDim`). Because upstream `vcDim` is `0` for classes of infinite VC dimension, the meaningful statement for those is `littlestoneDim_eq_top_of_not_hasFiniteVCDim`. [Thresholds](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Thresholds.lean) show the gap: VC dimension `1`, Littlestone dimension `⌊log₂ (N + 1)⌋` for `θ ≤ N`, and `⊤` for all thresholds on `ℕ`.

The [perceptron](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Perceptron.lean) is an `OnlineLearner` on any real inner product space. Novikoff's bound `mistakes_perceptron_mul_sq_le` is stated per sequence, with margin `γ ≥ 0` and radius `R` as hypotheses on the presented examples. [Checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/MistakeBound.lean) run halving and the perceptron, compute the threshold gap, and show the bound failing for a negative margin.

[WINNOW1](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Winnow.lean) learns monotone disjunctions `monotoneDisjunction R` on `Fin n → Bool`. Its weights are real numbers, the update factor `α` and threshold `θ` are real parameters, and `tie` fixes the prediction when the score equals `θ`, which the source leaves open. Littlestone's Theorem 7 is `mistakes_winnow_le`: for every sequence labelled by a disjunction of `k` variables, with `α > 1` and `α θ ≥ 1`, there are at most `α k (log_α θ + 1) + n / θ` mistakes, for every choice of `tie`. The [lower bound](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/MonotoneDisjunctions.lean) builds the shattered set of Littlestone's Lemma 6, giving `k ⌊log₂ (n / k)⌋` as a lower bound on both the VC dimension and the optimal mistake bound of `monotoneDisjunctions n k`. For `1 ≤ k ≤ n`, WINNOW1 with `α = 2` and `θ = n / k` makes at most `2 k log₂ (n / k) + 3 k` mistakes (`mistakeBound_winnow_two_div_le`, hence `optimalMistakeBound_monotoneDisjunctions_le`), so for `1 ≤ k` and `2 k ≤ n` the optimal bound is `Θ(k log (n / k))`. The hypothesis `1 ≤ k` is needed: for `k = 0` the threshold is `0`, and WINNOW1 can err on the constant-false target. For `k > n` the class is empty. The source's second lower bound, stated in terms of `k (1 + log₂ (n / k))`, is not formalized. [Checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/Winnow.lean) run WINNOW1 through a promotion step and an elimination step and evaluate both bounds for `n = 8`, `k = 2`.

[Online-to-batch conversion](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/OnlineToBatch.lean) turns a mistake bound into a realizable PAC guarantee. `prefixHypothesis A S t` is the hypothesis of `A` after the first `t` examples of the sample `S`. On i.i.d. examples it depends only on those examples, so its expected error is the probability that `A` errs on example `t` of a sample of size `m + 1` (`lintegral_error_prefixHypothesis_eq`). If `mistakeBound A C ≤ M` and `D` is realizable by `C`, summing over `t ≤ m` bounds the expected errors of the `m + 1` prefix hypotheses by the expected number of mistakes, at most `M` (`sum_lintegral_error_prefixHypothesis_le`). A uniformly random prefix hypothesis therefore has expected error at most `M / (m + 1)`. This random-stopping bound in expectation is the form used by Cesa-Bianchi, Conconi and Gentile (2004); online-to-batch conversion itself originates with Littlestone (COLT 1989). Both locators are unverified. Upstream `IsPACLearnerFor` asks for a deterministic learner, so the module uses the majority vote `prefixMajority A`, whose error is at most twice the average (`lintegral_error_prefixMajority_le`). Markov's inequality makes it an `IsPACLearnerFor m ε δ C (realizableDistributions C)` witness when `2 M ≤ ε δ (m + 1)` (`isPACLearnerFor_prefixMajority`), so `⌈2 M / (ε δ)⌉` examples suffice (`isPACLearnable_of_mistakeBound_le`). The confidence dependence is `1 / δ`; Littlestone's hypothesis-testing conversion with `log (1 / δ)` is not formalized. Fubini's theorem needs the learner to be measurable in the history and the instance (`IsMeasurableLearner`). Every learner on a countable domain with measurable singletons is (`isMeasurableLearner_of_countable`), and there a finite optimal mistake bound gives PAC learnability (`isPACLearnable_of_optimalMistakeBound_ne_top`). VC theory reaches the same conclusion for more classes: all thresholds on `ℕ` have VC dimension `1` and no finite mistake bound. The conversion adds a sample size stated through the mistake bound of a given learner. [Checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/OnlineToBatch.lean) evaluate the majority vote of halving's prefix hypotheses, give a seven-example PAC learner for three concepts on `Fin 2` and show that zero examples do not suffice, and learn the thresholds `θ ≤ 7` through SOA.

[Randomized learners](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Randomized.lean) map the history and the instance to a `PMF Bool`. Against a labelled sequence fixed in advance (an oblivious adversary), `expectedMistakes A S` sums the probabilities of wrong predictions; deterministic learners embed with `expectedMistakes = mistakes`. `hedgeLearner η E` draws one of finitely many deterministic experts with the Hedge distribution of the [multiplicative-update core](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MultiplicativeUpdate.lean) for their 0-1 losses, recomputed from the history. `expectedMistakes_hedgeLearner_le` transfers `hedge_regret_le_sqrt`: with the tuned rate `η = √(log N / T)` for `N` experts, on every sequence of at most `T` rounds and with `log N ≤ T`, the expected mistakes exceed those of every expert by at most `2 √(T log N)`.

[Agnostic online learning](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Agnostic.lean) drops realizability: the labels are arbitrary and the learner competes with the best concept of `C`. Following Ben-David, Pál and Shalev-Shwartz (COLT 2009, §3.2, Algorithm 3), the expert `flipExpert (soa C) I` runs SOA on its own predictions, negated at the rounds in `I`. For each concept `c ∈ C`, negating at SOA's mistakes on the `c`-labelling (at most `littlestoneDim C` rounds) reproduces `c` exactly (`mistakes_flipExpert_mistakeRounds`; the source's Lemma 12 and Corollary 13). `agnosticSOA C T d` (Algorithm 4) runs Hedge over the at most `(T + 1)^d` sets of at most `d` rounds below `T`. `expectedMistakes_agnosticSOA_le`, the upper half of the source's Theorem 1 in its Littlestone-dimension form, bounds its expected mistakes by those of any `c ∈ C` plus `2 √(T d log (T + 1))` when `littlestoneDim C ≤ d`. The source's constant is `√(½ d T ln T)`; the one here comes from the library's Hedge bound, and the source's `ln |C|` branch for finite classes is not stated. The learner needs `T` and `d` in advance. Cover's observation (1965, as cited by the source), `exists_mistakes_eq_length_of_const_mem`, shows that randomization is necessary: against `Adversary.cover`, which negates each prediction, every deterministic learner errs in all `n` rounds, while if `C` contains both constant classifiers one of them errs in at most `n / 2`. The matching lower bound `Ω(√(Ldim(C) T))` for randomized learners and adaptive adversaries are not formalized. [Checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/AgnosticOnline.lean) compare the two over `100` rounds for the two constant classifiers.

These are information-theoretic statements. `soa` decides emptiness and compares Littlestone dimensions classically, WINNOW1 updates real weights, the agnostic learner enumerates its experts and weighs them with real arithmetic, and no learner here has a charged running time.

## Online convex optimization

[Online gradient descent](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/OnlineGradientDescent.lean) plays points of a set `K` in a real inner product space. Its iterates are `x (t + 1) = proj (x t - η t • grad t (x t))`, where `grad : ℕ → E → E` is an arbitrary subgradient oracle. Rounds are indexed from `0`, so `η t` is the source's `η_{t+1}`. The projection is any map with `IsPythagoreanProjection K proj`: it lands in `K` and satisfies `‖proj y - z‖ ≤ ‖y - z‖` for `z ∈ K`. The [metric projection](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/MetricProjection.lean) onto a nonempty complete convex set is such a map; that module also proves the variational characterization, the fixed points and nonexpansiveness. Losses enter only through the subgradient inequality `f t x + ⟪grad t x, y - x⟫ ≤ f t y` on `K`, or its `α`-strongly convex strengthening. [First-order convexity](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Analysis/Convex/FirstOrder.lean) derives both from Mathlib's `ConvexOn` and `StrongConvexOn` with `HasGradientWithinAt K` (so boundary points of `K` are allowed) or `HasGradientAt`.

Each regret bound holds against every comparator `u ∈ K`, under the hypotheses that `D` bounds the diameter of `K` and `G` bounds the subgradient norms on `K`. `ogd_regret_le_three_halves` is Hazan's Theorem 3.1: step sizes `D / (G √(t + 1))` give regret at most `3 / 2 · G D √T` for every `T`, assuming `D, G > 0`. `ogd_regret_le_zinkevich` is Zinkevich's Theorem 1, and `ogd_regret_le_fixed` gives `G D √T` (with `D, G > 0`) for a known horizon; both assume `T ≥ 1`. `ogd_regret_le_log` is Hazan's Theorem 3.3: for `α > 0`, `α`-strongly convex losses and step sizes `1 / (α (t + 1))` give `G² / (2α) · (1 + log T)`. It assumes no separate diameter bound, because its hypotheses already force `‖x - y‖ ≤ 2G / α` on `K` (`norm_sub_le_of_strongConvex`). OGD is deterministic, so these bounds also cover adaptive adversaries. [Checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/OnlineGradientDescent.lean) instantiate the bounds on `[-1, 1]` with linear and quadratic losses, and show maps that are not Pythagorean projections. As with the experts setting, nothing charges the cost of projections or arithmetic.

[Follow the regularized leader](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/FTRL.lean) works in any real normed space. `IsFTRL K ψ ℓ x` says that every `x t` lies in `K` and minimizes `ftrlObjective ψ ℓ t = ψ + ∑ s < t, ℓ s` on `K`. The regularizer `ψ` is fixed, and rounds are indexed from `0`. The predicate constrains the realized sequence, and `x t` depends only on earlier losses, so the bounds also cover adaptive adversaries. `exists_isFTRL` gives runs on nonempty compact `K` when `ψ` and the losses are continuous on `K`. `IsFTRL.unique` shows they are unique when `ψ` is `μ`-strongly convex with `μ > 0` and the losses are convex. `ftrl_regret_eq` is Orabona's Lemma 7.1 and holds for every sequence. `IsFTRL.add_sum_le` and `IsFTRL.regret_le_add_sum_sub` are Hazan's Lemmas 5.4 and 5.3. The headline `IsFTRL.regret_le` assumes `ψ` is `μ`-strongly convex on `K` with `μ > 0` (Mathlib's `StrongConvexOn`, for the ambient norm), the losses are convex and `ℓ t (x t) - ℓ t y ≤ L t * ‖x t - y‖` on `K`. It bounds the regret against `u ∈ K` by `ψ u - ψ (x 0) + ∑ t < T, L t ^ 2 / (2μ)`. Its per-round step is the stability bound `StrongConvexOn.sub_le_of_isMinOn` in [strongly convex functions](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Analysis/Convex/Strong.lean), proved from quadratic growth at a minimizer without subgradients. The positivity matters: `StrongConvexOn K 0 ψ` is plain convexity, and with `μ = 0`, `ψ = 0` the displayed bound is false. `IsFTRL.regret_le_div` (regularizer `R / η` for a `σ`-strongly convex `R`, with `σ, η > 0`) is the constant-learning-rate case of Orabona's Corollary 7.9. `IsFTRL.regret_le_sqrt` tunes `η` to get `D L √(2T / σ)` when `σ, D, L > 0`, `T ≥ 1`, `R y - R z ≤ D ^ 2` for `y, z ∈ K`, and every round has the same `L`. `IsFTRL.regret_le_of_subgradient` and `IsFTRL.regret_le_of_hasFDerivWithinAt` cover FTRL on linearized losses with the operator norm of the subgradient. In a real inner product space, for a nonempty complete convex `K` and `η > 0`, `isFTRL_euclidean_iff` shows that the regularizer `‖y - x₀‖ ^ 2 / (2η)` with linear losses gives the metric projection of `x₀ - η ∑ s < t, g s` (lazy projection, Hazan §5.4.1). `lazyOGD` runs it with gradients chosen at the played points. When the gradients satisfy the subgradient inequality `f t x + ⟪grad t x, y - x⟫ ≤ f t y` on `K`, `lazyOGD_regret_le` bounds the regret against `u ∈ K` by `‖u - x₀‖ ^ 2 / (2η) + η / 2 ∑ t < T, ‖grad t (x t)‖ ^ 2`. `lazyOGD_regret_le_fixed` takes `η = D / (G √T)` and gives `G D √T` when, in addition, `D, G > 0`, `‖grad t x‖ ≤ G` on `K`, `‖u - x₀‖ ≤ D` and `T ≥ 1`. [Checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/FTRL.lean) compute a run on `[-1, 1]`, show a sequence that is not a run, and instantiate each bound. Time-varying regularizers, entropic FTRL (Hedge), mirror descent and the lower bounds are not formalized yet.

[Convex online-to-batch conversion](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/ConvexOnlineToBatch.lean) turns regret bounds into bounds on the expected true risk (`trueRisk` from [LossMinimization/Basic](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/LossMinimization/Basic.lean)). A learner plays `w t S ∈ K` on an i.i.d. sample `S : Fin T → Z` from a probability measure `D`. The only assumption about the learner is that `w t S` does not depend on `S t` (`hfresh`); a learner that uses only the earlier examples satisfies it. Each `w t S` must be measurable in `S`, the losses `ℓ · z` must be convex on the convex set `K`, and an integrable `b` must dominate `|ℓ w z|` for `w ∈ K`. Under these assumptions `integral_trueRisk_average_le` bounds the expected true risk of the average point `(1 / T) ∑ t, w t S` by the average expected online loss. `integral_trueRisk_average_le_add` turns a regret bound `R` against `u ∈ K` into `L_D u + R / T`. The conditional step integrates out the fresh example under the product measure with `MeasureTheory.integral_apply_eval_eq_integral_integral` in the [one-coordinate resampling module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/MeasureTheory/Integral/PiUpdate.lean). [Stochastic gradient descent](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/LossMinimization/StochasticGradientDescent.lean) is `sgd`, OGD on the losses `ℓ · (S t)` with subgradient oracle `g`. `integral_trueRisk_sgd_average_le` is Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David's Theorem 14.8 and Corollary 14.12 (locators unverified) for the average of the first `T` iterates: subgradients of norm at most `ρ` on `K`, a comparator `u ∈ K` with `‖x₀ - u‖ ≤ B` and the step size `B / (ρ √T)` give `L_D u + B ρ / √T`. It requires `ρ, B > 0`, `T ≥ 1`, measurable `proj`, `ℓ` and `g`, and a `D`-integrable `ℓ x₀`. `K` may be the whole space with `proj = id`. The regret step is `ogd_regret_le_fixed_of_norm_sub_le`, which, unlike `ogd_regret_le_fixed`, needs no diameter bound. Integrability comes from `norm_ogd_sub_le` and `abs_sub_le_of_forall_add_inner_le`, which keep the iterates in a ball around `x₀` on which the loss is Lipschitz, and `convexOn_of_forall_add_inner_le` recovers convexity from the subgradient inequality. [Checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/StochasticGradientDescent.lean) verify the hypotheses for the absolute loss `|w - z|` on `ℝ`, compute an iterate, and instantiate the bound for four unconstrained steps under any `D` with a first moment. These bounds are in expectation; the conversion in the next paragraph gives confidence bounds for bounded losses. The faster rates for strongly convex losses and running time are not formalized.

[High-probability online-to-batch conversion](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/HighProbabilityOnlineToBatch.lean) replaces expectations by confidence bounds. In round `t` a learner commits to a loss function `f t S : Z → ℝ` with values in `[a, b]`, which must not depend on the examples `S t, S (t + 1), …` of the i.i.d. sample `S : Fin T → Z` (hypothesis `hfresh`, for all `t ≤ s`). For `δ ∈ (0, 1]`, the average expected loss then exceeds the average online loss by more than `(b - a) √(ln (1 / δ) / (2 T))` with probability at most `δ` (`measureReal_average_integral_gt_le`; the sum form is `measureReal_sum_integral_gt_le`). For the iterates `w t S` of a learner and a loss `ℓ`, this bounds their average true risk (`measureReal_average_trueRisk_gt_le`). For a convex loss, Jensen's inequality and a regret bound `R` against `u` that holds on every sample give `L_D (w̄) ≤ L_D u + R / T + c √(2 ln (1 / δ) / T)` outside probability `δ`, where `|ℓ w z - ℓ u z| ≤ c` on `K` (`measureReal_trueRisk_average_gt_le`). Each bound is stated as `(Measure.pi fun _ => D).real` of the failure event. The concentration step is the Azuma–Hoeffding inequality under a product measure in [AzumaPi](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Probability/AzumaPi.lean). Suppose `Y i` ignores the coordinates after `i`, has mean `0` over coordinate `i`, and ranges over an interval of length `c i` as coordinate `i` varies. Then `∑ i, Y i` is sub-Gaussian with parameter `∑ i, (c i / 2) ^ 2` (`ProbabilityTheory.hasSubgaussianMGF_sum_pi`), and it is at least `ε` with probability at most `exp (-2 ε² / ∑ i, c i ^ 2)` (`ProbabilityTheory.measure_sum_ge_le_pi`). Mathlib's `measure_sum_ge_le_of_hasCondSubgaussianMGF` needs conditional sub-Gaussian bounds on a standard Borel space; this form needs only measurable coordinate spaces. [Checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/HighProbabilityOnlineToBatch.lean) derive Hoeffding's inequality for a fixed hypothesis, apply the conversion to the previous-example predictor under the 0-1 loss, and apply the convex bound to the clipped previous example under the absolute loss, with the trivial regret bound `T`.

## Property testing as decision trees

The existing BLR local test compiles to a decision tree through `PCP.LocalTest.decisionTree`. [depth_decisionTree_repeatAll_blrTest_le](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/Linearity.lean#L52) proves that k independent repetitions use at most 3k queries, accept every parity with probability one and accept a function at uniform relative Hamming distance at least epsilon from every parity with probability at most `(1-epsilon)^k`.

The corresponding [one-sided lower bound](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/LinearityLowerBound.lean) shows that a tester of depth at most two that accepts every parity perfectly cannot reject the specified nonlinearity witness with value false at zero. This establishes the three-query boundary under those hypotheses. It is a lower bound for worst-case query depth, not expected queries, and it is formulated for arbitrary randomness support through the tested tree property.

The Fourier analysis behind these bounds lives in [BooleanAnalysis/Linearity](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/BooleanAnalysis/Linearity.lean): `blrAcceptance_eq_sum_fourierCoeff_pow_three` and soundness `exists_hammingDist_parityOn_le_of_le_blrAcceptance` (O'Donnell, Theorem 1.30 [odonnell14]). [LinearityRejection](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/BooleanAnalysis/LinearityRejection.lean) proves the converse rejection bound of O'Donnell's Exercise 1.28. If `f` is at relative distance `δ` from the parity on `T`, every other coefficient of `(-1)^f` has absolute value at most `2δ` (`abs_fourierCoeff_signVal_le_of_ne`). The test then accepts with probability at most `1 - 3δ + 10δ² - 8δ³` (`blrAcceptance_le_of_hammingDist_parityOn`); this holds for any parity, not only the nearest. The constant `3` is sharp: the indicator of a nonzero point on `n ≥ 2` bits is at distance `δ = 2⁻ⁿ` from its nearest parity and is rejected with probability exactly `3δ - 6δ²` (`blrAcceptance_decide_eq`). Hence, for every `c > 3`, no bound `cδ - Cδ²` holds for all functions at small distance from their nearest parity (`not_forall_mul_sub_mul_sq_le_one_sub_blrAcceptance`). [Affinity](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/BooleanAnalysis/Affinity.lean) covers Exercise 1.29, with affine functions written `b ^^ parityOn S x`. They are characterized by `f (x + y + z) = (f x ^^ f y ^^ f z)` (`exists_eq_xor_parityOn_iff`) and by `f (x + y) = (f x ^^ f y ^^ f 0)` (`exists_eq_xor_parityOn_iff_add`). The four-query test with uniform queries `x`, `y`, `z`, `x + y + z` accepts with probability `1/2 + 1/2 Σ_S F̂(S)⁴` (`affinityAcceptance_eq_sum_fourierCoeff_pow_four`). Affine functions pass with probability one, and acceptance at least `1 - ε` gives an affine function at relative distance at most `ε` (`exists_hammingDist_xor_parityOn_le_of_le_affinityAcceptance`). The anchored variant replaces the fourth query by the point `0`; it is the BLR test on `x ↦ f x ^^ f 0` (`anchoredAffinityAcceptance_eq_blrAcceptance`) and has the same guarantees. Both acceptance probabilities are expectations over the cube; neither test is packaged as a `PCP.LocalTest` with query-depth bounds yet. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/BooleanAnalysisLinearityTesting.lean) include a three-bit point indicator rejected with probability `9/32`, strictly between the cubic bound `15/64` and `3δ = 3/8`. They also include the conjunction of two bits, which attains the coefficient bound and passes the affinity test with probability `5/8`, and a negated parity that both affinity tests accept and the BLR test always rejects.

For finite abelian groups, import
[HomomorphismTesting](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Algebra/Group/HomomorphismTesting.lean)
and use `Cslib.HomomorphismTesting`. Here `rejection f` is the uniform independent probability
that `f x + f y ≠ f (x + y)`, and `distance f` is the attained minimum normalized Hamming
disagreement over bundled additive homomorphisms. `min_le_rejection` proves
`min (1/6) (distance f / 2) ≤ rejection f` (Goldreich draft Exercise 9.21).
Choose any `φ` satisfying `IsPlurality f φ`, whose existence is `exists_plurality`.
`concentration` bounds the probability that the two-read vote `f (x + y) - f y` equals `φ x`
by at least `1 - 2 * rejection f`, for every `x`. If rejection is strictly below `1/6`,
`correction f hφ hε` bundles this same function as a homomorphism and `closeness` bounds its
normalized disagreement by twice rejection. Trivial groups are included; no tie-breaking rule
is required. The [checked clients](../../CslibFrontierTests/HomomorphismTesting.lean) show
perfect passage on `ZMod 3` and trivial groups and assemble the corrected homomorphism and
distance bound. This is a finite probability API without a group encoding or runtime model.
The Boolean Fourier guarantee above remains the sharper choice on the cube.

## Monotonicity testing

[Monotonicity/Basic](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/Monotonicity/Basic.lean) works on the cube `ι → Bool` for a finite index type `ι` (for example `Fin n`), with Mathlib's coordinatewise order and Mathlib's `Monotone`. `monotone_iff_forall_update_le` reduces monotonicity to the edges `update x i false ≤ update x i true`. `violatedEdges f i` lists, by their lower endpoints, the edges in direction `i` along which `f` decreases. Below, `A_i = violatedEdges f i`. `f` is monotone exactly when there are none (`monotone_iff_forall_violatedEdges_eq_empty`). `distMonotone f` is the least Hamming distance from `f` to a monotone function, and `relDistMonotone f` divides it by `2 ^ n`. "`f` is `ε`-far from monotone" is stated as `ε ≤ relDistMonotone f`: every monotone function differs from `f` on at least `ε 2 ^ n` points (`le_relDistMonotone_iff`). `violatedEdgeFraction f` is `Σ_i |A_i|` divided by the `n 2 ^ (n - 1)` edges of the cube.

The squashing operator `sortAlong i f` sorts the values of `f` along every edge in direction `i`. It changes `f` at exactly the `2 |A_i|` endpoints of the violated edges in that direction (`hammingDist_sortAlong`) and leaves no violated edge there (`violatedEdges_sortAlong_self`). The Key Claim of the source's proof is `card_violatedEdges_sortAlong_le`: sorting along `i` does not increase the number of violated edges in any direction `j`. It is proved by checking the 16 labellings of each square spanned by `i` and `j`. Sorting along every direction in turn gives a monotone function within distance `2 Σ_i |A_i|` (`distMonotone_le_two_mul_sum`). Hence an `ε`-far function has at least an `ε / n` fraction of violated edges (`div_card_le_violatedEdgeFraction`). In the other direction, `card_violatedEdges_le_distMonotone` gives `|A_i| ≤ distMonotone f`.

The [edge tester](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/Monotonicity/EdgeTester.lean) reuses the BLR format: `edgeTest ι` is a two-query `PCP.LocalTest` with random string `(i, x)`. It reads `f` at both endpoints of the edge in direction `i` through `x` and accepts when `f` does not decrease along it. One trial rejects with probability exactly `violatedEdgeFraction f` (`one_sub_acceptProb_edgeTest`). `k` independent trials make at most `2k` non-adaptive queries (`depth_decisionTree_repeatAll_edgeTest_le`) and always accept monotone functions (`acceptProb_repeatAll_edgeTest_of_monotone`). They accept `ε`-far functions with probability at most `(1 - ε / n) ^ k` (`acceptProb_repeatAll_edgeTest_le`), hence at most `1/3` once `0 < ε` and `2n ≤ εk` (`acceptProb_repeatAll_edgeTest_le_one_third`). `acceptProb_repeatAll_edgeTest_ceil_le_one_third` takes `k = ⌈2n / ε⌉`, so the tester makes `2 ⌈2n / ε⌉` queries. This is the upper bound of Theorem 8.2 in Roughgarden's lecture notes ([roughgarden15cc]), which attributes the tester to Goldreich, Goldwasser, Lehman, Ron and Samorodnitsky ([gglrs00]); the proof follows those notes. The theorem takes `t = Θ(n / ε)` trials. Only the upper bound is formalized, with the explicit choice `t = ⌈2n / ε⌉`; nothing is claimed about fewer trials. The completeness theorems need `Nonempty ι`: the cube of dimension `0` has no edges, and an expectation over an empty set of random strings is `0` by convention. These are query bounds only; no running time is claimed. Three published results are not formalized here: the `Ω(√n)` lower bound for non-adaptive one-sided testers, the testers for larger ranges and hypergrids, and the `Õ(√n / ε²)` path testers. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/PropertyTestingMonotonicity.lean) include:

- a monotone function on `Fin 2` that is always accepted;
- the anti-dictator `x ↦ ¬x₀` on `Fin 3`, with four violated edges, distance `4`, relative distance `1/2` and single-trial acceptance probability `2/3`;
- a tester with twelve trials at proximity `1/2`;
- an example showing that one trial is too few.

## Distribution testing from samples

[IsTester](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/Basic.lean) is the sample-access model. A `k`-sample tester for a set `P` of distributions on a finite type is a randomized rule `T : (Fin k → α) → PMF Bool`. It runs on `iid p k` and must accept every member of `P` with probability at least 2/3. It must accept with probability at most 1/3 every `p` whose statistical distance (`dist`, total variation) from every member of `P` is at least epsilon. The metric space on `PMF α` is a scoped instance, so `dist` needs `open Cslib.Probability.PMF` (or `open scoped Cslib.Probability.PMF`). Testers are semantic kernels: the theorems count samples, not running time. `one_third_le_dist_iid_bind` is Le Cam's method in mixture form, and `IsTester.map_equiv` relabels the domain. It rests on `abs_bind_apply_toReal_sub_le_dist` in [StatisticalDistance/Event](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/StatisticalDistance/Event.lean): a randomized distinguisher's output probabilities move by at most the statistical distance.

The [collision tester](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/UniformityCollision.lean) accepts when the number of colliding sample pairs is at most `C(k,2)(1+2ε²)/n`. Under `40 √n ≤ (k-1) ε²` and `0 < ε`, `isTester_collisionTester` proves it is a uniformity tester. The proof applies the finite Chebyshev inequality `sum_mul_ite_le_sum_mul_sq_div` from [Probability/PMF/Chebyshev](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/PMF/Chebyshev.lean) to the exact mean and variance of `collisionCount` from [Probability/IID/CollisionCount](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/IID/CollisionCount.lean). Paninski's [lower bound](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/UniformityLowerBound.lean) `card_le_of_isTester_uniform` gives `n ≤ 52 k² δ⁴` for every tester with proximity `δ ≤ 1/2` on a domain of even size `n`. At `δ = 1/2` it is the birthday bound `4n ≤ 13k²`. The odd-size lower bound and tolerant testing remain open. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/DistributionTestingUniformity.lean) include a satisfiable instance of the sample hypothesis, a far distribution and an impossible single-sample tester.

[Identity testing](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/IdentityReduction.lean) to a fixed distribution `q` on `n` points reduces to uniformity testing. `IsTester.bind_pi` in [Basic](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/Basic.lean) runs a tester on samples passed independently through a randomized map `F`. It requires that `F` sends the property into the target property and far distributions to far distributions, and it rests on `iid_bind` in [Probability/IID](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/IID.lean). Goldreich's kernel `identityKernel q` maps `q` exactly to uniform on the `4n` cells of `IdentityDomain q` (`bind_identityKernel_self`). It shrinks statistical distance by at most a factor 3 (`dist_le_three_mul_dist_bind_identityKernel`). Hence `isTester_identity_of_uniform`: a uniformity tester on the cells with proximity `ε/3` tests identity to `q` with proximity `ε` and the same number of samples. `exists_isTester_identity_of_uniform` accepts a uniformity tester on any domain of size `4n`. With the collision tester, `isTester_identityTester` needs `0 < ε` and `720 √n ≤ (k-1) ε²`. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/DistributionTestingIdentity.lean) compute block sizes, including a nonempty and an empty leftover block, and a satisfiable sample-size instance.

[Closeness testing](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/ClosenessCollision.lean) compares two unknown distributions. In the two-sample model in [Basic](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/Basic.lean), a rule `T : (Fin k → α) → (Fin k → α) → PMF Bool` gets `k` samples of each distribution, with acceptance probability `acceptProb₂ T p q`. `IsClosenessTester T ε` requires acceptance probability at least `2/3` when `p = q`, and at most `1/3` when `ε ≤ dist p q`. The collision statistic `closenessStatistic x y = C₁ + C₂ - ((k-1)/k) C₃` combines the self-collisions of each sample with the cross-collisions `crossCollisionCount` from [Probability/IID/CrossCollisionCount](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/IID/CrossCollisionCount.lean). Its mean is `C(k,2) ‖p-q‖₂²` (`sum_sum_iid_mul_closenessStatistic`), and its variance has an exact closed form (`sum_sum_iid_mul_closenessStatistic_sub_sq`). When both collision probabilities are at most `b`, that closed form is bounded by `(k-1)(2k-1) b + 2(k-1)²(k+1) √b ‖p-q‖₄²` (`sum_sum_iid_mul_closenessStatistic_sub_sq_le_sqrt_sum_pow_four`, which implies DGPP's Lemma 10), and hence by the same expression with `‖p-q‖₂²` (`sum_sum_iid_mul_closenessStatistic_sub_sq_le`). The tester `closenessTester k ε` accepts when the statistic is at most `C(k,2) ε²/2`. `le_acceptProb₂_closenessTester` and `acceptProb₂_closenessTester_le` show it errs with probability at most `δ` on each side once `0 < δ` and `36 √b ≤ (k-1) ε² δ`. The hypotheses are the squared distances `‖p-q‖₂² ≤ ε²/4` and `ε² ≤ ‖p-q‖₂²`; for `0 < ε` this distinguishes `‖p-q‖₂ ≤ ε/2` from `‖p-q‖₂ ≥ ε`. `isClosenessTester_closenessTester` turns this into a closeness tester in statistical distance under `0 < ε` and `27 n ≤ (k-1) ε²`. That is `O(n/ε²)` samples, which is not optimal: learning both distributions empirically to statistical distance `ε/4` and comparing achieves the same rate. The optimal tester applies the `ℓ₂` tester after flattening. Statistical distance `dist` needs `open Cslib.Probability.PMF` (scoped instance). The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/DistributionTestingCloseness.lean) include exact cross-collision counts and means, an `ℓ₂` instance using `√b = 1/2`, and a closeness tester on `Bool` with `217` samples that rejects two distinct point masses.

[Flattening](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/ClosenessFlattening.lean) gives the optimal closeness tester. The split distribution `split p c` in [Probability/Split](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/Split.lean) divides each point `a` into `c a + 1` equal cells. It preserves statistical distance (`dist_split`). Its collision probability `∑ p(a)² / (c a + 1)` is antitone in `c` (`collisionProbability_split_antitone`). When `c` counts the occurrences in `m` independent samples of `p` itself, the expected collision probability is at most `1/(m+1)` (`sum_iid_mul_collisionProbability_split_le`). That rests on the inverse occurrence moment `(m+1) μ(a) E[1/(N+1)] ≤ 1` in [Probability/IID/Occurrence](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/IID/Occurrence.lean), which also holds `occurrenceCount` and the splitting lemmas `sum_iid_succ_mul` and `sum_iid_add_mul` for independent draws.

`closenessFlatteningTester m k ε` draws `m + k` samples of each distribution. The first `m` of both set `c = flatteningCount x₁ y₁`, the combined occurrence counts, so the split domain has `n + 2m` points. The other `k` of each pass through the split kernel into `closenessTester k (2ε/√(n+2m))`. `acceptProb₂_append` and `acceptProb₂_bind_pi` in [Basic](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/Basic.lean) reduce the acceptance probability to an average, over the flattening samples, of the `ℓ₂` tester's acceptance probability on the split distributions (`acceptProb₂_closenessFlatteningTester`). Markov's inequality at collision level `16/(m+1)` and `four_mul_dist_sq_le_card_mul_sum_sq` (Cauchy–Schwarz, in [Probability/Collision](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/Collision.lean)) then give `isClosenessTester_closenessFlatteningTester` under `0 < ε` and `864 (n + 2m) ≤ 5 (k-1) ε² √(m+1)`. With `m = ⌈min(n, n^{2/3} ε^{-4/3})⌉`, `exists_isClosenessTester_closenessFlatteningTester` gets `m + k ≤ 520 max(n^{2/3} ε^{-4/3}, √n ε^{-2}) + 3`. That is the Chan–Diakonikolas–Valiant–Valiant rate. The construction follows Diakonikolas–Kane Proposition 2.10 with two changes. It uses a fixed number of flattening samples instead of a Poisson number. It also flattens with samples of both distributions, so both split norms are small and the norm estimation of their Lemma 2.3 is not needed. The matching lower bound is not formalized. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/DistributionTestingClosenessFlattening.lean) include split masses and domain sizes, a satisfiable instance on `100` points, and a tester on `Bool` that rejects two distinct point masses.

## Dense graph testing

[Dense graph testers](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/DenseGraph/Basic.lean) read a graph on `Fin n` through its adjacency matrix. A `Tester n` is a `ProbOracleComp` that tosses private coins and asks vertex-pair queries adaptively. `acceptProb T G` runs it against the deterministic adjacency oracle `adjOracle G`. `editDist G H` counts the unordered vertex pairs on which two graphs differ. `IsFar G P ε` requires every graph with property `P` to differ from `G` in more than `ε n²` pairs. Goldreich normalizes by the `n²` ordered matrix entries, so his proximity `ε` is `IsFar G P (ε / 2)` here. `IsOneSidedTester T P ε q` bundles a worst-case query bound (`ProbOracleComp.IsQueryBounded`), acceptance probability one on `P` and acceptance probability at most 1/3 on `ε`-far graphs. `OneSidedTestable P q` asks for such a tester with `q ε` queries for every `ε > 0` and every `n`. `IsFar.farFromTriangleFree` and `isFar_of_farFromTriangleFree` compare `IsFar` with Mathlib's deletion distance `SimpleGraph.FarFromTriangleFree`. `isOneSidedTester_repeatAll` amplifies a perfectly complete test that rejects far graphs with probability at least `δ` by `k` repetitions with `2 ≤ δ k`. A new tester is written with `queryAdj` and `ProbOracleComp.sample`. `evalWith_queryAdj`, `adjOracle_apply` and the general `ProbOracleComp.evalWith_sumHandler_unifOracle_sample` evaluate its steps, `IsOneSidedTester.evalWith_false_eq_zero` says that a one-sided tester never rejects a graph with the property, `acceptProb_repeatAll` and `isQueryBounded_repeatAll` handle repetition with `OracleComp.repeatAll`, and `IsFar.deleteFar` and `isFar_of_deleteFar` compare `IsFar` with Mathlib's `DeleteFar` for any property.

The [triangle tester](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/DenseGraph/TriangleFree.lean) samples `k` uniform ordered vertex triples and queries their three pairs. It rejects when one sampled triple is a triangle. `acceptProb_triangleTester` gives its exact acceptance probability `(1 - 6 t / n³) ^ k` on a graph with `t` triangles. Mathlib's triangle removal lemma gives at least `triangleRemovalBound ε · n³` triangles in an `ε`-far graph. Hence `isOneSidedTester_triangleTester`: for `ε > 0` and `1 ≤ 3 · triangleRemovalBound ε · k`, the tester is one-sided with `3k` queries. `oneSidedTestable_cliqueFree_three` states the resulting bound `3 ⌈(3 · triangleRemovalBound ε)⁻¹⌉`, independent of `n`. Its constant is the tower-type constant of the regularity lemma. The theorems count queries, not running time. General `H`-freeness remains open. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/DenseGraphTesting.lean) include a far graph on three vertices and a one-sided tester with explicit parameters (five trials, 15 queries, proximity 1/10). They also show that one trial is too few.

The [bipartiteness tester](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/DenseGraph/Bipartite.lean) follows Goldreich–Goldwasser–Ron. `bipartiteTester n s m` samples `s` uniform vertices `u` and `m` uniform vertex pairs `(x k, y k)`, independently. It queries the `m (2 s + 1)` pairs `(x k, u i)`, `(y k, u i)` and `(x k, y k)`, and accepts when the sample positions admit a proper 2-colouring (`bipAccept`). Colours are attached to positions, so a vertex sampled twice may receive two colours. This is laxer than asking that the observed subgraph be bipartite: with `s = 0` and `m = 3` the tester can observe a triangle and still accept. `acceptProb_bipartiteTester_eq_one` is perfect completeness: a 2-colouring of the graph induces one of the sample positions. `IsFar.two_mul_lt_sum_monochromatic` states that a graph `ε`-far from bipartite has more than `2 ε n²` ordered monochromatic adjacent pairs under every 2-colouring. Soundness, `acceptProb_bipartiteTester_le_one_third`, assumes `12 ≤ ε s` and `s + 3 ≤ ε m`. In its proof, a colouring of `u` forces the colour of every vertex with a neighbour in `u`. Markov's inequality bounds the probability of a large degree mass outside the neighbourhood of `u` by `2 / (ε (s + 1))`. This expectation bound replaces the coverage step of Goldreich–Goldwasser–Ron (§6.1, Theorem 6.1.2 and Claims 6.1.4 and 6.1.6), which uses a sample of `O(log (1 / (ε δ)) / ε)` vertices and `O(log² (1 / (ε δ)) / ε³)` queries. The refinement removes the logarithmic factors. It is our adaptation, with no exact source and no claim of novelty. A union bound over the `2 ^ s` colourings of `u` bounds the rest by `2 ^ s (1 - ε) ^ m`. `isOneSidedTester_bipartiteTester` bundles these with the query bound, and `oneSidedTestable_isBipartite` fixes `s = ⌈12 / ε⌉` and `m = ⌈(s + 3) / ε⌉`. `bipartiteQueryBound_le` bounds the resulting query count by `459 / ε³` for `0 < ε ≤ 1`. That is polynomial in `1 / ε` and independent of `n`, but weaker than the `Õ(ε⁻²)` queries that follow from the `Õ(1 / ε)`-vertex sample analysis of Alon and Krivelevich. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/DenseGraphTestingBipartite.lean) show that the triangle is `1/10`-far from bipartite and is rejected by the tester with `s = 120` and `m = 1230`. They also show that `s = m = 0` accepts every graph, and they evaluate the query bound at `ε = 1/10` (296430 queries).

[Canonical testers](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/PropertyTesting/DenseGraph/Canonical.lean) formalize the one-sided half of Goldreich–Trevisan's Theorem 2 (Theorem 4.5). A property `P` of graphs on `Fin n` is `IsInvariant` when it is closed under relabelling. Then `IsFar.comap` (their Claim 2.1, from `editDist_comap_equiv`) says that relabelling preserves the distance to `P`. `canonicalTester n s hs D` samples a uniform permutation `σ`, queries all pairs among `σ 0, …, σ (s - 1)` (`queryInduced`) and accepts exactly when the induced labelled graph on `Fin s` has property `D`. It makes `s.choose 2` queries (`isQueryBounded_canonicalTester`), and `acceptProb_canonicalTester` gives its acceptance probability as an event over `σ`. `InducedOf P K` says that `K` is an induced subgraph (`K ⊴ H`) of some graph `H` with property `P`; it is itself invariant (`isInvariant_inducedOf`). `acceptProb_canonicalTester_eq_one_iff` shows that a canonical tester is perfectly complete on `P` exactly when `D` contains `InducedOf P`. The main theorem, `isOneSidedTester_canonicalTester`, takes a one-sided tester with `q` queries for an invariant `P` (adaptive or not). It gives the canonical tester with `min (2q) n` sample vertices and decision property `InducedOf P`, which is one-sided for `P` with the same proximity and `(2q).choose 2` queries. `OneSidedTestable.canonical` states this for families. The proof runs the original tester against a lazily sampled relabelling (`Cslib.RandomPermutation`) and uses induction on the query bound in place of the paper's vertex-uncovering tester. The proximity enters only through `IsFar.comap`, so the result does not depend on how the distance is normalized. Limitations: the two-sided half (`O(q)` sample vertices after error reduction and their Lemma 4.4) is not formalized. The decision property `InducedOf P` depends on `n`, as the paper allows. Deciding by `P` itself (their §4.2.4, Proposition D.2 and Corollary 5.9) is not addressed. The theorems count queries, not running time. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/DenseGraphTestingCanonical.lean) show that triangle-freeness is invariant and derive the canonical triangle tester from `oneSidedTestable_cliqueFree_three`. On three vertices the canonical tester rejects the triangle with certainty and accepts the empty graph. Of two trivial decision properties, rejecting everything is not perfectly complete and accepting everything is. With two sample vertices the triangle is accepted with certainty, so that tester is not sound. For the labelled property "`0` and `1` are adjacent", which is not invariant and has a one-query one-sided tester, the canonical tester accepts the far empty graph with certainty, so the invariance hypothesis is needed.

## Known Limitations

The examples use semantic hypotheses and real probability/analysis. These results provide formal sample/query guarantees and formal bridges to PAC/query models. Claims of polynomial native training time or succinct executable output hypotheses require separate resource theorems. Keep these limitations in view when choosing a learning model: the notions of efficiency differ.

## Replicable algorithms

Shared-seed replicable sample algorithms ([Impagliazzo–Lei–Pitassi–Sorrell 2022](../sources.md#ilps22), formalized in [`Basic.lean`](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Replicable/Basic.lean)) capture randomized algorithms `Algorithm X Ω α m` whose shared seed `r : Ω` fixes a common source of randomness across two independent runs on fresh i.i.d. `m`-sample draws from a distribution `D : PMF X`.

* **Core interface and `ProbComp` programs**: [`Basic.lean`](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Replicable/Basic.lean) defines `Algorithm`, `ProbComp`-backed [`Program`](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Replicable/Basic.lean) (whose `Program.toAlgorithm` lowers `seed` and `run` semantically via `ProbComp.eval`), weak family-restricted replicability `IsReplicableFor`, distribution-free strong replicability `IsReplicable`, and accuracy predicates `IsAccurateFor`, `EstimatesFor`, and `Estimates`.
* **Composition**: [`Composition.lean`](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Replicable/Composition.lean) proves additive replicability preservation under sequential pairing (`seqPair`), sequential binding (`seqBind`), product families (`pi`), and `k`-fold replication (`replicate`).
* **Randomized rounding**: [`Rounding.lean`](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Replicable/Rounding.lean) provides discrete randomized threshold rounding (`thresholdRound`, ideal real-arithmetic `thresholdProg`), periodic offset-grid rounding (`roundGrid`, `roundGridProg`), continuous `[0, 1)` Lebesgue-measure equivalents, and generic concentration-to-replicability theorems (`isReplicableFor_roundGridProg_of_concentrates`, `isAccurateFor_roundGridProg_of_concentrates`).
* **Confidence and replicability amplification**: [`Amplification.lean`](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Replicable/Amplification.lean) formalizes `majorityVote` and `medianOfRuns` confidence amplification (`β₀ < 1 / 2`), finite-domain heavy-hitter amplification (`amplifyFinite`) yielding strong `ρ`-replicability from accuracy `β₀ < 1 / (|α| + 1)`, and general-domain collision-threshold amplification (`amplifyReplicability`) boosting any `ρ₀ < 1 / 2` over `[DecidableEq α]` while preserving `(good, β)` accuracy (`isAccurateFor_amplifyReplicability`, `exists_amplifyReplicability_accurate`) whenever the input algorithm satisfies an `O(β / T)` initial-error bound (since `pickFirstThresholded` selects a single passing candidate seed rather than amplifying error from `≈ 1 / 2`).
* **Bounded-mean and coin bias clients**: [`BiasEstimation.lean`](../../CslibFrontier/MachineLearning/Replicable/BiasEstimation.lean) defines the `ProbComp`-backed ideal real-arithmetic programs `replicableMeanProg` and `replicableBiasProg`, combining Hoeffding concentration with `roundGridProg` to obtain strong `ρ`-replicability and `(ε, β)`-accuracy under `Program.toAlgorithm`.

Checked counterexamples (including exact `disagreeProb = 1 / 2` on a fair coin and weak-vs-strong replicability separation) and concrete instantiations live in [`Replicable.lean`](../../CslibFrontierTests/Replicable.lean). Note on scope: `Program` here interprets `ProbComp` computations into semantic `Algorithm`s (`Program.toAlgorithm`); `thresholdProg`, `roundGridProg`, `replicableMeanProg`, and `replicableBiasProg` are `noncomputable` ideal real-arithmetic specifications (using arbitrary real estimators, real comparisons, and real floor), so finite-representation/precision realizations and native computational resource bounds remain open.
