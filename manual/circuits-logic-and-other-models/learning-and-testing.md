# Learning and property testing

The learning development makes a useful distinction between statistical/sample complexity, membership-query complexity and native running time. Its main local algorithms prove the first two. They should not be described as computational PAC learners merely because their sample or query bounds are polynomial or quasi-polynomial. [complexitylib][complexitylib]

## Upstream PAC and local uniform learning

Upstream [ConceptClass](https://github.com/leanprover/cslib/blob/254e30e6d4299c51323aaeaf5f51a4763b375d4a/Cslib/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Defs.lean#L121) defines a concept class as a set of functions and a learner as a function from an indexed labelled sample to a hypothesis. Success probabilities and excess error are measured over an explicit family of input/label distributions. It provides deterministic and internally randomized PAC notions and sample-complexity operators. The `R` in `IsRPACLearnerFor` denotes randomized, not realizable; realizability is imposed through the chosen distribution family. A learner is not required to be an encoded algorithm in this definition. [cslib][cslib]

Local [uniformExamples](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/Basic.lean#L91) specializes to Boolean functions on a finite cube and independent uniform examples. `uniformError` is the fraction of cube inputs on which the hypothesis differs from the target. `Learns` bounds this error with failure probability delta after a prescribed number of labelled samples. Its bridge to upstream PAC instantiates the uniform realizable family; it does not establish distribution-free learning.

The [low-degree Fourier learner](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/LowDegree.lean) estimates low-degree coefficients from examples and thresholds the resulting polynomial. With N retained coefficients, its sample condition is of the form `m >= (4N/epsilon) log(2N/delta)` together with a Fourier-tail assumption. The [AC0 learning theorem](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/AC0.lean) combines this learner with Linial–Mansour–Nisan concentration, giving quasi-polynomial sample bounds for the stated bounded-depth/size circuit class. The local exponent is the implementation's quantitative version, with its circuit-depth convention; native Fourier arithmetic and hypothesis-evaluation time are not part of this theorem.

The upstream randomized variant, [IsRPACLearnerFor](https://github.com/leanprover/cslib/blob/254e30e6d4299c51323aaeaf5f51a4763b375d4a/Cslib/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Defs.lean#L181), chooses a sample-to-hypothesis learner using an internal probability space and bounds the expected sample-failure probability, with the stated almost-everywhere measurability condition. This is a separate semantic randomization layer from random training examples.

## Distribution-free realizable PAC learning

Realizable PAC learning over arbitrary distributions uses the upstream deterministic notion `IsPACLearnerFor` with the family [realizableDistributions](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/PACLearning/Realizable.lean) of joint distributions on which some concept of the class has zero error. That module is a copy of the open CSLib pull request #858. [RealizableSample](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/PACLearning/RealizableSample.lean) shows that i.i.d. samples from such a distribution are almost surely realizable. It follows that a learner which is consistent in the upstream `VersionSpace` sense almost surely outputs a concept agreeing with the whole sample. [ERM](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/PACLearning/ERM.lean) defines empirical risk minimization through the upstream `empiricalMiscount` and proves that every ERM learner is consistent on realizable samples.

[FiniteClass](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/PACLearning/FiniteClass.lean) proves the Occam bound for a finite class `H`, following Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Corollary 2.3. Every consistent learner, in particular every ERM learner, fails to reach error `ε` with probability at most `|H| (1 - ε)^m`, hence at most `δ` once `log |H| + log (1/δ) ≤ ε m`. The corollaries give `IsPACLearnerFor` over `realizableDistributions H` and the sample-complexity bound `⌈(log |H| + log (1/δ))/ε⌉`. The probability statements assume that each hypothesis has a measurable misclassification set. `measurableSet_misclassified` supplies this when the hypotheses are measurable and the label type has measurable equality, for example `Bool`. The learners are semantic maps: no running time or hypothesis representation is claimed.

[GrowthFunction](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/PACLearning/GrowthFunction.lean) defines `restriction`, the set of labelings a binary class induces on a sequence of `m` points, and the growth function, the maximum size of that set. It proves Sauer's lemma, `∑_{i ≤ d} (m choose i)`, for any class whose shattered finite sets have at most `d` points; the combinatorial core is Mathlib's Sauer–Shelah lemma for set families. It also proves the forms `(m + 1)^d` and `(e m / d)^d` for `d ≤ m`, and `2^m` on a shattered set. The general binomial bound `∑_{i ≤ d} (n choose i) ≤ (n + 1)^d` lives in [Foundations/Data/Nat/Choose/Sum](../../Complexity/Foundations/Data/Nat/Choose/Sum.lean), which the low-degree Boolean analysis also uses. [PACLearning/VCDimension](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/PACLearning/VCDimension.lean) adds two facts about the upstream `vcDim`: a shattered finite set has at most `vcDim C` points when `HasFiniteVCDim C` (`SetShatters.card_le_vcDim`), and every class on a finite domain has finite VC dimension (`hasFiniteVCDim_of_finite`).

[EpsilonNet](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/PACLearning/EpsilonNet.lean) proves the ε-net theorem by double-sample symmetrization. The measure-theoretic steps are reusable and live in [DoubleSample](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/PACLearning/DoubleSample.lean). `swapSamples σ` exchanges chosen coordinates between two samples and preserves `μ^m × μ^m` on any measurable space with σ-finite `μ` (`measurePreserving_swapSamples`). If at most `2^m K` of the `2^m` swaps of every double sample land in a measurable set `J`, then `J` has probability at most `K` (`measure_prod_le_of_card_filter_swapSamples_mem_le`). The ghost-sample bound `ν B ≤ 2 (ν × ν') J` holds when every `S ∈ B` extends into `J` with probability at least `1/2` (`measure_le_two_mul_measure_prod`). A sample is an ε-net for a class `G` under `μ` if it meets every `g ∈ G` of mass at least `ε` (`IsEpsNet`). For `εm ≥ 8`, `m` i.i.d. samples fail to be an ε-net with probability at most `2 Π_G(2m) / 2^⌈εm/2⌉` (`measure_pi_not_isEpsNet_mul_two_pow_le`). With Sauer's lemma, `vcSampleSize d ε δ = (8/ε)(d log (48/ε) + log (4/δ))` samples bring this below `δ` (`measure_pi_not_isEpsNet_le_of_vcSampleSize_le`). Here `d` bounds the size of every finite set shattered by the class; `hasFiniteVCDim_iff` supplies such a `d` from `HasFiniteVCDim`. [VCUpperBound](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/PACLearning/VCUpperBound.lean) applies the theorem to the loss class of `C`. Every consistent learner, in particular every ERM learner, then reaches error `ε` with confidence `1 - δ` from `vcSampleSize d ε δ` examples over every distribution realizable by `C` (`IsConsistent.measure_pi_lt_error_le_of_vcSampleSize_le`). This is the VC upper bound of Blumer, Ehrenfeucht, Haussler and Warmuth (Theorem 2.1) with different constants of the same form. The corollaries are `isPACLearnerFor_realizableDistributions_of_vcSampleSize_le`, the realizable sample-complexity bound `⌈vcSampleSize (vcDim C) ε δ⌉` (`sampleComplexity_realizableDistributions_le_of_hasFiniteVCDim`), and `isPACLearnable_realizableDistributions_of_hasFiniteVCDim` for every class of finite VC dimension. These results assume a countable domain with measurable singletons and `Bool` labels, so that every set of samples is measurable. General domains need a measurability ("well-behaved") hypothesis on the class; issue #2868 tracks that case. The matching EHKV lower bound `Ω(d/ε)` is being developed upstream in CSLib.

Complete clients: [RealizablePAC.lean](../../ComplexityTests/RealizablePAC.lean) discharges the measurability side condition with `measurableSet_misclassified (measurable_of_countable _)`. It also checks the realizable and non-realizable distributions, ERM and non-consistent learners, Occam and VC sample-complexity bounds for small parameters, a tight Sauer bound for thresholds, and the swap-counting and ghost-sample bounds on one-point samples.

## Agnostic PAC learning

The upstream `IsPACLearnerFor` already measures success against `optimalError D C`, the best error in the class, so agnostic PAC learning is `IsPACLearnerFor` over the family `Set.univ` of all distributions; no realizability is assumed. [UniformConvergence](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/PACLearning/UniformConvergence.lean) defines `IsRepresentative D C ε S`: the empirical error of every `h ∈ C` on `S` is within `ε` of its true error (Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David, Definition 4.1). Hoeffding's inequality for one hypothesis (`measure_pi_le_abs_empiricalMiscount_div_sub_error`) and a union bound give uniform convergence for a finite class: `m` i.i.d. examples fail to be `ε`-representative with probability at most `|H| · 2 exp (-2 m ε²)` (`measure_pi_not_isRepresentative_le`). On an `ε / 2`-representative sample every empirical risk minimizer has error at most `optimalError D C + ε` (`IsRepresentative.error_le_optimalError_add`, Lemma 4.2, for any class). [AgnosticFiniteClass](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/PACLearning/AgnosticFiniteClass.lean) combines them into Corollary 4.6. Under every distribution, an ERM learner for a finite class `H` misses `optimalError D H + ε` with probability at most `δ` once `2 log (2 |H| / δ) ≤ ε² m` (`IsERM.measure_pi_lt_error_le_of_log_le`). The corollaries are `isPACLearnerFor_univ_of_log_le`, the agnostic sample-complexity bound `⌈2 log (2 |H| / δ) / ε²⌉` (`sampleComplexity_univ_le_of_finite`) and `isPACLearnable_univ_of_finite`. The first two assume `[Nonempty (α → β)]`: for empty `H` the sample-size condition already holds at `m = 0`, and a learner from zero examples exists only if some hypothesis `α → β` does. The domain and labels are arbitrary measurable spaces; as in the realizable case, each hypothesis needs a measurable misclassification set, and the learners are semantic maps. The agnostic VC bound for infinite classes is tracked by issue #3005.

Complete client: [AgnosticPAC.lean](../../ComplexityTests/AgnosticPAC.lean) uses a distribution that labels one point both ways. No class realizes it, and the two constant hypotheses have optimal error `1 / 2` under it. The file checks a representative sample and a non-representative one. It also places the agnostic sample complexity of the constants at `(1/2, 1/2)` in `[1, 17]`.

## Membership-query learners

[QueryLearner](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/MembershipQuery.lean#L76) takes a random seed to a decision tree. A queried coordinate is itself an input point for the unknown target function, and a leaf is a hypothesis function. `QueriesAtMost` bounds tree depth for every seed. This represents adaptive membership queries directly, while nonadaptive point lists are a specialization.

Uniform random examples can be simulated by querying independently sampled points, with exact distributional correctness. The [Kushilevitz–Mansour development](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/MachineLearning/UniformLearning/KushilevitzMansour.lean) reuses a nonadaptive sample set across stages, with the explicit query bound `m(2n+3)` and a sample schedule depending on spectral norm, error and confidence. Bounded-leaf decision trees supply a concrete learnable class through a spectral-norm bound. These statements charge oracle queries only; they do not bound the time or finite representation needed for all Fourier operations and output hypotheses.

## Statistical queries

The [statistical query model](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/StatisticalQuery/Basic.lean) follows Kearns's Definition 3 on a finite domain `X` with a fixed distribution `D : PMF X`. A `Query X` is a function `X → Bool → ℝ` with values in `[-1, 1]`, so Kearns's `{0, 1}` predicates and Reyzin's `±1` queries are special cases. A learner is a deterministic `OracleComp (spec X) (X → Bool)` program whose calls are queries answered by reals. `OracleComp.IsQueryBounded` bounds its query count. `Learns A D C τ ε` requires error at most `ε` for every target in `C` and every answer sequence within `τ` of the true expectations, including adaptive answers (`OracleComp.supportWith`). `LearnsStateless` only quantifies over fixed answer functions. It is the weaker notion (`Learns.learnsStateless`), so lower bounds are stated for it. There is a single tolerance and no confidence parameter. A learner whose per-query tolerances are all at least `τ` is covered by the single tolerance `τ`.

The [lower bounds](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/StatisticalQuery/LowerBound.lean) use Szörényi's argument as presented by Reyzin. `LearnsStateless.card_le_of_abs_corr_le` takes concepts `f i ∈ C` with pairwise `|corr D (f i) (f j)| ≤ γ`, a learner using at most `q` queries, `γ < τ²` and `γ < (1 - 2ε)²`. It bounds the number of concepts by `q (1-γ)/(τ²-γ) + (1-γ)/((1-2ε)²-γ)`. `card_le_of_corr_le` assumes only a one-sided correlation bound and doubles the first term. With `γ = 1/d`, so that the statistical query dimension is at least `d`, `card_mul_sq_sub_one_le_two_mul_add_one` takes Reyzin's Theorem 12 hypothesis of accuracy `τ` (`1 - 2ε ≥ τ`) and gives `d τ² - 1 ≤ 2q + 1`. Its exact-learning special case `ε = 0`, `card_mul_sq_sub_one_le_two_mul`, gives Reyzin's query count `d τ² - 1 ≤ 2q`. The [parity corollary](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/StatisticalQuery/Parity.lean) `LearnsStateless.two_pow_card_le_of_parityOn_mem` gives `2^n ≤ q/τ² + 1/(1-2ε)²` for any class containing all parities on `n` bits under the uniform distribution. [ComplexityTests/StatisticalQuery.lean](../../ComplexityTests/StatisticalQuery.lean) has a concrete one-query learner, an edge case where tolerance `1` fails, and parity instances.

The [simulation](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/StatisticalQuery/Simulation.lean) runs a learner on random examples in the block form of Reyzin's Section 2.1. `simulate n blocks A` answers the `i`-th call `φ` by the empirical mean `empirical φ (blocks i)` of `φ` on the `i`-th block of `m` labelled examples. `toOuterMeasure_simulate_notMem_le` is the union bound along an adaptive run: each call is answered from a block independent of the earlier ones, whatever the earlier answers were. It applies to any program with a query budget, any per-query set of acceptable answers and a uniform per-query failure bound `β`, giving `n β` for `n` queries. Hoeffding's inequality for an empirical average under an arbitrary finite distribution (`PMF.pi_abs_sum_div_sub_sum_mul_ge_le` in [Concentration](../../Complexity/Probability/Concentration.lean)) gives `toOuterMeasure_empirical_notMem_valid_le`. Together they give `Learns.toOuterMeasure_lt_disagreement_simulate_le`: a learner with tolerance `τ ≥ 0`, error `ε` and at most `q` queries, run on `q` independent blocks of `m` examples of a target in `C`, has error above `ε` with probability at most `2 q exp(-m τ² / 2)`. `Learns.isPACLearnerFor` packages the learner `simulateLearner A q m` on `q m` examples, split into blocks along `finProdFinEquiv` (`PMF.pi_map_finProdFinEquiv` in [Product/Reindex](../../Complexity/Probability/Product/Reindex.lean)). It is an `IsPACLearnerFor (q * m) ε δ C (realizable D C)` witness whenever `2 q exp(-m τ² / 2) ≤ δ`. This condition needs a positive tolerance. For `τ > 0` it holds once `m ≥ 2 log(2q/δ) / τ²`. At `τ = 0` it reads `2 q ≤ δ < 1`, so it applies only to learners making no queries. `realizable D C` contains the distributions of a `D`-random point labelled by a concept of `C`, so this is fixed-distribution PAC learning. Under the uniform distribution it is `UniformLearning.uniformRealizable C` by definition.

The [low-degree algorithm](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/StatisticalQuery/LowDegree.lean) `lowDegree ι t` is a non-adaptive learner. Through `askAll` it asks `charQuery S`, `(x, y) ↦ signVal y · χ_S(x)`, for each of the `N` sets `S` of at most `t` coordinates, so it makes `N` queries (`isQueryBounded_lowDegree`), and returns the sign of `∑ c_S χ_S` for the answers `c_S`. Under the uniform distribution, `expect` of `charQuery S` is the Fourier coefficient on `S`, and `disagreement` is `UniformLearning.uniformError`. `learns_lowDegree` gives error `N τ² + κ` for every class whose Fourier weight above degree `t` is at most `κ`. `learns_lowDegree_parityOn` gives error `N τ²` for the parities on at most `t` coordinates, in contrast to the lower bound for all parities. [ComplexityTests/StatisticalQuerySimulation.lean](../../ComplexityTests/StatisticalQuerySimulation.lean) runs the one-query learner on two concrete blocks, giving different hypotheses. It checks a 40-example PAC instance and the low-degree learner on three bits, including the PAC learner it yields.

These are information-theoretic bounds on query count, tolerance and sample size. They do not charge running time, and the learners are deterministic. Simulation under random classification noise (Kearns's Lemma 2 and Theorem 3), the single-sample form of Kearns's Theorem 1 (with its finite-query-space and finite-VC-dimension sample bounds), and a distribution-free form are not yet formalized.

## Online learning with experts

The [multiplicative-update core](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MultiplicativeUpdate.lean) fixes the experts-setting conventions. Experts form a finite type, losses are an arbitrary real sequence `ℓ : ℕ → ι → ℝ`, and an update factor `φ` gives expert `i` the weight `∏ s < t, φ (ℓ s i)`; the learner plays `prob φ ℓ t`, the normalized weights, and pays `∑ i, prob φ ℓ t i * ℓ t i`. The distribution in round `t` depends only on earlier losses (`prob_congr`), and `adaptiveLoss_eq` shows the bounds cover adversaries that see the current distribution. Every analysis passes through the single potential inequality `sum_log_le_log_card_add`.

[Multiplicative weights](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MultiplicativeWeights.lean) (`mwFactor η x = 1 - η x`) has the Arora–Hazan–Kale regret bound `mw_regret_le` with its exact constants, the mixed-strategy form, a second-order variant and the tuned `2 √(T ln n)` bound. [Hedge](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/Hedge.lean) (`exp (-η x)`) has the bound with the learner's expected squared loss and its tuned `2 √(T ln n)` form. [Weighted majority](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/WeightedMajority.lean) proves `M(T) ≤ 2 (1 + η) mᵢ(T) + 2 ln n / η` for every tie-breaking rule, stated as a hypothesis on the predictions; the concrete rule `weightedMajority` satisfies it.

The hypotheses decide which statement applies. Multiplicative weights needs `0 < η ≤ 1 / 2` and losses in `[-1, 1]` (the second-order variant needs only `η ℓ ≤ 1 / 2`), and its tuned bound needs `n ≥ 2` and `T ≥ 4 ln n`. Hedge needs `0 < η ≤ 1` and losses in `[-1, 1]` (or only `η ℓ ≥ -1`), and its tuned bound needs `n ≥ 2` and `T ≥ ln n`. Weighted majority needs `0 < η ≤ 1 / 2`. The zero-sum theorems need entries in `[0, 1]`, `0 < ε ≤ 1`, `T > 0` with `ε ^ 2 T ≥ 4 ln n`, and columns that best respond to the learner's distribution in every round. The declarations live in the namespace `Cslib.MachineLearning.OnlineLearning.Experts`.

[Zero-sum games](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/ZeroSum.lean) run multiplicative weights on the rows against best-responding columns. After `T ≥ 4 ln n / ε ^ 2` rounds, the average row distribution and the empirical column distribution are `ε`-optimal, and the average payoff lies in `[v, v + ε]`. The value `v` is [IsMinimaxValue](../../Complexity/Probability/Minimax/Value.lean), whose existence comes from the exact Sion-based minimax theorem in `Probability/Minimax`; `sum_averageProb_mul_le_add_of_forall_exists_sum_le` is the explicit `ε`-approximate counterpart of that theorem. The best-response property is a hypothesis, so any oracle can be used; `bestResponsePlay` (defined by choice) shows it is satisfiable. [Checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/OnlineLearning.lean) compute distributions, solve matching pennies, and show the mistake bound failing without the weighted-majority hypothesis.

All of these are real-valued statements about the defined update rules. They do not charge arithmetic, sampling or oracle calls, and randomized weighted majority is not yet modelled as a probabilistic computation.

## Online learning in the mistake-bound model

The [mistake-bound core](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Basic.lean) formalizes Littlestone's realizable model. A deterministic `OnlineLearner α` predicts a `Bool` label from the labelled history and the current instance. `mistakes A S` counts its wrong predictions on a finite labelled sequence `S`. `Realizable C S` asks that some concept of the upstream `ConceptClass` agree with all of `S`. `mistakeBound A C : ℕ∞` is the supremum of the mistakes over realizable sequences, and `optimalMistakeBound C` is the infimum over learners. `mistakeBound_eq_iSup_adversary` shows that adaptive adversaries choosing instances and labels from the history add nothing for deterministic learners, and `mistakeBound_eq_iSup_concept` gives Littlestone's form with a fixed target. This model has no experts and no real-valued losses: the learner pays for each mistake against the realizability requirement. The non-realizable extension, with randomized learners and regret against the best concept, is described below under randomized learners and agnostic online learning.

[Mistake trees](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Littlestone.lean) are defined by recursion on their height (`HasMistakeTree`); `hasMistakeTree_iff_exists_tree` relates them to explicit instance trees. `littlestoneDim` takes values in `ℕ∞` and is `0` for the empty class (Littlestone uses `-1`). The adversary lower bound is `littlestoneDim_le_mistakeBound`. The [standard optimal algorithm](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/SOA.lean) matches it, so `optimalMistakeBound_eq_littlestoneDim` holds for every class, finite or not. [Halving](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Halving.lean) bounds finite classes by `⌊log₂ |C|⌋`. [VC dimension](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/VCDimension.lean) is at most the Littlestone dimension (`vcDim_le_littlestoneDim`). Because upstream `vcDim` is `0` for classes of infinite VC dimension, the meaningful statement for those is `littlestoneDim_eq_top_of_not_hasFiniteVCDim`. [Thresholds](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Thresholds.lean) show the gap: VC dimension `1`, Littlestone dimension `⌊log₂ (N + 1)⌋` for `θ ≤ N`, and `⊤` for all thresholds on `ℕ`.

The [perceptron](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Perceptron.lean) is an `OnlineLearner` on any real inner product space. Novikoff's bound `mistakes_perceptron_mul_sq_le` is stated per sequence, with margin `γ ≥ 0` and radius `R` as hypotheses on the presented examples. [Checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/MistakeBound.lean) run halving and the perceptron, compute the threshold gap, and show the bound failing for a negative margin.

[WINNOW1](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Winnow.lean) learns monotone disjunctions `monotoneDisjunction R` on `Fin n → Bool`. Its weights are real numbers, the update factor `α` and threshold `θ` are real parameters, and `tie` fixes the prediction when the score equals `θ`, which the source leaves open. Littlestone's Theorem 7 is `mistakes_winnow_le`: for every sequence labelled by a disjunction of `k` variables, with `α > 1` and `α θ ≥ 1`, there are at most `α k (log_α θ + 1) + n / θ` mistakes, for every choice of `tie`. The [lower bound](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/MonotoneDisjunctions.lean) builds the shattered set of Littlestone's Lemma 6, giving `k ⌊log₂ (n / k)⌋` as a lower bound on both the VC dimension and the optimal mistake bound of `monotoneDisjunctions n k`. For `1 ≤ k ≤ n`, WINNOW1 with `α = 2` and `θ = n / k` makes at most `2 k log₂ (n / k) + 3 k` mistakes (`mistakeBound_winnow_two_div_le`, hence `optimalMistakeBound_monotoneDisjunctions_le`), so for `1 ≤ k` and `2 k ≤ n` the optimal bound is `Θ(k log (n / k))`. The hypothesis `1 ≤ k` is needed: for `k = 0` the threshold is `0`, and WINNOW1 can err on the constant-false target. For `k > n` the class is empty. The source's second lower bound, stated in terms of `k (1 + log₂ (n / k))`, is not formalized. [Checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/Winnow.lean) run WINNOW1 through a promotion step and an elimination step and evaluate both bounds for `n = 8`, `k = 2`.

[Randomized learners](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Randomized.lean) map the history and the instance to a `PMF Bool`. Against a labelled sequence fixed in advance (an oblivious adversary), `expectedMistakes A S` sums the probabilities of wrong predictions; deterministic learners embed with `expectedMistakes = mistakes`. `hedgeLearner η E` draws one of finitely many deterministic experts with the Hedge distribution of the [multiplicative-update core](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MultiplicativeUpdate.lean) for their 0-1 losses, recomputed from the history. `expectedMistakes_hedgeLearner_le` transfers `hedge_regret_le_sqrt`: with the tuned rate `η = √(log N / T)` for `N` experts, on every sequence of at most `T` rounds and with `log N ≤ T`, the expected mistakes exceed those of every expert by at most `2 √(T log N)`.

[Agnostic online learning](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/MistakeBound/Agnostic.lean) drops realizability: the labels are arbitrary and the learner competes with the best concept of `C`. Following Ben-David, Pál and Shalev-Shwartz (COLT 2009, §3.2, Algorithm 3), the expert `flipExpert (soa C) I` runs SOA on its own predictions, negated at the rounds in `I`. For each concept `c ∈ C`, negating at SOA's mistakes on the `c`-labelling (at most `littlestoneDim C` rounds) reproduces `c` exactly (`mistakes_flipExpert_mistakeRounds`; the source's Lemma 12 and Corollary 13). `agnosticSOA C T d` (Algorithm 4) runs Hedge over the at most `(T + 1)^d` sets of at most `d` rounds below `T`. `expectedMistakes_agnosticSOA_le`, the upper half of the source's Theorem 1 in its Littlestone-dimension form, bounds its expected mistakes by those of any `c ∈ C` plus `2 √(T d log (T + 1))` when `littlestoneDim C ≤ d`. The source's constant is `√(½ d T ln T)`; the one here comes from the library's Hedge bound, and the source's `ln |C|` branch for finite classes is not stated. The learner needs `T` and `d` in advance. Cover's observation (1965, as cited by the source), `exists_mistakes_eq_length_of_const_mem`, shows that randomization is necessary: against `Adversary.cover`, which negates each prediction, every deterministic learner errs in all `n` rounds, while if `C` contains both constant classifiers one of them errs in at most `n / 2`. The matching lower bound `Ω(√(Ldim(C) T))` for randomized learners and adaptive adversaries are not formalized. [Checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/AgnosticOnline.lean) compare the two over `100` rounds for the two constant classifiers.

These are information-theoretic statements. `soa` decides emptiness and compares Littlestone dimensions classically, WINNOW1 updates real weights, the agnostic learner enumerates its experts and weighs them with real arithmetic, and no learner here has a charged running time.

## Online convex optimization

[Online gradient descent](../../Complexity/MachineLearning/OnlineLearning/OnlineGradientDescent.lean) plays points of a set `K` in a real inner product space. Its iterates are `x (t + 1) = proj (x t - η t • grad t (x t))`, where `grad : ℕ → E → E` is an arbitrary subgradient oracle. Rounds are indexed from `0`, so `η t` is the source's `η_{t+1}`. The projection is any map with `IsPythagoreanProjection K proj`: it lands in `K` and satisfies `‖proj y - z‖ ≤ ‖y - z‖` for `z ∈ K`. The [metric projection](../../Complexity/Foundations/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/MetricProjection.lean) onto a nonempty complete convex set is such a map; that module also proves the variational characterization, the fixed points and nonexpansiveness. Losses enter only through the subgradient inequality `f t x + ⟪grad t x, y - x⟫ ≤ f t y` on `K`, or its `α`-strongly convex strengthening. [First-order convexity](../../Complexity/Foundations/Analysis/Convex/FirstOrder.lean) derives both from Mathlib's `ConvexOn` and `StrongConvexOn` with `HasGradientWithinAt K` (so boundary points of `K` are allowed) or `HasGradientAt`.

Each regret bound holds against every comparator `u ∈ K`, under the hypotheses that `D` bounds the diameter of `K` and `G` bounds the subgradient norms on `K`. `ogd_regret_le_three_halves` is Hazan's Theorem 3.1: step sizes `D / (G √(t + 1))` give regret at most `3 / 2 · G D √T` for every `T`, assuming `D, G > 0`. `ogd_regret_le_zinkevich` is Zinkevich's Theorem 1, and `ogd_regret_le_fixed` gives `G D √T` (with `D, G > 0`) for a known horizon; both assume `T ≥ 1`. `ogd_regret_le_log` is Hazan's Theorem 3.3: for `α > 0`, `α`-strongly convex losses and step sizes `1 / (α (t + 1))` give `G² / (2α) · (1 + log T)`. It assumes no separate diameter bound, because its hypotheses already force `‖x - y‖ ≤ 2G / α` on `K` (`norm_sub_le_of_strongConvex`). OGD is deterministic, so these bounds also cover adaptive adversaries. [Checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/OnlineGradientDescent.lean) instantiate the bounds on `[-1, 1]` with linear and quadratic losses, and show maps that are not Pythagorean projections. As with the experts setting, nothing charges the cost of projections or arithmetic. Regularized follow-the-leader, mirror descent and the lower bounds are not formalized yet.

## Property testing as decision trees

The existing BLR local test compiles to a decision tree through `PCP.LocalTest.decisionTree`. [depth_decisionTree_repeatAll_blrTest_le](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/Linearity.lean#L52) proves that k independent repetitions use at most 3k queries, accept every parity with probability one and accept a function at uniform relative Hamming distance at least epsilon from every parity with probability at most `(1-epsilon)^k`.

The corresponding [one-sided lower bound](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/LinearityLowerBound.lean) shows that a tester of depth at most two that accepts every parity perfectly cannot reject the specified nonlinearity witness with value false at zero. This establishes the three-query boundary under those hypotheses. It is a lower bound for worst-case query depth, not expected queries, and it is formulated for arbitrary randomness support through the tested tree property.

## Distribution testing from samples

[IsTester](../../Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/Basic.lean) is the sample-access model. A `k`-sample tester for a set `P` of distributions on a finite type is a randomized rule `T : (Fin k → α) → PMF Bool`. It runs on `iid p k` and must accept every member of `P` with probability at least 2/3. It must accept with probability at most 1/3 every `p` whose statistical distance (`dist`, total variation) from every member of `P` is at least epsilon. The metric space on `PMF α` is a scoped instance, so `dist` needs `open Cslib.Probability.PMF` (or `open scoped Cslib.Probability.PMF`). Testers are semantic kernels: the theorems count samples, not running time. `one_third_le_dist_iid_bind` is Le Cam's method in mixture form, and `IsTester.map_equiv` relabels the domain. It rests on `abs_bind_apply_toReal_sub_le_dist` in [StatisticalDistance/Event](../../Complexity/Probability/StatisticalDistance/Event.lean): a randomized distinguisher's output probabilities move by at most the statistical distance.

The [collision tester](../../Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/UniformityCollision.lean) accepts when the number of colliding sample pairs is at most `C(k,2)(1+2ε²)/n`. Under `40 √n ≤ (k-1) ε²` and `0 < ε`, `isTester_collisionTester` proves it is a uniformity tester. The proof applies the finite Chebyshev inequality `sum_mul_ite_le_sum_mul_sq_div` from [Probability/PMF/Chebyshev](../../Complexity/Probability/PMF/Chebyshev.lean) to the exact mean and variance of `collisionCount` from [Probability/IID/CollisionCount](../../Complexity/Probability/IID/CollisionCount.lean). Paninski's [lower bound](../../Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/UniformityLowerBound.lean) `card_le_of_isTester_uniform` gives `n ≤ 52 k² δ⁴` for every tester with proximity `δ ≤ 1/2` on a domain of even size `n`. At `δ = 1/2` it is the birthday bound `4n ≤ 13k²`. The odd-size lower bound and tolerant testing remain open. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/DistributionTestingUniformity.lean) include a satisfiable instance of the sample hypothesis, a far distribution and an impossible single-sample tester.

[Identity testing](../../Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/IdentityReduction.lean) to a fixed distribution `q` on `n` points reduces to uniformity testing. `IsTester.bind_pi` in [Basic](../../Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/Basic.lean) runs a tester on samples passed independently through a randomized map `F`. It requires that `F` sends the property into the target property and far distributions to far distributions, and it rests on `iid_bind` in [Probability/IID](../../Complexity/Probability/IID.lean). Goldreich's kernel `identityKernel q` maps `q` exactly to uniform on the `4n` cells of `IdentityDomain q` (`bind_identityKernel_self`). It shrinks statistical distance by at most a factor 3 (`dist_le_three_mul_dist_bind_identityKernel`). Hence `isTester_identity_of_uniform`: a uniformity tester on the cells with proximity `ε/3` tests identity to `q` with proximity `ε` and the same number of samples. `exists_isTester_identity_of_uniform` accepts a uniformity tester on any domain of size `4n`. With the collision tester, `isTester_identityTester` needs `0 < ε` and `720 √n ≤ (k-1) ε²`. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/DistributionTestingIdentity.lean) compute block sizes, including a nonempty and an empty leftover block, and a satisfiable sample-size instance.

[Closeness testing](../../Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/ClosenessCollision.lean) compares two unknown distributions. In the two-sample model in [Basic](../../Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/Basic.lean), a rule `T : (Fin k → α) → (Fin k → α) → PMF Bool` gets `k` samples of each distribution, with acceptance probability `acceptProb₂ T p q`. `IsClosenessTester T ε` requires acceptance probability at least `2/3` when `p = q`, and at most `1/3` when `ε ≤ dist p q`. The collision statistic `closenessStatistic x y = C₁ + C₂ - ((k-1)/k) C₃` combines the self-collisions of each sample with the cross-collisions `crossCollisionCount` from [Probability/IID/CrossCollisionCount](../../Complexity/Probability/IID/CrossCollisionCount.lean). Its mean is `C(k,2) ‖p-q‖₂²` (`sum_sum_iid_mul_closenessStatistic`), and its variance has an exact closed form (`sum_sum_iid_mul_closenessStatistic_sub_sq`). When both collision probabilities are at most `b`, that closed form is bounded by `(k-1)(2k-1) b + 2(k-1)²(k+1) √b ‖p-q‖₄²` (`sum_sum_iid_mul_closenessStatistic_sub_sq_le_sqrt_sum_pow_four`, which implies DGPP's Lemma 10), and hence by the same expression with `‖p-q‖₂²` (`sum_sum_iid_mul_closenessStatistic_sub_sq_le`). The tester `closenessTester k ε` accepts when the statistic is at most `C(k,2) ε²/2`. `le_acceptProb₂_closenessTester` and `acceptProb₂_closenessTester_le` show it errs with probability at most `δ` on each side once `0 < δ` and `36 √b ≤ (k-1) ε² δ`. The hypotheses are the squared distances `‖p-q‖₂² ≤ ε²/4` and `ε² ≤ ‖p-q‖₂²`; for `0 < ε` this distinguishes `‖p-q‖₂ ≤ ε/2` from `‖p-q‖₂ ≥ ε`. `isClosenessTester_closenessTester` turns this into a closeness tester in statistical distance under `0 < ε` and `27 n ≤ (k-1) ε²`. That is `O(n/ε²)` samples, which is not optimal: learning both distributions empirically to statistical distance `ε/4` and comparing achieves the same rate. The optimal tester applies the `ℓ₂` tester after flattening. Statistical distance `dist` needs `open Cslib.Probability.PMF` (scoped instance). The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/DistributionTestingCloseness.lean) include exact cross-collision counts and means, an `ℓ₂` instance using `√b = 1/2`, and a closeness tester on `Bool` with `217` samples that rejects two distinct point masses.

[Flattening](../../Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/ClosenessFlattening.lean) gives the optimal closeness tester. The split distribution `split p c` in [Probability/Split](../../Complexity/Probability/Split.lean) divides each point `a` into `c a + 1` equal cells. It preserves statistical distance (`dist_split`). Its collision probability `∑ p(a)² / (c a + 1)` is antitone in `c` (`collisionProbability_split_antitone`). When `c` counts the occurrences in `m` independent samples of `p` itself, the expected collision probability is at most `1/(m+1)` (`sum_iid_mul_collisionProbability_split_le`). That rests on the inverse occurrence moment `(m+1) μ(a) E[1/(N+1)] ≤ 1` in [Probability/IID/Occurrence](../../Complexity/Probability/IID/Occurrence.lean), which also holds `occurrenceCount` and the splitting lemmas `sum_iid_succ_mul` and `sum_iid_add_mul` for independent draws.

`closenessFlatteningTester m k ε` draws `m + k` samples of each distribution. The first `m` of both set `c = flatteningCount x₁ y₁`, the combined occurrence counts, so the split domain has `n + 2m` points. The other `k` of each pass through the split kernel into `closenessTester k (2ε/√(n+2m))`. `acceptProb₂_append` and `acceptProb₂_bind_pi` in [Basic](../../Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/Distribution/Basic.lean) reduce the acceptance probability to an average, over the flattening samples, of the `ℓ₂` tester's acceptance probability on the split distributions (`acceptProb₂_closenessFlatteningTester`). Markov's inequality at collision level `16/(m+1)` and `four_mul_dist_sq_le_card_mul_sum_sq` (Cauchy–Schwarz, in [Probability/Collision](../../Complexity/Probability/Collision.lean)) then give `isClosenessTester_closenessFlatteningTester` under `0 < ε` and `864 (n + 2m) ≤ 5 (k-1) ε² √(m+1)`. With `m = ⌈min(n, n^{2/3} ε^{-4/3})⌉`, `exists_isClosenessTester_closenessFlatteningTester` gets `m + k ≤ 520 max(n^{2/3} ε^{-4/3}, √n ε^{-2}) + 3`. That is the Chan–Diakonikolas–Valiant–Valiant rate. The construction follows Diakonikolas–Kane Proposition 2.10 with two changes. It uses a fixed number of flattening samples instead of a Poisson number. It also flattens with samples of both distributions, so both split norms are small and the norm estimation of their Lemma 2.3 is not needed. The matching lower bound is not formalized. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/DistributionTestingClosenessFlattening.lean) include split masses and domain sizes, a satisfiable instance on `100` points, and a tester on `Bool` that rejects two distinct point masses.

## Dense graph testing

[Dense graph testers](../../Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/DenseGraph/Basic.lean) read a graph on `Fin n` through its adjacency matrix. A `Tester n` is a `ProbOracleComp` that tosses private coins and asks vertex-pair queries adaptively. `acceptProb T G` runs it against the deterministic adjacency oracle `adjOracle G`. `editDist G H` counts the unordered vertex pairs on which two graphs differ. `IsFar G P ε` requires every graph with property `P` to differ from `G` in more than `ε n²` pairs. Goldreich normalizes by the `n²` ordered matrix entries, so his proximity `ε` is `IsFar G P (ε / 2)` here. `IsOneSidedTester T P ε q` bundles a worst-case query bound (`ProbOracleComp.IsQueryBounded`), acceptance probability one on `P` and acceptance probability at most 1/3 on `ε`-far graphs. `OneSidedTestable P q` asks for such a tester with `q ε` queries for every `ε > 0` and every `n`. `IsFar.farFromTriangleFree` and `isFar_of_farFromTriangleFree` compare `IsFar` with Mathlib's deletion distance `SimpleGraph.FarFromTriangleFree`. `IsOneSidedTester.of_repeatAll` amplifies a perfectly complete test that rejects far graphs with probability at least `δ` by `k` repetitions with `2 ≤ δ k`. A new tester is written with `queryAdj` and `ProbOracleComp.sample`. `evalWith_queryAdj` and `evalWith_sample` evaluate its steps, `acceptProb_repeatAll` and `isQueryBounded_repeatAll` handle repetition, and `IsFar.deleteFar` and `isFar_of_deleteFar` compare `IsFar` with Mathlib's `DeleteFar` for any property.

The [triangle tester](../../Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/DenseGraph/TriangleFree.lean) samples `k` uniform ordered vertex triples and queries their three pairs. It rejects when one sampled triple is a triangle. `acceptProb_triangleTester` gives its exact acceptance probability `(1 - 6 t / n³) ^ k` on a graph with `t` triangles. Mathlib's triangle removal lemma gives at least `triangleRemovalBound ε · n³` triangles in an `ε`-far graph. Hence `isOneSidedTester_triangleTester`: for `ε > 0` and `1 ≤ 3 · triangleRemovalBound ε · k`, the tester is one-sided with `3k` queries. `oneSidedTestable_cliqueFree_three` states the resulting bound `3 ⌈(3 · triangleRemovalBound ε)⁻¹⌉`, independent of `n`. Its constant is the tower-type constant of the regularity lemma. The theorems count queries, not running time. General `H`-freeness and canonical testers remain open. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/DenseGraphTesting.lean) include a far graph on three vertices and a one-sided tester with explicit parameters (five trials, 15 queries, proximity 1/10). They also show that one trial is too few.

The [bipartiteness tester](../../Complexity/Computability/PropertyTesting/DenseGraph/Bipartite.lean) follows Goldreich–Goldwasser–Ron. `bipartiteTester n s m` samples `s` uniform vertices `u` and `m` uniform vertex pairs `(x k, y k)`, independently. It queries the `m (2 s + 1)` pairs `(x k, u i)`, `(y k, u i)` and `(x k, y k)`, and accepts when the sample positions admit a proper 2-colouring (`bipAccept`). Colours are attached to positions, so a vertex sampled twice may receive two colours. This is laxer than asking that the observed subgraph be bipartite: with `s = 0` and `m = 3` the tester can observe a triangle and still accept. `acceptProb_bipartiteTester_eq_one` is perfect completeness: a 2-colouring of the graph induces one of the sample positions. `IsFar.two_mul_lt_sum_monochromatic` states that a graph `ε`-far from bipartite has more than `2 ε n²` ordered monochromatic adjacent pairs under every 2-colouring. Soundness, `acceptProb_bipartiteTester_le_one_third`, assumes `12 ≤ ε s` and `s + 3 ≤ ε m`. In its proof, a colouring of `u` forces the colour of every vertex with a neighbour in `u`. Markov's inequality bounds the probability of a large degree mass outside the neighbourhood of `u` by `2 / (ε (s + 1))`. This expectation bound replaces the coverage step of Goldreich–Goldwasser–Ron (§6.1, Theorem 6.1.2 and Claims 6.1.4 and 6.1.6), which uses a sample of `O(log (1 / (ε δ)) / ε)` vertices and `O(log² (1 / (ε δ)) / ε³)` queries. The refinement removes the logarithmic factors. It is our adaptation, with no exact source and no claim of novelty. A union bound over the `2 ^ s` colourings of `u` bounds the rest by `2 ^ s (1 - ε) ^ m`. `isOneSidedTester_bipartiteTester` bundles these with the query bound, and `oneSidedTestable_isBipartite` fixes `s = ⌈12 / ε⌉` and `m = ⌈(s + 3) / ε⌉`. `bipartiteQueryBound_le` bounds the resulting query count by `459 / ε³` for `0 < ε ≤ 1`. That is polynomial in `1 / ε` and independent of `n`, but weaker than the `Õ(ε⁻²)` queries that follow from the `Õ(1 / ε)`-vertex sample analysis of Alon and Krivelevich. The [checked examples](../../ComplexityTests/DenseGraphTestingBipartite.lean) show that the triangle is `1/10`-far from bipartite and is rejected by the tester with `s = 120` and `m = 1230`. They also show that `s = m = 0` accepts every graph, and they evaluate the query bound at `ε = 1/10` (296430 queries).

## Known Limitations

The examples use semantic hypotheses and real probability/analysis. These results provide formal sample/query guarantees and formal bridges to PAC/query models. Claims of polynomial native training time or succinct executable output hypotheses require separate resource theorems. Keep these limitations in view when choosing a learning model: the notions of efficiency differ.
