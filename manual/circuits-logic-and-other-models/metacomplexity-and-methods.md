# Metacomplexity and general lower-bound methods

Metacomplexity treats a function's representation or truth table as input and asks about the cost of computing that function. cslib-frontier separates total tables, partial tables, sample constraints and promise gaps. Its general lower-bound interfaces then connect concrete circuits to covers, approximation schemes and cryptographic distinguishers. [cslib-frontier][complexitylib]

This chapter points to declarations and modules; it does not restate proofs. The headline metacomplexity results are registered in the [results inventory](../../results/README.md) under the topics `meta-complexity` and `Kolmogorov complexity`. Each entry lists its theorems, its source locator and its exact scope, and the IDs are given below. Most of these results are conditional implications, and an entry's scope note says which hypotheses it keeps.

| [MultiMCSP](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/Multi.lean) | Arity n, m component tables of `2^n` bits each, threshold s; one circuit of at most s gates has, for each component, a gate or input wire computing it. The basis is a parameter: the constant-free AND/OR/NOT basis of [DeMorgan.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/DeMorgan.lean) is Ilango, Loff and Oliveira's model, and the basis with constants is a library variant. The encoding writes n, m and s in unary, then the tables component by component. |

“Succinct” here means a sample-set specification, not an arbitrary circuit encoding of a longer truth table. Partial MCSP supplies both the default all-operation count and a binary AND/OR-only cost measure. The library also distinguishes unary and binary threshold encodings. A theorem stated in function arity n may become a qualitatively different bound when expressed in truth-table length `N=2^n`.

[Multi/NP.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/Multi/NP.lean) places Multi-output MCSP in NP: `MultiMCSP.mem_NP` over the constant-free basis and `MultiMCSP.mem_NP_boolean` for the variant with constants. A certificate is a circuit in the single-output certificate format, followed by each component's output wire in unary.

The library does not yet prove that Multi-MCSP is NP-hard. It does contain the window gadget of Ilango, Loff and Oliveira's reduction [ilo2020][ilo2020] (§4.2, pp. 22:20–22:21). Import `CslibFrontier.Computability.MCSP.MultiNPHard.Window` ([source](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/MultiNPHard/Window.lean)). For sets `A : Fin t → Finset (Fin (2 ^ p))` and a table `T : Fin (2 ^ p) → Bool`, `template A T` lists the functions computed by the gates of one constant-free De Morgan program on `p` inputs, and `tableFun T` is the function with truth table `T`. The layout (`Gate`, `gateCount`, `Gate.set`, `Gate.op`) depends only on `p` and `t`. The function at a gate of window `i` depends only on `T` restricted to `A i`, and every other gate computes a fixed function (`value_congr`). The last gate of window `i` computes `T` on `A i` and false elsewhere (`value_window_last`). For `p ≥ 1` and over any De Morgan basis, `ecomplexity_template` gives the template's exact cost as `#(nonInputComponents (template A T))`, the analogue for this template of Proposition 14. Given a cover `C` of the inputs by windows, `ecomplexity_append_le` bounds the template followed by `T` by that count plus `#C - 1`, the analogue for this template of Lemma 15. The soundness count is in `CslibFrontier.Computability.MCSP.MultiNPHard.WindowCount` ([source](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/MultiNPHard/WindowCount.lean)). It follows the library route: a union bound over small circuits replaces the compression count in Lemma 16 (pp. 22:22–22:23), so it needs no bound on the sizes of the sets. `three_mul_card_cheap_le` assumes that every set of at most `ℓ + 1` windows misses a common set of at least `d` inputs, with `p (5ℓ + 5) ≤ d`, `ℓ ≤ t ≤ 2 ^ p` and `64 ≤ 2 ^ p`. It concludes that for at most a third of the tables the template followed by `T` costs at most the count plus `ℓ`. For a set system on residues modulo `N`, `lift p N S` gives the windows. `lift_cover` turns covers of the residues into covers of the inputs, and `three_mul_card_cheap_lift_le` reduces the soundness hypothesis to a residue missed by every set of at most `ℓ + 1` of the sets, with `d = 2 ^ p / N`. The source writes each window as a canonical DNF (pp. 22:19–22:20). Here the windows share negations, a constant-false gate and prefix conjunctions, and each window is an OR chain. That is a construction choice inside the proof, with the same locality. Run `scripts/build.sh CslibFrontierTests.MultiMCSPWindow` for [worked instances](../../CslibFrontierTests/MultiMCSPWindow.lean): the 15-gate layout for `p = 2, t = 1`, locality, exact cost, both directions for the sets `{0}` and `{1}` lifted to `2 ^ 6` inputs, and soundness at `ℓ = 1` for three singletons lifted to `2 ^ 9` inputs.

`CslibFrontier.Computability.MCSP.MultiNPHard.Reduction` ([source](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/MultiNPHard/Reduction.lean)) proves the reduction correct, from the library's `GapSetCover 3` (the source reduces from Trevisan's `r`-bounded Set Cover). It does not yet prove that the reduction runs in polynomial time, so Multi-MCSP is not yet proved NP-hard. `reduce` takes `(S, k)` and a random string. Write `N` for the length of `S.ground` and `t` for the number of sets. The elements to cover are the positions of the ground list rather than its distinct elements: `residues S i` holds the positions whose element lies in set `i`, and `forall_residues_iff` shows that sets covering every position are exactly the covers, so repeated elements only repeat an obligation, and set elements outside the ground list are ignored, as `SetSystem.IsCover` ignores them. `mem_yes_iff` and `mem_no_iff` state membership in `GapSetCover 3` over `ℕ`: a no-instance is coverable with `3k ≤ optCoverSize` and `0 < k`, and `ground_ne_nil_of_mem_no` shows its ground list is nonempty. The arity is `arity S = 6 · size (N + t + 4)`, with `(N + t + 4)^6 < 2 ^ arity S ≤ 64 (N + t + 4)^6`. For a nonempty ground list, `ofTable S k T` is the template of the lifted windows `lift p N (residues S i)` followed by `T`, with threshold the template's distinct non-input gate functions plus `min k t` (the clamp keeps the unary threshold polynomial in the binary `k`, and costs nothing since a cover never needs more than `t` sets), built with `Instance.ofFunction` from `CslibFrontier.Computability.MCSP.Multi`, and `T` is the first `2 ^ p` bits of the random string. An empty ground list gives the fixed instance `fallback`. `reduce_mem_of_yes` sends yes-instances to yes-instances for every random string: this is the source's one-sided error. `three_mul_card_ofTable_mem_le` and `three_mul_card_reduce_mem_le` show that on a no-instance at most a third of the tables, or of the random strings, give a yes-instance. `reduce_positive` shows that every output has positive arity, number of outputs and threshold, as Definition 1 asks. `rpReducible_of_computableInPolyTime` gives the randomized reduction (`RPReducible`) to positive Multi-MCSP instances over any De Morgan basis, assuming that `reduce` runs in polynomial time. Run `scripts/build.sh CslibFrontierTests.MultiMCSPReduction` for [worked instances](../../CslibFrontierTests/MultiMCSPReduction.lean): the parameters, positions with a repeated ground element, the empty ground list, completeness for both bases, and soundness for three singletons with `k = 1`.

[PartialETH.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/PartialETH.lean) states NP membership and ETH-conditional hardness of Partial MCSP at the `2^o(n log n)` scale, equivalently `N^o(log log N)`, following Ilango [ilango2020focs][ilango2020focs] (registry ID `partial-mcsp`). It preserves the different cost/threshold conventions. The library also contains a separate [native fixed-width SAT counting endpoint](../machines-and-classes/native-counting.md) deriving the negation of ETH. Consequently the ETH-conditional implication remains a valid formal theorem, but its premise is false within the combined development. That chapter gives the native encodings, quantitative contract, manuscript mapping and review limits; this interaction affects how the conditional result may be applied and is not a proof inconsistency.

## Time-bounded Kolmogorov complexity

[TimeBounded.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Kolmogorov/TimeBounded.lean) defines `K^t` on the native multi-tape universal machine: `timeBoundedAt t x` is the shortest program producing `x` within `t` steps, `timeBounded t` uses the clock `t |x|`, and `timeBoundedNat` is the natural-valued wrapper. `levinKt` is Levin's `Kt`, minimizing program length plus the logarithm of running time. The [description-method chapter](../machines-and-classes/computability-and-description.md#clock-conventions) gives the clock conventions and the plain, conditional and Partrec-side models. [Counting.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Kolmogorov/TimeBounded/Counting.lean) counts objects of small `K^t` for every step budget: under an injective encoding into strings, fewer than `2 ^ (s + 1)` objects have `K^t` at most `s` (`encard_setOf_timeBoundedAt_le_lt`, and `card_filter_timeBoundedAt_le_lt` for finite types). `card_filter_timeBoundedAt_truthTable_le_lt` applies this to Boolean functions on `L` inputs, with truth tables in the order of `BitString.equivFin`. Since no time bound enters, counting arguments such as the no case of partial-`MINKT` hardness ([#4447](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4447)) can use it at any budget.

Four decision problems use these measures, with membership only:

- [MINKT.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Kolmogorov/MINKT.lean): `MINKT` takes a string with time and size thresholds in unary. `MINKT.mem_NP` and, for fixed thresholds computable in polynomial time, `MINKTAt.mem_NP` place it in NP (registry ID `minkt-np`). The problem follows Hirahara's Definition 9 (after Ko), and the fixed-threshold `MINKTAt` follows Liu and Pass, Section 2.2. [hirahara2018][hirahara2018] [ko1991][ko1991] [liupass20][liupass20]
- [MINcKT.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Kolmogorov/MINcKT.lean): the conditional analogue, with `MINcKT.mem_NP`. No recorded source states this membership, so it has no registry entry.
- [MINKTStar.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Kolmogorov/MINKTStar.lean): the partial-string variant `MINKTStar` (MINKT\*), whose pattern lists entries in `{0, 1, *}` (`Option Bool`). An instance is a yes-instance when some total string `Consistent` with the pattern has `K^t` at most the threshold. `MINKTStar.mem_NP` places it in NP, and `MINKTStar.polyTimeReducible_MINKT_MINKTStar` reduces `MINKT` to it. The problem follows Hirahara's FOCS 2022 introduction and footnote 5 [hirahara2022][hirahara2022]. The source does not state the membership, so it has no registry entry.
- [MKtP.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Kolmogorov/MKtP.lean): `MKtP.mem_E` and `MKtP.mem_EXP` place the minimum-`Kt` problem in E and EXP when the threshold is unary; `MKtPAt.mem_EXP` covers fixed thresholds (registry ID `mktp-exp`). Liu and Pass write the threshold in binary, so the unary form is a different encoding. [liupass2021crypto][liupass2021crypto]

`GapMINKT`, `GapMINcKT` and `GapMKtP` are the corresponding promise problems. NP-hardness of `MINKTStar` under randomized reductions [hirahara2022][hirahara2022] is tracked in [#4447](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4447). NP-hardness of the total problems `MINKT` and `MINcKT` is open, so it is not planned. Ko introduced `MINKT` and showed that no relativizing proof gives NP-hardness of learning programs [ko1991][ko1991], as Hirahara recounts (abstract, p. 968; Section I, p. 970) [hirahara2022][hirahara2022]. Liu and Pass prove a conditional problem NP-complete under randomized reductions (Theorem 1.2, p. 36:5), but for RAM programs that query the condition, not for Turing machines (footnote 4, p. 36:4) [liupass2022ccc][liupass2022ccc].

For plain complexity, Kolmogorov–Levin symmetry of information ([SymmetryOfInformation.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Kolmogorov/SymmetryOfInformation.lean), registry ID `symmetry-of-information`) is described under [abstract description methods](../machines-and-classes/computability-and-description.md#abstract-description-methods). Chaitin's incompleteness theorem ([Incompleteness.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Kolmogorov/Incompleteness.lean), registry ID `chaitin-incompleteness`) is described under [clock conventions](../machines-and-classes/computability-and-description.md#clock-conventions).

## MCSP and one-way functions

[OneWay/MCSP.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Crypto/Computational/OneWay/MCSP.lean) proves the Kabanets–Cai implication [kabanetscai2000][kabanetscai2000] (registry ID `kabanets-cai`). If MCSP is in BPP, then `not_pseudorandomFunction_of_MCSP_mem_BPP` distinguishes every polynomial-time keyed family from a random function. `not_oneWay_of_MCSP_mem_BPP` and `not_oneWay_of_MCSP_mem_P` then rule out one-way functions. MCSP here is the total truth-table language with all gates counted and a binary threshold, and the adversary is a uniform strict oracle PPT machine.

The Liu–Pass equivalence between one-way functions and mild average-case hardness of `K^t` is in [OneWay/LiuPass.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Crypto/Computational/OneWay/LiuPass.lean) (registry ID `liu-pass`). The [average-case hardness chapter](../programs-crypto-and-probability/average-case-hardness.md#main-equivalences-and-their-exact-scope) gives its heuristic model and quantifiers. Only the polynomial-threshold form of Theorem 3.1 (c) is proved. [liupass20][liupass20]

## Hardness magnification

[Magnification.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/Magnification.lean) (registry ID `magnification`) gives decision, no-oracle and size-only specializations following McKay, Murray and Williams [mckaymurraywilliams2019][mckaymurraywilliams2019]. Under `NP subset P/poly`, there is a constant c such that every threshold function s and every arity n admit a decision circuit for the corresponding MCSP slice of size at most `2^n c (n+s(n)+1)^c`; a polynomial threshold yields `2^n c(n+1)^c` after changing c. Contrapositive lower bounds therefore imply `NP` is not contained in P/poly and, through the native containment bridge, `P != NP`.

The [implementation](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/MCSP/Magnification.lean) explicitly maps this version to the cited McKay–Murray–Williams lemma/theorem and explains its stronger lower-bound premise and restricted scope. It uses fixed-threshold witness certificates, binary subcube merging and a polynomial-hierarchy collapse. It should not be cited as every search, oracle or fine quantitative formulation of magnification in the literature.

[MergeTree.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/Magnification/MergeTree.lean) prepares the McKay–Murray–Williams `[MMW19]` bullet of Proposition 63 of Chen, Hirahara, Oliveira, Pich, Rajgopal and Santhanam [chenhiraharaoliveirapichrajgopalsanthanam2020][chenhiraharaoliveirapichrajgopalsanthanam2020], which asks for a merge tree whose nodes merge many children at once. Iterating the binary merge step `k` times merges the states of the `2^k` subcubes below a prefix into its state (`iterMergeVec_eq_subcubeState`). The oracle `MergeTree.oracle` answers a bit of such a `k`-level merge, either of `2^k` child states or, at a leaf, of `2^k` table bits; it is one language for every `n` and `s`, and no bound on its complexity is claimed. At the root, bit 0 is the answer of `MCSP[s]`. [MergeTree/Circuit.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/Magnification/MergeTree/Circuit.lean) builds the tree as an explicit circuit over the oracle basis, one layer of oracle gates per level, and proves that it decides `MCSP[s]` under that oracle (`eval_circuit`), with exact oracle-gate and gate counts, adaptivity and fan-in. With `k = ⌊log₂ s⌋` bits per level and leaves of at least `s³/8` points when `3k ≤ n` (`mmwCircuit`), for `2 ≤ s` and `n ≤ s` and with `N = 2^n`, the circuit has at most `1 + 128N/s` oracle gates, at most `3 + 129N/s` gates (the oracle gates and two constant gates per layer), adaptivity at most `n/⌊log₂ s⌋ + 1` and fan-in at most `12s⁴`. The gate counts are `O(N/s)` once `s ≤ N`, as in the bullet's regime `s ≤ 2^n/poly(n)`, so this is the bullet with `poly(s) = s` in the counts and `s⁴` in the fan-in. Stating it as membership in the local circuit class waits on that class (#4430).

[GapMCSP.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/Magnification/GapMCSP.lean) (registry ID `gap-mcsp-magnification`) states Theorem 1.4 of Oliveira, Pich and Santhanam [oliveirapichsanthanam2019][oliveirapichsanthanam2019] with their constant `10`. Suppose that for some `ε > 0` and every small enough `β > 0`, for infinitely many `n`, no Boolean function that separates `Gap-MCSP[⌊2^(βn)/(10n)⌋₊, ⌊2^(βn)⌋₊]` on `2^n`-bit truth tables has complexity at most `N^(1+ε)`, where `N = 2^n`. Then `NP` is not contained in P/poly and `P != NP`. The proof does not follow OPS. Under `NP subset P/poly`, the McKay–Murray–Williams circuit for `MCSP[s₂]` above separates `Gap-MCSP[s₁, s₂]` whenever `s₁ ≤ s₂`, and its size is below `N^(1+ε)` once `β` is small. Size is the library's De Morgan measure counting all gates; OPS say gate types do not matter, and no translation to another basis is claimed. OPS's efficient anti-checker (their Lemma 4.1) is not formalized; see [Anti-checkers](#anti-checkers).

Oliveira and Santhanam's formula magnification [oliveirasanthanam2018][oliveirasanthanam2018] is in [FormulaMagnification.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/FormulaMagnification.lean) and its submodules [StretchedExponential.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/FormulaMagnification/StretchedExponential.lean), [Subexponential.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/FormulaMagnification/Subexponential.lean), [AverageCase.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/FormulaMagnification/AverageCase.lean) and [SublinearAverageCase.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/FormulaMagnification/SublinearAverageCase.lean). Each theorem assumes a lower bound, measured by leaf size, either for De Morgan formulas computing approximate `(1, 1 - 1/k)`-MCSP (`ApproxMCSP`) or one-sided average-case MCSP, or for ternary zero-error devices that answer `?` on at most a bounded fraction of truth tables and are correct elsewhere. The NP language is succinct MCSP. Source locators refer to ECCC TR18-139.

[SublinearAverageCase.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/FormulaMagnification/SublinearAverageCase.lean) states Theorem 5 for ternary zero-error devices ([ZeroErrorAverageCase.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/ZeroErrorAverageCase.lean)) at the threshold `⌊2^√n⌋`: if no device family with `o(N)` total leaves solves `MCSP[⌊2^√n⌋]` while answering `?` on at most a `1/n` fraction of truth tables for every large n (Definition 43), then `NP ⊄ Formula[poly]`. The section 1.2 form, with `?` on at most half of the tables, is a corollary, and a library generalization takes any subexponential threshold.

[Locality.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/FormulaMagnification/Locality.lean) (registry ID `formula-magnification-locality`) proves the MCSP `[OS18]` bullet of Proposition 63 of Chen, Hirahara, Oliveira, Pich, Rajgopal and Santhanam [chenhiraharaoliveirapichrajgopalsanthanam2020][chenhiraharaoliveirapichrajgopalsanthanam2020]: the reduction above is local. For `n ≤ s(n)` and `δ > 0`, `MCSP[(s, 0), (s, δ)]` on `N`-bit truth tables is in their class `[N, poly(s/δ), 1]–Formula[N·poly(s/δ)]` (`LocalFormulaClass`, Definition 11, in [OracleFormula.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/OracleFormula.lean)). The formula is a conjunction of at most `N` oracle gates. Each gate reads constants and input bits that encode a succinct-MCSP instance sampling the truth table at fixed seed points, so no oracle gate feeds another. A single succinct-MCSP oracle serves every length. `LocalFormulaClass` is the formula instance of the model-generic class `OracleModel.LocalClass`, whose circuit instance `LocalCircuitClass` is described in [circuits and formulas](circuits-and-formulas.md#boolean-bases-and-related-models). No bullet of Proposition 63 is proved for circuits yet ([#4430](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4430)), and the other bullets and the locality barrier (Theorem 59) are open.

## Consequences of NP-hardness of MCSP

[HardnessConsequences.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/HardnessConsequences.lean) (registry ID `murray-williams`) assumes that total MCSP is NP-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions. From this, `EXP_eq_NEXP_of_MCSP_npHard_of_EXP_subset_PPoly` derives `EXP = NEXP` when `EXP ⊆ P/poly`, and `EXP_ne_NP_inter_PPolyOn_of_MCSP_npHard` and `EXP_ne_ZPP_of_MCSP_npHard` give Murray and Williams's Theorems 4.1 and 1.6 [murraywilliams2017][murraywilliams2017]. Theorem 4.1 needs reductions only from sparse NP languages, so the library proves its full-NP-hardness specialization. `PH_eq_SigmaP_two_of_MCSP_npHard_of_mem_PPolyOn` adds the Karp–Lipton collapse when MCSP is also in P/poly. Consequences of hardness under local or weak reductions are not formalized.

## Anti-checkers

An anti-checker for `f` against a family `F` is a set of inputs on which every member of `F` differs from `f` (`IsAntiChecker` in [AntiChecker.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/AntiChecker.lean), registry ID `anti-checker`) [liptonyoung1994][liptonyoung1994] [oliveirapichsanthanam2019][oliveirapichsanthanam2019]. `exists_isAntiChecker` gives one of at most `16 b` inputs against a family of fewer than `2^b` functions on at most `2^m` inputs, if `m > 0` and no `m` members have `f` as strict majority. `exists_lt_complexityOn` and `exists_ofFunction_notMem` specialize this to circuits and to Succinct MCSP no-instances. Counting ordered tuples gives the share `1/16`, where the source counts sets and gets `1/(2m)`. [AntiChecker/Certificate.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/AntiChecker/Certificate.lean) counts circuit certificates instead of functions. Both are nonuniform existence results.

[AntiChecker/Threshold.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/AntiChecker/Threshold.lean) chooses the inputs explicitly. Its greedy asks an oracle, in round `u = R - 1, …, 0`, whether the `ℓ`-th power of the number of descriptions still consistent with the samples is about `2^u`, and appends the first input on which the answer is yes. The oracle must be a two-sided threshold oracle (`IsThresholdOracle`): a yes guarantees fewer than `2^(u+1)`, a yes at `u = 0` guarantees none, and fewer than `2^u/(u+1)` forces a yes. `consistent_greedy_eq_empty` refutes every description in `L ℓ + 1` rounds when the family has at most `2^L` members, every nonempty subfamily is refuted on a sixteenth of its members by some input, and `4 (L ℓ + 1) 15^ℓ ≤ 16^ℓ`, which `ℓ = L + 200` satisfies. `isThresholdOracle_hashOracle` builds such an oracle from covering by `u + 1` affine hash functions ([Covering.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Probability/PairwiseIndependentHash/Covering.lean)), and `isAntiChecker_hashAntiChecker` applies it to valid circuit certificates. `isAntiChecker_greedy` gives the same anti-checker against any threshold oracle for the certificates. This is the library's own deterministic route, not the proof of Oliveira, Pich and Santhanam's Lemma 4.1: it uses no randomized approximate counting and no final listing.

[AntiChecker/Oracle.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/AntiChecker/Oracle.lean) fixes one language, `antiCheckerOracle`, for all of these questions. A `Query` gives `n`, `s`, `ℓ` and `u` in unary, then the labelled samples; `length_encQuery_ofSamples` gives its exact length, `2 (n + s + ℓ + u) + 4 + t (4 n + 5)` for `t` samples. On the greedy's queries the language is the hash oracle, and `isThresholdOracle_of_eq_true_iff` turns any oracle that agrees with it there into a threshold oracle. At `ℓ = 1` and `u = 0` a query is a no-query exactly when some circuit with at most `s` gates fits the samples (`encQuery_ofSamples_one_zero_notMem_antiCheckerOracle_iff`), so the same language also decides the complement of Succinct MCSP. [Oracle/PolyTime.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/AntiChecker/Oracle/PolyTime.lean) proves that, under `NP ⊆ P/poly`, some `P/poly` language agrees with it on every query (`exists_mem_PPoly_agrees_antiCheckerOracle`). Being large is an `∃ ∀ ∃` statement over a polynomial-time covering test, and the Karp–Lipton quantifier elimination removes the quantifiers.

[AntiChecker/Generator.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/MCSP/AntiChecker/Generator.lean) builds the greedy as an explicit oracle circuit, `generator n s ℓ R`, over the truth table of `f`, from the selection rounds of [circuits and formulas](circuits-and-formulas.md). Round `r` has an oracle gate for each of the `2 ^ n` inputs `y`, and the gate's query is exactly the `encQuery` word of the earlier samples and `(y, f y)`, read from constants and wires. Queries are not padded, so their lengths grow by one sample per round. Under any oracle the output's samples are the greedy's against that oracle's answers (`samplesOf_eval_generator`), so each is a pair `(y, f y)`. With `ℓ = L + 200` and `R = L (L + 200) + 1`, an oracle that agrees with `antiCheckerOracle` on every query, such as the `P/poly` language above, makes the chosen inputs an anti-checker (`isAntiChecker_samplesOf_generator`). The generator has `R (2 + 2 ^ n (3 n + 5))` gates, `R 2 ^ n` oracle gates, at most `R` oracle gates on any path and fan-in at most `queryLength n s ℓ R R`, the length of a query with threshold `R` and `R` samples. For these parameters `R` and that length are at most `610 m ^ 4` and `5000 m ^ 5`, where `m = n + s + 1`. `map_querySources_blockSources` reads the output's samples as a query word for a later query. Replacing the oracle gates by circuits for the oracle is not formalized.

## Hardness versus randomness

[IsAverageCaseHard](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/HardnessAmplification.lean#L58) says that every Boolean function of De Morgan circuit complexity at most s agrees with a target function on at most a `1/2 + 1/s` fraction of uniformly distributed inputs. Both the gate bound and agreement bound are non-strict. This is a nonuniform circuit predicate, distinct from hardness against uniform probabilistic heuristics. XOR amplification and the Nisan–Wigderson generator use it as an intermediate interface.

The [average-case endpoint](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BPP/Derandomization.lean#L302) assumes a binary language in native E and a positive natural c such that its n-bit slice satisfies this predicate at size `2^(n/c)` at all sufficiently large lengths, with natural-number division. It proves `P enc = BPP enc` for every supplied instance encoding. The native deterministic decider computes a Nisan–Wigderson generator and enumerates its short seeds. The result credits Nisan and Wigderson. [nisanwigderson94][nisanwigderson94]

The [worst-case endpoint](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/BPP/WorstCaseHardness.lean#L80) assumes instead a native E language and a real `gamma > 0` whose slices require at least `2^(gamma*n)` De Morgan gates at **all sufficiently large lengths**. It derives the same native class equality through Reed–Muller/Hadamard encoding and local list decoding. The endpoint is credited to Impagliazzo and Wigderson, while the source identifies the Sudan–Trevisan–Vadhan proof route. These declarations assume their circuit lower bounds; an infinitely-often hardness hypothesis and simulation variant are not formalized here. [impagliazzowigderson97][impagliazzowigderson97] [sudantrevisanvadhan01][sudantrevisanvadhan01]

The [machine endpoint account](../machines-and-classes/endpoints-and-hierarchies.md#hardness-versus-randomness) states the uniform class side and the literature-check scope. Here the inspected evidence is the circuit-hardness definition, the final theorem statements and their direct native bridge, not an independent reconstruction of the hardness amplification or generator proof.

## Hard-core measures and hard-core sets

Impagliazzo's hard-core lemma turns mild average-case hardness into near-maximal hardness on a dense part of the input space. [impagliazzo95][impagliazzo95] Two forms are formalized for Boolean functions `f` on `n` bits and nonuniform De Morgan circuits, with sizes counted by `Cslib.Circuits.complexity`. A hard-core measure ([`IsHardCoreMeasure`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/HardCore.lean)) has weights `M x ∈ [0, 1]` and mass at least `δ 2^n`. Every function of complexity at most `s` agrees with `f` on at most a `1/2 + ε` fraction of that mass. A hard-core set ([`IsHardCoreSet`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/HardCore/Set.lean)) has at least `δ 2^n` elements, and every such function agrees with `f` on at most a `1/2 + ε` fraction of them. `isHardCoreSet_iff_isHardCoreMeasure` identifies hard-core sets with the hard-core measures that have `0`/`1` weights, so the set form is the stronger conclusion.

`exists_isHardCoreMeasure` is the measure form, proved by Impagliazzo's boosting argument. Take `T` rounds with `3 ε² δ² T ≥ 1`. If every function of complexity at most `T s + 2 + 2T(T + 2)` agrees with `f` on at most a `1 - δ` fraction of the inputs, `f` has a hard-core measure of density `δ` against size `s` with advantage `ε`. `exists_isHardCoreMeasure_of_le` restates this from hardness at a given size `S`. Yao's XOR lemma (`card_eq_xorPower_div_le` in [XorLemma.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/XorLemma.lean)) is derived from the measure form.

The set form is obtained by randomized rounding, following Holenstein's Lemma 2.2: each input joins the set independently with probability `M x`. [holenstein05][holenstein05] Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound over the `C` functions of complexity at most `s` (`computableFunctions n s`) give a good outcome in `exists_finset_card_agree_lt`. Rounding costs no circuit size. Padding the measure to `(1 - γδ) M x + γδ` first makes up for the density that rounding loses (`IsHardCoreMeasure.pad`). So `IsHardCoreMeasure.exists_isHardCoreSet` turns a hard-core measure of density `δ ∈ (0, 1)` and advantage `ε ≥ 0` into a hard-core set with at least `δ 2^n` elements and advantage `ε + 2γ`, for `0 < γ ≤ 1`. It assumes the explicit counting condition `(C + 1) exp(-2 (γ δ (1 - δ))² 2^n) < 1`. Since `log C = O((s + 1) log(n + s + 1))` by the De Morgan counting bound, the condition holds for polynomial `s` and fixed `γ` and `δ` once `n` is large. `exists_isHardCoreSet` and `exists_isHardCoreSet_of_le` chain the two steps into the set form of the lemma.

Scope: hardness `δ` gives density `δ`, the parameters of Impagliazzo's lemma as Holenstein recalls them in Section 2. Holenstein's optimal density, a set of density `δ` from hardness `δ/2` (his Lemma 2.1), is not formalized, and neither are uniform versions of the lemma ([#3557](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3557)). Existence is nonuniform: no construction of the measure, the set or the circuits is claimed. [HardnessAmplification.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/HardnessAmplification.lean) checks a concrete hard-core measure. [HardCoreSet.lean](../../CslibFrontierTests/HardCoreSet.lean) checks a hard-core set of two-bit parity and shows that a constant function has no hard-core set of positive density. It then rounds the constant measure `1/2` for thirteen-bit parity, verifying the counting condition `14 exp(-16) < 1`, and instantiates the parameters of `exists_isHardCoreSet`.

## Random graph connectivity

[The connectivity module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Probability/Combinatorics/BinomialRandomGraph/Connectivity.lean) proves the upper half of the Erdős–Rényi connectivity threshold for Mathlib's binomial random graph `G(V, p)` (`SimpleGraph.binomialRandom`, `p : unitInterval`), in the `G(n, p)` form proved in the proof of [Frieze and Karoński, Theorem 4.1](../sources.md#friezekaronski2016). A fixed finite set of non-diagonal edges is absent with probability `(1 - p) ^ |t|` (`binomialRandom_real_disjoint_edgeSet`). A disconnected graph on `n ≥ 1` vertices has an empty cut `S` with `1 ≤ |S| ≤ n / 2` (`exists_forall_not_adj_of_not_connected`, a pure graph-theory lemma in [the connectivity topic module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Connectivity/Connected.lean)), so `P(G(n, p) is not connected) ≤ ∑_{k=1}^{⌊n/2⌋} C(n, k) (1 - p) ^ (k (n - k))` (`binomialRandom_real_not_connected_le_sum`). For `n ≥ 1` and `n exp (-(n p)) ≤ 1 / 64` this is at most `35 n exp (-(n p))` (`binomialRandom_real_not_connected_le`), and `tendsto_binomialRandom_real_connected` gives `P(G(n, p(n)) is connected) → 1` whenever `n p(n) - log n → ∞`. The union bound over cuts replaces the source's count of components through spanning trees; the constants `1 / 64` and `35` are not optimized. [The simple-graph measurability module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/MeasureTheory/Constructions/SimpleGraph.lean) provides a `MeasurableSingletonClass` instance for countable `V`. For finite `V` this makes every event about a graph measurable (`MeasurableSet.of_discrete`). The [checked instances](../../CslibFrontierTests/BinomialRandomGraphConnectivity.lean) cover `p = 0` and `p = 1`, the union bound on two vertices, and show the explicit bound is nonvacuous: `G(20, 1/2)` is disconnected with probability below `1 / 20`.

To use it, `import CslibFrontier.Foundations.Probability.Combinatorics.BinomialRandomGraph.Connectivity` and `open SimpleGraph` for the scoped notation `G(V, p)`. An edge probability is a `unitInterval` element such as `(⟨1 / 2, by norm_num, by norm_num⟩ : unitInterval)`. To apply `tendsto_binomialRandom_real_connected` to a sequence `p : ℕ → unitInterval`, prove `Tendsto (fun n : ℕ => n * (p n : ℝ) - Real.log n) atTop atTop`; the checked instances do this for constant `p = 1 / 2` from `log n ≤ n / e`.

[The isolated-vertex module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Probability/Combinatorics/BinomialRandomGraph/IsolatedVertices.lean) proves the lower half, through the sharp threshold for isolated vertices: the `G(n, p)` form of [Frieze and Karoński, Lemma 1.11](../sources.md#friezekaronski2016) (stated there for `G(n, m)`) and the case `c_n → -∞` of their Theorem 4.1. The isolated-vertex predicate is Mathlib's `SimpleGraph.IsIsolated`. Let `X` count the isolated vertices and `μ = n (1 - p) ^ (n - 1)`. A vertex is isolated with probability `(1 - p) ^ (n - 1)` (`binomialRandom_real_isIsolated`) and two distinct vertices both are with probability `(1 - p) ^ (2 n - 3)` (`binomialRandom_real_isIsolated_and`). So `E[X] = μ` (`integral_natCard_isIsolated`) and `E[X ^ 2] = μ + n (n - 1) (1 - p) ^ (2 n - 3)` (`integral_natCard_isIsolated_sq`). The first moment gives `P(X > 0) ≤ μ` (`binomialRandom_real_exists_isIsolated_le`). Chebyshev's inequality (Mathlib's `meas_ge_le_variance_div_sq`, not a separate second-moment lemma) gives `P(X = 0) ≤ 1 / μ + p / (1 - p)` for `n ≥ 1` and `p < 1` (`binomialRandom_real_forall_not_isIsolated_le`). Since a connected graph on `n ≥ 2` vertices has no isolated vertex, the same bound holds for `P(G(n, p) is connected)` (`binomialRandom_real_connected_le`). Asymptotically, `tendsto_binomialRandom_real_exists_isIsolated_atTop` and `tendsto_binomialRandom_real_exists_isIsolated_atBot` give `P(X > 0) → 0` when `n p(n) - log n → ∞` and `P(X > 0) → 1` when `n p(n) - log n → -∞`. `tendsto_binomialRandom_real_connected_atBot` gives `P(G(n, p(n)) is connected) → 0` in the second case. Its [checked instances](../../CslibFrontierTests/BinomialRandomGraphIsolatedVertices.lean) cover `p = 0`, `p = 1`, two vertices, the mean `3 / 4` for `G(3, 1/2)`, and the nonvacuous bound `P(G(20, 1/20) is connected) < 1 / 5`. They also cover the sequence `p(n) = 1 / (n + 1)`. To apply the lower half, `import CslibFrontier.Foundations.Probability.Combinatorics.BinomialRandomGraph.IsolatedVertices` and prove `Tendsto (fun n : ℕ => n * (p n : ℝ) - Real.log n) atTop atBot`; for `p(n) = 1 / (n + 1)` the checked instance bounds the left side by `1 - log n`.

The limit `e ^ (-e ^ (-c))` at `p = (log n + c) / n` (the Poisson limit of `X`), the hitting-time theorem and the translation to `G(n, m)` are not formalized ([#3485](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3485)).

## Explicit Ramsey graphs

Explicit Ramsey graphs are a standard derandomization benchmark: the counting argument shows that most graphs have no large homogeneous set, but it names none. [`SimpleGraph.ramseyNumber`](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Ramsey/Basic.lean) is the least `n` such that every simple graph on `n` vertices has a `k`-clique or an `l`-independent set. It is characterized on any finite vertex type by `ramseyNumber_le_card_iff` (upper bounds) and `card_lt_ramseyNumber_iff` (lower bounds: exhibit one graph with neither). The Erdős–Szekeres recursion gives `ramseyNumber_le_four_pow`. [erdosszekeres1935][erdosszekeres1935] The [Erdős module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Ramsey/Erdos.lean) proves the lower bound `two_rpow_half_lt_ramseyNumber`, `2 ^ (k / 2) < R(k, k)` for `k ≥ 3`, by finite counting. The underlying count `lt_ramseyNumber_of_two_mul_choose_lt` (`2 C(n, k) < 2 ^ C(k, 2)`) needs no probability space. [erdos1947][erdos1947]

The explicit counterpart is [Nagy's graph](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Ramsey/Nagy.lean). Its vertices are the `3`-subsets of a `t`-element type, and two vertices are adjacent when they meet in exactly one point. Oddtown and the uniform Fisher inequality bound its cliques and independent sets by `t`. Both are proved through one [Gram-matrix rank bound](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/UniformGram.lean) over `ZMod 2` and `ℚ` ([set-family module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SetFamily/Oddtown.lean)). Hence `choose_three_lt_ramseyNumber` gives `C(t, 3) < R(t + 1, t + 1)`. [nagy1972][nagy1972]

Graph bounds and explicitness are separate obligations. [`Cslib.ExplicitRamsey`](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/ExplicitRamsey.lean) names a vertex by its increasing triple `(a, b, c)` with `a < b < c < t`. `nagyLabel_injective` and `range_nagyLabel` show that these names are a bijection onto the triples accepted by `nagyIsLabel`. `nagyGraph_adj_iff_nagyAdj` transfers adjacency to `nagyAdj` on names. `computableInPolyTime_nagyAdj` and `computableInPolyTime_nagyIsLabel` are plain `ComputableInPolyTime` certificates for binary naturals; one machine serves every `t`. The cost is therefore polylogarithmic in the number of vertices, the strongly explicit regime. The [checked clients](../../CslibFrontierTests/Ramsey.lean) include `R(3, 3) = 6` and a Fano-plane instance attaining the uniform Fisher bound.

The [Ray-Chaudhuri–Wilson module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SetFamily/RayChaudhuriWilson.lean) proves intersection bounds by the multilinear polynomial method of Alon, Babai and Suzuki, using the [low-degree functions on the cube](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/BooleanAnalysis/LowDegree.lean) and their dimension bound. Over any field, if no member size lies in `L` and every pairwise intersection size does, the family has at most `∑_{i ≤ |L|} C(n, i)` members (`Fintype.card_le_sum_choose_of_card_inter_mem`). A `d`-uniform family has at most `C(n, |L|)` members when also `(j : K) ≠ d` for `j < |L|` (`Fintype.card_le_choose_of_card_inter_mem`). Two specializations follow. `Fintype.card_le_choose_of_card_inter_mem_of_lt` is Ray-Chaudhuri–Wilson for a set of naturals below `d`. `Fintype.card_le_choose_of_card_inter_mem_zmod` is the modular Frankl–Wilson bound over `ZMod p` with `d mod p ∉ L`. [alonbabaisuzuki1991][alonbabaisuzuki1991] [raychaudhuriwilson1975][raychaudhuriwilson1975] [franklwilson1981][franklwilson1981]

The modular bound is a restricted form: it assumes `|L| ≤ d mod p`, the condition the triangular polynomial argument needs. Some side condition is necessary, and a checked counterexample in the [tests](../../CslibFrontierTests/FranklWilson.lean) shows the bound failing without it. Frankl and Wilson's inclusion-matrix theorem reportedly needs only `|L| ≤ d` (not checked against the paper). It covers, for example, `p = 3`, `d = 4`, `L = {0, 2}`, which the restricted form does not, and it is not formalized.

The [Frankl–Wilson graph](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Ramsey/FranklWilson.lean) `franklWilsonGraph α p d` has the `d`-subsets of `α` as vertices. Two vertices are adjacent when their intersection size is not congruent to `d` mod `p`. For a prime `p` with `d ≡ -1 (mod p)`, the modular bound excludes cliques of size `C(n, p - 1) + 1` (`cliqueFree_franklWilsonGraph`). Ray-Chaudhuri–Wilson excludes independent sets of size `C(n, ⌊d / p⌋) + 1` (`indepSetFree_franklWilsonGraph`). With `n = p³` and `d = p² - 1`, `choose_lt_ramseyNumber_franklWilson` gives `C(p³, p² - 1) < R(k, k)` for `k = C(p³, p - 1) + 1`. `pow_lt_ramseyNumber_franklWilson` records `k ^ c < R(k, k)` for every prime `p ≥ 6c + 3`, a superpolynomial explicit bound along this sequence of `k`.

The [explicitness module](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/ExplicitRamsey/FranklWilson.lean) names a `d`-subset of `Fin n` by its increasing list of elements. `subsetLabel_injective` and `range_subsetLabel` identify the names with the lists accepted by `isSubsetLabel`. `franklWilsonGraph_adj_iff_subsetAdj` transfers adjacency to `subsetAdj`. The four `computableInPolyTime_*` theorems are plain `ComputableInPolyTime` certificates for binary naturals. `franklWilsonIsLabel` and `franklWilsonAdj` take only `p`, so one machine serves every prime. A name has `O(p² log p)` bits, logarithmic in the number of vertices.

Nagy's graph has `Θ(k ^ 3)` vertices for homogeneous-set bound `k`. Frankl and Wilson's has `k ^ Θ(log k / log log k)`, and Erdős's existential bound is exponential in `k`. Erdős's bound overtakes Nagy's at `k = 20`. The two-source disperser and extractor graphs are not formalized. An explicit graph on `2 ^ Ω(k)` vertices is an open problem ([#3041](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3041)).

## Random graphs and thresholds

The binomial random graph `G(V, p)` is Mathlib's `SimpleGraph.binomialRandom`, a measure on `SimpleGraph V` with `p : unitInterval`. The library adds its basic probabilities, the first moment method for every fixed subgraph and the second moment method for triangles. To use it, `import CslibFrontier.Foundations.Probability.Combinatorics.BinomialRandomGraph.Clique` and `open SimpleGraph` for the scoped notation `G(V, p)`; an edge probability is a `unitInterval` element such as `(⟨1 / 2, by norm_num, by norm_num⟩ : unitInterval)`. [Binomial random graph basics](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Probability/Combinatorics/BinomialRandomGraph/Basic.lean) show that a fixed finite set of non-diagonal edges is present with probability `p ^ |s|` (`binomialRandom_real_subset_edgeSet`) and a fixed subgraph with probability `p ^ e(H)` (`binomialRandom_real_setOf_le`). These are constant cases of the edge-independence law in [EdgePattern](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Probability/Combinatorics/BinomialRandomGraph/EdgePattern.lean): a prescribed pattern `b` of edges and non-edges on a finite set `t` of non-diagonal pairs has probability `∏ e ∈ t, (if b e then p else 1 - p)` (`binomialRandom_real_edgePattern`, and `binomialRandom_real_edgePattern_of_injective` for pairs indexed by an injective family). The expected number of labelled copies of `H` is the falling factorial `|V| (|V| - 1) ⋯ (|V| - v(H) + 1)` times `p ^ e(H)` (`integral_labelledCopyCount`), and the union bound gives the same bound on the probability of containing `H` (`binomialRandom_real_isContained_le`). `tendsto_binomialRandom_real_isContained` is the first-moment half of the small-subgraph threshold theorem: if `n ^ v(H) p(n) ^ e(H) → 0`, then `G(n, p(n))` contains `H` with probability tending to `0`. Applied to the densest subgraph of `H`, this is the `0`-statement below `n ^ (-1 / m(H))`. [jansonluczakrucinski2000][jansonluczakrucinski2000]

[The clique module](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Probability/Combinatorics/BinomialRandomGraph/Clique.lean) proves the **triangle threshold** `p = 1 / n` with explicit bounds. A fixed `k`-set is a clique with probability `p ^ C(k, 2)` (`binomialRandom_real_isClique`), so the expected number of `k`-cliques is `C(|V|, k) p ^ C(k, 2)` (`integral_card_cliqueFinset`). The first moment gives `P(G(n, p) has a triangle) ≤ (n p) ^ 3 / 6` (`binomialRandom_real_not_cliqueFree_three_le`). The second moment gives `P(G(n, p) is triangle-free) ≤ 1 / (C(n, 3) p ^ 3) + 3 (n - 3) / (C(n, 3) p)` for `n ≥ 3` and `p > 0` (`binomialRandom_real_cliqueFree_three_le_choose`), and hence `≤ 16 / (n p) ^ 3 + 48 / (n p)` for `n ≥ 4` and `p > 0` (`binomialRandom_real_cliqueFree_three_le`). The `48 / (n p)` term is loose, since the exact covariance term is of order `1 / (n ^ 2 p)`, but a bound in `n p` alone suffices for the threshold. The asymptotic forms are `tendsto_binomialRandom_real_not_cliqueFree_three` (`n p(n) → 0`) and `tendsto_binomialRandom_real_cliqueFree_three` / `tendsto_binomialRandom_real_not_cliqueFree_three_nhds_one` (`n p(n) → ∞`). [alonspencer2016][alonspencer2016]

The second-moment step uses two measure-theoretic statements in [SecondMoment](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Probability/SecondMoment.lean). Under a probability measure, `measureReal_nonpos_le_variance_div_sq` bounds `P(X ≤ 0)` by `Var X / (E X) ^ 2` for square-integrable `X` (`MemLp X 2`) with `E X > 0`. For measurable events `A k` with `S = ∑ P(A k) > 0`, `measureReal_forall_notMem_le` bounds the probability that none of them occurs by `(∑∑ P(A k ∩ A l) - S ^ 2) / S ^ 2`. `integral_sum_indicator_one` computes the mean `S` of the count of events. For a count of edge-set events, each pair probability is again `p ^ |s ∪ t|`, so the variance computation reduces to counting overlaps. The [checked instances](../../CslibFrontierTests/BinomialRandomGraph.lean) cover `p = 0` and `p = 1`, the expected numbers of edges and triangles, and both sides of the threshold. They also show the bound is nonvacuous: `G(1000, 1/2)` contains a triangle with probability above `9 / 10`.

Only the triangle case (`v = e = 3`) of the threshold `n ^ (-v / e)` for balanced graphs is proved; the general balanced-graph second-moment theorem, Janson's inequality and the Poisson limit at the threshold are not formalized ([#3275](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/3275)). The connectivity threshold `log n / n` is covered under [random graph connectivity](#random-graph-connectivity). The uniform `G(n, 1/2)` of the [first-order 0-1 law](finite-model-logic.md) is a separate PMF construction; `binomialRandom_real_eq_randomGraphProb` shows that it agrees with `G(Fin n, 1/2)`, and the 0-1 law for constant `0 < p < 1` is stated for `binomialRandom` directly.

## Natural proofs and cryptographic assumptions

[NaturalProof.IsLarge](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/NaturalProof.lean#L147) separates largeness, constructivity and usefulness. A property is a family of sets of Boolean functions. Constructivity concerns deciding from a full truth table; polynomial in that input length is exponential in the function's arity. Usefulness excludes every easy language's slices eventually. Largeness is a quantitative density condition.

The Razborov–Rudich barrier is stated through concrete circuit distinguishers: a property excluding a small-circuit keyed family distinguishes its truth tables from uniform random function tables with advantage equal to the property's density. An exponentially secure keyed family prevents a P/poly-natural useful property. A [generator-to-barrier bridge](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/NaturalProof/Generator.lean) uses the GGM construction and its circuit-size analysis. In the current library, [not_isUseful_PPoly_of_isNatural_of_generator](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/NaturalProof/Generator.lean#L51) (registry ID `natural-proofs`) says that if some family of length-doubling generators of polynomial circuit complexity is subexponentially secure, then no P/poly-natural property is useful against P/poly. [razborovrudich1997][razborovrudich1997] [ggm86][ggm86] Security is an explicit hypothesis and belongs to the cryptographic model in the [programs section](../programs-crypto-and-probability/index.md), not an unconditional result.

## Lower-bound interfaces beyond one theorem

[Atom](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Fusion/Basic.lean#L60) represents a gate together with argument values as an atom and a finite collection of invariant failures as a cover. The central inequality `coverComplexity_le_ecomplexityFrom` lets a combinatorial cover lower bound imply a weighted source-relative circuit lower bound. This factors semantic lower-bound arguments away from a particular syntax traversal.

[SemifilterWitness](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Fusion/Semifilter.lean#L127) specializes the argument to monotone truth-valued maps on subsets that preserve the empty and full set. These maps need not preserve intersections, so they are not filters in the usual algebraic sense. Pair covers connect their witness failures to AND complexity, including the conondeterministic model in which both input polarities and OR gates are free.

[Scheme](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/13e6a31951bf1c89a1a6258c44f9444aecbbefd8/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Approximation.lean#L55) compares exact and approximate interpretations of the same circuit program. A correctness relation can be one-sided; each gate has a finite exception set with bounded cardinality. The approximate value is correct outside the union of gate exceptions, giving a failure bound by total charged cost. A shared gate is charged once even if its output has many uses. This reusable statement supports the monotone approximation method rather than reproving the same union argument for every lower bound.

Two finite counting lemmas in `Foundations/Combinatorics` turn a reconstruction or decoding argument into a count. [HybridPrediction.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/HybridPrediction.lean) is Yao's hybrid argument with the predictor's coins fixed. Suppose a decoder recovers a bit `b` from `T` pairs `(z t, E t (z t) + b)`, so the same `b` masks every channel. Then `exists_card_add_decode_update_eq` returns a channel `t`, fixed pairs at the other positions and a trial bit whose guess agrees with `E t` on at least `|Z t| (T + 1) / (2T)` inputs. The proof follows the next-bit telescoping in [Boneh and Shoup, Section 3.5][bs23]. Use it, not `Crypto.PRG.Generator.exists_half_add_div_le_predictionExperiment`, when the predictor sees public inputs `z t`: that lemma would count the bits of each `z t` among its hybrid positions. [WeightedTail.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Combinatorics/WeightedTail.lean) bounds how many functions `f : ∀ i, α i` hit fixed sets `C i` on a set of coordinates with a given property (`card_filter_pi_le_sum_prod`). With weights `w i` and `|C i| ≤ e^(-w i) |α i|`, at most a `2^|ι| e^(-T)` fraction of them hit coordinates of total weight at least `T` (`card_filter_le_sum_weight_le`). Union bounds over small circuits take their size from [CountingBound.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/CountingBound.lean): at most `2 ^ computableFunctionsLogBound n s` functions have complexity at most `s`. The [checked examples](../../CslibFrontierTests/HybridPrediction.lean) instantiate all three, including the `1/2 + 1/(2T)` form of the prediction bound.

A randomized reduction that samples Boolean functions from its random bits can use [FunctionTables.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Foundations/Data/BitString/FunctionTables.lean). `BitString.decodeTables B ℓ r` reads the truth table of the `v`-th function, which has `ℓ v` inputs, from bits `v * B + equivFin y` of the list `r`. Missing bits read `false`. When each table fits its block (`2 ^ ℓ v ≤ B`) and the `n` blocks fit in `p` bits, every family is decoded from the same number of the `2 ^ p` strings (`card_filter_decodeTables_mem_mul`). So a bound of the form "at most a `b / a` fraction of the families are bad" becomes the same bound on random strings (`mul_card_filter_decodeTables_mem_le`). The proof applies the library's `Nat.card_subtype_comp_injective_mul` (restricting functions along an injection has uniform fibers) to the positions of the tables. [CMMSA/Size.lean](../../CslibFrontier/Computability/Complexity/Problems/CMMSA/Size.lean) bounds the number of formulas, the number of variables, each weight and the threshold of a CMMSA instance by the length of its encoding, and shows that `GapCMMSA` is monotone in `Δ`. Both feed the seeded reduction to Partial MCSP planned in [#4362](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4362).

These are mathematical proof interfaces, not automatically encoded certificates or proof-search procedures. Other reusable measures in the query and formula layers—sensitivity, certificate complexity, degree, Khrapchenko edge counts—play a similar role while using different objects and costs.

## Known Limitations

The baseline survey at `13e6a319` inspected the conditional statements, truth-table scales and source-specific quantitative scope, but the complete magnification and cryptographic reduction proofs were not independently reconstructed in that worker pass. Distinguish a valid conditional theorem, a conventional open assumption, and a premise that this same library proves false elsewhere; the registry scope notes for results added since then record which case applies.

The [metacomplexity track](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/798) and the ROADMAP's Kolmogorov and metacomplexity entries list the remaining directions. A selection of those not formalized:

- Liu–Pass Theorem 3.1 (c) in the near-linear range `t(n) ≥ (1 + ε) n`, which needs a rate-1 efficient conditional EP-PRG.
- The efficient anti-checker of Oliveira, Pich and Santhanam (their Lemma 4.1). Existence, the greedy against a threshold oracle, its oracle circuit and, under `NP ⊆ P/poly`, `P/poly` circuits for the oracle on its queries are proved, but replacing the oracle gates by those circuits is not ([#4383](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4383)). Their Theorem 1.4 is proved by another route (see [Hardness magnification](#hardness-magnification)).
- Hirahara's NP-hardness of Partial MCSP and of `MINKTStar` (FOCS 2022; [#4362](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4362), [#4447](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4447)), and Hirahara–Watanabe on the limits of reductions to MCSP. NP-hardness of the total problems `MINKT` and `MINcKT` is an open problem.
- NP-hardness of Multi-output MCSP under randomized reductions (Ilango, Loff and Oliveira, CCC 2020); the problem, its NP membership, the window gadget and the reduction's correctness from `GapSetCover 3` are formalized; the reduction's polynomial running time and the hardness theorem are not yet proved ([#4405](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4405)).
- The minimum formula size problem (MFSP) as a separate problem, and the locality barrier (Theorem 59) of Chen, Hirahara, Oliveira, Pich, Rajgopal and Santhanam. Of their Proposition 63, the MCSP `[OS18]` bullet is formalized as class membership, and the `[MMW19]` bullet as an explicit oracle circuit with its counts, whose class-membership statement waits on the local circuit class ([#4430](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4430)); the other bullets are not formalized.
- Prefix-free complexity, Chaitin's `Ω`, exact prefix symmetry of information, and time-bounded symmetry of information.
